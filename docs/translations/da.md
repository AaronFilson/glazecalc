# Dansk: stilark og ordliste (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Danish, for potters in Denmark (rigsdansk, da-DK). Every Danish
message and guide follows this page: one Danish word for one thing. The terms come from Danish suppliers' own pages and
catalogues (Silica Nordic, Cerama), a Danish potter's glaze pages, Nabertherm's Danish manuals, Danish Wikipedia and
lex.dk, and the Danish texts of the law (Arbejdstilsynet's limit-value order, Fødevarestyrelsen's food-contact order, the
CLP Regulation), not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal, met by leaving out the pronoun. Danish software says _du_ (MS: »Tiltal brugeren med du i stedet
for De«); the old formal _De/Dem/Deres_ reads stiff and odd today. Glazecalc avoids both wherever it can:

- **Buttons and menu commands**: the imperative (bydeform), no pronoun: »Gem«, »Gem som kopi«, »Udskriv«,
  »Sammenlign«, »Tilføj råvarer«, »Kassér prøveperioden«. Not the infinitive (»At gemme«) and not a noun (»Gemning«).
  Imperatives of verbs in _-ere_ take the accent where they could be misread (MS: »markér«, »kontrollér«, »kopiér«):
  »Registrér«, »Kassér«, »Markér«.
- **Instructions** (help text and guides): the imperative: »Sigt glasuren to gange.« »Vælg Sammenlign.« Two
  imperative clauses take a comma (MS: »Klik på Start, og vælg Indstillinger.«); short button labels do not (»Gem og
  luk«).
- **Questions and confirmations**: the passive, as MS does (»Skal ændringerne i Dokument1 gemmes?«): »Skal opskriften
  slettes?«, »Gemt: …«.
- **The user's own things**: the definite form, not _din/dit/dine_: »Opskriften er gemt.«, »Prøveperioden udløber
  {date}.« First-person labels stay first person, as in Danish Windows: »Mine råvarer«, »Slet min konto«.
- **Warnings**: imperative with _altid/aldrig_, or the passive with _skal_ (must) and _bør_ (should), as the English
  has them: »Bær altid åndedrætsværn.« »Glasuren bør testes for afgivelse af metaller.« Never turn _bør_ into _skal_.
- **"Please"** is left out (MS: »Prøv igen senere.«); "Sorry" is »Beklager« only where the English has it.
- **When a sentence cannot do without a pronoun** and rephrasing makes it unclear, write _du_ (lower case), never _De_.
  Keep this rare; first try the passive, the definite form or a noun phrase. _Man_ is fine for general statements
  (»Man kan også …«), sparingly. _Vi_ for the app only where the English says "we".

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs** (the convention of Danish Windows and Office, MS):

- Buttons and commands: imperative, as above.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a noun or noun phrase (»Opskrifter«, »Indstillinger«,
  »Brændingsjournaler«, »Kegler og varmepåvirkning«). A heading that is a question or an instruction in English may
  stay one.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: »Råvarens navn:«.
- Confirmations: past participle first, as the English: "Saved: …" → »Gemt: …«.

**Capitalisation.** Danish rules (MS 4.1.6): only the first word and proper nouns take a capital, in headings, buttons
and labels too; nouns are lower case (unlike German). No English Title Case: "Fired Weight" → »Brændt vægt«. Languages,
nationalities, months and weekdays are lower case (»engelske fagudtryk«, »dansk«). After a colon, a capital only if a
full sentence follows. Names keep their own form (Orton, Seger, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc).

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Commas: the grammatical (traditional) comma, as MS uses, including before subordinate clauses; no comma before _og_
  or _eller_ in a list (»kaolin, kvarts og kridt«).
- Quotation marks: »…« (U+00BB, U+00AB), as Danish print and the EU's Danish texts write them (CLP-DA: »signalord«,
  »fare«); inside them ›…‹. Not "…" or “…”.
- Apostrophe: the typographic ’ (U+2019), used in the genitive of words ending in s, x, z and with inflected
  abbreviations (»SDS’et«, MS »cd’er«). Write `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`, `}` or `#`, and in
  `safety`, `regions` and `records`). Do not add a genitive _-s_ to a placeholder ("{name}'s recipe"): rephrase
  (»opskriften {name}«).
- No space before ; : ! ? (as in English).
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, unit once at the end: »1222–1240 °C«, »kegle 06–04«, »1–3 %«. (MS replaces it
  with a hyphen for technical reasons; Glazecalc keeps the English's en dash.) In a sentence »fra 1222 til 1240 °C« is
  also fine; never mix (»fra 1222–1240 °C«).
- Compounds are one word, as Danish writes them (»glasurberegner«, »ovnplade«). With a number, symbol or abbreviation,
  a hyphen: »FFP3-maske«, »SiO₂-indhold«, »CMC-gummi«.

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma, always with the leading zero (MS): »1,5 %«, »0,01 g«, »0,5«. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as
  written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped (»1240 °C«, »1000 g«), as the app prints them
  (`useGrouping: 'min2'`); five digits and more take a period (»12.500 g«). The check reads both.
- A space before °C, °F, % and every unit (MS: »50 %«, »150 MB«): »1240 °C«, »15 %«, »100 °C/h«, »80 mesh«. (Suppliers
  often print »1241°C«; do not copy that.) A plain space is fine.
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word (MS spells out 0–10 in running text; not here). Clock times
  with a colon (»kl. 8:30«), which the check reads as the English.

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Danish `one` and `other`:

- `one`: 1 → »1 opskrift«, »1 råvare«, »1 felt«. CLDR also puts decimals whose whole part is 0 or 1 here (0,5, 1,5),
  as Danish says »1,5 time«.
- `other`: 0, 2, 5, 100 → »0 opskrifter«, »2 råvarer«, »5 felter«.
- Danish nouns have no cases, only the genitive _-s_. Adjectives and participles agree: plural _-e_ (»1 gemt opskrift«,
  »3 gemte opskrifter«; »1 udgået råvare«, »2 udgåede råvarer«), neuter singular _-t_ (»1 nyt felt«, »2 nye felter«).
  A unit symbol does not change (»2 g«, »5 kg«).

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Danish shops sell Orton cones under that name, »Ortonkegle«
(SN »Ortonkegle nr. 6 (1241 °C)«, CE »Ortonkegle, cone 010, 915 °C«); Danish Wikipedia still calls cones in general
»segerkegler«, and true Seger cones (SK) are a different series whose numbers do not match Orton's. So:

- "cone 6" → »kegle 6«; "cone 06" → »kegle 06« (read »nul seks«; the zero stays: kegle 06 is far cooler than kegle 6).
  Not »cone 6« (as CE's product names) and not »nr. 6«.
- "Orton cone" → »Ortonkegle« (one word, as SN and CE); at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are
  compared: »Ortonkegle 6«, then »kegle 6«.
- "cone 04 bisque" → »forglødning til kegle 04«; "fire to cone 6" → »brænd til kegle 6«; "a cone 6 glaze" → »en glasur
  til kegle 6«; "cone 5–6" → »kegle 5–6«.
- **Never** »segerkegle 6« or »SK 6« for an Orton number. »Segerkegle« appears only where the English says "Seger
  cones".

**The unity formula.** »Seger-formel« (en; Seger-formlen, Seger-formler), as Danish Wikipedia writes it (»ved at
benytte Seger-formler«), with »(UMF)« after it the first time on a page: »Seger-formel (UMF)«. After that
»Seger-formel«. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are all »Seger-formel« /
»Seger-formel:«.

**Gender-neutral wording.** Danish person nouns are neutral (keramiker, pottemager, bruger). For a generic person
avoid _han/hun_ (MS): use the plural, the role, or _vedkommende_. Do not use _hen_.

**The reader in the guides.** The imperative and the passive, in short plain sentences, as the English: »Sigt glasuren
to gange gennem en sigte på 80 mesh.« »Test glasuren på det ler, der bruges.« Not »De«, and _du_ only as the register
rule allows. Avoid nominal style (»Sigtningen af glasuren foretages …«). Quotations from sources are translated into
»…« and keep their figures as the English writes them.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with a capital first letter. Where the English wraps the
name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag and add no quotation marks. Where it has no tag, put the button's name in
»…« when the reader is told to use it (Vælg »Sammenlign«.), and otherwise use the ordinary noun (Sammenligningen viser …).
"Settings" is never in quotes: »under Indstillinger«. The fixed names:

**The guides.** "guide" is »vejledning« (en; vejledningen, vejledninger), as the menu says ("Guides" → »Vejledninger«);
a guide calls itself »denne vejledning«, never »guide« or »artikel«. The five titles are written exactly the same in the
guide's `title:`, its page title (`titles` in `da.json`, with » - Glazecalc« after it), the guide list
(`guides/da.json`, `list`) and the text of every link that names the guide:

| English                       | Danish                         |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ |

| How to make a glaze           | Sådan laver man en glasur      |
| Safe mixing and ventilation   | Sikker blanding og ventilation |

Not »Glasering fra bunden« or »Sådan laves en glasur«. A link may name a guide by what it is about instead (»vejledningen
om brænding«); then it is not a title.

**EU law.** As the EU's and Denmark's own Danish texts write it: "Directive 84/500/EEC" → »Rådets direktiv 84/500/EØF«
(EØF, not EEC or EWG), amended by »Kommissionens direktiv 2005/31/EF« (BEK 681); "Directive (EU) 2017/2398" →
»direktiv (EU) 2017/2398«; regulations »forordning (EF) nr. 1272/2008« (CLP), »forordning (EF) nr. 1935/2004«.
_direktiv_ and _forordning_ are lower case in running text. Danish orders: »bekendtgørelse nr. 681 af 25. maj 2020«.

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **Stentøj** is stoneware; **lertøj** is earthenware. German _Steingut_ (earthenware) looks like _stentøj_: never
  confuse them. **Fajance** is tin-glazed earthenware (and wall tiles), not earthenware in general; **flintegods** is
  white industrial earthenware, neither stoneware nor flint.
- **Glasur**, never »lasur« (wood stain) or »emalje« (enamel on metal). **Råglasur** in Danish books is an unfritted
  glaze (lex.dk: »Råglasur er en blanding af bestanddele, fx kaolin, kvarts og feldspat«), not raw glazing.
- **Blank** means glossy (»Stentøjsglasur Transparent blank«), not blank.
- **Vægt** is both weight and the weighing scale; say »vægten« (the scale) or »vægt i gram« (the weight) so the
  sentence is clear.
- **Soda** is sodium carbonate (soda ash), **natron** is baking soda, **pottaske** is potassium carbonate. The potters'
  oxides "soda" and "potash" are »natrium (Na₂O)« and »kalium (K₂O)«.
- **Silicium** is the element, **silikone** the rubber; the oxide is »silica« (da.wiki: »Siliciumdioxid også kendt som
  silica«), the material »kvarts«, »kvartsmel«.
- **Lerjord** (old Danish for Al₂O₃) now reads as clay soil: write »aluminiumoxid«.
- **Kalk** is lime (CaO) or limestone; the material whiting is »kridt« (as on the bag).
- **Respirator** in Danish is a hospital ventilator. The mask is »åndedrætsværn« or »støvmaske«; see 2.7.
- **Ovn** is a kiln and a kitchen oven: write »keramikovn« where the reader could take one for the other (the
  home-safety guide).
- **Brænding** is a firing; **brand** is a fire (Nabertherm: »I tilfælde af brand«). In safety text use »brand«,
  »brandfare«.
- **Farlig** in the CLP hazard statements means _harmful_ (»Farlig ved indtagelse«, H302), while the signal word
  »Fare« means _Danger_; keep them apart (2.7).
- **Lavapletter** (SN), »lavaplet« (CE) are decorative specks sold for glazes; dark spots as a defect are »mørke
  prikker«.
- **Mesh**: Danish shops sell sieves by mesh (»Sigte Ø 25 cm – 72 mesh«, SN; »60 mesh«, CE). Keep "80 mesh" as »80
  mesh«.
- **Swedish and Norwegian forms** to avoid: »glasyr«, »skrøjbrænding« (Swedish _skröjbränning_), »råbrænding«
  (Norwegian _råbrenning_), »kägla«. Danish sources say »forglødning« for bisque (NT, CE, EB, da.wiki).
- **English in Danish shop names**: CE writes »Ortonkegle, cone 010«, SN »Feldspat Potash«, »Ball Clay / Pibeler«. Use
  the Danish words this glossary gives; keep the English only in names (see 3).

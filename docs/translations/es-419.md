# Español (Latinoamérica): style sheet and glossary

How to translate Glazecalc into Latin American Spanish (`es-419`): one Spanish for all of Latin America, Mexico first
(the largest market, region MX), with the Southern Cone's forms (Argentina, Chile) recorded in the Notes. Read
[README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term. This sheet is built on
[es.md](es.md) (Spain): **every rule and glossary row of es.md applies unless this page changes it.** The table in
section 0 lists every change; sections 1 and 2 give the full rule or row where it differs. Translate from the English;
es.md is a reference for terms only, never for its register (usted) or its Spain-only words.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Verified on 2026-10-31: the title and dates of NOM-231-SSA1-2016
([platiica][nom231]); the number and date formats of CLDR es-419, es-MX, es-AR, es-CL, es-CO, es-PE (read from Node's
ICU, see section 1); that Microsoft publishes a Spanish (Mexico) style guide ([MS es-MX][ms-mx]). **Not verified:** the
guide's own text on _tú_/_usted_ (the PDF's text could not be extracted; the register rule below rests on the plan's
reading and on Microsoft's current Latin American products, and the lead should confirm it in the PDF, section "Software
considerations" / address forms). No Mexican or Argentine ceramics shop catalogue was read this round, so the ceramics
rows that differ from es.md are **Native check** unless a source is named. Sources are listed at the end; none is
invented.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register: _tú_, always, in the interface and the guides.** Instructions use the _tú_ imperative: _Elige el cono_,
_Tamiza el esmalte dos veces_, _Pesa cada material en la báscula_. Possessives _tu/tus_. The plural "you" is
_ustedes_ (_si ustedes comparten el taller_), never _vosotros_. Never _vos_ or voseo forms (_elegí_, _tamizá_), even
though Argentina, Uruguay and much of Central America use them: they read as one country. Never mix _tú_ and _usted_
on one page. Source: Microsoft's Spanish (Mexico) style guide is the reference for Latin American software ([MS
es-MX][ms-mx]); its text was not extracted this round (see the top of this page). If the lead finds it says _usted_,
this rule changes before translation starts.

**Buttons, menu items, tabs and links that do something: the infinitive**, as es.md (_Guardar_, _Imprimir_, _Comparar_,
_Quitar_, _Descartar la prueba_). Messages that tell the reader what to do are _tú_ sentences: _Escribe un número, como
{example}._ A link that reads as a sentence keeps _tú_ (_Crea una cuenta para conservarlo_); a button-like link takes the
infinitive (_Crear una cuenta_).

**Capitalisation, spacing, `¿ ¡`, the degree sign, ranges, plurals, cones, gender-neutral wording**: as es.md. Gender:
not _¿Estás seguro?_ but _¿Quieres quitarla?_; not _Bienvenido_ but _Te damos la bienvenida_ or _Hola de nuevo_; not
_Conectado como {name}_ but _Iniciaste sesión como {name}_ (Latin American Spanish prefers the simple past here, not
_Has iniciado_).

**Quotation marks: “ ” first, ‘ ’ inside** (_“un ‘cono’ caído”_). The full stop or comma goes after the closing mark,
as in es.md. Never the straight `"`.

**Numbers.** The translated text follows **CLDR es-419**, which the numbers check reads (scripts/i18n-check.mjs). Read
from Node 24's ICU on 2026-10-31:

| Locale              | 1234.5  | 12345.6  | Date (14 Oct 2026)    | Plural categories |
| ------------------- | ------- | -------- | --------------------- | ----------------- |
| es-419              | 1,234.5 | 12,345.6 | 14 de octubre de 2026 | one/many/other    |
| es-MX               | 1,234.5 | 12,345.6 | 14 de octubre de 2026 | one/many/other    |
| es-PE, es-US        | 1,234.5 | 12,345.6 | 14 de octubre de 2026 | one/many/other    |
| es-AR, es-CL, es-CO | 1.234,5 | 12.345,6 | 14 de octubre de 2026 | one/many/other    |

- Decimals with a **point**: _1.5_, _0.45_, _gravedad específica 1.45_.
- Temperatures and four-digit amounts: write them as the English writes them (_1222 °C_, _1000 g_); do not add a
  separator the English lacks, and do not remove one it has. The app's own formatted numbers come from the reader's
  region.
- With region AR or CL chosen, formatted numbers show 1,5 while the translated text says 1.5. This is owner question 1;
  do not decide it in a translation.
- A space before `%` and `°C` as in es.md (_1.5 %_, _1240 °C_). Dates _14 de octubre de 2026_; the 24-hour clock
  (_20:00_). The format example "12,345.6 or 12.345,6" stays as it is.

**Plurals**: `one`, `many`, `other`, as es (same CLDR rules, table above). Write all three.

**Cones**: as es.md (_cono 6_, _cono 06_, _conos Orton_). Mexico, Argentina and Chile buy Orton cones; never write Seger
for an Orton number.

**The unity formula: _fórmula unitaria_.** First time on a page: _fórmula unitaria (fórmula Seger, UMF)_; after that
_fórmula unitaria_. App short forms: panel "Unity formula" _Fórmula unitaria_; "Unity:" _Unitaria:_; "Molecular / Unity"
_Molecular / Unitaria_. Native check: if a Latin American potter says _fórmula Seger_ is the common name, swap the two.

**Lead: never softened.** Mexico has a living tradition of lead-glazed _barro vidriado_ (the glaze is called _greta_),
and lead in glazed ware is regulated by NOM-231-SSA1-2016, "Artículos de alfarería vidriada, cerámica vidriada,
porcelana y artículos de vidrio – Límites máximos permisibles de plomo y cadmio solubles – Método de ensayo" (DOF,
25 October 2016; in force 24 December 2016; [platiica][nom231]). So:

- Every lead warning keeps the English's full force: _tóxico_ stays _tóxico_, "never" stays _nunca_, "do not" stays
  _no_ + verb. No _evita en lo posible_, _se recomienda no_, _podría_ where the English is plain.
- Do not add reassurance the English lacks (_el barro tradicional es seguro si…_). Do not add figures from NOM-231; the
  translation names only what the English names.
- _Sin plomo_ is not _apto para alimentos_ (as es.md). _Libre de plomo_ is also common in Mexico: use _sin plomo_.
- Where the English says lead glaze in a traditional context, _esmalte de plomo_; _greta_ only beside it for Mexico if
  the English names that ware (_el esmalte de plomo, la greta del barro vidriado_).

**Law and bodies** (glossary words, not added text): Mexico NOM-231-SSA1-2016 (food contact), NOM-010-STPS-2014
(chemical agents at work) and the STPS; Argentina Código Alimentario Argentino and SRT; Chile DS 594. Only NOM-231 was
read this round; give the others exactly as the region data writes them.

**Home-safety guide.** _horno_ is also the kitchen oven: say _horno de cerámica_ where it could be read either way.
Babies crawling: _gatear_.

**Pitfalls added for Latin America**:

- _Coger_ is vulgar in much of Latin America: use _tomar_, _agarrar_ (informal) or _sujetar_.
- _Concha_, _pico_, _pinche_ and others are innocent in one country and rude in another: keep to the glossary.
- _Ordenador_, _móvil_, _fichero_, _vale_, _vosotros_, _serrín_, _ratón_ for a mouse is fine (also Latin American).
- _Mufla_ is a lab furnace in most countries: not the kiln.
- _Barniz_ is varnish (and, in Mexico, the lacquer craft): never the glaze.
- _Plomo_ appears in _barro vidriado_ ware sold for food: never translate a lead warning into something milder.

### The five guides' titles

One each, the same in the guide's `title:`, `titles` in `es-419.json`, the list in `guides/es-419.json` and every
link: Glazing basics → **Los fundamentos del esmaltado**; Making a glaze → **Cómo preparar un esmalte**; Safe mixing and
ventilation → **Preparación segura y ventilación**; Firing a basic kiln → **La cocción en un horno sencillo**; Don't
poison your family → **No envenenes a tu familia** (the owner's blunt form, _tú_).

## Choices for the owner and a native potter

1. **Decimal mark** (owner): text follows CLDR es-419 (1.5); AR, CL, CO readers' formatted numbers show 1,5. Accept?
2. **_tú_** (lead): confirm in the Microsoft es-MX PDF before translation.
3. Native potter: _fórmula unitaria_ vs _fórmula Seger_; _gravedad específica_ vs _densidad_; _cocción_ vs _quema_;
   _bizcocho_ vs _sancocho_; _Configuración_; kiln wash; _respirador_.
4. **Four-digit grouping** (lead): CLDR es-419 groups 1,240 (es groups only from five digits). Check that
   scripts/i18n-check.mjs accepts _1222 °C_ in es-419 text unchanged before translating.

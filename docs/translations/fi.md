# Suomi: tyyliohje ja sanasto (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Finnish (`fi`), standard written Finnish (yleiskieli) for potters in Finland. Read
[README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Finnish message
and guide follows this page: one Finnish word for one thing. The terms come from the two Finnish shops that sell glaze
materials (Kerasil, Varnia), the Finnish ceramics textbooks of Aalto University and of Airi Hortling, a potters' guild,
the Finnish texts of EU law, and Työterveyslaitos (TTL) for safety, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree
or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a Finnish potter to check.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

Formal, met by leaving the reader unnamed. Finnish software does not address the reader with the formal _Te_
(_teitittely_), which reads as stiff and old-fashioned. MS (4.1.12) addresses the reader in the second person singular
but drops the pronoun: "sinä" and "te" are used sparingly, "you" is often left untranslated; sentences should not begin
with a second-person verb (4.1.16); instructions use the imperative ("Tallenna muutokset valitsemalla Käytä."); error
messages are usually passive ("Tiedostoa ei voi tallentaa."). Glazecalc takes the impersonal side of that practice.
The forms:

- **Commands** (buttons, menu items, links that do something): the **imperative singular**, verb first, no pronoun:
  ”Tallenna”, ”Tulosta”, ”Vertaa”, ”Poista tili”. This is the form of every major Finnish interface (MS: "Valitse
  Tallenna."; Kerasil's cart button ”Lisää ostoskoriin”). Not the infinitive (”Tallentaa”), not a noun (”Tallennus”),
  not the plural (”Tallentakaa”).
- **Instructions** in guides and help: the imperative singular, no pronoun: ”Siivilöi lasite kahdesti.”, ”Käytä
  hengityksensuojainta.”, ”Älä hengitä pölyä.” The EU's official Finnish precautionary statements use exactly this form
  (CLP: ”Älä hengitä pölyä/savua/kaasua/sumua/höyryä/suihketta.”, ”Käytä hengityksensuojainta.”), as do Kerasil's
  instructions (”Hauduta lasituspoltossa 10-30 minuuttia”).
- **Statements about the reader** ("if you fire to cone 6", "your clay"): no second person. Use the passive (”Kun
  poltetaan keilaan 6, …”), the zero person (”Jos lasite valuu, sitä voi ohentaa.”), or name the thing (”oma savi”,
  ”käytetty savi”). Do not begin a sentence with a second-person verb (”Poltat …”) and avoid second-person possessive
  suffixes (_savesi_, _tilisi_) where ”oma” or no possessive reads naturally.
- **Questions and confirmations**: the passive: ”Poistetaanko resepti?”, ”Tallennetaanko muutokset?” Not ”Haluatko
  …?”.
- **Errors and warnings**: passive or a noun phrase, as MS's standard phrases: ”Reseptiä ei löydy.”, ”Tallennus ei
  onnistunut.” Warnings to act take the imperative: ”Älä koskaan …”.
- "please": leave it out (”Yritä uudelleen.”). "we": ”Suosittelemme …” is fine for the app's own advice (MS allows it);
  "We can't find …" becomes the passive (”… ei löydy”).

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- Buttons and menu commands: imperative singular, as above: ”Tallenna kopiona”, ”Lisää raaka-aineita”, ”Hylkää
  kokeilu”.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: ”Reseptit”, ”Asetukset”,
  ”Polttopäiväkirjat”, ”Keilat ja lämpötyö”. A heading that names an activity takes the _-minen_ noun (MS 4.1.16):
  "Mixing the glaze" → ”Lasitteen sekoittaminen”, "Typing amounts" → ”Määrien kirjoittaminen”. A heading that is a
  question stays one.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: ”Raaka-aineen nimi:”.
- Confirmations: the past participle first, as the English: "Saved: …" → ”Tallennettu: …”.

### Capitalisation

Finnish rules only (MS 4.1.4): the first word and proper names capitalised, everything else lower case, in headings,
buttons, labels and tabs too. No English Title Case: "Fired Weight" → ”Paino poltettuna”. Languages, nationalities,
months and days are lower case (”suomi”, ”saksalainen liitu”). Trade and product names keep their own form (Orton,
Seger, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc, ”Kaoliini Grolleg”).

### Punctuation

- Quotation marks: ”…” (U+201D at both ends); inside them ’…’ (U+2019 at both ends). Not straight "…", not the
  English “…” or the German „…“, and not »…«. MS allows straight quotes only where a tool cannot produce ”. Use
  quotation marks for real quotations only; never put a button or page name in quotes (MS: "Click "Save."" →
  ”Valitse Tallenna.”).
- Apostrophe: the typographic ’ (U+2019). Finnish needs it inside words where consonant gradation drops a k: _vaaka_ →
  _vaa’an_, _vaa’assa_ (the weighing scale). Write `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`, `}` or `#`, and in
  the `safety`, `regions` and `records` files).
- No space before ; : ! ?.
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, the unit once at the end: ”1222–1240 °C”, ”keilat 06–04”, ”1–3 %”. As a dash in a
  sentence (_ajatusviiva_), the en dash takes a space on both sides. Never the em dash (MS).
- Case endings after a numeral, abbreviation or symbol follow a colon: ”EU:n”, ”10 %:n”, ”1240 °C:ssa”, ”2 cm:n”
  (Kotus). Where the sentence allows, give the case to a noun instead and leave the figure bare: ”lämpötilassa 1240 °C”,
  ”keilaan 6” (see Numbers and Cones).
- Compounds with a numeral, symbol or code take a hyphen: ”FFP3-suojain”, ”SiO₂-pitoisuus”, ”Orton-keila”. When the
  first part is several words, a space comes before the hyphen: ”Ferro 3134 -fritti”, ”Ehdota määriä -toiminto” (MS:
  ”Näytä piilotetut kuvakkeet -painiketta”).

### Numbers and units

- Decimal comma: ”1,5 %”, ”0,01 g”, ”1,45”. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: Kotus groups from 1 000 (”1 609 m”), but the app prints four-digit numbers ungrouped ("1222 °C",
  `useGrouping: 'min2'` in `client/app/shared/format.ts`), and the `{{…}}` figures in the guides come out the same way.
  So write **four-digit numbers ungrouped** (”1240 °C”, ”1000 g”), and group five digits and more with a **no-break
  space** U+00A0 (”12 500 g”). Never a plain space (the check reads 12 500 with a plain space as two numbers) and never
  a dot.
- A space before °C, °F, % and every unit: ”1240 °C”, ”15 %”, ”100 °C/h”, ”80 mesh”. Degrees of angle take no space.
- A number in the English is a number in the Finnish, with the same symbol. Never replace the symbol with a word:
  ”1240 astetta” for "1240 °C" and ”15-prosenttinen” for "15%" break the check and the README's rule. Write ”15 %:n
  lisäys”, ”lämpötilassa 1240 °C” or ”1240 °C:ssa”. A unit's case ending goes after a colon and follows how the unit
  is read: °C and °F are both read ”astetta”, so ”{{2232 °F; 1222 °C}}:ssa” reads right on either scale; prefer
  ”lämpötilassa {{2232 °F; 1222 °C}}”, which needs no ending.
- Inches and feet: ”tuuma” (2 tuumaa), ”jalka” (2 jalkaa), with the metric figure in brackets as the English
  gives it.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Finnish two categories:
`node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('fi').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"` prints `[ 'one', 'other' ]`.

| Category | Numbers                  | Example                                                    |
| -------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `one`    | 1                        | 1 raaka-aine, 1 resepti                                    |
| `other`  | 0, 2–20, 21, 101, 1,5, … | 2 raaka-ainetta, 5 raaka-ainetta, 21 reseptiä, 1,5 grammaa |

After a numeral other than 1, in the nominative or accusative, the noun is **partitive singular** (”2 reseptiä”), and
an adjective with it too (”2 uutta reseptiä”). A numeral subject takes a **singular verb**: ”2 raaka-ainetta puuttuu”.
`=0` takes the partitive plural: ”ei reseptejä”. In every other case numeral and noun agree and the noun stays singular
(”kahdessa reseptissä”, ”viidellä raaka-aineella”); `#` and `{count}` cannot take an ending, so build the sentence so
the number phrase is nominative or partitive: ”Reseptejä: {count}”, ”{count} reseptiä sisältää lyijyä”, not
”{count}:ssa reseptissä”. Write both forms even where they read alike.

### Cones

The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Finnish shops sell Orton cones as ”keila”: Kerasil ”Keila Orton nro 6
1222°C/1243°C”, Varnia ”Orton-keilat” (self-supporting, standard and mini). Varnia also sells **real Seger cones**
(”Seger-keilat”, numbers such as 06a at 995 °C and 6a at 1260 °C), a different scale whose numbers do not match
Orton's, and Hortling gives stoneware firing in Seger numbers (”Sk.8-10”). Aalto: ”On olemassa itsestään seisovia
(esimerkiksi Orton) ja tukemista tarvitsevia keiloja (esimerkiksi Seger).” So:

- "cone 6" → ”keila 6”; "cone 06" → ”keila 06” (the zero stays; read ”nolla kuusi”; keila 06 is far cooler than keila
  6).
- The number follows the noun and stays bare; the **noun takes the case** (Lasi- ja keramiikkakeskus Kuu: ”vähintään
  keilan 10 kestäviä”): "fire to cone 6" → ”polttaa keilaan 6”; "at cone 6" → ”keilassa 6”; "a cone 6 glaze" →
  ”keilan 6 lasite” or ”lasite keilaan 6”; "cone 5–6" → ”keilat 5–6”. Never ”6:een” or ”keila 6:ssa”.
- "cone 04 bisque" → ”raakapoltto keilaan 04”; "a cone 04 bisque" → ”keilan 04 raakapoltto”.
- "Orton cone" → ”Orton-keila” (Varnia's spelling); at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared:
  ”Orton-keila 6”, then ”keila 6”.
- **Never** write ”Seger-keila 6”, ”Sk 6” or ”SK 6a” for an Orton number. ”Seger-keila” appears only where the English
  says "Seger cones", and then means the SK series.
- Not ”kartio”: that is the geometric and the traffic cone.

### The unity formula

”Segerin kaava”, with ”(UMF)” after it the first time on a page: ”Segerin kaava (UMF)”. Aalto and Hortling both write
”Empiirinen kaava eli Segerin kaava”, and Aalto names "Unit molecular formula eli UMF". After the first mention
”Segerin kaava” (gen. Segerin kaavan, iness. Segerin kaavassa). The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula"
and "Unity:" are all ”Segerin kaava” / ”Segerin kaava:”. Do not alternate with ”empiirinen kaava” in the app: one name
for one thing.

### Gender-neutral wording

Finnish has no grammatical gender and one pronoun, _hän_, for everyone, so most sentences need nothing. Follow MS 3.1:
avoid compounds with _-mies_ (”ammattilainen”, not ”ammattimies”; ”esihenkilö”, not ”esimies”) and give
generalisations in the plural (”keraamikot”, ”aloittelijat”). A potter is ”keraamikko”.

### The reader in the guides

The imperative singular for every step, in short plain sentences as the English: ”Siivilöi lasite kahdesti 80 meshin
seulan läpi.” The passive or zero person for explanations where the English says "you": ”Kun lasite on liian paksu,
se valuu.”, ”Raakapoltetun esineen voi lasittaa heti.” No _sinä_, no _Te_, no sentence opening with a second-person
verb, and no nominal style (”Siivilöinnin suorittaminen tapahtuu …”). Quotations from sources are translated into ”…”
and keep their figures as the English writes them.

### The app's feature names

Written exactly as on the button or tab, first letter capital, **without quotation marks** (MS). Where the English
wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag. A name cannot take a case ending without changing, so attach a
generic noun with a hyphen and inflect that (space before the hyphen when the name is several words): ”Valitse
Vertaa.”, ”Vertaa-toiminto näyttää …”, ”Ehdota määriä -toiminnolla”. "Settings" in running text is the ordinary noun:
”asetuksissa”; inside `<settings>…</settings>` write the word the sentence needs (”<settings>asetuksissa</settings>”).

| Suggest amounts (and compare) | Ehdota määriä (ja vertaa) | Ehdota määriä -toiminto                         |

### EU law

As the EU's own Finnish texts name them, with the lower-case generic word: ”neuvoston direktiivi 84/500/ETY” (ETY =
Euroopan talousyhteisö), amended by ”komission direktiivi 2005/31/EY” (EUR-Lex FI); ”Euroopan parlamentin ja neuvoston
direktiivi (EU) 2017/2398”; ”asetus (EY) N:o 1272/2008” (CLP). Short form in running text: ”direktiivi 84/500/ETY”.
Never ”84/500/EEC” or ”84/500/EWG”.

### Pitfalls

- **Lasite** is a glaze; **lasitus** is the act of glazing, and also window and balcony glazing; **emali** is enamel on
  metal (Varnia sells ”Emalityötarvikkeet” apart from its glazes); **lakka** is varnish. Never emali or lakka for glaze.
- **Sulate** means both frit and flux: Kerasil's frit category is ”Sulatteet” (”Ferro-sulate 3134”), Hortling writes
  ”sulatteina (fritteinä)”, while fi.wikipedia (Lasite) calls the flux ”sulate”. The app writes **fritti** for frit and
  **sulattaja** for flux (Aalto: ”Sulattajat RO”), and never ”sulate” on its own.
- **Säätöaine** means both flocculant (Kerasil ”Säätöaine lasitteelle Stellmittel”) and deflocculant (Aalto
  ”Säätöaineet eli deflokkulantit”; Kerasil ”Säätöaine Dispex”, ”Vesilasi säätöaine”). Write ”flokkulantti” and
  ”deflokkulantti” (2.6).
- **Raaka-** is a trap: ”raakapoltto” is the bisque firing and ”raakapoltettu” bisqued ware; ”raaka-aine” is a raw
  material; Hortling's ”raakalasite” is a glaze mixed from raw materials. So greenware is ”polttamaton esine”, "raw"
  as in not calcined is ”kalsinoimaton”, and "raw lead" is ”fritittämätön lyijy”.
- **Fajanssi** is tin-glazed or white earthenware (faience) and **terrakotta** unglazed red ware; earthenware in general
  is **savitavara** (fi.wikipedia), and the Finnish red earthenware clay **punasavi**.
- **Kalkki** is lime (CaO, calcium compounds) and **kalkkikivi** limestone; whiting is **liitu**.
- **Sooda** is sodium carbonate (soda ash) and **potaska** potassium carbonate; the potters' oxides "soda" and "potash"
  are ”natrium (Na₂O)” and ”kalium (K₂O)”.
- **Pii** is the element silicon and **silikoni** the rubber; the oxide SiO₂ is **piidioksidi**, the material
  **kvartsi**.
- **Respiraattori** in Finnish is a hospital ventilator. The mask is ”hengityksensuojain” (CLP, TTL).
- **Uuni** is a kiln and a kitchen oven; write ”keramiikkauuni” where the context does not settle it. **Poltto** is a
  firing; a fire as a hazard is ”palo” (”palovaara”), flames ”tuli”.
- **Pilkku** is also the decimal comma; ”pilkuttaja” in Varnia's list is a decorative speckling granule, not a defect
  (2.5).
- **Consonant gradation** changes stems: maasälpä → maasälvän, fritti → fritin, poltto → polton, liitu → liidun, talkki
  → talkin, kolmijalka → kolmijalan, vaaka → vaa’an. The glossary gives these forms.
- **Placeholders and trade names cannot be inflected.** Put a generic noun in the needed case beside them: ”raaka-aine
  {name}”, ”reseptissä {name}”, ”{name} -reseptissä”, ”fritti Ferro 3134” (MS 4.1.7: compounds with a stem element
  where the placeholder cannot take an ending).
- **mesh**: Finnish sieves are sold and described in mesh (Kerasil ”80 mesh seula”, Aalto ”100 meshin seulan läpi”).
  Keep ”80 mesh”, inflected ”80 meshin”.
- **Numbers as words**: Finnish style (MS 4.1.10, Kotus) spells out one to ten in running text, but in Glazecalc a
  number in the English stays a number (README), so the check finds it: ”3 kerrosta”, not ”kolme kerrosta”.
- **"should" and "must"** in safety text (README): "should" → ”pitäisi” or ”tulisi” (”Lasten ei pitäisi katsoa uuniin
  lainkaan.”), "must" → ”on …-ttava” or ”täytyy” (”Kupin tai mukin on täytettävä …”). ”ei pidä” reads as a
  prohibition, so "should not" is ”ei pitäisi”. Where the English only reports a source's figure ("Rio Grande says at
  least 12 inches"), do not make it ”on oltava”.
- **Regional and spoken forms to avoid**: spoken Finnish (”me tehdään”, ”sä”), Swedish-based slang. Write yleiskieli.
  Potters' everyday loanwords that suppliers print (dreija, samotti, engobe) are fine.

## 2. Glossary

Finnish has no grammatical gender. Nouns are given with the genitive and partitive singular (gen., part.) where the
stem changes, which is where translators slip. "show English" marks the terms whose English a reader can ask to see
after them (`<en>…</en>`, `{{en: …}}`).

# Bahasa Indonesia: style sheet and glossary

For translating Glazecalc into Indonesian (`id`), in standard written Indonesian (bahasa baku, as PUEBI's successor, the
Ejaan yang Disempurnakan Edisi V of 2022, and KBBI give it), for potters in Indonesia. Read [README.md](README.md)
first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Indonesian message and guide follows
this page: one Indonesian word for one thing. Indonesia has no region data in the library yet (Phase C: the ID entry is
researched in English, `research_notes/Asia/regions-CN-HK-ID-TH-VN-PH-2026-10-16.md`), so the Indonesian pages must read
well with Orton cones and temperatures (`cones: temperature`) and say nothing about SK cones. The terms come from
Indonesian ceramics pages and glossaries, a feldspar supplier, the Indonesian Wikipedia, school and university texts, the
Google and Apple help pages, and the Microsoft Indonesian style guide, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources
disagree or none was read, the Notes say so: those rows are for an Indonesian potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Indonesian has a plain school-and-craft vocabulary for ceramics (glasir,
tembikar, gerabah, porselen, bakar biskuit, bakar glasir) that the Wikipedia articles, the Keramik88 glossary and the
Indonesian vocational (SMK) pages use, so the main material and process rows rest on usage read today. Studio potters
mix in English loanwords (stoneware, underglaze, stain, kiln, frit, batch), and the sources do too; the choice is given
row by row. No Indonesian shop that sells glaze materials and electric kilns to studio potters was found (the region
research found none either; the searches returned mineral suppliers such as Sathir, vocational pages and blogs), so the
shop-name rows rest on blogs, the Keramik88 glossary and the mineral suppliers. The kiln-sitter, controller and
firing-log rows are American kiln words with no Indonesian source at all. The laws' own words (NAB, pangan, Berita Negara
numbers) are quoted from the research notes and the pages read; the Permenaker and BPOM texts were not re-read today.
Rows marked **Usage** are words seen in blogs, course notes or listings but in no single authoritative source; rows
marked **No source** are best choices. The rows an Indonesian potter should check first are marked **Native check**:
stabilizer (penstabil), formula Seger, susut pijar (LOI), kalium and natrium for potash and soda, berat jenis,
menggumpal (crawling), retak seribu (crackle), pelindian (leaching), tungku and kiln, stoneware, kering total,
silika kristalin respirabel, kata sinyal, kunci penyiapan and autentikasi dua langkah.

## Choices for the owner and an Indonesian potter

These are the questions this sheet cannot settle from sources; each has a recommendation, used below until answered.

- **Register:** "Anda" and standard Indonesian, as Microsoft, Google and Apple write it, not "kamu" (used by some newer
  apps). Recommended.
- **Loanwords or Indonesian words:** the recommended split is Indonesian where the sources have one (glasir, pelebur,
  gerabah, porselen, biskuit, tungku) and the English loanword where studio potters and shops use it (stoneware,
  underglaze, stain, frit, batch, mesh). **tungku** against **kiln** is the closest call: Wikipedia and the school texts
  say tungku; studio potters say kiln.
- **formula Seger or rumus Seger:** formula Seger, as the papers write it. Recommended.
- **Poison line:** Indonesia has no public poison line confirmed in the region research, so the Indonesian text says to
  call the emergency number (112, or 119 for medical emergencies) where the English says "poison line".

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

**Standard Indonesian (bahasa baku) with "Anda"** in the interface, errors, notices, emails, the safety text and the
guides alike: one register across the app. Statements are plain declaratives; requests use the bare verb or **silakan**:
"Simpan resep ini.", "Silakan periksa kata sandi Anda." The Microsoft style guide writes "Anda" with a capital and
keeps the voice natural, warm and short (2.2.1: "Jika Anda ingin mengubah warna latar, nonaktifkan kontras tinggi di
pengaturan PC."), and uses "kami" for the maker. Google's and Apple's Indonesian help pages read the same ("Anda dapat
…"). No "kamu" or "lu"/"gue", no "sampeyan", no "Bapak/Ibu". A subject is dropped where Indonesian allows it
("Hasil disimpan." for "Your result is saved.", and guide steps).

- **Instructions** (guides and help): the bare verb, or the passive with "perlu": "Ayak glasir dua kali.", "Pakai
  masker respirator saat menimbang bahan." The base form of the verb (no meN- prefix) is the imperative; the meN- form
  is the descriptive. Not "Anda harus mengayak".
- **Prohibitions:** "jangan …" for "do not", "jangan pernah …" for "never": "Jangan pernah menyapu bubuk kering." A
  rule with the law's force: "tidak boleh …" ("must not").
- **Should, must, may:** keep them apart. "must" → **harus** ("Benda harus diuji."); "must not" → **tidak boleh**; "should"
  → **sebaiknya** ("Sebaiknya pakai sarung tangan."); "should not" → **sebaiknya tidak**; "may" (a hazard, a possibility)
  → **dapat** or **bisa** ("dapat menyebabkan kanker"; the H-statements use "dapat"); "may" (permission) → **boleh**. Never
  "harus" for "should", never "sebaiknya" for "must". "must be tested" → "harus diuji" (the test is done, not passed);
  never "harus lulus uji". "some" (some fine quartz) → "sebagian", "beberapa", never "sedikit".

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- **Buttons and menu commands:** the bare verb (the base form), as Indonesian software writes it: "Simpan", "Cetak",
  "Bandingkan", "Hapus", "Tambahkan bahan", "Batal". Not the meN- form ("Menyimpan"), not "Simpan!"
- **Headings, tabs, page titles:** a noun phrase: "Resep", "Pengaturan", "Catatan pembakaran", "Kerucut dan kerja panas". A
  heading that names an activity takes the meN- form or the noun: "Mencampur glasir", "Mengetik jumlah". A heading that is
  a question stays one: "Mengapa glasir meleleh?"
- **Field labels:** a noun; keep the colon where the English has one, no space before it: "Nama bahan:".
- **Confirmations:** "Saved: {name}" → "Tersimpan: {name}"; "Removed" → "Dihapus".
- **Sentence case** everywhere: only the first word and proper names take a capital, as the English's sentence case does;
  the English's Title Case means nothing here.

### Capitalisation

Sentence case; proper names, months (**Oktober**, as Indonesian writes them), days, **Anda**, Orton, Seger, product names and
acronyms (UMF, LOI, NAB, SDS) keep their capitals. Headings and buttons are not in Title Case. Words after a colon are
lower-case unless a proper name begins them. Latin words and units keep the English's case.

### Spaces, hyphens and affixes

- **di-:** attached as a prefix to a verb ("diglasir", "dibakar", "dihapus"), a separate word as a preposition ("di
  tungku", "di dalam", "di Indonesia"). A placeholder cannot take the prefix: "{name}" never becomes "di{name}"; write "di
  {region}" (preposition, spaced) or put a verb after it ("resep yang disimpan"). The same holds for the suffix -nya:
  never "{name}nya"; use a noun ("resep {name}").
- **Hyphen:** in reduplication ("kupu-kupu") and in a prefix before a capital or a figure ("non-Orton"); a
  reduplicated plural is avoided (see Plural forms). Compounds are written separate ("kata sandi", "nomor darurat",
  "lembar data").
- **Loanwords** in standard spelling (EYD): "aktivitas", "kualitas", "analisis", "silika", "kalsium", "oksida"; the verb
  prefixes follow KBBI ("mengeklik", "mengunggah", "mengglasir").
- **Space** between a figure and its unit (10 g, 1222 °C, 25 mm), none before % or ° as part of °C (5%, 1222 °C). The
  English's figure-space-unit stays.

### Punctuation

Indonesian uses the Latin marks as English does. No comma before "dan" or "atau"; a series of adjectives takes no comma
(MS). Quotation marks: double quotes as the English has them. Where the English puts a comma or full stop inside the closing
quote, the translation does the same (Indonesian follows the American order for a quoted sentence; native check). The en
dash for ranges with no spaces (10–60%); the em dash is avoided. A colon introduces a list as in English.

### Numbers and units

- **Decimal comma and thousands point**, as CLDR's `id` and the app format them: 1,5; 0,025; 12.500; the four-digit
  temperatures are not grouped (1222 °C, 1000 g), as the English writes them and the app prints them. MS: "5,25 cm",
  "1.526".
- **Percent:** 5%, with no space (CLDR `id`), a range 10–60% (en dash, one sign after the last figure, as the English
  writes it).
- **Units:** the English's symbols, as the app prints them: g, kg, mg, µg, mL, L, mm, cm, °C, °F, °Bé, %, mg/m³, µg/cm²; not
  "gram", "derajat Celsius" in running text. Inches and feet are the words **inci** (MS) and **kaki**, with the metric figure
  in brackets as the English gives it: "12 inci (30 cm)".
- **A number the English writes as a word** ("two coats", "three tiles", "once") stays a word here, since a digit would be
  a figure the English lacks and the check rejects it: "dua lapis", "tiga ubin", "sekali", "dua atau tiga kali". Where the
  English has the digit, the digit stays: "3 coats" → "3 lapis".
- **Dates:** "14 Oktober 2026", "Oktober 2026" (the month named, beside its year; never a numeric date the check cannot
  match to the English's month name). The Gregorian calendar, no era.
- **Clock times:** the 24-hour clock, written the way CLDR prints it, with a point: **pukul 20.00**, **pukul 20.30**,
  "20.00 WIB". The check reads "pukul 20.00" as 20 and "pukul 20.30" as 20:30, as it reads 8pm and 8:30pm, so **pukul or
  jam must stand before the hour**, or a time zone (WIB, WITA, WIT) after it. "8am to 5pm" → "pukul 08.00 sampai pukul
  17.00" (repeat pukul: a bare "17.00" is read as a number). Never "pukul 8 malam", "pukul 5 sore" (the check keeps these
  whole and reports them). "24 hours" → "24 jam". The colon (20:30) is also read right.
- **No figures the English lacks:** no converted units, no added years, no SK or Orton equivalents.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Indonesian one category: `node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('id').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"`
prints `[ 'other' ]`.

| Category | Numbers               | Example                                                         |
| -------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `other`  | every number, 0, 1.5… | 1 bahan, 2 bahan, 21 resep, 1,5 g, 3 kali pembakaran, 5 kerucut |

Every plural message is written with `other` only, and the English's `one` is dropped:
`{count, plural, one {# material} other {# materials}}` → `{count, plural, other {# bahan}}`. `=0 {…}` stays where the
English has it (`=0 {Belum ada bahan}`). The noun does not change with the number, and **is not reduplicated** (not
"bahan-bahan" after a figure; "bahan-bahan" without a figure is acceptable in a guide, but the app uses the singular
form). A **counter** (penggolong) goes between the figure and the noun only where Indonesian needs it:

| Counted thing                 | Form             | Example                    |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| materials, oxides, recipes    | no counter       | # bahan, # oksida, # resep |
| firings, coats, times         | kali, lapis      | # kali pembakaran, # lapis |
| tiles                         | buah             | # buah ubin uji            |
| pieces of ware                | buah             | # buah benda               |
| cones                         | buah             | # buah kerucut             |
| minutes, hours, days          | menit, jam, hari | # menit, # jam, # hari     |
| characters (password, a name) | karakter         | minimal # karakter         |
| codes (recovery codes)        | buah             | # kode pemulihan           |
| devices, passkeys             | buah             | # perangkat, # kunci sandi |

A weight or a temperature needs no counter: `{n} g`.

### Placeholders and names

Indonesian has no case endings or grammatical gender, so {name}, {region} and {library} slot in as they are. Two rules:
the prefix **di-** and the suffix **-nya** cannot attach to a placeholder (see Spaces, hyphens and affixes), and a
generic noun makes the sentence read with any value: "resep {name}", "di {region}", "pustaka {library}". {region} is the
country's name in Indonesian from the browser (Jerman, Amerika Serikat).

### Cones

Orton cones are written "Orton" wherever the English says Orton: "kerucut Orton 6". SK cones are written "SK" with the
number as printed (SK 6a) where the English has them; Indonesia does not use them and Indonesian potters do not name
them, so the Indonesian pages rarely show one. **Seger** is written only where the English says Seger: the older European
Seger cones of the firing guide ("kerucut Seger") and the **formula Seger** (the unity molecular formula, UMF), which is
another thing and never names a cone. Never give an SK number for an Orton number or the reverse, nor say they are equal.
"one number hotter" → "satu nomor lebih panas" and "one number cooler" → "satu nomor lebih dingin", never "lebih tinggi",
"lebih rendah" (cone 05 is cooler than 04). "cone 06" is read "kerucut nol enam" and written 06, with its zero.

### The unity formula

**formula Seger (UMF)**: the papers write "formula Seger" ("pendekatan empiris dari kimiawan Jerman", LIPI); write
"formula Seger (UMF)" once in a text and "formula Seger" afterwards; the abbreviation UMF stays Latin. The app's short
"Unity formula", "Unity" becomes "Formula Seger" and "Seger"; "unity" as the scaling is **kesatuan** ("dalam kesatuan":
fluxes total 1). "Limit formula" is "formula batas". The English "unity formula" is not searched in Indonesian.

### Gender-neutral wording

Indonesian has no grammatical gender; **dia** and **ia** are neutral, and Anda is neutral. Use "pengguna" for the user
where the English says "the user", "pembuat tembikar" or "keramikus" for a potter ("pembuat keramik"), and avoid
"Bapak" and "Ibu".

### The reader in the guides

"Anda" only where the sentence needs a subject; steps are bare verbs; explanations may use the passive ("Glasir yang
terlalu tebal akan mengalir."). Quotations from sources are translated into “…” and keep their figures as the English
writes them. Short plain sentences, as the English.

### The app's feature names

Written exactly as on the button or tab, first letter capital, **without quotation marks**; where the English wraps a name
in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag. A name takes no ending, so attach a generic noun: "Pilih Bandingkan.", "fitur
Bandingkan", "tab Bahan saya". "Settings" in running text is the ordinary noun "pengaturan"; inside
`<settings>…</settings>` write the word the sentence needs.

### Law in Indonesia

As the law writes it, never paraphrased: the words below are fixed, and the region texts must use them.

- **Limits:** "at most", "no more than" → **tidak lebih dari**, **paling banyak** or the law's **batas maksimal** (the
  limit itself passes); "less than", "under" → **kurang dari**, **di bawah** (it does not); "at least", "or more" →
  **paling sedikit**, **sekurang-kurangnya**; "more than", "over" → **lebih dari**, **melebihi**. Never "kurang dari" for "at
  most". A slip moves a legal limit; check every one in the food-contact, silica and mask texts.
- **Silica:** Permenaker 5/2018: **nilai ambang batas (NAB)**, the 8-hour average ("rata-rata selama 8 jam sehari");
  **silika kristalin** with α-quartz as **kuarsa** and **kristobalit**, "(R)" for respirable (**respirabel**, native check).
- **Food contact:** BPOM 11/2026 on **kemasan pangan**, "peralatan makan dan peralatan pengolahan pangan"; limits in mg/dm²
  and mg/L, "batas maksimal"; the test is an extraction with 4% acetic acid ("asam asetat 4%"), the third extraction judged.
  The English's "cooking ware" is "peralatan memasak" and the law's own "peralatan pengolahan pangan" is quoted only where
  the English quotes it. Write Berita Negara numbers and dates exactly as the notes give them.
- **GHS:** **kata sinyal** (Bahaya, Peringatan), **piktogram**, **pernyataan bahaya** (hazard statement), **lembar data
  keselamatan (SDS)**; native check.
- **Who to call:** **112** (police and general), **119** (medical emergency, Kemenkes), 118, 113 (fire), 115 (search and
  rescue). No poison line is confirmed: where the English says "call the poison line", the Indonesian region text says to
  call 119 (or 112). Bodies: **Kementerian Ketenagakerjaan** (Kemnaker), **Kementerian Kesehatan** (Kemenkes), **BPOM**.
- **EU law**, where the English names it (Directive 84/500/EEC, Directive (EU) 2017/2398, CLP): there is no official
  Indonesian text; write **Direktif UE 84/500/EEC** and keep the number, so that the 3 L still applies only to packaging
  and storage vessels. US and UK bodies keep their English names with an Indonesian gloss once: OSHA (badan keselamatan
  kerja AS), HSE (badan kesehatan dan keselamatan Inggris).

### Pitfalls

- **glasir, enamel, pernis, cat:** a glaze is **glasir** (material and coat), "to glaze" **mengglasir**, passive
  **diglasir**. "Glasir" also means a food icing and a confectionery coating: titles and descriptions say **glasir
  keramik**. **Email** (enamel, on metal) and **pernis** (varnish) and **cat** (paint) are never used for a glaze.
- **frit and pelebur:** a frit is **frit** (a factory glass); a flux is **pelebur** (the oxide's role); not **fluks**
  (magnetic flux) and not "solder".
- **tungku, kiln, oven:** a potter's kiln is **tungku** (tungku listrik, tungku gas); "kiln" is the loan studio potters
  use; an **oven** is a kitchen oven, used only where the English says oven.
- **biskuit and bakar glasir:** bisque (the firing and the ware) is **biskuit** (bakar biskuit); the glaze firing is
  **pembakaran glasir**. "Biskuit" is also a cookie: with the ceramics word it is clear.
- **gerabah, tembikar, stoneware, porselen:** earthenware is **gerabah** (porous, low-fired; "tembikar tanah liat"),
  stoneware is **stoneware** (Wikipedia's "tembikar batu"), porcelain is **porselen**. "Tembikar" is pottery in general.
- **silika, silikon, kuarsa:** silica is the oxide **silika** (SiO₂); the element is **silikon**, and so is the rubber
  ("karet silikon"): never use "silikon" for silica. The mineral is **kuarsa**.
- **kalsium karbonat, kapur:** whiting is **kalsium karbonat** (CaCO₃); **kapur** is lime and chalk (and the quicklime
  CaO, "kapur tohor"): it appears only in "lime" frit names and where the English means CaO.
- **boron, boraks, asam borat:** B₂O₃ is **boron (B₂O₃)**, formally boron oksida; **asam borat** is boric acid; **boraks** is
  borax. Never "asam borat" for B₂O₃.
- **soda, kalium, natrium, potas:** "soda" (Na₂O) is **natrium (Na₂O)**, formally natrium oksida; soda ash is **abu soda**;
  "potash" (K₂O) is **kalium (K₂O)**; the safety guide's potash is **kalium karbonat**. "Potas" is the fertiliser; "soda" in
  everyday Indonesian also means a fizzy drink.
- **stain and pewarna:** a stain (factory-made) is **stain**; a colorant (any oxide that colours) is **pewarna**.
- **respirator, ventilator, ventilasi:** a respirator is **respirator** ("masker respirator"); a hospital ventilator is
  never meant here; ventilation (air in a room) is **ventilasi**.
- **panduan and buku petunjuk:** the app's five guides are **panduan**; a kiln's, controller's or sitter's manual is
  **buku petunjuk**. Never the same word.
- **retak halus and retak seribu:** crazing is **retak halus** (a defect); crackle is **retak seribu** (wanted); a crack
  through the pot from cooling is **dunting** (retak akibat suhu).
- **berat jenis and massa jenis:** specific gravity is **berat jenis**, a ratio with no unit (1,45); **massa jenis** is
  density in kg/m³ and is never used for a glaze's SG.
- **pelindian:** leaching is **pelindian**; the leach test is **uji pelindian**, not narrowed to lead and cadmium.
- **Placeholders:** see Placeholders and names.

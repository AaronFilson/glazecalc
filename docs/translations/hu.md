# Magyar: stíluslap és szójegyzék (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Hungarian (`hu`), for potters in Hungary, in the standard language
of Hungary as the Academy's spelling rules (AkH, 12th edition) write it. Every Hungarian message and guide follows this
page: one Hungarian word for one thing. The terms come from Hungarian suppliers' price lists, catalogues and data sheets
(BP Kerámia, Interkerám and its shop Agyagbolt, Ceramic Center, Keraker), the Hungarian Chamber of Commerce's master
potter's study notes, Nabertherm's Hungarian manuals, Hungarian Wikipedia, and for safety the Hungarian decree on
chemical agents at work, the Hungarian text of the CLP Regulation and of Directive 84/500/EEC, not from a dictionary
alone. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a Hungarian potter to check.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal, as Microsoft and the suppliers write: the polite third person ("magázás"), **without
the pronoun Ön** wherever the verb already says it (MS 4.1.12). Instructions use the polite imperative: „Válassza ki
…”, „Szitálja át kétszer a mázat.”, „Viseljen maszkot.” (IK's library: „Olvassa el az adatlapot, mielőtt használni
kezdi az anyagot!”). Use „Ön” only where a sentence would be unclear without it, and the possessive endings rather
than „az Ön …” („Receptjei”, „Fiókja”). Never the familiar tegezés („Válaszd ki”), and never the textbook „we”
(„szitáljuk át”, which IK and MKIK use) for instructions to the reader. Where the English says "we" for the app itself,
„mi” is fine („Javasoljuk, hogy …”, MS 2.2.2). Use „kérjük” only where the English has "please" („Kérjük, próbálja
újra.”).

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs** (the Hungarian software convention, MS 4.1.5: "Save" →
„Mentés”; Hungarian shop sites write the same, KK: „Beállítások mentése”, „Kosárba”):

- Buttons and menu commands: a **verbal noun in -ás/-és**, after its object, with the possessive ending:
  „Mentés”, „Mentés másolatként”, „Nyomtatás”, „Összehasonlítás”, „Nyersanyagok hozzáadása”, „Próbahasználat elvetése”.
  Not the imperative („Mentse”) and not the infinitive („Menteni”). Where the user tells the app something about
  themselves, the first person is idiomatic and allowed, as in consent buttons („Elfogadom”) and the app's "My …"
  names (see below).
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: „Receptek”, „Beállítások”, „Égetési
  naplók”, „Gúlák és felvett hőmennyiség”. A heading that is a question in English may stay a question („Mire
  figyeljen?”).
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: „A nyersanyag neve:”.
- Confirmations: the adverbial participle first, as the English puts "Saved" first: "Saved: …" → „Mentve: …”;
  "Removed" → „Eltávolítva”.
- Yes/No answers in dialogs: „Igen, eltávolítás”, „Mégse”.

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case everywhere (MS 4.1.5: copying English title case "is an error"): only the first word
of a heading, button, tab or label and proper names take a capital: "Fired Weight" → „Égetett tömeg”. After a colon,
a complete sentence starts with a capital; a list or a phrase does not (MS 4.1.13). Names of nations, languages and
months are lower case („angol”, „október”). Proper-name compounds keep the capital and take a hyphen: „Seger-gúla”,
„Orton-gúla”, „Seger-képlet” (AkH; WP, MKIK). Chemical names of compounds take a hyphen: „cink-oxid”, „kalcium-karbonát”,
„szilícium-dioxid” (AkH; BP writes them so; IK and KK often write „Cinkoxid”, do not copy that).

**Punctuation and spacing** (AkH):

- Quotation marks: „…” (U+201E, U+201D); inside them »…« (U+00BB, U+00AB). Not "…" or “…”.
- Apostrophe: Hungarian hardly needs one; where it does, the typographic ’ (U+2019). Write `''` only where the README
  asks (just before `{`, `}` or `#`, and in `safety`, `regions` and `records`).
- No space before ; : ! ? or after an opening bracket or quotation mark.
- Ranges: the en dash with **no spaces** (nagykötőjel), the unit once at the end: „1222–1240 °C”, „1–3%”, „06–04-es
  gúla”. In a sentence „1222 és 1240 °C között” is also fine. An approximation written with a hyphen („5-6”) is AkH's
  other use; in Glazecalc use the en dash for every range the English gives as a range.
- Imperative sentences end with a full stop, as the English does (MS: Hungarian grammar asks for "!" after an
  imperative, but software uses it only for serious warnings). Use "!" exactly where the English has it.
- Suffixes on a number, symbol or abbreviation are joined with a hyphen and follow the **spoken** form (AkH 282):
  „1240 °C-on” (Celsius-fokon), „10%-os”, „6%-kal”, „100 g-ot”, „25 mm-es”, „06-os gúla”. Never join a suffix to a
  placeholder (see Pitfalls).

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: „1,5”, „0,01 g”, „1,45”. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped („1240 °C”, „1000 g”), as AkH and the app print them; five digits
  and more are grouped in threes with a **no-break space** (U+00A0): „12 500 g”. Never a full stop as the separator
  (suppliers' prices write „4.500.-”; do not copy that). The check reads the no-break space, not a plain one.
- A space (a no-break space where possible) before °C, °F and every unit: „1240 °C”, „100 g”, „80 mesh”, „100 °C/h”.
  **No space before %**: „15%”, „1,5%” (AkH: „48%”; CLDR prints „15%”). Suppliers often print „1240°C”; do not copy that.
- °C/h stays °C/h (Hungarian kiln texts write „°C/óra”, but units are never translated).
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word, even where AkH would spell a small number out.

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Hungarian two categories, `one` (exactly 1) and `other` (0, 2, 5, 1,5 and the rest). After
a number, Hungarian keeps the noun **in the singular**, so both forms read the same, and the verb is singular too:
„1 nyersanyag”, „2 nyersanyag”, „5 nyersanyag”, „1,5 nyersanyag”; „5 recept van mentve”, never „5 receptek” or „5
recept vannak”. Write both forms all the same:
`{count, plural, =0 {nincs nyersanyag} one {# nyersanyag} other {# nyersanyag}}`. A case ending goes on the noun after
the number, never on the number: „{count} nyersanyagot”, „{count} receptből”. The same singular follows „sok”, „több”,
„néhány”, „kevés” („sok nyersanyag”).

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Hungarian shops sell them as „Orton gúla” or „hőfokjelző
gúlák (Orton)” (BP, IK), and also sell Seger cones („Seger gúla”, BP), a different series (SK) whose numbers do not
match Orton's (Seger SK 06a about 990 °C, Orton 06 about 1013 °C at 150 °C/h). The pyrometric cone is a **gúla**
(pyramid) in Hungarian (WP: „SK (Seger-Kegel, magyarul Seger-gúla)”; MKIK: „a 26-os Seger-gúla (1580 °C)”), never
„kúp” (which also means a suppository) and never „hőmérő”. So:

- "cone 6" → „6-os gúla”; "cone 06" → „06-os gúla” (read „nulla hat”; the zero stays: the 06-os gúla is far cooler
  than the 6-os). The number takes the adjective ending of its spoken form, joined with a hyphen: 022-es, 019-es,
  018-as, 010-es, 09-es, 08-as, 07-es, 06-os, 05-ös, 04-es, 03-as, 02-es, 01-es, 1-es, 2-es, 3-as, 4-es, 5-ös, 6-os,
  7-es, 8-as, 9-es, 10-es, 11-es, 12-es, 13-as, 14-es, 26-os, 42-es. The case ending goes on „gúla”: „a 6-os gúlán”,
  „6-os gúláig égetni”.
- "Orton cone" → „Orton-gúla”; at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared, „6-os Orton-gúla”,
  then „6-os gúla”.
- "fire to cone 6" → „6-os gúlára égetni”; "at cone 6" → „6-os gúlán”; "a cone 6 glaze" → „6-os gúlás máz” (as
  potters say „1240 fokos máz”); "cone 04 bisque" → „zsengélés 04-es gúlára”; "cone 5–6" → „5–6-os gúla”; "cone 06–04
  glazes" → „06–04-es gúlás mázak”.
- With a placeholder the ending cannot follow the number, so name the cone with a colon or in brackets: "for cone
  {cone}" → „(gúla: {cone})”, „Orton-gúla: {cone}”.
- **Never** write „6-os Seger-gúla”, „SK 6” or „SK 6a” for an Orton number. „Seger-gúla” appears only where the English
  says "Seger cones", and then means the SK series.

**The unity formula.** „Seger-képlet”, with „(UMF)” after it the first time on a page: „Seger-képlet (UMF)”, then
„Seger-képlet”. MKIK writes „általános Seger-képlete: 1 mól RO, R₂O …” throughout its glaze chapter; WP says
„Seger-formula”, which is understood but less usual in glaze texts. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity
formula" and "Unity:" are all „Seger-képlet” / „Seger-képlet:”. Not „egységképlet” (a calque) and not
„molekulaképlet” (a chemistry term for a single compound).

**Gender-neutral wording.** Hungarian has no grammatical gender and one pronoun, „ő”, for everyone. Use the neutral
nouns („fazekas”, „keramikus”, „felhasználó”, „munkavállaló”) and plurals for generalisations; avoid „-nő” forms
(„keramikusnő”) unless a source names a woman so (MS 3.1).

**The reader in the guides.** The polite imperative, in short plain sentences, as the English: „Szitálja át a mázat
kétszer egy 80 mesh-es szitán.” For general facts and rules the impersonal „kell” + infinitive is natural: „A mázat
kétszer kell átszitálni.” Avoid the inclusive „we” of Hungarian textbooks („szitáljuk”), the pronoun „Ön” at every turn,
and nominal style („Az átszitálás elvégzése …”). Quotations from sources are translated into „…” and keep their
figures as the English writes them.

**Should and must**, in safety text above all, keep their strength (README: "should" is not "must"). "must", "have
to" → „kell” („a szénnek 650 °C előtt ki kell égnie”). "should" → „kellene” or „javasolt / ajánlott …-ni”: „a
kemencébe mindig szemvédővel kellene benézni”, „ólomkioldódását laboratóriumban kellene megvizsgáltatni”, „Az
élelmiszerrel érintkező felületekre szánt mázakat ajánlott kioldódásra vizsgálni.” "should not" → „nem kellene
…-ni” („Gyermekeknek egyáltalán nem kellene benézniük a kemencébe.”), never the imperative „ne …-jon” or „nem szabad”,
which read as "must not"; „nem szabadna” is acceptable. "we suggest" → „javasoljuk”; "never" → „soha ne”.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with a capital first letter and **no quotation marks**
(MS: „kattintson a Mégse gombra”). The label itself never takes a case ending: a following common noun carries it
(„gomb”, „lap”, „beállítás”, „funkció”, „oldal”): „Kattintson az Összehasonlítás gombra.”, „a Saját nyersanyagok
lapon”, „a Beállítások oldalon”. The article before a label follows its first sound: „az Összehasonlítás”, „a
Beállítások”. Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag. The fixed names:

„Könyvtár”, so the word reads naturally for a collection of material data.

**EU directives and regulations** take the Hungarian official form, as EUR-Lex writes them: „84/500/EGK irányelv”
(title: „A Tanács 84/500/EGK irányelve … az élelmiszerekkel rendeltetésszerűen érintkezésbe kerülő kerámiatárgyakra
vonatkozó tagállami jogszabályok közelítéséről”), amended by „2005/31/EK irányelv”; „(EU) 2017/2398 irányelv”;
„1272/2008/EK rendelet (CLP)”; „1907/2006/EK rendelet (REACH)”. The number comes before „irányelv” or „rendelet”, and
the case ending goes on that word: „a 84/500/EGK irányelvben”. EGK (Európai Gazdasági Közösség) and EK are never „EEC”
or „EWG”. Hungarian standards add MSZ: „MSZ EN 1388-1”.

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **máz** is a glaze. **zománc** is enamel on metal („tűzzománc”), **glazúr** is cake icing, **lakk** is varnish. The
  verb is **mázaz** („mázazás”, „mázazott”); **mázol** in everyday Hungarian means to paint walls („mázoló” = house
  painter). IK and MKIK also write „mázolás” for glazing; prefer „mázazás”.
- **kőagyag** is stoneware (WP, Terebess, NAB „Mázas égetés, kőagyag”); **kőcserép** is the same thing in older and
  IK texts. **kőedény** is, in the reference works, a porous white earthenware (Steingut: Terebess, WP „Miskolci
  kőedénygyárak”), but BP, CC and AB sell their high-fire bodies as „kőedénymassza” (CC: „kőedény/félporcelánmassza
  (1180–1280 °C)”). Write „kőagyag” for stoneware and never „kőedény” for either stoneware or earthenware in the app's
  own text; in the guides it may be named once as the shops' word.
- **folyósító** (folyósítószer) is a **deflocculant** to IK and BP's own list of auxiliaries (Giessfix, Dolaflux), yet
  BP also labels spodumene „máz folyósító”, meaning flux. The flux is always **olvasztóanyag**; „folyósító” only for the
  deflocculant.
- **szóda** on a bag is sodium carbonate (BP „Szóda (nátrium-karbonát)”) and in daily life soda water; **hamuzsír** is
  potassium carbonate; **nátron** is the old name of soda that survives in „nátronföldpát”. The potters' oxides "soda"
  and "potash" are „nátrium-oxid (Na₂O)” and „kálium-oxid (K₂O)”.
- **szilícium** is the element, **szilikon** the rubber, **kovasav** silicic acid (old books used it for SiO₂, as
  German Kieselsäure; do not). The oxide is „szilícium-dioxid (SiO₂)”, the material „kvarc”, „kvarcliszt”.
- **timföld** is alumina (Al₂O₃; KK sells „Timföld”), not clay („agyag”) and not earth.
- **nyersmáz** is an unfritted glaze (MKIK „ólommentes nyersmázak”, IK „frittelt mázról vagy nyers mázról”), not a raw
  formula and not raw glazing. "Raw-glazed ware" is „nyersen mázazott áru”.
- **biszkvit** is unglazed fired porcelain, and potters' slang for bisque ware (CC). Write „zsengélt áru”, „zsengélés”.
- **respirátor** also means a hospital ventilator („respirátorra került”). The mask is „légzésvédő maszk”,
  „részecskeszűrős félálarc” (see 2.7).
- **Specks** on BP's list are decorative granules sold to add on purpose; dark spots as a defect are „pöttyök”, „foltok”.
- **kréta** is chalk and also the island of Crete; for whiting write „mészkőliszt” as on BP's bag.
- **égetés** is firing (in a kiln); **égés** is burning, **égési sérülés** a burn, **tüzel** is to stoke a fire or to
  shoot. "fire to cone 6" is „6-os gúlára égetni”.
- **mész** can be lime (CaO), limestone or quicklime; for the oxide write „kalcium-oxid (CaO)” or „kalcium”.
- **Placeholders.** Hungarian adds case endings, and the article changes with the next sound („a 6-os”, „az 5-ös”,
  „az 1240 °C”, „a 2-es”). A placeholder can take neither: write „a(z) {name}” as Hungarian software does (MS, KK:
  „a(z) Keraker Kft.”), and put a noun after the placeholder to carry the ending: `a(z) „{title}” receptet`,
  `{count} nyersanyagból`, never `{name}-t` or `{count}-ból`. Postpositions are separate words and may follow a
  placeholder: `{year} óta`, `{count} után`. A country name needs „-ban/-ben” or „-on/-en/-ön”, which the code cannot
  choose („Magyarországon”, „Ausztriában”), so write „Boltok – {region}” or „{region}: boltok”, never
  `Boltok {region}ban`.
- **Definite and indefinite conjugation.** The verb form depends on the object: „Mérje le a nyersanyagokat” (definite),
  „Mérjen le 100 g kvarclisztet” (indefinite), „Adja meg a nevet” / „Adjon meg egy nevet”. A placeholder object with
  „a(z)” is definite. Buttons avoid the question by being verbal nouns.
- **Word order.** A verb prefix moves behind the verb in the imperative, in negation and under focus: „kinyitja”, but
  „Nyissa ki a kemencét.”, „Ne nyissa ki a kemencét 100 °C felett.” Put the number and its unit before the noun („80
  mesh-es szita”, „5% bentonit”).
- **Jargon to recognise, not to write**: „hárisz”, „háriszos” (crazing, from German Haarriss; CC), „súber” (damper,
  IK-gáz), „magastüzű”, „alacsonytüzű” (shops' spelling of „magas tüzű”, „alacsony tüzű”).
- **Regional variants**: write the Hungarian of Hungary (AkH). Transylvanian, Slovakian or Vojvodina Hungarian words
  and spellings, and Germanisms of the workshop, are out of place in the app.

# Terms to translate consistently

The English terms that recur in Glazecalc's messages, material notes and five guides, or that have a pottery sense or a
trap, with the sense each has here. Each language's glossary, `docs/translations/<code>.md`, is built from this list and
gives one term in that language for every row. Rows marked **show English** are the key terms whose English a reader can
ask to see in brackets (Settings, English terms): `<en>…</en>` in messages, `{{en: …}}` in the guides.

## 1. Glaze and its chemistry

| Term                                         | Meaning here                                                                                                                          | Notes                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| glaze                                        | A thin coat of glass fused to a pot; as a verb, to put that coat on.                                                                  | **show English**. Noun and verb ("glazed ware", "glazing"). Not window glazing, not varnish. French, Spanish and Italian potters usually use the word that also means enamel. |
| glass former                                 | The oxide that builds the glass: silica (SiO₂), the RO₂ column.                                                                       | **show English**. UI column "Glass Formers - RO₂".                                                                                                                            |
| flux                                         | An oxide that lowers the melting point to one a kiln reaches: the R₂O and RO oxides.                                                  | **show English**. Also a material category ("Fluxes") and a verb ("fluxing power", "a strong flux"). Not soldering flux, not magnetic flux.                                   |
| stabilizer                                   | An oxide, mainly alumina (the app also places boron here), that stiffens the melt so it stays on the pot: the R₂O₃ column.            | **show English**. UI column "Stabilizers - R₂O₃". Not a food or plastics additive.                                                                                            |
| oxide                                        | A compound of an element with oxygen; a fired glaze is described by its oxides.                                                       | Formulas are never translated. The app names each oxide two ways: formally ("Calcium oxide") and as potters do ("calcium").                                                   |
| R₂O and RO                                   | The alkali fluxes (Na₂O, K₂O, Li₂O) and the alkaline-earth fluxes (CaO, MgO, BaO, SrO, with ZnO).                                     | Keep the symbols; "R" stands for any metal of the family. The UI heading "Fluxes - RO" covers both groups.                                                                    |
| alkali, alkaline                             | Rich in soda and potash (sometimes lithium): "alkaline frit", "alkaline copper blues", "a lot of alkali".                             | Not "alkaline" in the pH sense. The alkalis are the R₂O oxides.                                                                                                               |
| unity molecular formula (UMF)                | A glaze's oxides as counts of molecules, scaled so the fluxes add up to 1; also called the Seger formula.                             | **show English**. Use the language's established name (often after Seger) with "UMF" beside it. The app shortens it to "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:".         |
| unity                                        | The scaling that makes the fluxes total 1: "in unity", "Molecular / Unity".                                                           | Not togetherness or agreement.                                                                                                                                                |
| limit formula (target formula)               | Typical unity-formula ranges for stable glazes at a cone: a guide, not a rule.                                                        | **show English**. "Limit" here is not a legal limit; keep it apart from lead limits and exposure limits.                                                                      |
| silica to alumina ratio                      | Silica divided by alumina in the unity formula; higher tends to gloss, lower to matte.                                                | **show English**. UI: "Ratio of Silica to Alumina : {ratio}", "Silica to alumina", "silica to alumina {ratio}:1".                                                             |
| flux balance                                 | How the fluxes divide between R₂O and RO, such as 0.2 R₂O to 0.8 RO.                                                                  | UI: "Flux balance", "Balance", "balance of fluxes".                                                                                                                           |
| oxide analysis (analysis, chemical analysis) | The share of each oxide in a material or glaze, by weight percent.                                                                    | UI: "Analysis", "Oxide analysis, fired, by weight".                                                                                                                           |
| formula (raw formula, fired)                 | A chemical formula; "raw" is the material as sold, "fired" what stays after firing.                                                   | UI: "Raw formula:", "Enter the formula (fired):", "Formula, as sold:".                                                                                                        |
| loss on ignition (LOI)                       | The weight a material loses in firing as water, carbon dioxide, organic matter or sulfur.                                             | **show English**. Use the language's established term and abbreviation; the UI also writes "LOI" alone.                                                                       |
| mole                                         | A count of molecules, the chemistry unit.                                                                                             | Not the animal or a mark on the skin.                                                                                                                                         |
| molecular weight                             | The weight of one mole of a material or oxide.                                                                                        | UI: "Raw molecular weight of the formula".                                                                                                                                    |
| equivalent weight, fired weight              | The weight of a material that supplies one mole of an oxide; what it weighs once its LOI has gone.                                    | UI columns "Equivalent Weight", "Fired Weight".                                                                                                                               |
| melt (noun)                                  | The molten glaze in the kiln: "stiffens the melt", "gas bubbling out of the melt".                                                    | A noun as well as the verb.                                                                                                                                                   |
| thermal expansion (expansion)                | How much a glaze or body grows when heated and shrinks when cooled; what decides glaze fit.                                           | **show English**. UI "Expansion, ×10⁻⁶/°C"; frit notes give "CTE" and "x 10^-7/K". Keep the figures and units.                                                                |
| stiff, fluid, runny (viscosity)              | How readily the melt flows.                                                                                                           | "runny" also describes a thin slurry; context decides.                                                                                                                        |
| raw, fired                                   | Raw: not fired, calcined or fritted ("raw whiting", "raw clay", "raw lead"). Fired: after firing ("fired oxides", "the fired piece"). | Not "uncooked". "Raw-glazed" ware is unfired ware glazed for once-firing.                                                                                                     |
| gloss, satin, matte                          | Surfaces: reflective and smooth; a subdued sheen; dull.                                                                               | "Satin" is not the fabric here. "matte" is the sources' spelling.                                                                                                             |
| opaque, transparent, translucent, clear      | Hides the clay; lets it show; lets light through (porcelain); a transparent, usually colourless glaze.                                | "clear" is a glaze type, not "obvious".                                                                                                                                       |
| stable, durable                              | A glaze balanced enough to melt fully and resist leaching and wear; hard-wearing.                                                     | "stable" is not "steady" or "fixed".                                                                                                                                          |
| crystals, crystalline                        | Crystals growing in the glaze as it cools (matte surfaces, crystalline glazes).                                                       | "Crystalline silica" is the dust hazard (section 7): same word, different topic.                                                                                              |
| boron blue                                   | A milky blue-white where a glaze rich in calcium and boron pools.                                                                     | In quotes in the UI; translate the sense and keep the quotes.                                                                                                                 |
| celadon                                      | A reduction glaze, blue-green to grey-green, from about ½–2% iron.                                                                    | Also a palette name ("Celadon green").                                                                                                                                        |
| tenmoku (temmoku)                            | A brown-black iron glaze, about 5–6% iron.                                                                                            | A Japanese loanword; both spellings occur. Palette "Tenmoku rust".                                                                                                            |

## 2. Materials and oxides

| Term                                              | Meaning here                                                                                                         | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| silica (SiO₂)                                     | The glass former, both the oxide and the material (ground quartz, flint).                                            | **show English**. Not silicon (the element) or silicone (the rubber). Also a category ("Silica").                                                                                                                 |
| alumina (Al₂O₃)                                   | The stabilizer, from clays and feldspars; also sold as a material (calcined alumina, alumina hydrate).               | Category "Alumina". The formal oxide list says "Aluminum oxide".                                                                                                                                                  |
| boron (B₂O₃)                                      | Boric oxide: formally a glass former, used as a flux; essential from cone 06 to cone 6.                              | Potters say "boron" for the oxide. Category "Boron sources"; formal name "Boric oxide".                                                                                                                           |
| potash (K₂O)                                      | Potassium oxide, as potters name it.                                                                                 | **show English**. In the safety guide "potash" is the material potassium carbonate; check which. "KNaO" means potash and soda together.                                                                           |
| soda (Na₂O)                                       | Sodium oxide, as potters name it.                                                                                    | **show English**. Not soda water or soda ash (Na₂CO₃). In firing logs "Soda" is a soda firing.                                                                                                                    |
| lithium (Li₂O)                                    | Lithium oxide, a strong low-expansion flux; from lithium carbonate, spodumene, petalite, lepidolite.                 | Records also write "lithia".                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| calcium, lime (CaO)                               | Calcium oxide, the main high-fire flux.                                                                              | The UI says "calcium"; frit notes say "lime" ("soda-potash-lime frit"). Not the fruit, though "lime green" (a colour) is.                                                                                         |
| magnesium, magnesia (MgO)                         | Magnesium oxide: matte, low expansion; does not melt at low fire.                                                    | The app writes "magnesium", "magnesia" and "Magnesium oxide" for it.                                                                                                                                              |
| barium (BaO), barium carbonate                    | Barium oxide; its raw source barium carbonate is toxic (once sold as rat poison).                                    |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| strontium (SrO)                                   | Strontium oxide; from strontium carbonate, the less toxic stand-in for barium.                                       |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| zinc (ZnO), zinc oxide                            | Zinc oxide: a flux grouped with RO, and the raw material that supplies it.                                           | "Calcined zinc" crawls less.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| lead (PbO)                                        | Lead oxide: a strong, toxic flux, now used only as a frit if at all.                                                 | The metal, never the verb "to lead" or a lead (cable, clue). Also the setting "Lead".                                                                                                                             |
| iron (red iron oxide, black iron oxide)           | Iron oxide as colorant and flux: red (Fe₂O₃) and black (Fe₃O₄, older books FeO).                                     | "Crocus martis" is a red iron oxide.                                                                                                                                                                              |
| titanium, titania (TiO₂)                          | Titanium dioxide: opacity, variegation, crystals.                                                                    |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| raw materials (materials)                         | The powders a glaze is weighed from: minerals, clays and frits.                                                      | "Materials" is also an app page and a recipe column.                                                                                                                                                              |
| feldspar (spar)                                   | A mineral supplying alkali flux with its own alumina and silica.                                                     | **show English**. "spar" is the short form in names ("Custer Spar", "potash spar"). Category "Feldspars and stones".                                                                                              |
| potash feldspar, soda feldspar                    | Feldspar mainly with K₂O (orthoclase, microcline) or Na₂O (albite).                                                  | An old recipe's plain "feldspar" may mean any of them.                                                                                                                                                            |
| nepheline syenite                                 | A silica-poor feldspathic rock, a stronger flux than feldspar.                                                       | **show English**.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Cornish stone (Cornwall stone, china stone)       | A feldspar-quartz rock from Cornwall, no longer quarried; sold now as substitutes.                                   | Three names for one material. "stone" is a material, not a pebble.                                                                                                                                                |
| kaolin (UK: china clay)                           | A pure white clay supplying alumina and silica; keeps glaze suspended. Australia: Eckalite.                          | **show English**. "china" means porcelain, not the country.                                                                                                                                                       |
| calcined                                          | Heated beforehand to drive off water or gas: calcined kaolin, calcined flint, calcined zinc.                         |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ball clay                                         | A fine, plastic clay with more iron and organic matter than kaolin.                                                  | **show English**. "ball" is part of the name, not a shape to describe.                                                                                                                                            |
| bentonite                                         | A very fine, swelling clay used at 1–3% to keep glaze from settling.                                                 |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| slip clay                                         | A clay that melts by itself into a glaze (Albany slip, Alberta Slip).                                                | Not slip (clay in water).                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| whiting                                           | Calcium carbonate, the main calcium flux; loses over 40% in firing.                                                  | **show English**. Not the fish. German suppliers sell it as Kreide (chalk).                                                                                                                                       |
| dolomite, talc                                    | Calcium magnesium carbonate; magnesium silicate. Both supply MgO: silky mattes, lower expansion.                     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| wollastonite                                      | Calcium silicate: calcium without the carbon dioxide.                                                                |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| flint                                             | UK: calcined flint pebble; US recipes: ground quartz. Both nearly pure silica.                                       | **show English**. Which one a recipe means depends on where it was written.                                                                                                                                       |
| quartz (free quartz, free silica)                 | Crystalline silica, the mineral; "free" quartz is loose in a material, not bound in another mineral.                 | Free silica matters for dust (section 7).                                                                                                                                                                         |
| frit                                              | Glass made in a factory: minerals melted, quenched in water and ground, so soluble or toxic oxides become insoluble. | **show English**. Also "fritted". Kinds in the sources: base frit, boron frit, standard borax frit (UK), calcium borate frit, high-alkaline frit, low-expansion frit, leadless frit, lead frit (lead bisilicate). |
| raw borates (borax, colemanite, ulexite)          | Natural or simple boron compounds, soluble or slightly soluble.                                                      | Gerstley Borate and Gillespie Borate are trade names. Borax and boric acid are reproductive toxins (section 7).                                                                                                   |
| soda ash, bone ash, wood ash                      | Sodium carbonate (soluble); calcium phosphate; ash from burnt wood (ash glazes).                                     | Only wood ash is ash.                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| colorant                                          | A metal oxide or carbonate that colours a glaze.                                                                     | **show English**. Category "Colorants"; UI "Colorants and additives".                                                                                                                                             |
| stain                                             | A factory-made, pre-fired colorant, more stable than raw oxides.                                                     | **show English**. Not a mark or dirt (though "stain" as a verb also occurs: a glaze stops a pot staining).                                                                                                        |
| cobalt, copper, chrome, manganese, nickel         | Colorant metals, named by the metal for their oxides and carbonates.                                                 | "chrome" is chromium oxide, not chrome plating.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| rutile                                            | Natural titanium ore, about 95% TiO₂: mottled, variegated colour.                                                    |                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| opacifier                                         | A material that makes a glaze opaque: zircon, tin oxide, titanium dioxide.                                           | Category "Opacifiers". Tin oxide opacifies poorly in reduction.                                                                                                                                                   |
| zircon                                            | Zirconium silicate (ZrSiO₄), the usual opacifier, sold as Zircopax, Superpax and others.                             | Not zirconia (ZrO₂), which is rarely used raw.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| suspender                                         | A material that keeps particles suspended in the slurry: bentonite, clay, hectorite.                                 | Category "Suspenders". Not braces or garters.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| refractory                                        | Resisting heat: melting only far above glaze temperatures.                                                           | Said of materials (alumina, chrome) and kiln parts.                                                                                                                                                               |
| softening point, half-sphere point (fusion, flow) | Temperatures on frit data sheets: where the frit starts to soften, slumps to a half sphere, flows.                   | Use the terms frit makers' sheets use in the language.                                                                                                                                                            |
| substitute (stand-in, replacement)                | A current material used in place of one no longer sold.                                                              | See "like for like" (section 8).                                                                                                                                                                                  |

## 3. Clay, ware and firing stages

| Term                            | Meaning here                                                                                 | Notes                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| clay body (body)                | The clay a pot is made of.                                                                   | **show English**. Not a human body: "the glaze melts into the body", "body stain".           |
| ware (pot, piece)               | Pots in general, often by stage: greenware, bisque ware, glazed ware. A piece is one object. | Not software or merchandise.                                                                 |
| functional ware                 | Pots used for food and drink.                                                                | Not "working" or "practical".                                                                |
| hollowware, flatware            | Cups, bowls and jugs; plates and saucers (FDA lead-limit classes).                           | "flatware" here is plates, not cutlery.                                                      |
| earthenware                     | A low-fire body (about cone 06–02) that stays porous.                                        | **show English**. See the traps below.                                                       |
| stoneware                       | A mid- to high-fire body (about cone 5–10) that becomes vitreous or nearly so.               | **show English**. See the traps below.                                                       |
| porcelain                       | A white, kaolin-rich, high-fire body, often translucent.                                     |                                                                                              |
| vitreous, vitrification         | Glassy and non-porous; a body becoming so as it fires.                                       |                                                                                              |
| porous, porosity                | Having open pores that soak up water.                                                        | Bisque should stay porous.                                                                   |
| mature, maturity                | A body or glaze reaching the state it was made for: "matures the clay", "nearing maturity".  | Not age.                                                                                     |
| plastic, plasticity             | How well clay can be shaped and hold its shape.                                              | Not the polymer.                                                                             |
| greenware                       | Unfired ware.                                                                                | **show English**. Not green in colour.                                                       |
| leather-hard                    | Stiff but slightly damp: the stage for trimming and handles.                                 |                                                                                              |
| bone dry                        | No free water left: very fragile, ready to fire.                                             |                                                                                              |
| bisque (UK: biscuit)            | The first, lower firing (usually cone 08–04), and the strong, porous ware it makes.          | **show English**. Also "bisque firing", "bisqued", "soft/hard bisque". Not soup or a cookie. |
| glaze firing (UK: glost firing) | The firing that melts the glaze.                                                             | **show English**.                                                                            |
| once-firing (single firing)     | Glazing unfired ware and firing it only once.                                                | **show English**.                                                                            |
| slip                            | Clay suspended in water, for casting, joining or decorating.                                 | Not underwear, not slipping.                                                                 |
| engobe                          | A slip-like coating, often coloured, with more non-clay material.                            |                                                                                              |
| underglaze                      | Coloured slip or oxide decoration, usually under a clear glaze; "underglaze pencil".         |                                                                                              |
| foot                            | The base a pot stands on; kept free of glaze ("dry-footing").                                | Not the body part. "leave a wider foot" = a wider unglazed band.                             |
| low fire, mid fire, high fire   | Firing ranges: about cone 06–04, 5–6 and 9–10.                                               | Nouns and adjectives ("mid-fire glazes").                                                    |
| raku                            | A firing in which ware is taken from the kiln red-hot; known for crackle.                    | A Japanese loanword.                                                                         |

## 4. Kilns, cones and firing

| Term                                                                        | Meaning here                                                                                                                                                  | Notes                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| electric kiln, gas kiln, manual kiln                                        | Kilns by fuel; a manual kiln is switched by hand, often with a kiln sitter, without a controller.                                                             |                                                                                                                        |
| firing, fire (verb), load                                                   | Heating ware in a kiln; "a firing" is one load fired. To fire is to heat in a kiln ("fire to cone 6").                                                        | Not dismissing someone, not flames. "load", "loading", "unloading" are the kiln's contents and filling or emptying it. |
| kiln shelf (shelf)                                                          | A refractory slab the ware stands on.                                                                                                                         | "shelf" also means the materials on hand (section 8).                                                                  |
| element                                                                     | A heating coil in an electric kiln.                                                                                                                           | The Materials form uses "Elements" for chemical elements.                                                              |
| cone (pyrometric cone)                                                      | A slender ceramic pyramid that bends at a set heatwork; also names a firing ("cone 6").                                                                       | **show English**. Not an ice-cream or traffic cone.                                                                    |
| cone number, cone chart                                                     | 022 to 42; a leading zero marks the cooler series, read "oh": cone 06 is cooler than cone 6. The chart gives each cone's bending temperature by heating rate. | Never change the digits or the zero. Orton's chart is the one used.                                                    |
| self-supporting, large and small (junior) cones, bars                       | Cone kinds: two stand on the shelf as witness cones (large ones in a plaque or a pat of clay); the small cone or bar goes in a kiln sitter.                   | "junior" is the old name for small.                                                                                    |
| witness cone                                                                | A cone on the shelf beside the ware, showing the heatwork the ware received.                                                                                  | **show English**.                                                                                                      |
| cone pack                                                                   | Three witness cones set together, one number apart.                                                                                                           | **show English**.                                                                                                      |
| guide cone, firing cone, guard cone                                         | In a cone pack: one number cooler than the target; the target; one hotter.                                                                                    |                                                                                                                        |
| heatwork                                                                    | The combined effect of temperature and time; what cones measure.                                                                                              | **show English**.                                                                                                      |
| heating rate (rate, climb), peak                                            | Degrees per hour, read over the last 100 °C; the highest temperature of the firing.                                                                           | Cone chart columns "Slow", "Medium", "Fast". Firing-log field "Rate".                                                  |
| ramp                                                                        | A rate of heating or cooling in a schedule.                                                                                                                   | **show English**. Firing-log field "Ramp".                                                                             |
| hold (soak)                                                                 | Keeping the kiln at one temperature for a set time.                                                                                                           | **show English**. "soak" is the same thing, not soaking in water. Firing-log field "Hold".                             |
| schedule (firing schedule, program, preset, segment)                        | The plan of a firing: segments, each a ramp, a target temperature and an optional hold.                                                                       | Preset names ("Slow Bisque", "Fast Glaze") stay as on the controller. Firing-log field "Segment".                      |
| controller                                                                  | An electronic unit that fires the kiln through a schedule.                                                                                                    | Setting: "By temperature, as a kiln controller's program".                                                             |
| cone offset (offset)                                                        | A controller setting that moves its temperature for a cone up or down to match the witness cones.                                                             |                                                                                                                        |
| kiln sitter (Kiln-Sitter, sitter)                                           | A mechanical switch that turns the kiln off when a small cone bends; a convenience, not a fail-safe.                                                          | **show English**. "Kiln-Sitter" is Dawson's trademark. Not a babysitter.                                               |
| sensing rod, claw, weight, trigger plate, cone supports, tube, firing gauge | The parts of a kiln sitter and the gauge for adjusting it.                                                                                                    | Follow a local kiln-sitter manual where one exists.                                                                    |
| limit timer                                                                 | A clock on a kiln sitter that turns the kiln off after a set time, as a backup.                                                                               |                                                                                                                        |
| infinite switch (Off, Low, Medium, High)                                    | A switch that cycles the elements fully on and off, marked Off, 1 to 11, High; older kilns have four positions.                                               | Not literally infinite. Switch positions are labels.                                                                   |
| pyrometer, thermocouple                                                     | A temperature gauge; the heat probe through the kiln wall that it reads (type K).                                                                             | Firing-log fields "Probe {n}".                                                                                         |
| peephole (spy hole)                                                         | A small hole through the kiln wall, closed with a plug.                                                                                                       | Firing-log field "Spy".                                                                                                |
| vent, downdraft vent, hood vent                                             | A fan unit that draws fumes out of the kiln and outdoors; a canopy over the kiln.                                                                             | "vent" is also the verb ("vent the kiln") and a vent hole.                                                             |
| candling (preheat)                                                          | Holding the kiln at a low temperature, lid propped or vent on, until the last moisture leaves the ware.                                                       | **show English**. The sources do not use "water smoking". The "mirror test" checks it.                                 |
| burnout                                                                     | Burning off carbon and organic matter early in the firing.                                                                                                    | "insufficient burnout".                                                                                                |
| red heat, dull red                                                          | The kiln's visible glow; dull red is its first stage.                                                                                                         |                                                                                                                        |
| quartz inversion                                                            | The sudden change in size of silica in clay at 573 °C (1063 °F), up and down.                                                                                 | **show English**.                                                                                                      |
| oxidation                                                                   | Firing with plenty of oxygen, as in electric kilns.                                                                                                           | **show English**.                                                                                                      |
| reduction                                                                   | Firing with the oxygen restricted, usually in gas or wood kilns.                                                                                              | **show English**. Not a decrease or a discount.                                                                        |
| atmosphere                                                                  | The gases in the kiln: oxidation, reduction or neutral.                                                                                                       | Firing-log field "Atmosphere".                                                                                         |
| drop-and-hold                                                               | Firing to the top, cooling quickly by about 56 °C (100 °F) and holding, to heal pinholes.                                                                     | **show English**. One source title says "drop-and-soak".                                                               |
| slow cooling (slow cool)                                                    | Cooling at a set rate under the controller instead of letting the kiln fall freely.                                                                           |                                                                                                                        |
| underfired, overfired                                                       | Given less, or more, heatwork than intended.                                                                                                                  | Also spelled "under-fired", "over-fired".                                                                              |
| kiln wash                                                                   | A heat-resisting coating, often kaolin and silica, that protects shelves from drips.                                                                          | Not washing.                                                                                                           |
| stilt                                                                       | A small stand with points that holds low-fire ware off the shelf.                                                                                             |                                                                                                                        |
| cookie                                                                      | A disc of bisque set under a pot to catch drips.                                                                                                              | Not a biscuit.                                                                                                         |
| Seger cones, Staffordshire cones, Bullers rings, Holdcroft bars             | Other heatwork indicators, not convertible to Orton numbers.                                                                                                  | Names are not translated; "rings" and "bars" are.                                                                      |
| Damper, Primary Air, Secondary Air, Burner, Pilots, Blower                  | Firing-log fields for fuel kilns: flue plate; air mixed at the burner, air drawn in around it; gas burners; small standing flames; a fan.                     | Short column labels. "Pilots" are pilot flames, not people.                                                            |
| Ox Probe                                                                    | Firing-log field: an oxygen probe reading the atmosphere.                                                                                                     |                                                                                                                        |
| Draw Tile                                                                   | Firing-log field: a test piece pulled from the kiln during firing.                                                                                            | "draw" means pull out.                                                                                                 |
| Delta, Temp Diff, Cones Top, Cones Bottom                                   | Firing-log fields: temperature differences; the cones at the top and bottom of the kiln.                                                                      |                                                                                                                        |
| Salt, Soda, Sawdust                                                         | Firing-log fields: what is added in salt, soda and sawdust firings.                                                                                           | "Soda" here is not Na₂O.                                                                                               |
| Ring {n}                                                                    | Firing-log field; the sources do not say what it is (a Bullers ring reading or a burner ring).                                                                | Ask before translating.                                                                                                |

## 5. Defects and glaze fit

| Term                        | Meaning here                                                                                                         | Notes                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| glaze fit (fit)             | How well glaze and body expansion match; a good fit leaves the glaze slightly compressed.                            | **show English**. "fit" is not fitness or a seizure; "only just fitted your clay". "Over-compressed" leads to shivering. |
| defect                      | A fault in the fired glaze.                                                                                          |                                                                                                                          |
| crazing (craze)             | A network of fine cracks: the glaze shrank more than the body.                                                       | **show English**. Not "crazy".                                                                                           |
| crackle                     | Crazing made on purpose, as in raku.                                                                                 |                                                                                                                          |
| shivering                   | Glaze flaking off rims and edges because it is over-compressed; the flakes are sharp.                                | **show English**. Not shivering with cold.                                                                               |
| crawling (crawl)            | Glaze pulling back into beads or islands in the firing, leaving bare clay; often after the coat cracked as it dried. | **show English**. The home-safety guide also uses "crawling" for babies.                                                 |
| pinholing (pinholes)        | Small holes or pits where gas escaped and the glaze did not heal over.                                               | **show English**.                                                                                                        |

## Words that look alike across languages but are traps

- **stoneware**: German _Steingut_ is earthenware (porous, low-fired); stoneware is _Steinzeug_.
- **glaze**: Polish _glazura_ is wall tile; a glaze is _szkliwo_.
- **biscuit**: once-fired, unglazed ware, not a sweet biscuit. In the firing guide "cookie" is also kiln furniture,
  not food.
- **soda**: in German and Polish _Soda_ (_soda_) is sodium carbonate, which English potters call soda ash; potters'
  "soda" is the oxide Na₂O.
- **potash**: German _Pottasche_ is potassium carbonate; potters' "potash" is the oxide K₂O.
- **silica**: the oxide SiO₂ (_silice_, _sílice_, _sílica_), not the element silicon (_Silizium_, _silicium_,
  _silicio_, _silício_, _krzem_) and not silicone, the rubber (_Silikon_, _silicone_, _silicona_).
- **respirator**: Spanish and Portuguese _respirador_ also means a hospital ventilator; make the mask sense clear.
- **china clay**: "china" is porcelain, not the country.

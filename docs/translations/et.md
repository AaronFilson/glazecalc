# Eesti: stiilijuhend ja sõnastik (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Estonian (`et`), standard written Estonian (eesti kirjakeel, as ÕS and the EKI teatmik
give it) for potters in Estonia. Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every
English term below. Every Estonian message and guide follows this page: one Estonian word for one thing. The terms come
from the two Estonian shops that sell glaze materials to potters (Emilie and Loovuspood, both in Tallinn), Nabertherm's
Estonian kiln manual, Valli Eller's ceramics handbook, Estonian Wikipedia, the EKI term bases, the Estonian texts of EU
law and of Estonian law, and the Estonian NEPSI guide on silica, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or
none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for an Estonian potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Both shops print Estonian names on their raw materials (Kriit, Põldpagu,
Pallsavi, Booraksfritt), and Loovuspood adds a line on what each one does, so the material names rest on firm ground.
Emilie shares article numbers with the Finnish shop Kerasil (740001, 747146) and often follows its spelling
(Strontiumkarbonaat, Sirkoonsilikaat, Kalsiumboraat, "Orton nro 6"); where that differs from standard Estonian, this
page follows ÕS and Loovuspood (strontsium, tsirkoon, kaltsium, nr). The only Estonian ceramics textbook found online is
Valli Eller's handbook, written in 1961 and published from the manuscript in 2002; a few of its words are dated (hapend
for oxide, keegel for cone, toorpõletus) and are not used. Estonian studio potters fire electric kilns by °C on a
controller: no Estonian source names the cones of a cone pack, the Kiln-Sitter, heatwork, line blends, the unity formula
or specific gravity, and those rows say "No source". Web search ran thin for potters' own forums and course notes, so
the defects and the kiln words rest on Eller, the shops and Nabertherm's manual more than on current studio usage.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

Formal: the second person plural, _teie_, almost always without the pronoun, as Estonian software does. MS (4.1.13):
"Use the second-person plural (teie) to address the user … Normally, the pronoun teie (sina) itself isn't needed, but if
you use it, make it lowercase"; (2.1.3) „Saate teha järgmist“ rather than „Te saate teha järgmist“; and "Avoid using
'we' in translations when referring to the computer or system": "We couldn't save the file." → „Faili ei saanud
salvestada.“ The forms:

- **Commands** (buttons, menu items, links that do something): the **imperative singular**, which Estonian software uses
  as the name of a command, never as _sina_-address: „Salvesta“, „Prindi“, „Võrdle“, „Kustuta minu konto“. MS: „Valige
  käsk Kopeeri.“, „valige nupp Loobu“, „nupp Proovi uuesti või … Välju“, „Salvesta nimega“. Not the plural
  („Salvestage“), not the _da_-infinitive („Salvestada“), not a noun („Salvestamine“) on a button.
- **Instructions** in running text and the guides: the **imperative plural**, verb first, no pronoun: „Sõeluge glasuur
  kaks korda läbi.“, „Kandke respiraatorit.“, „Ärge hingake tolmu sisse.“ (MS: „Proovige uuesti.“, „Vaadake see üle ja
  proovige uuesti.“). The CLP's precautionary statements use the _da_-infinitive („Kanda hingamisteede
  kaitsevahendeid.“): quote them as they stand, but write the app's own instructions in the imperative plural.
- **Statements about the reader** ("if you fire to cone 6", "your clay"): the second person plural verb without the
  pronoun: „Kui põletate koonuseni 6, …“; "your" is often dropped or is „oma“: „oma ahju kasutusjuhend“, „savimass, mida
  kasutate“.
- **Questions and confirmations**: „Kas soovite retsepti kustutada?“ (MS: „Kas soovite jätkata?“).
- **Errors and warnings**: a whole sentence ending in a full stop, impersonal (MS 4.1.14: error messages always end with
  a period): „Retsepti ei leitud.“, „Salvestamine ebaõnnestus.“ A warning to act takes the imperative plural: „Ärge
  kunagi …“.
- **"please"**: leave it out („Proovige uuesti.“). **"we"**: rephrase without it: "We could not confirm a public poison
  line here." → „Avalikku mürgistusteabeliini ei õnnestunud siin kinnitada.“; in the app's messages "We recommend" →
  „Soovitatav on …“. Never „me“, „meie“, „soovitame“.
- **"we" in the guides**: the guide's own advice is the guide's: "we suggest" → „see juhend soovitab“ (also where a
  first draft wrote „soovitatav on“); "in our view" → „selle juhendi hinnangul“; "we would not rely on them" → „selle
  juhendi hinnangul ei tasu neile loota“; "Our advice:", "Our suggestion:" → „Selle juhendi soovitus:“; "Our starting
  point:" → „Selle juhendi lähtepunkt:“; "our estimate", "our arithmetic", "our worked example" → „selle juhendi
  hinnang“, „selle juhendi arvutus“, „selle juhendi näide“; "we found no …" → „… ei leitud“; "we treat X as" → „see
  juhend peab X-i …“. The guide advises, estimates and counts; it never does a step itself (not „see juhend kannab
  respiraatorit“): the reader does, in the imperative plural.
- Not _sina_: the poison centre (MTK: „Kui sul on küsimusi …“) and Loovuspood's articles address readers informally; the
  app keeps _teie_.

Examples. Button: „Salvesta koopiana“. Error: „Faili ei saanud lugeda.“ Guide: „Kandke kuivade toorainetega töötades
FFP3-respiraatorit.“

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- Buttons and menu commands: imperative singular, as above: „Salvesta koopiana“, „Lisa tooraineid“, „Loobu
  proovikasutusest“. A command whose English ends in "…" keeps the ellipsis with no space before it (MS: „Muuda
  nime...“).
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: „Retseptid“, „Sätted“, „Põletuspäevikud“,
  „Koonused ja kuumustöö“. A heading that names an activity takes the _-mine_ noun (MS: "Downloading..." →
  „Allalaadimine...“): "Mixing the glaze" → „Glasuuri segamine“, "Typing amounts" → „Koguste sisestamine“. A heading
  that is a question stays one: "What to watch for" → „Millele tähelepanu pöörata“, "What to have ready when you call"
  → „Mida helistades käepärast hoida“. The same rule on the recipe page, in its help and in the guides: "Compare two
  recipes" as a heading → „Kahe retsepti võrdlemine“, "Record the Firing" → „Põletuse salvestamine“, "Be there when
  the firing ends" → „Kohalolek põletuse lõpus“, "Names change, so buy by chemistry" → „Muutuvad nimed: ostmine
  keemilise koostise järgi“. Two panel headings on the recipe page keep the English's call to the reader, as the review
  decided: "Bring back what the old materials gave" → „Taastage see, mida andsid vanad toorained“, "Make it from what
  you have" → „Koostage see oma toorainetest“.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: „Tooraine nimi:“. After a colon the text goes on in
  lower case (MS 4.1.4).
- Column headings and check boxes: capital first letter, no full stop unless a whole sentence (MS 4.1.14).
- Confirmations: the participle first, as the English: "Saved: …" → „Salvestatud: …“.
- Cross-references: "see X" in brackets → „(vt …)“, as MS's „Lisateavet vt …“; in a sentence, the imperative plural
  „vaadake“. A guide named in running text is a title of a work and takes quotation marks (EKI teatmik): „vt juhendit
  „Glasuuri valmistamine““; in a Markdown link the link text needs none: „vt juhendit
  [Glasuuri valmistamine](/guides/making-a-glaze)“.

### Capitalisation

Estonian rules (MS 4.1.4): the first word and proper names capitalised, everything else in lower case, in headings,
buttons, labels and tabs too. No English Title Case: "Fired Weight" → „Mass põletatult“. A name made of several words
capitalises only the first in a UI element (MS: „Valige Salvesta nimega.“). Short text in brackets that is not a
sentence starts in lower case: "(Recommended)" → „(soovitatav)“ (MS). Languages, nationalities, months and days are
lower case („eesti“, „saksa kriit“, „oktoober“). Names keep their capital in the genitive: „Ortoni koonus“, „Segeri
valem“. Trade and product names keep their own form (Orton, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc, BOTZ, „Kaoliin Grolleg“).

### Punctuation

- Quotation marks: **„…“** (U+201E, U+201C). EKI teatmik: ÕS 2013 adopted „ “, "millel on eesti keeles kõige tugevam
  traditsioon"; MS lists the same pair. Quotes inside quotes repeat the same marks (EKI teatmik: „Sa oleksid pidanud
  ütlema: „Teist on väga lahke …!“,“). Not straight "…", not „…” (U+201D, ÕS 2006's shape), not “…”, not «…» or »…«.
  Quotation marks are for quotations, titles of works (the guides' titles in running text) and words cited as words;
  never for a button or page name (MS: rephrase or use the name bare).
- Apostrophe: the typographic ’ (U+2019); MS prefers the curly one. Estonian needs it only when a foreign name or word
  is declined and its written end is not pronounced as written: „Cornish stone’i“, „Gerstley Borate’i“. Names that end
  as they sound take the ending straight on: „Ortoni“, „Segeri“, „Bullersi“, „Glazecalci“ (EKI teatmik: „Strasbourgis“).
  Prefer a generic noun before a trade name (see Pitfalls). Write `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`, `}`
  or `#`, and in the `safety`, `regions` and `records` files).
- No space before ; : ! ?.
- Dashes: only the hyphen and the en dash (MS). Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, the unit once at the end: „1222–1240
  °C“, „koonused 06–04“, „1–3%“ (MS: „700–800 eurot“, „1–6 km“). As a dash in a sentence, the en dash takes a space on
  both sides (MS; EKI teatmik rules 53–54). Minus: „–15 °C“. Never the em dash. The dash in a label such as "Fluxes -
  RO" is a spaced en dash: „Sulandajad – RO“.
- Ratios: a colon with a space each side (EKI teatmik: „79 : 81“): „8,5 : 1“, „SiO₂ : Al₂O₃“, "silica to alumina
  {ratio}:1" → „ränidioksiid : alumiiniumoksiid {ratio} : 1“.
- A figure followed by a full stop is an ordinal (EKI teatmik: „5. juuli“): „6. koonus“ is the sixth cone. Write „koonus
  6“, never „6. koonus“. Dates take the stop („9. oktoober 2026“).
- Case endings on an abbreviation or a number written in figures follow a hyphen: „CLP-i“, „KOK-i“, „EL-is“, „FFP3-le“.
  Formulas are not declined: put a noun before them („SiO₂ sisaldus“, „Al₂O₃ hulk“). A compound with an abbreviation or
  code takes a hyphen (MS: „PDF-fail“): „FFP3-respiraator“, „H-lause“, „ABC-tulekustuti“.

### Numbers and units

- Decimal comma (MS 4.1.14): „1,5%“, „0,01 g“, „1,45“. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders
  it.)
- Thousands: EKI teatmik groups digits from five upwards ("Viie- ja suuremanumbrilistes arvudes … kasutades tühikut (nt
  7 748 422)"), and the app prints four-digit numbers ungrouped ("1222 °C", `useGrouping: 'min2'` in
  `client/app/shared/format.ts`). So write **four-digit numbers ungrouped** („1240 °C“, „1000 g“) and group five digits
  and more with a **no-break space** U+00A0 („12 500 g“; MS „10 000“). Never a plain space (the check reads 12 500 with
  a plain space as two numbers), never a dot or a comma.
- A space before °C, °F and every unit symbol: „1240 °C“, „100 °C/h“, „30 g“, „80 mesh“ (EKI teatmik: „30 g, 15 €, –6
  °C“). But the **per cent sign and the degree of angle are written close up**: „15%“, „0,4‰“, „4°“ (EKI teatmik:
  "Arvuga kokku kirjutatakse kraadimärk … protsendi-, promillimärk (nt … 9%, 0,4‰)"). This differs from the Finnish and
  Croatian pages; the check reads both. Shops print „1050°C“ (Emilie, Loovuspood): do not copy it.
- A number in the English is a number in the Estonian, with the same symbol. MS (4.1.11) spells out one to ten in
  running text, but the README rules: „3 kihti“, not „kolm kihti“. Never replace a symbol with a word („1240 kraadi“ for
  "1240 °C" breaks the check). A temperature needs no ending: „1240 °C juures“ (the potters' idiom: Eller, Loovuspood,
  NAB) or „temperatuuril 1240 °C“.
- Time: the 24-hour clock with a **colon** („8:00–20:00“). EKI teatmik also allows the Estonian dot („8.30“), but the
  check reads 8.30 as a decimal, so never write it. "8am to 8pm" → „kell 8–20“; "8 to 5" → „8–17“. Dates are written by
  the app ({date}); in text, „9. oktoober 2026“ or „09.10.2026“.
- Inches and feet: „toll“ and „jalg“, the same in every file, with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives
  it: „2 tolli (51 mm)“, „3 jalga (91 cm)“.
- **Specific gravity** (the key `guides.density.sg`, English "SG {value}"): **„suhteline tihedus {value}“**; the setting
  "Specific gravity" → „Suhteline tihedus“. IATE gives „suhteline tihedus“ for "relative density; specific gravity": a
  ratio to water, which is 1,00, with no unit. Not „tihedus“ alone (kg/m³ or g/cm³), not the Soviet-era „erikaal“
  (specific weight). No potter source: Estonian shops give the water per kilogram of powder instead (Loovuspood: „1kg
  glasuuripulbrile lisada vett ca. 650-750ml“ for brushing). In the guides `{{SG 1.45}}` shows as „suhteline tihedus
  1,45“ (or „38 °Bé (suhteline tihedus 1,45)“), so the sentence never names the quantity again: „glasuur, mille näit on
  {{SG 1.45}}“, „lahjendage, kuni näit on {{SG 1.43}}“, „Läbipaistev: {{SG 1.46–1.48}}“; not „mille suhteline tihedus on
  {{SG 1.45}}“.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Estonian two categories:
`node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('et').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"` prints `[ 'one', 'other' ]`. The
rule: `one` is the integer 1 with no decimals shown; everything else is `other`.

| Category | Numbers                     | Example                                                      |
| -------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `one`    | 1                           | 1 tooraine, 1 retsept, 1 koonus                              |
| `other`  | 0, 2–20, 21, 101, 1,5, 1,0… | 2 toorainet, 5 toorainet, 21 retsepti, 3 koonust, 1,5 grammi |

After a numeral other than 1, where the phrase is nominative or accusative, the noun is **partitive singular** („2
retsepti“), and an adjective with it too („2 uut retsepti“). In every other case numeral and noun agree and the noun
stays singular, and a numeral in figures takes no ending: „2 retseptis“, „5 toorainega“. So `#` and `{count}` work in
any case: „{count} retseptis on pliid“. A numeral subject may take the verb in the singular or the plural; prefer „on“
(the same for both) or the impersonal: „Lisatud on {count} toorainet.“ `=0` takes the plural partitive or „ühtki“:
„Retsepte pole“, „ühtki retsepti pole“. Write both forms even where they read alike. A number argument outside a plural
("up to {most} materials") takes the partitive: „kuni {most} toorainet“.

### Cones

The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Emilie sells Orton cones under „Koonused“ („Orton nro 6 1222°C“, „Orton
nro 10 1305°C“; "nro" is the Finnish abbreviation, Estonian writes „nr“); Eller describes cones („koonuseid“) and their
numbers; MTB has „püromeetriline koonus“ and lists "Seger cone" as an English synonym of it. No Estonian shop sells
Seger cones, and no Estonian Seger table was found. So:

- "cone 6" → „koonus 6“; "cone 06" → „koonus 06“, read „null kuus“ (the zero stays: koonus 06 is far cooler than koonus
  6). "cone" → „koonus“ (gen. koonuse, part. koonust, pl. koonused).
- The number follows the noun and stays bare; **the noun takes the case**: "fire to cone 6" → „põletada koonuseni 6“;
  "at cone 6" → „koonuse 6 juures“ (as „1240 °C juures“); "a cone 6 glaze" → „koonuse 6 glasuur“; "cone 5–6" → „koonused
  5–6“; "cone 04 bisque" → „eelpõletus koonuseni 04“. Never „6-ni“ or „6. koonus“.
- **One cone hotter or cooler**: „ühe numbri võrra kuumem“ / „ühe numbri võrra jahedam“: "one cone hotter in the sitter"
  → „Kiln-Sitterisse ühe numbri võrra kuumem koonus“; "from cone 6 up" → „alates koonusest 6 ja kuumematel koonustel“;
  "below cone 03" → „koonusest 03 jahedamal“. Never „kõrgem“ / „madalam“, „üles“ / „alla“ for a cone: in the cooler
  series a higher number is cooler (koonus 05 on jahedam kui koonus 04). "go one cone hotter in the sitter" →
  „pange Kiln-Sitterisse ühe numbri võrra kuumem koonus“; "for each cone hotter" → „iga ühe numbri võrra kuumema
  koonuse kohta“. Temperatures stay „kõrgem“ / „madalam“ (and a cone offset moves a temperature „üles või alla“).
- "Orton cone" → „Ortoni koonus“; at the first mention on a page and wherever systems are compared: „Ortoni koonus 6“,
  then „koonus 6“.
- **Never** write „Segeri koonus 6“ or „SK 6“ for an Orton number, although MTB equates the English terms. „Segeri
  koonused“ only where the English says "Seger cones", and then they mean the SK series.
- Not „keegel“ (Eller's old word; today a bowling pin).

### The unity formula

„**Segeri valem**“, with „(UMF)“ after it the first time on a page: „Segeri valem (UMF)“, then „Segeri valem“ (gen.
Segeri valemi, iness. Segeri valemis). The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are all
„Segeri valem“ / „Segeri valem:“. No source: Eller leaves glaze chemistry out ("välja jäänud spetsiaalne ainestik nagu
keraamika keemia … glasuuride retseptuur") and neither shop sells or explains calculation. The name follows the one
European potters use after Seger (German _Segerformel_, Finnish _Segerin kaava_, Croatian _Segerova formula_), with
„valem“, the Estonian chemistry word for formula. Not a literal „ühikmolekulaarvalem“, and do not alternate with
„empiiriline valem“: one name for one thing. For an Estonian potter to confirm.

**The scaling is not the formula.** The step that divides everything by the total of the fluxes ("Unity" as a step,
"unification", "in unity") is **„ühele viimine“**, never „Segeri valem“: "4. Unity" → „4. Ühele viimine“;
"Unification is just a scaling tool" → „ühele viimine on „lihtsalt teisendusvahend““; "fluxes, alumina and silica in
unity" → „sulandajad, alumiiniumoksiid ja ränidioksiid ühele viidud kujul“. "in the unity formula" stays „Segeri
valemis“.

### Gender-neutral wording

Estonian has no grammatical gender and one pronoun, _tema_, for everyone, so most sentences need nothing. Follow MS 3.2:
"Avoid the use of compounds containing gender-specific terms (mees, naine, etc.)": „abiline“, not „abimees“; „juhataja“,
not „esimees“; „müügiesindaja“, not „müügimees“; no slashed pairs („ärimees/ärinaine“). Give generalisations in the
plural („keraamikud“, „algajad“), and render a singular "they" for one person as „tema“ (MS). A potter is **„keraamik“**
(EKSS; Loovuspood „hobikeraamikule“, „keraamiku tööpõll“), not „pottsepp“, who in Estonian also builds tiled stoves.

### The reader in the guides

The imperative plural for every step, in short plain sentences as the English: „Sõeluge glasuur kaks korda läbi 80 mesh
sõela.“ For explanations where the English says "you", the second person plural verb without the pronoun, or an
impersonal sentence: „Kui glasuurikiht on liiga paks, see voolab.“, „Eelpõletatud eset saab kohe glasuurida.“ The
purpose comes before the steps (MS 2.2.4): „Ahjuplaatide kaitsmiseks katke need kaitseainega.“ No _sina_, no _me_, and
no nominal style („sõelumise teostamine“). Quotations from sources are translated into „…“ and keep their figures as the
English writes them.

### Pitfalls

- **Glasuur** is a glaze (et.wikipedia, both shops, Eller); **glasuurima** is to glaze. **Email** is enamel on metal,
  and in ceramics an overglaze enamel (et.wikipedia: „glasuuripealseteks emailideks“); **vaap** is an old synonym of
  glaze (EKSS) that today means the enamel of a bath or a tooth; **lakk** is varnish (Loovuspood sells „Lakk“ for
  unfired clay and plaster). Never email, vaap or lakk for glaze.
- **Sulandaja** is a flux (Loovuspood: kriit „Sulamistemperatuuri alandaja“, baariumkarbonaat „Mati glasuuri sulandaja“;
  Eller „sulandajana“). **Räbusti** is a welding, soldering or smelting flux (IATE, MTB), and „fluss“ is not used.
  **Fritt** is a frit, never a flux.
- **Ahi** is a kiln and also a kitchen oven or a stove; write „keraamikaahi“ (Loovuspood) where the context does not
  settle it. **Põletus** is a firing, but „põletusoht“ is the danger of being burnt (NAB) and a burn is „põletushaav“:
  reread safety sentences. **Põletama** is both to fire and to burn.
- **Earthenware** is „madalkuumuskeraamika“ (et.wikipedia, Kivikeraamika: „madalkuumuskeraamika (1000–1200 °C)“);
  **fajanss** is fine white, tin-glazed faience (et.wikipedia, EKSS), **terrakota** unglazed red ware, **savinõu** any
  clay pot, and Esterm's „jämekeraamika“ and „peenkeraamika“ are building-material classes, not earthenware and
  stoneware. Stoneware is „kivikeraamika“.
- **Kriit** is whiting (both shops' bag: „Kriit“, CaCO₃); **lubi** is lime (CaO, and slaked lime), **lubjakivi**
  limestone.
- **The material and the oxide**: the potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are „naatrium (Na₂O)“ and „kaalium (K₂O)“;
  **sooda** is sodium carbonate (IATE), and potassium carbonate is „kaaliumkarbonaat“. The oxide SiO₂ is
  **ränidioksiid**, the material **kvarts**; **räni** is the element silicon (and Eller's old word for silica),
  **silikoon** the rubber, **ränikivi** the flint pebble.
- **Respiraator** in Estonian is the filtering mask (IATE: "particulate respirator; FFP mask" → „respiraator“;
  Loovuspood: „ühekordne respiraator hingamisteede kaitseks“), and a hospital ventilator is „hingamisaparaat“ or
  „ventilaator“ (IATE). „Ventilaator“ is also a fan: never use it for the mask, and write „väljatõmbeventilaator“ for a
  vent's fan.
- **Signal words**: in the CLP's Estonian text "Danger" is **„Ettevaatust“** and "Warning" is **„Hoiatus“** (CLP Article
  2; Terviseamet's table pairs H300 with „Ettevaatust“). „Ohtlik“ is not a signal word. Kiln manuals use the
  machine-safety words the other way round: in NAB, „ETTEVAATUST“ is the lowest level (caution), below „HOIATUS“ and
  „OHT“. In hazard text, use the CLP's.
- **Biskviit** is a sponge cake or a biscuit (EKSS), and „biskviitportselan“ (NAB) is unglazed porcelain. Bisque is
  „eelpõletus“.
- **Toores** is a trap: „toores savi“ is unfired clay (Ars Keraamika „toorele savile“; Helena pesa), **tooraine** a raw
  material, and „toorglasuur“ (EKSS) an unfritted glaze. So "raw" as in not calcined is „kaltsineerimata“, as in not
  fritted „frittimata“.
- **Kaal** is both a weighing scale and weight. Write „kaal“ for the instrument and „mass“ for a weight in labels
  ("Fired Weight" → „Mass põletatult“, "Batch weight" → „Segu mass“); rescaling a recipe is „teisendama“, never „kaal“.
- **Spelling**: write the ÕS forms „strontsium“, „tsirkoon“, „tsirkoonsilikaat“, „kaltsium“, „alumiinium“ (Loovuspood),
  not Emilie's Finnish-led „Strontiumkarbonaat“, „Sirkoonsilikaat“, „Kalsiumboraat“, „Alumiinuimoksiid“; product names
  themselves stay as printed.
- **Placeholders and trade names cannot be declined.** A placeholder takes no ending, and a trade name ending in figures
  or a silent letter reads badly with one, so put a generic noun in the needed case beside it: „toorainega {name}“,
  „retseptis {name}“, „fritti Ferro 3134“, „toorainet Gerstley Borate“, „ahjus Skutt KilnMaster“. {region} is a country
  name in the nominative, as the browser gives it („Läti“, „Ühendkuningriik“): put it after a dash or a colon, or after
  a generic noun: „Mürgistusabi – {region}“, „Poed: {region}“, „Kellele helistada (riik: {region})“; never „{region}-s“.
  „Glazecalc“ itself is declined (see section 3).
- **Units after numbers**: „80 mesh“ stays as it is and is not declined; let the noun take the case: „läbi 80 mesh
  sõela“, „80 mesh sõelaga“ (sieve openings „0,18 mm“).
- **"should" and "must"** in safety text (README): "should" → „peaks“ / „soovitatav on“ („Lapsed ei peaks ahju sisse
  vaatama.“); "must" → „peab“ / „tuleb“ („Tass peab vastama …“). "should not" is „ei peaks“, not „ei tohi“, which is
  "must not". "must be tested" is „tuleb katsetada“ (the test must be done), not „peab katse läbima“ (must pass it).
  "some fine quartz" is „teatud koguses peent kvartsi“ or simply „peent kvartsi“, never „veidi“, „pisut“ or „natuke“ (a
  little). Where the English only reports a source's figure, do not make it „peab“ or „tuleb“: "Rio Grande says at
  least 12 inches" → „Rio Grande annab vahekauguseks … vähemalt 12 tolli“. A heading or label that only names a thing
  takes no modal ("What to have ready" → „Mida … käepärast hoida“, not „Mis peab … olema“).
- **"not fully" is not "not at all"**: "may not melt fully" → „ei pruugi täielikult sulada“; "do not fully dissolve" →
  „ei lahustu täielikult“; "not always 1:1" → „ei ole alati 1 : 1“. „Täielikult“ or „alati“ stays inside the negation;
  never „ei pruugi üldse sulada“ or „ei sula“.
- **The United Kingdom** is „Ühendkuningriik“ („Ühendkuningriigis“, „Ühendkuningriigist“) everywhere, also for "UK" in
  running text and in "(UK: biscuit)" → „(Ühendkuningriigis biscuit)“, because the region picker shows the browser's own
  name from CLDR (`Intl.DisplayNames('et')` gives „Ühendkuningriik“). The adjective is „Briti“ ("UK potters" → „Briti
  keraamikud“). The region label "UK" stays (2.8).
- **Colloquial and Finnish-led forms** in shops are fine to recognise but not the app's words: „nro“, „automaatika“ for
  controller, „Reguleeraine“ (Emilie, a suspender), „pakkejalg“.

structure. For a native reader to confirm. A shop's "stock" is „müüma“ („poed, mis müüvad selle tootevalikut“), never
„pidama“, which reads as "must"; "in stock at" → „laos“; "Stock changes" → „Valik muutub“. „Sissehingatav fraktsioon“ is VV49's word for the inhalable fraction (notes 30 and 31); the respirable fraction
is „respireeritav fraktsioon“ (2.7). "per 100 g" keeps the symbol („g/100 g“), never „100 grammi kohta“ written out, so
the check finds the same figure and unit.

# Nederlands: stijlgids en woordenlijst (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Dutch (`nl`), for potters in the Netherlands and in Flanders
(Belgium). Both write the same standard Dutch; where Dutch and Flemish suppliers or potters differ, the Notes say so and
the translation uses the word both understand. Every Dutch message and guide follows this page: one Dutch word for one
thing. The terms come from Dutch and Flemish suppliers' catalogues (Keramikos, Silex, Creavisie, Colpaert, Keramiek
Centrum Limburg), a Dutch glaze textbook, potters' sites and the Dutch potters' forum, Nabertherm's Dutch manuals,
Dutch Wikipedia, IATE, and the safety regulators of both countries, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree
or none was found, the Notes say **Check**.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal throughout: _u_ and _uw_, as the plan decided, written in lower case (Dutch does not capitalise
_u_ as German does _Sie_). Never _je_, _jij_ or _jouw_, and never a mix. MS notes a trend towards _je_ in Dutch software,
and several sources here (KK, KCL, DFB) write _je_; the app does not. Short sentences in plain words, as the English is.
Use _alstublieft_ only where the English says "please" and a sentence needs it; "Please try again." →
_Probeer het opnieuw._ Error messages: "Could not …" → _… is niet gelukt._ or _Kan … niet …_; always a full stop.

**Should and must**, above all in safety text (the README: "should" is not "must"): "should" → _zou … moeten_ or
_het is aan te raden_ (“Welders glasses should be used” → _Er zou een lasbril moeten worden gebruikt_); "should not" →
_zou niet … moeten_; "must" → _moet_; "must not", "must never" → _mag niet_, _mag nooit_ (Dutch _moet niet_ can read as
"need not"). Not _hoort … te_ for "should", and not _moet_ for "should" or for a plain "the equipment to wear"
(_de te dragen beschermingsmiddelen_). "says never to leave" → _zegt … nooit … te laten_, not _zegt dat u … nooit …
moet laten_.

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels and tabs**, as Dutch software does (MS: _Toolbar nu installeren_,
_Klik op de knop Opslaan_; Windows and Office write Opslaan, Afdrukken, Annuleren, Verwijderen):

- Buttons and menu commands: the **infinitive, verb last**: _Opslaan_, _Opslaan als kopie_, _Afdrukken_, _Vergelijken_,
  _Grondstoffen toevoegen_, _Proefperiode wissen_. Not the imperative (_Sla op_) and not a noun (_Opslag_).
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: _Recepten_, _Instellingen_,
  _Stookverslagen_, _Kegels en heatwork_. A heading that is a question or an instruction in English may stay one.
- Field labels: a noun phrase, with the colon where the English has one: _Naam van de grondstof:_.
- Confirmations: the past participle first, as the English does: "Saved: …" → _Opgeslagen: …_.
- Progress: "Removing..." → _Verwijderen..._ (MS: _Updates zoeken..._, not _Bezig met …_).

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case everywhere: only the first word and proper names take a capital, in headings, buttons,
labels and tabs (MS 4.1.5: _Download de mobiele Teams-app_). English Title Case goes: "Fired Weight" →
_Gewicht na stoken_. Unlike French, Dutch capitalises languages and nationality adjectives: _Engels_, _Nederlands_,
_de Engelse term_, _Amerikaanse recepten_. Months and days are lower case. No capital after a colon unless more than one
sentence follows (MS). A word starting with _ij_ capitalises both letters: _IJzeroxide_. Orton, Seger, Glazecalc keep
their capitals; _kegel 6_ does not take one.

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: double typographic quotes **“…” (U+201C, U+201D)**; inside them, single ‘…’ (U+2018, U+2019).
  Dutch allows single quotes for the outer pair too, but the closing ‘ ’ ’ is the same character as the apostrophe,
  which Dutch uses often (_regio’s_, _foto’s_), so double quotes keep them apart. Not „…“ (dated in Dutch) or "…".
  UI names in running text take **no** quotation marks (MS: _Klik op de knop Opslaan_).
- Apostrophe: the typographic **’ (U+2019)**: _regio’s_, _pH’s_, _’s avonds_. This also avoids ICU's rule about a
  straight `'` before `{`, `}` or `#`. Never add an apostrophe genitive to a placeholder (`{name}’s`): write
  _de recepten van {name}_.
- No space before `;` `:` `!` `?` (unlike French).
- Ranges: an **en dash without spaces**, the unit once at the end, as the English has it: _1222–1240 °C_,
  _kegel 06–04_, _1–3%_. In a sentence _van 1222 tot 1240 °C_ is also fine; never mix (_van 1222–1240 °C_). (MS prefers
  a non-breaking hyphen for ranges; the en dash is kept because the English has it and the check reads it.)
- Hyphens in compounds with a symbol, number or code (MS, Woordenlijst): _SiO₂-gehalte_, _FFP3-masker_,
  _CLP-verordening_. A word group ending in a number takes the hyphen after it (_kegel 6-glazuur_), which reads badly:
  write _glazuur voor kegel 6_. Compounds with a person's name are one word: _Segerformule_, _Segerkegel_,
  _Ortonkegel_.
- Keep the trema and accents: _coëfficiënt_, _geïnstalleerd_, _beëindigen_, _craquelé_.

**Numbers in running text.**

- Decimal comma: _1,5_, _0,01 g_, _soortelijk gewicht 1,45_. (`{{SG 1.45}}` and `{{2232 °F; 1222 °C}}` in the guides stay
  as written; the page renders them.)
- Four-digit numbers are **not** grouped: _1222 °C_, _1000 g_, as the app prints them (`useGrouping: 'min2'`). Five
  digits and more take a period, as the browser's Dutch number format prints them: _12.500 g_. (TA prefers a space in
  technical texts and allows the period elsewhere; the check reads both.)
- A no-break space (U+00A0) before °C, °F and every unit: _1240 °C_, _100 g_, _60 °C/h_. **No space before %**:
  _15%_, as Dutch practice, Onze Taal and TA write it and as the app's own Dutch percent format prints it (TA notes the
  NBN norm's space for technical texts; not used here). A temperature in a compound loses its space: _de 573°C-grens_
  (avoid it).
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word, and the same unit follows it. Never add a number the English
  lacks: "To percent" is _Naar procenten_, not _Naar 100%_.
- After a number, Dutch measure words stay singular: _5 gram_, _2 kilo_, _3 liter_, _2 uur_, _5 procent_,
  _3 inch_, _10 voet_; but _10 graden_, _5 minuten_.
- "used at 1–3%", "added at about 2–10%" → _gebruikt in een hoeveelheid van 1–3%_, _toegevoegd in een hoeveelheid van
  ongeveer 2–10%_ (singular _hoeveelheid_, also for a range); not _bij 1–3%_, _met 1–3%_ or _in hoeveelheden van_.

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Dutch two categories, `one` and `other`
(`new Intl.PluralRules('nl')`: `[ 'one', 'other' ]`). `one` is exactly 1; everything else, 0 and 1,5 included, is
`other`: _1 recept_ (`one`); _0 recepten_, _2 recepten_, _5 recepten_ (`other`); a measure word after 1,5 stays
singular anyway (_1,5 kilo_). Write both forms; `=0 {…}` and `#` stay as
the English has them: `{count, plural, one {# grondstof} other {# grondstoffen}}`. Dutch has no case endings; what
changes with the number is the verb (_1 grondstof is verwijderd_, _3 grondstoffen zijn verwijderd_) and the adjective
before a _het_-word (_1 nieuw recept_, _5 nieuwe recepten_). An article before a placeholder depends on that noun's
gender, which the translator cannot see: write _de grondstof {name}_ or use a colon (_Verwijderd: {name}_), never
_het {name}_ or _de {name}_ alone.

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Dutch and Flemish shops sell Orton cones under that name,
in English (Silex “Orton cones large/small”, Colp “ORTON CONES GROOT 06 (995°-1011°C)”); Creavisie sells “Maxicones”
and “Minicones” numbered the same way (“SM-6 (±1241°C)”). Older Dutch books and many potters say _Segerkegels_:
GT's cone table gives true Seger numbers (SK 04a, 1020 °C), and DFB writes “cones (Seger kegels)” for Orton cones.
True Seger cones (SK) are a different series whose numbers do not match Orton's (44–52 °C apart at 05 and 04). So:

- "cone 6" → _kegel 6_; "cone 06" → _kegel 06_ (read _nul zes_; the zero always stays: kegel 06 is far cooler than
  kegel 6). A klei.nl post writes “cone no. 4 1020 graden” for cone 04: the lost zero is a real trap.
- "Orton cone" → _Ortonkegel_ (IATE 1434358 gives _pyrometrische kegel_ and _Segerkegel_ for the generic thing; Nab
  writes “pyrometrische kegel (cone)”); at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared:
  _Ortonkegel 6_, then _kegel 6_. The guides may say once that shops sell them as _Orton cones_.
- "cone 04 bisque" → _biscuitstook tot kegel 04_; "fire to cone 6" → _stoken tot kegel 6_; "a cone 6 glaze" →
  _een glazuur voor kegel 6_; "cone 5–6" → _kegel 5–6_; plural _kegels 5–6_.
- **Never** write _Segerkegel 6_ or _SK 6_ for an Orton number. _Segerkegel_ appears only where the English says
  "Seger cones", and then means the SK series.
- "one cone hotter", "one cone cooler" → _één kegel heter_, _één kegel koeler_; "one number hotter" → _één nummer
  heter_; "for each cone hotter" → _voor elke kegel heter_. Never _hoger_/_lager_ or _omhoog_/_omlaag_ for cones: in the
  zero series a higher number is cooler (kegel 05 is koeler dan kegel 04), so only _heter_ and _koeler_ say which way.
  _hoger_ and _lager_ stay for temperatures and numbers themselves (_een hoger nummer is koeler_).

**The unity formula.** _Segerformule_ (de), with _(UMF)_ after it the first time on a page (in the guides, in each
section where it first appears): _Segerformule (UMF)_, then _Segerformule_. WP and DFB write _Segerformule_, GT and klei.nl
_Seger formule_; one word, as _Segerkegel_. DFB glosses the English “unity formula” as _eenheidsformule_; do not use it.
The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" → _Segerformule_, _Segerformule:_. With English
terms on: `Segerformule<en>unity molecular formula</en>`, `{{en: unity molecular formula}}`.

**Gender-neutral wording.** Dutch needs little: address the reader as _u_; for people in general use the plural
(_pottenbakkers_, _keramisten_) or _wie pottenbakt_; _keramist_ is used for women and men alike. No forms such as
_pottenbakker/ster_ or with an asterisk, and no _hij_ for "the potter": rephrase with _u_ or the plural. Grammatical
gender of things (_de_/_het_) is unaffected.

**The reader in the guides.** _u_ and the imperative for instructions; the Dutch imperative is the bare verb, the same
for _u_ and _je_, so it stays formal: _Zeef het glazuur twee keer._, _Draag een stofmasker._, _Weeg de grondstoffen
af._ Not the dated _Zeeft u …_. For facts, the present: _Krijt smelt pas boven 1100 °C mee._ Explanations to the reader:
_Als u …, dan …_; _U kunt …_. Avoid the impersonal _men_ where the English says "you". Where the English says "we" (the
guides' authors), _we_: _We raden kegel 06–04 aan._ Quotations from sources are translated into “…” and keep their
figures as the English writes them.

**Feature names in running text.** The label as the UI shows it, capital first letter, no quotation marks and no
article (MS: _Ga naar Bureau-accessoires en start Rekenmachine_), after a verb or a common noun: _Kies Vergelijken._,
_de functie Lood vervangen_, _in Instellingen_. Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag.

**EU directives and regulations** take the form of the EU's Dutch texts: _Richtlijn 84/500/EEG_ (EEG, not EEC or EWG;
full title, as Warenwet quotes it: “Richtlijn 84/500/EEG van de Raad van 15 oktober 1984 betreffende de onderlinge
aanpassing van de wetgevingen van de Lid-Staten inzake keramische voorwerpen bestemd om met levensmiddelen in
aanraking te komen”), amended by _Richtlijn 2005/31/EG_; _Richtlijn (EU) 2017/2398_; _Verordening (EG) nr. 1272/2008_
(the _CLP-verordening_, as KK's bags say). Capital _R_ and _V_ before the number, as EUR-Lex's Dutch titles write them.

**Pitfalls in Dutch.**

- **Glazuur**, never _email_ (enamel on metal), _lak_ or _vernis_ (varnish) or _beglazing_ (windows). **verglazen** is
  to vitrify (WP Porselein “verglaast”), never to glaze: to glaze is **glazuren**.
- **Stoken** is to fire a kiln (Silex “Stookservice”, DFB, GT, KCL “stookprogramma's”); Flemish potters also say
  **bakken** (KCL “Bakservice”, Colp “tijdens het bakken”). The app writes _stoken_ and _de stook_, which both
  understand; _gebakken_/_ongebakken_ describe ware and clay (_ongebakken klei_). Not _branden_ or _brandprogramma_
  (Nab's Germanisms) and not _vuren_.
- **Soda** in Dutch is sodium carbonate (Silex “Natriumcarbonaat licht (soda)”), **potas** is potassium carbonate (KK
  “Potas (kaliumcarbonaat)”) and **natron** survives only in _natronveldspaat_. The potters' oxides "soda" and "potash"
  are _natrium (Na₂O)_ and _kalium (K₂O)_.
- **Silicium** is the element, **siliconen** the rubber; the oxide is _siliciumoxide_ (_siliciumdioxide_), the material
  _kwarts_; Arboportaal writes _silica_ in safety text.
- **Aardewerk** also means pottery in general in everyday Dutch (WP: “de voorwerpen die gebakken zijn uit aarde”); in
  the app it is always the porous low-fired body. **Gres** (Flemish, and Nab's Dutch manual) is stoneware, but also
  floor tiles: write _steengoed_.
- **Biscuit** is also a biscuit to eat, and _biscuitporselein_ is unglazed porcelain sculpture; in the app it is the
  first firing and its ware. Spell _biscuit_, not _bisque_ or _bisquit_.
- **Het stof** is dust; **de stof** is a substance or a fabric. In safety text the article matters.
- **Respirator** in Dutch is a hospital ventilator (_beademingsapparaat_); the mask is _stofmasker_ or
  _adembescherming_ (2.7). **Mondkapje** is a surgical or cloth mask, not protection against dust.
- **Ons** is 100 grams and **pond** 500 grams in the Netherlands: an ounce stays _oz_ (in words _ounce_), a pound _lb_
  (in words _Engels pond_). Never _ons_ or _pond_ for the imperial units.
- **Smeltmiddel**, not _vloeimiddel_ (also soldering flux; GT and Silex use both) and not _flux_.
- **Kegel** alone is ambiguous only across systems (see Cones). **Ovenplaat** is a baking tray in a kitchen; in the app
  it is always the kiln shelf.
- **Duurzaam** also means sustainable: for "durable" write _bestendig_.
- **Optrekken** is pulling up a wall on the wheel; do not use it for crawling (2.5).
- **Flemish and Netherlands Dutch where suppliers differ**: _bakken_ (BE) and _stoken_ (both) for firing; _densiteit_
  (BE, Colp “DENSITEITSMETER”) and _dichtheid_, _soortelijk gewicht_ (NL) for glaze density; Colp's _oxyde_, _zinkoxyde_
  (old spelling) and _oxide_; _technische fiche_ (BE) and _technisch informatieblad_ (NL); an electric circuit is a
  _groep_ in the Netherlands and a _kring_ in Belgium; three-phase is _krachtstroom_ (NL, Crea) and _driefasig_ (BE).
  In Belgian law _inadembare fractie_ is the respirable fraction and _inhaleerbare fractie_ the inhalable one (WASO);
  Dutch law says _respirabel_ and _inhaleerbaar_. The app writes the neutral form or the one both understand; where a
  region's text names its own law, keep that law's word.
- Do not turn a Dutch term back into the English loan where Dutch has a word: _fritte_ not _frit_, _houdtijd_ not
  _soak_, _ovenplaatbescherming_ not _kiln wash_. The English shows in brackets where the reader asks for it. Where
  Dutch potters use the English word themselves (ball clay, china clay, kiln sitter, heatwork), the glossary says so.

## 2. Glossary

Nouns carry their article: _de_ (common gender) or _het_ (neuter), and the plural where it helps. Dutch has no case
endings. "show English" marks the terms whose English a reader can ask to see after them (`<en>…</en>`, `{{en: …}}`).
In the Notes, Dutch words are in italics and quotations from sources in “…”.

# Slovenščina: slogovni priročnik in glosar (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Slovenian (`sl`), for potters in Slovenia. Every Slovenian message
and guide follows this page: one Slovenian word for one thing. Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md)
gives the meaning of every English term below. The terms come from what Slovenian shops sell and write (Manufaktura
Radovljica, Keraterm), Nabertherm's Slovenian kiln and controller manuals, Slovenian theses and course pages on pottery,
Slovenian Wikipedia, and, for safety, the Slovenian texts of EU law, Slovenian regulations and the CLP wording as the
Chamber of Commerce (GZS) publishes it. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for
a Slovenian potter to check.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal throughout: _vikanje_, the second person plural, as Slovenian software and Nabertherm's manuals
do: »Izberite …«, »Presejte glazuro dvakrat.«, »Nosite masko.« Never _ti_ in running text, and never _polvikanje_
(»ste shranila«): the formal past participle is the masculine plural, »Ste shranili recept?«, which also keeps it free of
gender. _Vi_, _vaš_ take a small letter (capital _Vi_ only in letters). Use _prosimo_ sparingly, only where the English
has "please"; MS answers a wrong password with »Poskusite znova.« alone. The app may speak as "we" where the English does
(»Priporočamo …«, MS 2.2). Drop most possessives (»Shranite recept«, not »Shranite svoj recept«; MS 4.1.3).

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels and tabs** (the Slovenian software convention, as in Microsoft's
Slovenian products: MS writes »v meniju ›Datoteka‹ kliknite ›Shrani‹«, »kliknite ›Prekliči‹«, »Organiziraj
priljubljene«, »Uporabi naveden profil po meri«):

- Buttons and commands: the **imperative singular**, which Slovenian software reads as the name of a command, not as
  _ti_: »Shrani«, »Shrani kot kopijo«, »Natisni«, »Primerjaj«, »Odstrani«, »Dodaj surovine«, »Zavrzi preizkus«. Not
  the formal imperative on a button (»Shranite«) and not a verbal noun (»Shranjevanje«), which reads as a heading.
  The imperative singular belongs on buttons and commands only, never in running text (there: kliknite »Shrani«, MS).
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: »Recepti«, »Surovine«, »Nastavitve«,
  »Dnevniki žganja«, »Stožci in toplotno delo«. A heading that is a question in English may stay one.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: »Ime surovine:«.
- Checkbox and option labels: the imperative singular or a noun phrase, as the English: »Prikaži angleške izraze«.
- Confirmations: past participle first, as the English: "Saved: …" → »Shranjeno: …«.

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case: only the first word of a heading, button, tab or label and proper names take a
capital (MS 4.1.4); never copy English Title Case. Short text in brackets after a label starts lower case:
»(priporočeno)«. Possessive adjectives made from a person's name keep the capital (SP): **Segerjeva formula, Ortonov
stožec, Segerjevi stožci**; adjectives in -ski do not. Names of languages and months are lower case (angleščina,
oktober). Product and system names keep their own form (Orton, Seger, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc).

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: **»…«** (U+00BB, U+00AB), the form Slovenian software uses everywhere (MS) and the first form SP lists;
  Nabertherm's manuals use it too. Inside them: ›…‹. Do not mix in „…“ or "…". A full stop after a quoted part of a
  sentence goes outside: … na »prvi vzorec«. (SP).
- Apostrophe: Slovenian hardly needs one; where a foreign name has one, use the typographic ’ (O’Neill). Names take
  case endings without an apostrophe: »Ortona«, »Segerja«, never »Orton’s«. Before `{`, `}` or `#` write `''` as the
  README says.
- No space before ; : ! ? (SP).
- Ellipsis: a space before it, »Nalaganje knjižnice …« (MS).
- Ranges: the en dash with **no spaces** (SP's _stični pomišljaj_: 80–100, 1941–1945; MS: »Strani 8–21«), unit once at the
  end: »1222–1240 °C«, »stožec 06–04«, »1–3 %«. In a sentence »od 1222 do 1240 °C« is also fine; never mix the two
  (»od 1222–1240 °C«). A minus sign before a number is attached: »–17 °C« (MS).
- Adjectives made with a figure take a hyphen (SP): »5-odstotna raztopina«, »100-odstoten«. A code follows its noun
  instead, with no hyphen: »maska FFP3«, not »FFP3-maska« (as IATE's _maska FFP_ and MS's _kodi PIN_).

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: »1,5 %«, »0,01 g«, »1,45«. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped (»1222 °C«, »1000 g«; SP's own examples write »8055«), as the app
  prints them. Five digits and more take a full stop, as SP, MS and the app's number format do: »12.500 g«. (SP allows a
  space for numbers over ten thousand in print; the app uses the full stop, so use it too. EU texts write »1 000«: do not
  copy that.)
- A space between a number and every unit, **including °C and %**: »1240 °C«, »15 %«, »100 °C/h«, »80 mesh« (MIRS:
  »t = 10 °C«, »3 %« are right, »10°C«, »3%« wrong). Shops print »1220 - 1280°c«; do not copy it. A plain space is fine
  in the files. An angle in degrees has no space (»3°«), but the app has none.
- Inches and feet are **palec** (pl. palci) and **čevelj** (pl. čevlji), never _col_ (colloquial); a pint is _pinta_, an
  ounce _unča_, a pound _funt_.
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word, even under 12 (MS allows words; the README does not).

**Dates.** The app writes a date in the nominative (»9. oktober 2026«), so a date cannot follow a preposition or a word
that needs another case. A label before a date takes a colon instead: »(preverjeno: {date})«, »Začetek veljavnosti:
{date}«, »Datum: {date}«; never »preverjeno {date}« or »velja od {date}«.

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Slovenian four categories: `one`, `two`, `few`, `other`. Write all four in every plural
message. The noun and the verb both follow the number:

| Category | Numbers                                       | Example                        | Verb with it                                     |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `one`    | 1, 101, 201 …                                 | 1 surovina, 1 recept, 1 polje  | singular: 1 polje _zahteva_                      |
| `two`    | 2, 102, 202 …                                 | 2 surovini, 2 recepta, 2 polji | **dual**: 2 polji _zahtevata_, _sta_             |
| `few`    | 3, 4, 103, 104 … (and, in CLDR, all decimals) | 3 surovine, 4 recepti, 3 polja | plural: 3 polja _zahtevajo_, _so_                |
| `other`  | 0, 5–100, 105 …                               | 5 surovin, 0 receptov, 5 polj  | **neuter singular**: 5 polj _zahteva_, _je bilo_ |

- After 5 and more the noun is in the genitive plural and the verb is singular (neuter in the past tense): »Zamenjanih je
  5 surovin.«, »Shranjenih je bilo 12 receptov.« A participle agrees the same way. Two examples:

  ```
  {count, plural, one {Zamenjana je # surovina.} two {Zamenjani sta # surovini.} few {Zamenjane so # surovine.} other {Zamenjanih je # surovin.}}
  {count, plural, one {# polje zahteva} two {# polji zahtevata} few {# polja zahtevajo} other {# polj zahteva}} pozornost.
  ```

- Decimals: CLDR puts every non-integer in `few`, but Slovenian writes a decimal with the genitive singular (»1,5
  grama«, »2,5 dela«). Counts in the app are whole numbers, so write `few` for 3 and 4. Where the number can be a decimal
  (the unit word in `{count, plural, =1 {part} other {parts}}`), use `one {del} two {dela} few {deli} other {delov}` and
  accept »1,5 deli«, or prefer a unit symbol, which does not inflect.
- `=0 {…}` and `#` stay where the English has them.

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). In Slovenia potters fire by °C on a controller: the shops
label every glaze and clay with a °C range (MR »Temperatura žganja 1220 - 1280°c«; KT »Botz tekoče glazure 1220 -
1280°C«), and no Slovenian shop listing cones was found. Nabertherm's AC590 controller has a cone assistant and calls
the cone »pirometrični stožec (konus)«. Seger cones, »Segerjevi stožci« (SK), are a different series whose numbers do
not match Orton's. So:

- "cone 6" → »stožec 6«; "cone 06" → »stožec 06«, read »nič šest« (the zero stays: stožec 06 is far cooler than
  stožec 6). Inflect the noun, never the number: »do stožca 6«, »pri stožcu 06«, »za stožec 6«.
- "Orton cone" → **»Ortonov stožec«** (pl. Ortonovi stožci): at the first mention on a page and wherever systems are
  compared (»Ortonov stožec 6«), then »stožec 6«.
- "cone 04 bisque" → »biskvitno žganje do stožca 04«; "fire to cone 6" → »žgati do stožca 6«; "a cone 6 glaze" →
  »glazura za stožec 6«; "cone 5–6" → »stožec 5–6«; "cone 6 glazes" → »glazure za stožec 6«.
- **Hotter and cooler cones**: "one cone hotter", "the next higher cone" → »stožec za eno številko vročejši«; "one cone
  cooler" → »za eno številko hladnejši«; "two numbers hotter" → »za dve številki vročejši«; "hotter cones" → »vročejši
  stožci«. **Never** »višji« or »nižji« for a cone: in the cooler series a bigger number is cooler (stožec 05 is cooler
  than stožec 04), so »višji stožec« says nothing certain. A temperature stays »višja« or »nižja«.
- **Never** write »Segerjev stožec 6«, »SK 6« or »SK 6a« for an Orton number. »Segerjevi stožci« appears only where the
  English says "Seger cones", and then means the SK series. Avoid »konus« outside a quotation from a manual.

**The unity formula.** **»Segerjeva formula« (f)**, with »(UMF)« after it the first time on a page: »Segerjeva formula
(UMF)«, then »Segerjeva formula«. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are all
»Segerjeva formula«, »Segerjeva formula:«. No Slovenian source was found for the name (see 2.1); it follows the
European pattern (Segerformel, Segerův vzorec, wzór Segera) and Slovenian word formation. Avoid a literal
»enotska molekulska formula«.

**Gender-neutral wording.** Follow MS 3.2: address the reader with _vi_, prefer plural or role nouns (»ljudje«,
»osebe, ki glazirajo«, »vsi, ki delajo v delavnici«) and impersonal forms (»treba je …«, »se …«), and avoid generic
_on/ona_. Formal _vi_ with the masculine plural participle (»ste stehtali«) is standard and gender-free. Where a person
noun cannot be avoided, the plural »keramiki« or »lončarji« covers everyone; use a pair form (»lončarke in lončarji«)
sparingly, and never slashes (»uporabnik/-ca«) in the UI. Grammatical gender of things is unaffected.

**The reader in the guides.** _Vi_ and the formal imperative, in short plain sentences, as the English: »Presejte
glazuro dvakrat skozi sito 80 mesh.« »Najprej žgite testno ploščico.« Avoid the impersonal »se« where the English says
"you", and avoid nominal style (»Izvedba sejanja se opravi …«). Quotations from sources are translated into »…« and
keep their figures as the English writes them.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with a capital first letter. Where the English wraps the
name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag and add no quotation marks. Where it has no tag, put the button's name in
»…« when the reader is told to use it (Kliknite »Primerjaj«.), and otherwise use the ordinary noun, lower case and
inflected (Primerjava pokaže …). Inside a tag that is not a button the noun inflects: "lead is off in
<settings>Settings</settings>" → »svinec je izklopljen v <settings>nastavitvah</settings>«. The fixed names:

| English                       | Slovenian (button, label)         | In running text                |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Compare                       | Primerjaj                         | primerjava; »Primerjaj«        |
| Suggest amounts (and compare) | Predlagaj količine (in primerjaj) | »Predlagaj količine«           |
| Match with what I have        | Sestavi iz mojih surovin          | »Sestavi iz mojih surovin«     |
| Replace lead                  | Zamenjaj svinec                   | »Zamenjaj svinec«              |
| Settings                      | Nastavitve                        | nastavitve, v nastavitvah      |
| Notebook                      | Beležnica                         | beležnica                      |
| Library                       | Knjižnica                         | knjižnica, v knjižnici         |
| Standard                      | Standardne                        | standardne surovine            |
| My materials                  | Moje surovine                     | vaše surovine; »Moje surovine« |
| Trial                         | Preizkus                          | preizkus                       |

**EU law.** As the Slovenian texts on EUR-Lex write it: »Direktiva Sveta 84/500/EGS« (EGS = Evropska gospodarska
skupnost), »Direktiva Komisije 2005/31/ES«, »Direktiva (EU) 2017/2398«, »Direktiva 2004/37/ES«, »Uredba (ES) št.
1272/2008«. _Direktiva_ and _Uredba_ keep their capital when they stand with the number, also mid-sentence (»v Direktivi
84/500/EGS«), and a regulation takes »št.« before its number. Never »84/500/EEC« or »84/500/EWG« in Slovenian text.

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **kamenina** (f) is stoneware; **kamnina** (f) is rock. One letter apart, and both appear here: the category
  "Feldspars and stones" is »Glinenci in kamnine«, the clay is »kamenina«. Proofread every one.
- **lončenina** (f) is earthenware. Do not use IATE's _fajansa_ (and _majolika_), which are tin-glazed earthenware
  only; Slovenian potters also call common red low-fire clay »majolika« (KAM), so avoid that word as the translation.
  Wikipedia also writes _lončevina_; use lončenina, as NAB and KAM do.
- **glazura** (f), never _lošč_ (old word for a lead glaze in folk pottery), _loščenje_ (burnishing), _emajl_ (enamel on
  metal), _lak_ (varnish) or _zasteklitev_ (windows; IATE's "glazing"). WP's synonym _posteklina_ is rare: avoid.
- **soda** in Slovenian is sodium carbonate (IATE: natrijev karbonat, soda, pralna soda) and _soda bikarbona_ is baking
  soda; **pepelika** is potassium carbonate. The potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are »natrij (Na₂O)« and »kalij
  (K₂O)«.
- **silicij** is the element, **silikon** the rubber, **silikat** a silicate. The oxide SiO₂ is »kremenica« (SSKJ) or
  formally »silicijev dioksid«; the material is »kremen« (quartz). **kremenjak** is flint; **kremenka** is diatomite (PR):
  do not use it.
- **glinica** (f) is alumina (SSKJ: the aluminium intermediate; Pleteršnik: Tonerde), not **glina** (clay).
- **tekoča glazura** in a shop is a ready-mixed liquid glaze (KT »Botz tekoče glazure«), not a runny one. For a glaze
  that runs write »glazura steče«, »odteka«; for a fluid melt »tekoča talina«.
- **talilo** (n) is a glaze flux; _fluks_ (IATE) is soldering or metallurgical flux.
- **respirator** in Slovenian is also a hospital ventilator (IATE has both senses). The mask is »zaščitna dihalna
  maska«; see 2.7.
- **biskvit** is bisque ware, but also a sponge cake; **žganje** is firing, but also brandy; **peč** is any kiln, oven
  or stove (a kitchen oven is _pečica_). Context settles each; where it does not, write »biskvitno žgani izdelki«,
  »žganje keramike«, »peč za keramiko«. Prefer _žganje_ to the colloquial _pečenje_.
- **šamot** (m) is grog, not a kiln shelf (that is »šamotna plošča«).
- **mol** is the mole (unit) and also the fish whiting (IATE): the material whiting is »kreda«.
- **preizkus / preskus**: »preizkus« is the app's trial; a laboratory test is »preskus« (EU: »preskusni pogoji«).
- **Dual**: two things take dual forms, in nouns, adjectives and verbs: »dve surovini sta«, »oba recepta«, »2 recepta
  sta shranjena«. Writing the plural for 2 (»2 recepti so«) is the commonest error. Formal _vi_ is never dual.
- **Regional and colloquial forms to avoid**: _materjal_ (write material, surovina), _col_ (palec), _farba_ (barva),
  _feldšpat_ (write glinenec, or Feldspat as on the bag), and the shops' anglicisms _spoti_ (speckles) and _semi_
  (semi-matt) outside product names.

## 2. Glossary

Nouns carry their gender: (m), (f), (n); pl. gives the plural, gen. the genitive where it helps. "show English" marks
the terms whose English a reader can ask to see after them (`<en>…</en>`, `{{en: …}}`).

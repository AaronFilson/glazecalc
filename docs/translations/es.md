# Spanish (Spain): style sheet and glossary

How to translate Glazecalc into the Spanish of Spain (`es`, European Spanish: _ordenador_, not _computadora_). The rules in [README.md](README.md) apply to every message; this page adds what is particular to
Spanish. The terms come from Spanish suppliers' shops and data sheets (Marphil, SIO-2 by Ceràmica Collet, Bisbal Ceram,
Prodesco), Nabertherm's Spanish controller manual, Infocerámica, Spain's official vocational syllabuses for ceramics, the Spanish texts of the safety law (INSST, BOE, CLP)
and Spanish Wikipedia; the sources are listed at the end. Rows marked _review_ are the decisions a Spanish potter should
check first.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register: usted, always.** Instructions use the formal imperative: _Elija el cono_, _Tamice el esmalte dos veces_,
_Pese cada material en la báscula_. Possessives are _su/sus_. Never _tú_ or _vosotros_ (Microsoft's es-ES guide now uses
_tú_; Glazecalc does not). Where _su_ could mean "his" or "their", use the article (_la receta_) or rephrase. Spanish
drops subject pronouns: write _usted_ only for emphasis or to clear an ambiguity.

**Buttons, menu items, tabs and links that do something: the infinitive**, as Spanish software does (Microsoft es-ES:
_Guardar como_, _Responder al grupo_, _Marcar todos como leídos_; [Microsoft style guide][ms-es]): _Guardar_,
_Imprimir_, _Comparar_, _Quitar_, _Descartar la prueba_. Field labels and column heads are nouns (_Cantidad_, _Nombre del
material:_). Headings are noun phrases or _Cómo_ + infinitive (_Cómo preparar un esmalte_). A message that tells the
reader what to do is a sentence in usted: _Introduzca un número, como {example}._ A link that reads as a sentence keeps
usted (_Cree una cuenta para conservarlo_); a link that is a button's label takes the infinitive (_Crear una cuenta_).

**Capitalisation: sentence case everywhere**: titles, headings, buttons, tabs, menus, column heads, captions. Capital
only on the first word and on proper names: _Registros de cocción_, _Guardar como copia_, _Fórmula Seger_. Lowercase:
languages, nationalities, months and days (_inglés_, _enero_), _cono 6_, material names (_feldespato potásico_) except
trade names. Accented capitals are required (_Óxido de zinc_). Short labels may drop the possessive where Spanish
software would (_Cuenta y ajustes_); sentences keep it.

**Quotation marks: « » first, “ ” inside them, ‘ ’ inside those** (_«Dijo: “un ‘cono’ caído”»_). This is the RAE's first
choice for print ([DPD: comillas][rae-comillas]); Spanish suppliers use it (Prodesco: «mojar», «pelar»); and it cannot
be confused with the straight `"` of JSON and ICU. Microsoft's guide uses “ ”; follow the RAE. A full stop or comma goes
after the closing mark: _…de cono 6»._

**Apostrophe**: Spanish needs none. In a foreign name (_O’Neill_) use `’`, never a straight `'`.

**Spacing**: no space before `; : ! ?` (unlike French). Opening `¿` and `¡` are required: _¿Quiere quitar esta receta?_
A label's colon follows the word: _Relación sílice/alúmina: {ratio}_ (the English "Alumina : {ratio}" has a space;
drop it). A space before `%` and before `°C`: _1,5 %_, _1240 °C_ ([DPD: símbolo, 5.4][rae-simbolo]); a no-break space is
better where the editor allows. Use the degree sign `°` (U+00B0), never the ordinal `º` that Spanish shops often type
(_1220ºC_): the checks do not read `ºC` as a unit. Ranges keep the English's en dash with no spaces: _1222–1240 °C_,
_cono 06–04_ (the RAE writes a hyphen; the en dash is the typographers' form and what the app prints). In a sentence
_de 1222 a 1240 °C_ or _entre 1222 y 1240 °C_ is equally good, numbers and unit kept.

**Numbers**: decimal comma: _1,5_, _0,45_, _densidad 1,45_. Four-digit numbers are never grouped: _1240 °C_, _1000 g_
([DPD: números][rae-numeros]; CLDR agrees). Five or more digits (rare): a narrow no-break space (U+202F), _10 000_,
never a point, which the RAE does not admit for thousands. Years, codes, CAS and phone numbers are never grouped. A unit
follows its number after a space: _100 g_, _500 ml_. Times on the 24-hour clock. The format example "12,345.6 or
12.345,6" stays as it is.

**Plurals**: Spanish has `one`, `many` and `other`; `many` is for round millions (_1 millón de_). Write all three even
when two read the same: `{count, plural, one {# receta} many {# recetas} other {# recetas}}`.

**Cones**: _cono 6_, _cono 06_ (lowercase _cono_; the zero stays and is read "cero seis"), _cono 5–6_. "Cone 04 bisque":
_bizcocho a cono 04_; "fire to cone 6": _cocer a cono 6_; "a cone 6 glaze": _un esmalte de cono 6_. "Orton cone": _cono
Orton_, plural _conos Orton_, as Spanish shops sell them (_Cono Orton 6_, _Cono Orton 06_, [SIO-2][sio2-conos]). Seger
cones (_conos Seger_) are a different series with different temperatures. Older Spanish books and some shops say "cono
Seger" for any cone (Prodesco: _Cono Seger 8_): never write Seger for an Orton number, and never convert one to the
other.

**The unity formula: _fórmula Seger_**, the name in Spain's official ceramics syllabus ([fritas][sepe-fritas]: "Fórmula
Seger", "formulación Seger"). The first time on a page write _fórmula Seger (UMF)_; after that _fórmula Seger_. The
app's short forms: panel "Unity formula" _Fórmula Seger_; "Unity:" _Seger:_; "Molecular / Unity" _Molecular / Seger_.
_Fórmula molecular unitaria_ may explain it once in a guide (_la fórmula Seger (UMF), o fórmula molecular unitaria_),
but is not the name.

**Gender-neutral wording**: the RAE rejects _@_, _x_ and _-e_, and doublets (_alfarero/a_) clutter a short UI. Use
words without gender: _ceramistas_ (for "potters"; _alfareros_ means traditional village potters), _personas_, _quien_,
_su taller_. Avoid adjectives and participles that agree with the reader: not _¿Está seguro?_ but _¿Quiere quitarla?_;
not _Bienvenido_ but _Le damos la bienvenida_ or _Hola de nuevo_; not _Conectado como {name}_ but _Ha iniciado sesión
como {name}_.

**The reader in the guides**: usted throughout. Steps in the formal imperative (_Tamice el esmalte_, _Deje enfriar el
horno_), facts in the present. The English's "we suggest" is _le recomendamos_ or _recomendamos_. _Su horno_, _sus
piezas_. Never the impersonal _uno_. Keep the English's short sentences; do not join them.

**Feature names in running text**: as on the button (infinitive, sentence case), bold where the English is bold, never
in quotes: **Comparar**, **Sugerir cantidades y comparar**, **Rehacer con lo que tengo**, **Sustituir el plomo**. The
others: Settings _Ajustes_ (a page name, capital: _en Ajustes, en la página de su cuenta_); Notebook _cuaderno de taller_
and Library _biblioteca_ (common nouns, lowercase); Standard and My materials are tab names, _Estándar_ and _Mis
materiales_; Trial _periodo de prueba_ (lowercase). "Click" is _pulse_ (works for mouse and touch). Glazecalc is not
inflected and takes no article; where an adjective must agree, it is masculine (_el programa_): _Glazecalc es gratuito_.

**Pitfalls in this field**:

- _Esmalte_ is the glaze. Not _glaseado_ (icing), not _barniz_ (varnish; an old word for glaze), not _vidriado_ except
  for traditional lead, tin or salt glazes when the English is historical. _Esmalte_ also means enamel on metal: context
  makes it clear.
- _Cristalino_ is crystalline. A clear glaze is _transparente_ (Italian _cristallina_ is the trap).
- _Loza_ is also everyday crockery (_fregar la loza_), and _gres_ is also a floor tile (_gres porcelánico_ is
  porcelain tile). Say _pasta_ or _pieza_ when the sense could slip.
- _La sílice_ (feminine; _el sílice_ is a common error) is not _silicio_ (the element) or _silicona_ (the rubber).
- _Potasa_ and _sosa_ are potassium and sodium carbonate or hydroxide (_sosa cáustica_). The oxides K₂O and Na₂O are
  _el potasio_ and _el sodio_, as potters say _el calcio_.
- _Respirador_ also means a hospital ventilator, and _mascarilla_ alone now suggests a surgical mask: name the class,
  _mascarilla FFP3_.
- _Horno_ is also the kitchen oven: in the home-safety guide say _horno cerámico_ where it could be read either way.
  Fire is _cocer_ and firing _cocción_ (two c's); not _hornear_ (bread) or _quemar_.
- _Bizcocho_ is also a sponge cake; in pottery it is the bisque, and needs no explanation.
- _Balance_ is accounts: use _equilibrio_. _Fórmula_ is chemistry; the list of materials is a _receta_.
- _Carga_ is a kiln load, _tanda_ an amount of glaze mixed, _lote_ a supplier's production lot: keep them apart.
- Latin American forms to avoid: _quema_ (firing), _sancochado_ (bisque), _aserrín_ (_serrín_), _horno a gas_ (_horno
  de gas_), _computadora_ (_ordenador_), _gravedad específica_ (_densidad_).
- Bag names keep old spellings: _bióxido de titanio_, _silicato de zirconio_ (the RAE writes _dióxido_, _circonio_).
  Use the bag's name for a material and the RAE's in chemistry: _dióxido de titanio (TiO₂)_.

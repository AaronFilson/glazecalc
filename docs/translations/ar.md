# العربية: style sheet and glossary

For translating Glazecalc into Arabic (`ar`), in Modern Standard Arabic, one page for every Arab country: Egypt's
region first (`EG`), then the United Arab Emirates, Morocco and Algeria, and the Levant, Iraq and the Gulf, who read the
same page. Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every
Arabic message and guide follows this page: one Arabic word for one thing. The Arab regions have no region data in the
library yet (Phase C: EG, AE, MA and DZ are researched in English, `scratch/round6/research/eg-ae-ma-dz.md` in the main
checkout), so the Arabic pages must read well with temperatures and Orton cones and say nothing about SK cones. The
terms come from an Egyptian supplier of clay, glazes and kilns, an Iraqi university paper on glazes, Arabic Wikipedia,
Algeria's Arabic Journal officiel, Oman's guide to chemical labels, the Google, Apple and Microsoft help pages, and the
Microsoft Arabic style guide, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was read, the Notes say so:
those rows are for an Arab potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Arabic pottery has a stable core (فخار, خزف, فرن, حرق, طين, فلسبار,
كاولين, كوارتز, أكسيد) that the shop, the paper and Wikipedia share. The glaze itself has three names by region: the
standard **الطلاء الزجاجي** (Egyptian and Saudi teaching, Algeria's law writes the ware "مزججة"), the studio loanword
**الجليز** (the Cairo shop's menus; Google suggests it first for cake and doughnut glazes) and the Iraqi **الزجاج** (the
Babylon paper). The glaze chemistry words (صواهر, مثبتات, صيغة سيغر) rest on one Iraqi paper and on usage; studio words
for kiln parts, controllers, firing logs and most defects have no Arabic source read: Arab potters often say the English
word. No Arabic text of an Egyptian decree or standard was read (the Egyptian Knowledge Bank's journals refused the
connection and then answered 429), and no ALECSO unified dictionary on chemistry or ceramics was found online. The rows
a native potter should check first are marked **Native check**: الطلاء الزجاجي against الجليز, الخلطة against الوصفة,
صيغة سيغر and its spelling, الصواهر and المثبتات, الكثافة النسبية, التكتل (crawling), الثقوب الدبوسية, التقشر, رشح
المعادن, the GHS wording, الأدلة and كتيّب التعليمات, the zero form of the plurals, and التحقق بخطوتين.

## Choices for the owner and a native potter

These are the questions this sheet cannot settle from sources; each has a recommendation, used below until answered.

- **The glaze:** **الطلاء الزجاجي** (the standard term, Egyptian and Saudi teaching, Google's school questions), short
  **الطلاء** where the sentence is plainly about glaze, and never **الجليز** alone: it is the Egyptian studio word (Clay
  Supply), but Google reads it as a cake or paint glaze. The English-terms setting shows "glaze" beside it. Recommended.
- **The recipe:** **الخلطة** (pl. الخلطات), as the Cairo shop ("خلطة الطلاء") and the Iraqi paper ("خلطة الزجاج") write
  it, rather than **الوصفة** (a cooking recipe or a prescription). A potter should confirm; "خلطة" alone also names home
  beauty mixtures on the web, so titles say "خلطات الطلاء الزجاجي".
- **Seger:** **سيغر** (غ, the pan-Arab spelling of a G), for the formula (**صيغة سيغر**) and the cones; Iraq writes
  سيكر (the Babylon paper), and Egyptian spelling would give سيجر (not seen in a source read). Native check.
- **Gender:** the reader is never addressed by a masculine or a feminine form where an impersonal form, a passive or a
  verbal noun will do (MS 3.1), although Google and Apple write masculine imperatives. Recommended: see Gender-neutral
  wording.
- **Digits:** the translation files write 0 to 9 only; the app writes Arabic-Indic digits for Egypt and the Gulf and 0 to
  9 for the UAE and the Maghreb when it formats a number (the owner's decision, 2026-10-20). Decided.
- **Units:** Latin symbols, left to right (°C, g, mg/m³), never the Arabic abbreviations the MS guide allows (كم, سم, جم)
  or that Algeria's law writes (مم, ملغ/ل) (the owner's decision, 2026-10-20). Decided.
- **Guide and manual:** the app's five guides are **الأدلة** (sing. **الدليل**); a kiln's, controller's or sitter's
  manual is **كتيّب التعليمات**, never "دليل", although Clay Supply lists "دليل" in a kiln's box.
- **Poison line:** Morocco's CAPM is verified (the region research); Egypt, the UAE and Algeria have none confirmed, so
  the text names the emergency number there.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

**Formal Modern Standard Arabic**, plain and short, as the MS guide asks ("clear, friendly and concise"), one register
across the interface, errors, notices, emails, the safety text and the guides. No colloquial word or grammar (no "اللي",
"بتاع", "ده", "مش"), no Gulf or Maghrebi forms, no rhetorical flourishes, no "إنّ" or "لقد" added for weight.

- **Buttons, menu items, tabs:** the verbal noun (المصدر), as Arabic software writes menu commands (MS 5.4.6: "حفظ",
  "طباعة", "حذف"): **حفظ**, **طباعة**, **مقارنة**, **إزالة**, **إلغاء**, **إضافة مادة**. Never an imperative ("احفظ") and
  never "قم بـ".
- **Errors:** a short plain statement, with the MS guide's phrases (5.4.1): "تعذّر حفظ الخلطة. يُرجى المحاولة مرة أخرى."
  ("Cannot save the recipe. Try again."), "تعذّر العثور على المادة.", "الخادم غير متوفر حالياً."
- **Notices:** "تم حفظ الخلطة." or "حُفظت الخلطة." for "Saved."; "لا يمكن التراجع عن هذا الإجراء." for "This can't be
  undone."
- **Guide sentences:** impersonal or passive: "يُنخَل الطلاء مرتين عبر منخل 80 مش." ("Sieve the glaze twice through 80
  mesh."); "يجب ارتداء كمامة تنفسية عند وزن المواد الجافة." ("Wear a respirator when weighing dry materials.").
- **Should, must, may:** keep them apart. "must" → **يجب** + verbal noun or أن ("يجب اختبار القطعة"); "must not" → **يجب
  ألا** or **لا يجوز**; "should" → **يُنصح بـ** or **ينبغي** ("يُنصح بارتداء القفازات"); "should not" → **لا يُنصح بـ**
  or **ينبغي ألا**; "may" (a hazard, a possibility) → **قد** with the present ("قد يسبب السرطان"); "may" (permission) and
  "can" → **يمكن**. Never يجب for "should", never يُنصح for "must". "must be tested" → "يجب اختبارها" (the test is done),
  never "يجب أن تجتاز الاختبار" (passed). "some" (some fine quartz) → "بعض الكوارتز الناعم", never "قليل من".
  "never" → "لا … أبداً" only where the English says never; no "إطلاقاً", "دائماً" or "حتماً" the English lacks.

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- **Buttons and menu commands:** the verbal noun: "حفظ", "طباعة", "مقارنة", "حذف", "إلغاء", "إضافة مادة", "حفظ كنسخة".
- **Headings, tabs, page titles:** a noun phrase: "الخلطات", "الإعدادات", "سجلات الحرق", "المخاريط والشغل الحراري". A
  heading that names an activity is a verbal noun: "خلط الطلاء", "طباعة الكميات". A heading that is a question stays one,
  with ؟: "لماذا يسيل الطلاء؟".
- **Field labels:** a noun; keep the colon where the English has one, with no space before it: "اسم المادة:".
- **Confirmations:** "Saved: {name}" → "تم الحفظ: {name}"; "Removed" → "تمت الإزالة".
- **Possessives:** "Your" is the suffix ـك once, or left out where the owner is plain (MS 2.1.3: "حسابك", not "الحساب
  الخاص بك"); "My" is ـي ("موادي", "خلطاتي"). Unvocalised ـك is the same for a man and a woman, so it is allowed.

### Capitalisation

Arabic has no capitals (MS 4.1.5). The English's Title Case and sentence case mean nothing here. A button or page name
the English capitalises in running text is set off by «» (MS 4.1.4 asks for quotation marks), unless the English wraps
it in a tag: then the tag stays and no quotation marks are added. Latin words inside Arabic text (Orton, Ferro 3134,
SiO₂, UMF, EnviroVent) keep the English's letters and case exactly.

### Script, direction and spaces

- **Right to left.** The page is set `dir="rtl"` by the app; the text is written in logical order, as typed. Latin runs
  (units, formulas, trade names, cone numbers with their letters, addresses, codes such as FFP2 or H360FD) stay exactly as
  the English writes them and run left to right inside the Arabic; the app isolates the figures with Latin units. A figure
  and its unit stay together in the English's order: "1222 °C", "10 g", "0.1 mg/m³".
- **No control characters.** No U+200E, U+200F, U+061C, U+202A–U+202E or U+2066–U+2069 in a translation: the app sets
  the direction; such marks also break the numbers check.
- **Spaces** as the English has them around Latin text and figures ("يُحرق إلى 1222 °C", "مخروط Orton 6"). The
  conjunction **و** is joined to the next Arabic word ("والكاولين") and stands with a space before a Latin word, a figure
  or a placeholder ("و Ferro 3134", "و {name}").
- **Letters and spelling:** hamzat al-qat' and al-wasl as the standard writes them (MS 4.1.9: "إضافة", "استخدام"); final
  ى and ي kept apart ("على", never "علي" for the preposition); ة never written ه; no tatweel (ـ) to stretch a word.
- **Vowel marks:** none, except where a word would be misread (MS 4.1.7): a passive verb takes its damma ("يُنصح",
  "يُحرق", "حُفظت") where the active reading would mislead; tanwin on the alef only ("أيضاً", "مجاناً").
- **Loanwords** as the sources spell them: كاولين, فلسبار, كوارتز, سيليكا, ألومينا, فريت, بنتونيت, روتيل, زركون, راكو;
  أكسيد (not the Iraqi أوكسيد).
- **Arabic and Markdown:** `**` and `*` around whole Arabic words render; never inside a word (the letters would lose
  their joins: "**ال**طلاء" is wrong). Keep Latin punctuation and brackets outside the emphasis. A paragraph, heading or
  list item starts with an Arabic word, not with a figure or a Latin name, so that no reader can take it for left to
  right: "يُحرق Ferro 3134 …", not "Ferro 3134 يُحرق …".

### Punctuation

The Arabic comma **،** (no space before, one after), semicolon **؛** and question mark **؟**; the full stop, colon and
exclamation mark as in English (MS 4.1.14). Quotations and names of buttons and pages in **«»**, the quotation marks of
Arabic print, which need no escaping in the JSON files (MS prefers straight quotes; «» are chosen for that reason);
parentheses as in English, which the browser mirrors. The en dash for ranges between figures (10–60%, cone 06–04) with
no spaces; the em dash is avoided. A list of three: "الكاولين والفلسبار والكوارتز" (no comma before و).

### Numbers in the translation files

- **The digits 0 to 9 only**, never Arabic-Indic (٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩) or Persian (۰۱۲۳) digits, in every message and guide. The
  app writes each country's own digits when it formats a number at runtime (Arabic-Indic for Egypt and the Gulf, 0 to 9
  for the UAE and the Maghreb; the owner's decision, 2026-10-20), and the numbers check reads 0 to 9 only. MS 4.1.12:
  "always use the Western Arabic Numbers (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, etc.)".
- **Decimal point and thousands comma** as the English writes them, never the Arabic decimal separator ٫ or thousands
  separator ٬: 1.5; 0.025; 12,500; four-digit temperatures ungrouped (1222 °C, 1000 g), as the English writes them and
  the check reads them (CLDR's bare `ar` writes 1,234.5).
- **Percent:** "5%", no space between the number and the sign (MS 4.1.15), the Latin sign, never ٪; a range "10–60%".
- **Units:** the English's symbols, in Latin letters, as the app prints them: g, kg, mg, µg, mL, L, mm, cm, °C, °F, °Bé,
  %, mg/m³, µg/cm²; never غ, جم, كغ, مم, سم, م° or "درجة مئوية" in running text. Inches and feet are the words **بوصة**
  and **قدم**, with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives it: "12 بوصة (30 cm)".
- **A number the English writes as a word** ("two coats", "three tiles", "once", "one number hotter") stays a word, since
  a digit would be a figure the English lacks and the check rejects it: "طبقتان", "ثلاث بلاطات", "مرة واحدة", "أسخن
  برقم واحد". Where the English has the digit, the digit stays: "3 coats" → "3 طبقات".
- **Dates:** the month named beside the year, Gregorian: "14 أكتوبر 2026", "أكتوبر 2026". The month names are CLDR's
  `ar` (Egypt's and the Gulf's): يناير، فبراير، مارس، أبريل، مايو، يونيو، يوليو، أغسطس، سبتمبر، أكتوبر، نوفمبر، ديسمبر.
  The Levant and Iraq say كانون الثاني … كانون الأول, Morocco يناير، فبراير … ماي، يوليوز، غشت، شتنبر، نونبر، دجنبر,
  Algeria and Tunisia جانفي، فيفري، أفريل، ماي، جوان، جويلية، أوت; all read the Egyptian names, which are used. Never a
  Hijri date (the English has no 1448), and no "م" after the year.
- **Clock times:** the 24-hour clock, "20:00", "20:30"; "8am to 5pm" → "من 8:00 إلى 17:00". Never ص or م with a figure
  ("8 م"), which the check would read as 8, never "الثامنة مساءً" (a figure written as a word).
- **No figures the English lacks:** no converted units, no Hijri years, no SK or Orton equivalents, no standard or decree
  numbers (ES, GSO) where the English names none.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Arabic six categories (cardinal), and every plural message needs all six, even where it has `=0`, which
stays where the English has it (the check asks for them all). `node -e "console.log(new
Intl.PluralRules('ar').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"` prints
`[ 'zero', 'one', 'two', 'few', 'many', 'other' ]` (Node 24).

| Category | Numbers                                      | Form of the noun                        | Example                                     |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `zero`   | 0                                            | no figure: "no …" (MS 4.1.12)           | لا توجد مواد                                |
| `one`    | 1                                            | singular, with واحد/واحدة, no figure    | مادة واحدة                                  |
| `two`    | 2                                            | dual, no figure (MS 4.1.12)             | مادتان (مادتين after a preposition or verb) |
| `few`    | 3–10, 103–110, 203–210 …                     | plural, genitive                        | # مواد                                      |
| `many`   | 11–99, 111–199 …                             | singular, accusative (tanwin unwritten) | # مادة                                      |
| `other`  | 100–102, 200–202 …, and fractions (1.5, 0.5) | singular, genitive                      | # مادة                                      |

sentence it may read "# مادة" (as `other`) if "لا توجد" does not fit, and a translator says which in the flags. Where the English has `=0`,
the `zero` form is never shown but is still written. The dual changes with its place in the sentence (مادتان as the
subject, مادتين after a preposition or as an object): write the case the sentence needs. Numbers 3 to 10 written as
words take the reverse gender of the noun (ثلاث بلاطات, ثلاثة أيام); with a figure the question does not arise.

### Placeholders and names

Placeholders and trade names take no article, no attached preposition and no case ending, so a generic noun carries the
grammar (MS 5.4.1: find out what replaces the placeholder):

- **{region}** is the country's name in Arabic from the browser, with or without its own article ("مصر", "ألمانيا",
  "الولايات المتحدة", "المملكة المتحدة"). Use separate prepositions ("في {region}", "من {region}", "إلى {region}"); never
  attach بـ, لـ or كـ ("ل{region}" would give "لالولايات" for "للولايات"); never a nisba adjective built on it
  ("{region}ية"). Where an adjective must agree, add a noun: "قوانين البلد في {region}".
- **{name}** is a recipe's or material's name, often Latin: put the noun before it and agree with the noun: "الخلطة
  «{name}»" (feminine), "المادة {name}", "تم حفظ الخلطة «{name}»". Never "ال{name}".
- **{count}** and `#`: as Plural forms says. **{library}** is US, UK, EU or AU: "مكتبة {library}".
- **Tags** stay around the words that mean the same; a tag never cuts an Arabic word.

### Cones

Orton cones are written "Orton" wherever the English says Orton: **مخروط Orton 6**, "مخاريط Orton". The pyrometric cone
is **المخروط الحراري** (pl. **المخاريط الحرارية**), short **المخروط** in running text where the sense is plain; titles
and the first use in a guide say المخروط الحراري ("مخروط" alone is also a traffic cone, a pine cone and the geometric
cone). SK cones are written "SK" with the number as printed (SK 6a) where the English has them; the Arab regions do not
use them. **Seger** (سيغر) is written only where the English says Seger: the older European Seger cones of the firing
guide (**مخاريط سيغر**) and **صيغة سيغر** (the unity molecular formula, UMF), which is another thing and never names a
cone. Arabic teaching may name pyrometric cones in general after Seger, as Turkish shops do (not seen in a source read
here): never so here; an Orton cone is never called a Seger cone.
Never give an SK number for an Orton number or the reverse, nor say they are equal. "one number hotter" → **أسخن برقم
واحد**, "one number cooler" → **أبرد برقم واحد**, never "أعلى" or "أدنى" (cone 05 is cooler than 04). "cone 06" keeps
its zero: "مخروط 06".

### The unity formula

**صيغة سيغر (UMF)**: write "صيغة سيغر (UMF)" once in a text and "صيغة سيغر" afterwards; UMF stays Latin. صيغة (a
chemical formula), never معادلة (an equation) or قاعدة (a rule), although the Babylon paper writes "قاعدة سيكر"
(Native check on the spelling and on صيغة). The app's short "Unity formula" and "Unity" become "صيغة سيغر" and "سيغر";
"unity" as the scaling is **التوحيد إلى الواحد** ("مجموع الصواهر يساوي واحداً", with the word, not a digit).
"Limit formula" is **صيغة الحدود**. "formula" for a chemical formula is **الصيغة الكيميائية**; a recipe is **الخلطة**:
never one for the other.

### Gender-neutral wording

Arabic marks gender in the second person and in adjectives: the imperative (احفظ / احفظي), the present (تريد / تريدين),
and any adjective or participle about the reader (متأكد / متأكدة). Unvocalised, the suffix ـك, the pronoun أنت and the
past "حفظت" read the same for both. So, as MS 3.1 asks:

- **Instructions** are verbal nouns or passives, never imperatives: "خطوات إنشاء خلطة جديدة: اختيار «إضافة مادة»، ثم
  إدخال الكمية." "يُحرَق الطلاء إلى 1222 °C."
- **Requests** use **يُرجى** + verbal noun ("يُرجى المحاولة مرة أخرى"), **يمكن** + verbal noun ("يمكن تغيير ذلك من
  «الإعدادات»"), **يجب** + verbal noun.
- **Questions** to the reader avoid a gendered verb: "Are you sure?" → "هل تريد المتابعة؟" is masculine; write "تأكيد
  الحذف؟" or "هل الحذف مؤكد؟".
- **People** in plural or collective nouns (MS 3.1): potters are **الخزافون** as a generic plural or "من يعمل
  بالخزف"; the user is "المستخدمون" or the account ("الحساب"); both forms ("الخزافون والخزافات") only where the English
  names both. No slash forms (ـكَ/ـكِ, "عزيزي/عزيزتي").
- Where "you" cannot be avoided, the unvocalised suffix ـك ("حسابك", "لديك") and the past ("أنشأت") are used, which
  read for both.

### The reader in the guides

Impersonal or passive throughout, with "we" for the app where the English says we ("نوصي", MS 2.2.4). Explanations
are statements about the glaze, the kiln or the dust ("الطلاء السميك جداً يسيل"). Quotations from sources are translated
inside «…» and keep their figures as the English writes them. Short plain sentences, as in the English; one English
paragraph is one Arabic paragraph.

### Law in the Arab countries

As the law writes it, never paraphrased. Only Algeria's text was read in Arabic for this page; the other words are
marked where they rest on less. Confirm each against the text when the region data is added.

- **Limits:** "at most", "no more than", "up to" → **لا يتجاوز**, **لا يزيد على** or **بحد أقصى** (the limit itself
  passes; Algeria's order writes "أقل أو يساوي 25 مم" for the depth and "يجب أن لا يتجاوز … الحدود"); "less than",
  "under" → **أقل من** (it does not); "at least", "or more" → **لا يقل عن**, **على الأقل**, **أو أكثر**; "more than",
  "over" → **أكثر من**, **يتجاوز** (Algeria: "أكبر من"). Never "أقل من" for "at most". A slip moves a legal limit: check
  every one in the food-contact, silica and mask texts.
- **Food contact:** Algeria's joint order of 16 January 2019 (JO No. 36, 6 June 2019): **الأشياء واللوازم المصنعة من
  الخزف الموجهة لملامسة المواد الغذائية**; its categories: **الأشياء واللوازم غير القابلة للملء** and those whose inner
  depth is at most 25 mm; **أواني الطبخ والتعبئة وأوعية التخزين** over 3 L; **كل الأشياء واللوازم الأخرى القابلة للملء**;
  **الرصاص**, **الكادميوم**. The app writes **أواني الطهي** (MSA) for cooking ware, Algeria's "أواني الطبخ" only inside
  its quoted title. Egypt's ES 7809-2/2022 (ceramic cookware, release of lead and cadmium) and the Gulf's GSO ISO 6486-2
  were not read in Arabic: keep their numbers as the English gives them.
- **Silica:** **السيليكا البلورية القابلة للاستنشاق**; the 8-hour average **متوسط التعرض خلال 8 ساعات**; Egypt's
  Ministerial Decree 211 of 2003 (Labour Law 12 of 2003) was not read, and no Arab region shows a silica limit yet. No
  source; native check.
- **GHS:** **النظام المنسق عالمياً (GHS)**; the signal words **خطر** (Danger) and **تحذير** (Warning) (Oman EA);
  **كلمة التنبيه** (signal word), **رمز الخطورة** (pictogram), **بيان الخطورة** (hazard statement), **صحيفة بيانات
  السلامة (SDS)** (Oman writes "بطاقة السلامة الكيميائية"; native check). The hazard statements' Arabic wording is the
  UN GHS's Arabic edition (not read): until it is, translate H302 "Harmful if swallowed" as "ضار في حالة البلع", H301
  "Toxic if swallowed" as "سام في حالة البلع", H350 "May cause cancer" as "قد يسبب السرطان", H372 as "يسبب تلفاً للأعضاء
  من خلال التعرض لفترات طويلة أو متكررة", H360FD as "قد يضر بالخصوبة أو بالجنين", keeping the codes; native check, and
  replace them with the UN's wording once read.
- **Who to call:** Egypt **123** (ambulance, **هيئة الإسعاف المصرية**; 122 police, 180 fire), the UAE **998**
  (ambulance; 999 police, 997 civil defence), Morocco **141** (medical emergencies) and the CAPM, **0801 000 180** (the
  centre's brochure: "المركز الوطني لمحاربة التسمم"; its official name is French, Centre Anti Poison et de
  Pharmacovigilance du Maroc, and is kept as published), Algeria **14** (**الحماية المدنية**). Ministries: **وزارة الصحة
  والسكان** (Egypt), **وزارة الصحة ووقاية المجتمع** (the UAE). From the region research; service names stay as
  published.
- **EU law**, where the English names it (Directive 84/500/EEC, Directive (EU) 2017/2398, CLP): there is no official
  Arabic text; write **التوجيه الأوروبي 84/500/EEC** and **لائحة CLP الأوروبية (EC) No 1272/2008**, keeping the numbers,
  so that the 3 L still applies only to packaging and storage vessels. US and UK bodies keep their English names with an
  Arabic gloss once: OSHA (إدارة السلامة والصحة المهنية الأمريكية), HSE (هيئة الصحة والسلامة البريطانية), FDA (إدارة
  الغذاء والدواء الأمريكية).

### Pitfalls

- **الطلاء الزجاجي, الطلاء, الجليز, الدهان, المينا, الورنيش:** a glaze is **الطلاء الزجاجي** (short **الطلاء** where
  plain); "طلاء" alone is also paint and coating, and "طلاء زجاجي" on the web is also window film: titles say "الطلاء
  الزجاجي للخزف". **الجليز** is the studio word (and a cake glaze): not used alone. **الدهان** is paint; **المينا** is
  enamel on metal (and tooth enamel); **الورنيش** is varnish: none is a glaze.
- **الفريت and الصواهر:** a frit is **الفريت** (a factory glass; Wikipedia "فريتة"); a flux is **مادة صاهرة** (the
  oxide's role; the Babylon paper's "مساعد صهر"), pl. **الصواهر**; not the soldering flux (**مادة لحام**) or magnetic
  flux.
- **فرن الخزف, فرن الطهي, القمين:** a potter's kiln is **الفرن** in context, **فرن الخزف** or **فرن الحرق** in titles
  (فرن كهربائي، فرن غاز); "فرن" alone is also the kitchen oven and the dental porcelain furnace ("فرن خزف اسنان"): the
  kitchen oven is **فرن الطهي**, only where the English says oven. **القمين** (Wikipedia) is a primitive kiln: not used.
- **نيء, الحرق الأول, حرق الطلاء:** unfired is **نيء** or **غير محروق** (**القطع النيئة**, greenware); the bisque firing
  is **الحرق الأول** and its ware **البسكويت**; the glaze firing is **حرق الطلاء**. They are never taken for each other.
- **الفخار, الخزف, الخزف الحجري, البورسلين:** earthenware is **الفخار** (Wikipedia "فخاريات"; Iraq "الفخاري"); stoneware
  **الخزف الحجري** (Wikipedia; Clay Supply "خزف حجري", "الأواني الحجرية"); porcelain **البورسلين** (Clay Supply;
  Wikipedia "الخزف الصيني أو البورسلان"). **الخزف** alone is ceramics in general (Algeria's order, Clay Supply); it never
  names stoneware alone. **السيراميك** is the trade word for ceramics and tiles: not used for a body.
- **السيليكا, السيليكون, الكوارتز, الرمل:** silica is the oxide **السيليكا** (SiO₂); the element is **السيليكون**, and
  the rubber is also written سيليكون: write it **مطاط السيليكون** where the English says silicone. The mineral is
  **الكوارتز** (Wikipedia "مرو", not used); silica sand **رمل السيليكا**.
- **كربونات الكالسيوم, الجير, الطباشير:** whiting is **كربونات الكالسيوم**; **الجير** (الكلس) is lime (CaO and slaked
  lime), and **الطباشير** is chalk: neither is written for whiting.
- **البورون, حمض البوريك, البوراكس:** B₂O₃ is **البورون (B₂O₃)**, formally **أكسيد البوريك**; **حمض البوريك** is boric
  acid; **البوراكس** is borax (Wikipedia "بورق", not used).
- **الصودا, البوتاس:** "soda" (Na₂O) is **الصودا (Na₂O)**, formally أكسيد الصوديوم; soda ash is **رماد الصودا (كربونات
  الصوديوم)**; "potash" (K₂O) is **البوتاس (K₂O)**, formally أكسيد البوتاسيوم; the safety guide's potash is **كربونات
  البوتاسيوم**. "صودا" in everyday Arabic is also soda water and baking soda.
- **الصبغة الخزفية and الملوِّنات:** a stain (factory-made, pre-fired) is **صبغة خزفية** (pl. الصبغات الخزفية; Clay
  Supply sells them as "بودرة بيجمنت"); a colorant (any oxide that colours) is **مادة ملوِّنة** (pl. الملوِّنات; Clay
  Supply "أكاسيد وملونات", Wikipedia "ملوّنات").
- **الكمامة التنفسية, جهاز التنفس الصناعي, التهوية:** a respirator is **الكمامة التنفسية** (Wikipedia: "قناع التنفس أو
  الكِمامة"); a hospital ventilator is **جهاز التنفس الصناعي**, never meant here; ventilation (air in a room) is
  **التهوية**. A surgical or cloth mask is **كمامة طبية** / **كمامة قماشية**: say which.
- **الأدلة and كتيّب التعليمات:** the app's five guides are **الأدلة**; a kiln's, controller's or sitter's manual is
  **كتيّب التعليمات**. Never the same word.
- **التشقق الشعري and التشقق الزخرفي:** crazing is **التشقق الشعري** (a defect); crackle is **التشقق الزخرفي**
  (wanted); a crack through the pot from heating or cooling is **التصدع الحراري** (dunting).
- **الكثافة النسبية and الكثافة:** specific gravity is **الكثافة النسبية**, a ratio with no unit (1.45); **الكثافة**
  alone is density in kg/m³ and is never used for a glaze's SG. **الوزن النوعي** and **الثقل النوعي** are its common
  synonyms (Wikipedia), but in physics الوزن النوعي is also weight per volume (N/m³): not used.
- **رشح المعادن:** leaching is **رشح المعادن** (Wikipedia "رشح" for leaching); "رشح" alone is also a runny nose and a
  nomination, so it always has its noun. The leach test is **اختبار رشح المعادن**, not narrowed to lead and cadmium.
- **الخلطة, الصيغة, الدفعة:** a recipe is **الخلطة**, a chemical formula **الصيغة الكيميائية**, the unity formula
  **صيغة سيغر**, a batch (an amount mixed) **الدفعة**: never bare "صيغة" or "خلطة" where two could be meant.
- **الطين, الصلصال, الطفلة:** clay in general is **الطين**; ball clay is **طين البول كلاي**; **الصلصال** is also
  modelling clay (play dough), and **الطفلة** is shale and an Egyptian word for clay: neither is used for a named clay.
- **المخروط:** see Cones; never "قمع" (a funnel) for a pyrometric cone.
- **Placeholders:** see Placeholders and names.

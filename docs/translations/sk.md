# Slovenčina: štýlová príručka a glosár (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Slovak (`sk`), for potters in Slovakia. Read [README.md](README.md)
first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Slovak message and guide follows this
page: one Slovak word for one thing.

Slovak potters buy from a handful of Slovak shops with partial ranges and order raw materials from Czech shops, so
much of what they read on a bag is Czech. The terms here are the Slovak forms: from Slovak shops, a Slovak producer's
data sheets, Slovak teaching texts and encyclopedias, the official Slovak ceramic specifications, the Slovak Nabertherm
manuals, Slovak law and EU law in Slovak. Czech sources are used only where no Slovak one was found, and the note says
so. Where sources disagree or none was found, the note says that too: those rows are for a Slovak potter to check.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal throughout: _vykanie_, the second person plural (_Vyberte_, _Uložili ste_, _Chcete
pokračovať?_), never _ty_. In the app and the guides the pronouns are written in lower case, _vy_, _váš_, as Microsoft
writes them in Slovak software (MS: „neuloženú prácu môžete stratiť vy aj…“); write them only where they are needed,
since Slovak drops most of the English "you" and "your" (MS 4.1.13: "Turn off your PC" → „Vypnite počítač.“). The two
password emails are letters to one person: there _Vy_, _Vás_, _Váš_ take a capital, as Slovak letters do. Use _prosím_
only where the English has "please". Where the English says "we" for the app's advice, _odporúčame_ is fine.

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs** (the Slovak software convention: MS 5.5.2, "The basic verbal
form is the infinitive", as in Windows and Office in Slovak; NB's controller icons do the same: „Uložiť“, „Pridať“,
„Zatvoriť“):

- Buttons and menu commands: the **infinitive**: _Uložiť_, _Uložiť ako kópiu_, _Tlačiť_, _Porovnať_, _Pridať
  suroviny_, _Odstrániť_, _Zrušiť_. Not the imperative (_Uložte_) and not a noun (_Uloženie_).
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: _Recepty_, _Suroviny_, _Nastavenia_,
  _Záznamy výpalov_, _Kužele a tepelná práca_. MS uses verbal nouns for dialog titles (_Výber zariadenia_). A heading
  that is a question or an instruction in English may stay one (_Na čo si dať pozor_).
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: _Názov suroviny:_.
- Confirmations: a past participle first, as the English does: "Saved: …" → „Uložené: …“.
- Tooltips, where there are any: the third person (MS: „Uloží vytvorený súbor“).

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case everywhere: only the first word of a heading, label, button or tab, and proper
names, take a capital (MS 4.1.6: capitalising every word of a translated title is marked wrong). "Fired Weight" →
„Hmotnosť po výpale“. Possessive adjectives made from a name keep the capital: _Segerov vzorec_, _Ortonov kužeľ_.
_Kôš_ (Trash) is always capitalised (MS). Product and system names keep their own form (Orton, Seger, Kiln-Sitter,
Glazecalc).

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: „…“ (U+201E, U+201C); inside them ‚…‘ (U+201A, U+2018). Not "…" or »…«. MS puts no quotation
  marks around the names of UI elements (see Feature names).
- Apostrophe: Slovak hardly ever needs one; where it does, the typographic ’ (U+2019). Never _Orton’s_ or _Seger’s_: use
  the possessive adjective (_Ortonov_, _Segerov_). Write `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`, `}` or `#`,
  and in `safety`, `regions` and `records`).
- No space before ; : ! ? (Slovak, like English).
- **Ranges:** the en dash **with a space on each side**, as Slovak writes it (STN: „1995 – 2000“, „strana 3 – 7“; MS;
  SKWIKI, „Pomlčka“: „1980 – 1990“), the unit once at the end: _1222 – 1240 °C_, _kužeľ 06 – 04_, _1 – 3 %_. MPC
  writes „1000 – 1080 ºC“. In a sentence _od 1222 do 1240 °C_ is also fine; never mix (_od 1222 – 1240 °C_). Never a
  hyphen for a range (shops' „1020-1080°C“ is not a model). The hyphen (_spojovník_) joins words with no spaces:
  _sodno-draselná frita_.

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: _1,5 %_, _0,01 g_, _1,45_ (STN: „desatinnou čiarkou bez medzery“). `{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays
  as written; the app renders it.
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped: _1240 °C_, _1000 g_ (STN: „Štvormiestne celé čísla sa vo vetnej
  súvislosti … píšu bez medzery“), as the app prints them. Five digits and more are grouped in threes with a **no-break
  space** (U+00A0): _12 500 g_. Never a plain space (the check reads _1 000_ written with a plain space as two
  numbers), never a dot. Beliana and some shops write „1 000 °C“; do not copy that.
- A space before °C, % and every unit: _1240 °C_, _15 %_, _100 °C/h_, _80 mesh_ (STN: the percent sign „sa oddeľuje
  medzerou“; SKWIKI: „22,5 °C“). Shops print „1200°C“; do not copy that. The adjective is written in words with a
  hyphen: _15-percentný_ (STN); prefer the noun form. A plain space between number and unit is fine in the files.
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word.
- Inches and feet (README): _palec_ (pl. _palce_, gen. pl. _palcov_) and _stopa_ (pl. _stopy_, gen. pl. _stôp_),
  with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives it.

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Slovak `one`, `few`, `many`, `other` (`Intl.PluralRules('sk')`):

| Category | Numbers                             | Example (surovina, f)                           | Example (recept, m) |
| -------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| `one`    | 1 only                              | 1 surovina                                      | 1 recept            |
| `few`    | 2, 3, 4 only                        | 2 suroviny                                      | 3 recepty           |
| `many`   | fractions: 0,5; 1,5; 2,5            | 1,5 suroviny (genitive singular)                | 2,5 receptu         |
| `other`  | 0, 5 and up, also 21, 22, 23, 24, … | 0 surovín, 5 surovín, 22 surovín (genitive pl.) | 5 receptov          |

- Write all four: `{count, plural, one {# surovina} few {# suroviny} many {# suroviny} other {# surovín}}`. Unlike
  Polish, Slovak `few` is only 2–4: 22 takes `other` (_22 surovín_), and `many` is only for fractions.
- (The Czech pattern "5 materiálů" is wrong in Slovak: _5 materiálov_, _5 surovín_.)
- **Agreement follows the category.** `one` and `few` take the noun's own case and a matching verb: _1 surovina bola
  pridaná_, _2 suroviny boli pridané_. `other` and `many` take the genitive and the verb in the neuter singular: _5
  surovín bolo pridaných_, _1,5 suroviny bolo_. Adjectives agree the same way (_2 uložené recepty_, _5 uložených
  receptov_). Where a sentence becomes awkward, put the number after a label: _Pridané suroviny: {count}_.
- `=0 {…}` and `#` stay as the English has them.

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Slovak sources show three different things a Slovak potter
may know:

- the general Slovak word for a pyrometric cone, _žiaromerka_ (f) ([mugs.cz/sk](https://www.mugs.cz/sk/clanky-1/vypalovacie-pece-1):
  „Teplota v peciach sa kontroluje buď žiaromerkami alebo pyrometrami“);
- the cones Prodeco sells as _žiaromerky_, a Czech series numbered by temperature: „Číslo žiaromerky“ 100 is
  1000 °C, 66 is 660 °C (PROD), neither Orton nor Seger;
- Seger cones (_Segerove žiaromerky_, SK numbers such as 6a), whose numbers mean other temperatures than Orton's
  (SK 06a about 990 °C, Orton 06 about 1013 °C at 150 °C/h).

So the app names its cones **_kužeľ_** (m; pl. _kužele_, gen. pl. _kužeľov_), never _žiaromerka_ with a number:

- "cone 6" → _kužeľ 6_; "cone 06" → _kužeľ 06_ (the zero stays, read „nula šesť“: kužeľ 06 is far cooler than
  kužeľ 6). Inflect the noun, never the number: _na kužeľ 6_, _do kužeľa 6_, _pri kuželi 06_.
- "Orton cone" → _Ortonov kužeľ_; at the first mention on a page and wherever systems are compared: _Ortonov kužeľ 6_,
  then _kužeľ 6_. In a guide's first explanation, once: _Ortonov kužeľ (žiaromerka)_.
- "cone 04 bisque" → _prežah na kužeľ 04_; "fire to cone 6" → _vypáliť na kužeľ 6_; "a cone 6 glaze" → _glazúra na
  kužeľ 6_; "cone 5–6" → _kužeľ 5 – 6_.
- **Never** write _SK 6_, _Segerova žiaromerka 6_ or a temperature-numbered _žiaromerka 120_ for an Orton number, and
  never convert an Orton number into another series. „SK“ is also Slovakia's country code: where the English says
  "Seger cones", write _Segerove žiaromerky_ in full.

No Slovak source writes Orton cone numbers at all (Slovak shops label glazes in °C), so this rule is a decision for a
Slovak potter to confirm.

**The unity formula.** _Segerov vzorec_ (m; gen. _Segerovho vzorca_), with _(UMF)_ after it the first time on a page:
_Segerov vzorec (UMF)_; after that _Segerov vzorec_. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and
"Unity:" are all _Segerov vzorec_ / _Segerov vzorec:_. The Czech sources Slovak potters read say „Segerův vzorec“
(VUT: „Složení glazur: Segerův vzorec“); no Slovak source was found, and _Segerov vzorec_ is its regular Slovak form.
Avoid a literal _jednotkový molekulový vzorec_.

**Gender-neutral wording.**

- Slovak past tense with _vykanie_ is plural and so gender-free: _Uložili ste recept._ Keep it.
- But an adjective or passive participle said of the reader takes a singular gender (_Boli ste odhlásený_ /
  _odhlásená_), and the plural _odhlásení_ is wrong for one person. Rephrase so the reader is not its subject: _Odhlásili
  sme vás._, _Odhlásenie prebehlo._
- For people in general use the plural or an impersonal sentence: _keramikári_, _tí, ktorí glazujú_. MS accepts the
  generic masculine and prefers plural nouns for generalisations; for a named person in a source, follow the source.
  Where a slash form cannot be avoided, MS writes _Vytvoril/-a_.

**The reader in the guides.** _Vykanie_ and the imperative plural, in short plain sentences, as the English: _Preosejte
glazúru dvakrát cez sito 80 mesh._ _Noste respirátor._ Slovak teaching texts often say "we" (MPC: „Suchú glazúru
rozmiešame vo vode“); where the English says "you", say _vy_ forms instead. No infinitive instructions in running text
(that is a button style), no heavy nominal style (_Vykonanie preosiatia sa uskutoční…_).

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with a capital first letter and **uninflected**, after a
common noun that takes the case, with no quotation marks (MS: „kliknite na tlačidlo OK“; „V časti … kliknite na
položku Listy“): _tlačidlo Porovnať_, _karta Štandardné_, _stránka Suroviny_, _nastavenie Hustota glazúry_. MS prefers
_Vyberte_ to _Kliknite na_. Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag. Otherwise use the
ordinary noun (_porovnanie ukáže…_). "Settings" is a plain noun in running text: _v nastaveniach_.

**EU law.** As the EU's Slovak texts write it: _smernica Rady 84/500/EHS_ (EHS = Európske hospodárske spoločenstvo;
lower-case _smernica_ in running text), its official title „Smernica Rady z 15. októbra 1984 o aproximácii právnych
predpisov členských štátov vzťahujúcich sa na keramické predmety, ktoré prichádzajú do styku s potravinami
(84/500/EHS)“, amended by _smernica Komisie 2005/31/ES_. The carcinogens directive is _smernica (EÚ) 2017/2398_; the
CLP Regulation _nariadenie (ES) č. 1272/2008_. Never _EEC_, _EWG_ or _CEE_ in Slovak text.

**Pitfalls in this field.**

**Czech words are not Slovak**, though Slovak shops copy them from Czech suppliers (PROD „Mastek - talek“, KCSK „nízký
výpal“). Write the Slovak form:

| Czech (on bags and Czech sites) | Slovak                          |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| glazura                         | glazúra                         |
| střep                           | črep                            |
| mastek, talek                   | mastenec, talk                  |
| křída                           | krieda                          |
| křemen, křemičitý               | kremeň, kremičitý               |
| tavidlo                         | tavivo                          |
| barvítko                        | farbítko                        |
| žároměrka                       | žiaromerka                      |
| přežah                          | prežah (not _predžah_; see 2.3) |
| zinečnatý, hořečnatý, měďnatý   | zinočnatý, horečnatý, meďnatý   |
| barnatý, lithný                 | bárnatý, lítny                  |
| ztráta žíháním                  | strata žíhaním                  |
| roztažnost, nasákavost          | rozťažnosť, nasiakavosť         |
| puchýře, odprýskávání           | pľuzgiere, odlupovanie          |
| nátěr, síto, nízký              | náter, sito, nízky              |

Other pitfalls:

- **biskvit** in Slovak is unglazed porcelain (SKWIKI, Porcelán: „nepolievaný matný porcelán sa nazýva biskvit“; IKA),
  though PECE and NB also write „biskvitový výpal“. Bisque is _prežah_.
- **sóda** is sodium carbonate (SKWIKI: „Uhličitan sodný alebo sóda“), _jedlá sóda_ is baking soda, **potaš** is
  potassium carbonate (SKWIKI). The potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are _sodík (Na₂O)_ and _draslík (K₂O)_.
- **kremík** is the element silicon, **silikón** the rubber, **silikagél** silica gel. The oxide SiO₂ is _oxid
  kremičitý_; the material is _kremeň_.
- **glazúra** also means icing on a cake (SKWIKI's disambiguation), **email**, **smalt** are enamel on metal, **lazúra**
  is a wood stain, **poleva** is the older and folk word (BEL: „Glazúra (poleva)“). Write _glazúra_.
- **kalivo** (opacifier) is not **kalenie** (hardening metal): PECE sells „kaliace komorové pece“, hardening furnaces.
- **fajansa** and **majolika** are tin-glazed earthenware, **terakota** is unglazed red ware, **belnina** is white
  earthenware (BEL); none of them is a translation of earthenware in general (_pórovina_). **kamenina** is stoneware.
- **respirátor** is the mask (official: _filtračná polmaska_), not **rúško** (a cloth or surgical face covering) and not
  a hospital **pľúcny ventilátor**; SKWIKI also lists „respiračný prístroj“ as a second meaning, so say _respirátor
  FFP2_ or _respirátor (filtračná polmaska)_ the first time.
- **pyrometer** in SKWIKI is a non-contact thermometer; potters' "pyrometer" reads a thermocouple (2.4).
- **pálenie** also means distilling spirits; for firing prefer _výpal_, _vypaľovanie_.
- **hlina** is the potters' word for clay, **íl** the geologist's; both occur (MPC: „íly, hliny a kaolíny“).
- **mesh**: Slovak sieves are not sold by mesh numbers. Keep "80 mesh" as _80 mesh_, never _80 ôk_.

## 2. Glossary

Nouns carry their gender: m (masculine), f (feminine), n (neuter), with the plural or genitive plural where it helps.
"show English" marks the terms whose English a reader can ask to see after them (`<en>…</en>`, `{{en: …}}`).

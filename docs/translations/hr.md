# Hrvatski: stilski priručnik i pojmovnik (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Croatian (`hr`), for potters in Croatia: standard Croatian as the
Institut za hrvatski jezik writes it ([Hrvatski pravopis](https://pravopis.hr/pravila/)). Read [README.md](README.md)
first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Croatian message and guide follows this
page: one Croatian word for one thing. The terms come from the two Croatian shops that sell to potters (Tapiker, Zagreb;
Ravitera's keramica.info, Strmec Samoborski, a kiln maker and dealer), Nabertherm's Croatian kiln manual, the Slovenian
and Hungarian shops Croatian potters order raw materials from, the encyclopedias of the Leksikografski zavod, the national
terminology base Struna, Croatian law and the EU's Croatian texts, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree
or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a Croatian potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** No shop in Croatia sells the full range of glaze raw materials. Tapiker
sells metal oxides, kaolin and quartz sand but no feldspar or whiting, and keramica.info sells kilns, clays, glazes and
a few additives. Croatian potters order the rest from Manufaktura Radovljica (Slovenia) and BP Kerámia (Hungary), whose
bags carry Slovenian or Hungarian names and TerraColor/BSZ numbers (Feldspat kalij 4012, China clay 4006, Fritt 3134
/BSZ 1042/). So the material names below follow Tapiker where it sells the material and Croatian chemical names
elsewhere, and the Notes give what the bag says. The largest Croatian potters' discussion found (the ceramics thread of
the DIY forum samsvojmajstor.com) could not be read (a script check blocks it), and web search ran out during this
research, so potters' own usage beyond the shops, a Petrinja studio and one association is thin, above all for the
defects and the cone words.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal: the second person plural, _vi_, written in lower case (_vi_, _vaš_, _vama_). MS (4.1.12) says not
to capitalise _vi_ in software, and Pravopis (4.1.3) keeps the capital _Vi_ for writing to one particular person; the app
speaks to every reader. Instructions use the imperative plural: „Odaberite …”, „Prosijte glazuru dvaput.”, „Nosite
masku.” Never _ti_, never an infinitive as an instruction in running text. Past tenses take the masculine plural
participle, the standard polite form for any reader: „Jeste li spremili recept?”, „Prijavljeni ste.” The app speaks as
"we" where the English does: „Preporučujemo …” (MS 4.1.12). "Please try again" is „Pokušajte ponovno.”; add „molimo”
only where the English insists.

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs** (MS 4.1.12 and 4.1.16, the convention of Croatian Windows and
Office):

- Buttons and commands that act at once: the **imperative singular**, as a command's name, never as _ti_-address:
  „Spremi”, „Spremi kao kopiju”, „Ispiši”, „Usporedi”, „Dodaj sirovine”, „Odustani”, „Odbaci probno korištenje”
  (MS: "Print → Ispiši", "Cancel → Odustani").
- A command that opens a further step (the English ends in "…"): a **verbal noun**, „Spremanje…”, „Ispis…” (MS).
- Options in which the reader speaks to the app: first person, with the imperative singular, as MS's „Spremi moje
  postavke”: "Match with what I have" → „Složi od onoga što imam”; "Show the English after key terms" → „Prikaži
  engleski naziv iza ključnih pojmova”.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: „Recepti”, „Postavke”, „Zapisnici
  pečenja”, „Stošci i toplinski učinak”. A heading that is a question or an instruction in English may stay one: "What
  to watch for" → „Na što pripaziti”.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: „Naziv sirovine:”.
- Confirmations: the participle first, as the English: "Saved: …" → „Spremljeno: …”.
- Error messages: whole Croatian sentences (MS 5.5.1): „Ne možemo pronaći sirovinu.”, not a clipped English shape.

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case everywhere: English Title Case is an error in Croatian (MS 4.1.4): "Fired Weight" →
„Masa nakon pečenja”. Languages, days, months and adjectives from country names are lower case (_engleski_, _hrvatski_).
Short text in brackets that is not a sentence starts lower case: "(Recommended)" → „(preporučeno)” (MS). Possessive
adjectives from names keep the capital: _Ortonov stožac_, _Segerova formula_. Product names keep their own form (Orton,
Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc).

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: „…” (U+201E, U+201D), as Pravopis 8.16 and MS 4.1.13 both give them. Not the German „…“ (closing U+201C) and
  not "…". Inside them: ‘…’ (Pravopis 8.17). Croatian law in Narodne novine quotes with »…«; our text still uses „…”.
- Apostrophe: the _izostavnik_ ’ (U+2019, Pravopis 8.18), which Croatian almost never needs. Foreign names take a
  suffix, not an apostrophe: _Ortonov_, _Segerovi_, _Bullersovi_. Write `''` only where the README asks (before `{`, `}`
  or `#`, and in `safety`, `regions` and `records`).
- No space before ; : ! ? (Pravopis 8.1).
- Ranges: the en dash **with a space each side**, unit once at the end: „1222 – 1240 °C”, „stožac 06 – 04”, „1 – 3 %”
  (Pravopis 8.14 d: „30 – 45 minuta”; MS: „10 – 15 stranica”). In a sentence „od 1222 do 1240 °C” is also fine; never
  mix („od 1222 – 1240 °C”). The dash in a label such as "Fluxes - RO" is the same spaced en dash: „Topitelji – RO”.
- Ratios: a colon with a space each side (Pravopis 8.19.1): „8,5 : 1”, „SiO₂ : Al₂O₃”.
- A figure followed by a full stop is an ordinal: „1. pečenje” is the first firing, „6. stožac” the sixth cone. Write
  „stožac 6”, never „6. stožac”; dates take the stops („15. listopada 1984.”).
- Abbreviations take case endings after a hyphen: „STL-a”, „KOPB-a” (HZJZ). Formulas are not declined: put a noun before
  them („udio SiO₂”, „sadržaj Al₂O₃”).

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: „1,5 %”, „0,01 g”, „1,45”. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Four-digit numbers are **not** grouped: „1240 °C”, „1000 g” (Pravopis 8.1 h: a space only from 10 000; the app prints
  them the same way). Five digits and more take a **no-break space** (U+00A0): „12 500”. Never a plain space, which the
  check cannot read. The app's own computed figures follow CLDR and show „12.500”; Pravopis allows the dot only in
  finance. Such numbers are rare in the texts.
- A space before °C, °F, % and every unit, no-break where you can: „1240 °C”, „15 %”, „100 °C/h”, „80 mesh”
  (Pravopis 8.1 k, l and 8.19.11; MS 4.1.15). Shops print „1050°C” (TAP): do not copy it.
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word. "percent" in running text is „posto” or „postotak”, never the
  Serbian „procenat”.
- Inches and feet are „inč” and „stopa”, the same in every file, with the metric figure in brackets as the English
  gives it: „1/4 inča”, „2 inča (51 mm)”, „18 inča (46 cm)”, „3 stope (91 cm)”, „25 stopa”. Never „palac”.

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Croatian three categories, `one`, `few` and `other` (`new Intl.PluralRules('hr')`):

| Category | Numbers                                                  | Masculine  | Feminine   | Neuter    |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ---------- | --------- |
| `one`    | 1, 21, 31, 101 … (not 11); also decimals ending in ,1    | 1 recept   | 1 sirovina | 1 pečenje |
| `few`    | 2–4, 22–24, 102–104 … (not 12–14); decimals ending ,2–,4 | 2 recepta  | 2 sirovine | 2 pečenja |
| `other`  | 0, 5–20, 25–30, 11–14, 111 …; most decimals (1,5)        | 5 recepata | 5 sirovina | 5 pečenja |

- Write all three: `{count, plural, one {# recept} few {# recepta} other {# recepata}}`. `=0 {…}` and `#` stay as the
  English has them.
- Agreement follows the category: `one` is singular („1 recept je spremljen”, „21 sirovina je dodana”); `few` is
  plural, the masculine and neuter in _-a_ („2 recepta su spremljena”, „3 sirovine su dodane”); `other` is the genitive
  plural with the verb in the neuter singular („5 recepata je spremljeno”, „5 sirovina je dodano”). Put adjectives and
  verbs inside each branch, not outside the plural.
- A decimal takes the genitive singular („1,5 kilograma”, „2,5 litre”), which CLDR's `other` does not tell apart from
  „5 litara”: never put a value that can be fractional into a plural message; give it as a label („Masa: {value} g”).
  Where a number argument is not a plural ("up to {most} materials"), rephrase so no noun has to agree with it:
  „Zaliha je popis sirovina (najviše {most}).”

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones**. Croatian studio potters fire by °C on a controller: Tapiker's firing
service and product pages give only °C („Pali se na temperaturi od 1000 – 1040 °C”), keramica.info names firings by
order and temperature („1. pečenje ili biskvitno pečenje”), and Nabertherm's programs are in °C. The one Croatian
mention of cones found is keramica.info's „Orton konusi” (describing Bentrup's „Cone Firing Assistant”); it sells PTCR
rings, not cones. Croatian's own word for the shape is _stožac_, and HJP sends _konus_ to it („konus – mat., v.
stožac”). No Croatian Seger table was found.

- "cone" → „stožac” (m; G stošca; pl. stošci; G pl. stožaca). "cone 6" → „stožac 6”; "cone 06" → „stožac 06”, read
  „nula šest” (the zero stays: stožac 06 is far cooler than stožac 6). Inflect the noun, never the number: „na stožac
  6”, „do stošca 6”, „pri stošcu 06”.
- "Orton cone" → „Ortonov stožac”; at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared: „Ortonov stožac
  6”, then „stožac 6”.
- "cone 04 bisque" → „biskvitno pečenje na stožac 04”; "fire to cone 6" → „peći na stožac 6”; "a cone 6 glaze" →
  „glazura za stožac 6”; "cone 5–6" → „stožac 5 – 6”.
- A cone hotter or cooler is „za jedan broj topliji” / „za jedan broj hladniji”: "one cone hotter in the sitter" →
  „u Kiln-Sitter stožac za jedan broj topliji”; "from cone 6 up" → „na stošcu 6 i toplijim stošcima”; "below cone 03" →
  „hladnije od stošca 03”. Never „viši” / „niži”, „gore” / „dolje” or „naviše” for cones: in the cooler series a higher
  number is cooler (stožac 05 hladniji je od stošca 04).
- Seger cones („Segerovi stošci”, SK) are a different scale: SK 06a falls at about 990 °C, Orton 06 at about 1013 °C (at
  150 °C/h). **Never** write „Segerov stožac 6” or „SK 6” for an Orton number; „Segerovi stošci” only where the English
  says "Seger cones".
- Not „kupa” (the Serbian word for the geometric cone), not „čunj” (a traffic cone). „konus” (KI's word) is understood but
  not the app's: a choice for a Croatian potter to confirm.

**The unity formula.** „Segerova formula” (f), with „(UMF)” after it the first time on a page: „Segerova formula
(UMF)”, then „Segerova formula”. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are all „Segerova
formula” and „Segerova formula:”. No Croatian glaze source naming it was found; this is the name European potters use
after Seger (German _Segerformel_, Czech _Segerův vzorec_, Polish _wzór Segera_). Never a literal „jedinična
molekulska formula”.

**Gender-neutral wording.** MS 3.1: don't use the masculine as a generic for people; use the plural, a collective noun,
„osoba”, or speak to the reader directly. The formal _vi_ with masculine plural participles is the accepted polite form
for anyone. No slashed forms (korisnik/korisnica) in the UI; where a person noun cannot be avoided, a pair, sparingly
(„keramičarke i keramičari”). Note that _keramičar_ in everyday Croatian is first a tile setter (the trade
_keramičar-oblagač_): for people who make pots prefer „osobe koje se bave keramikom”, „lončari” (at the wheel), or
simply _vi_.

**The reader in the guides.** _vi_ and the imperative plural, in short plain sentences, as the English: „Prosijte
glazuru dvaput kroz sito 80 mesh.” Avoid the impersonal _se_ where the English says "you" („Glazura se prosije …”), and
avoid the bureaucratic nominal style with _vršiti_, _izvršiti_, _putem_, _u svrhu_ („Izvršite prosijavanje glazure”).
Quotations from sources are translated into „…” and keep their figures as the English writes them.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, **not inflected**, with a common noun before them that takes
the case (MS 4.1.9): „kliknite gumb Usporedi”, „na kartici Standardne”, „na stranici Sirovine”. No quotation marks
around the names of controls (MS: "Don't use quotation marks for the names of controls"); where the English wraps the
name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag. "Settings" in running text is the ordinary noun: „u postavkama”. The fixed
names:

| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Compare                       | Usporedi                       | gumb Usporedi; usporedba                |
| Suggest amounts (and compare) | Predloži količine (i usporedi) | gumb Predloži količine                  |
| Match with what I have        | Složi od onoga što imam        | gumb Složi od onoga što imam            |
| Replace lead                  | Zamijeni olovo                 | gumb Zamijeni olovo                     |
| Settings                      | Postavke                       | postavke („u postavkama”)               |
| Notebook                      | Bilježnica                     | bilježnica                              |
| Library                       | Biblioteka                     | biblioteka                              |
| Standard                      | Standardne                     | standardne sirovine; kartica Standardne |
| My materials                  | Moje sirovine                  | vaše sirovine; kartica Moje sirovine    |
| Trial                         | Probno korištenje              | probno korištenje                       |

**Cross-references** use the formal imperative „pogledajte”, never „vidi” or „v.”: "see [Firing a basic kiln]" →
„pogledajte vodič [Pečenje u jednostavnoj peći]” or „… pogledajte u vodiču [Pečenje u jednostavnoj peći]”; "(see After
the firing, below)" → „(pogledajte odjeljak Nakon pečenja, niže)”; "see Colemanite (commercial)" → „pogledajte
Colemanite (commercial)”. The five guides have one title each, the same in their front matter, in every link that names
them, in the page titles and in the guide list: „Glaziranje od samih osnova”, „Kako napraviti glazuru”, „Sigurno
miješanje i ventilacija”, „Ne otrujte svoju obitelj”, „Pečenje u jednostavnoj peći”.

**The United Kingdom** is „Ujedinjeno Kraljevstvo” (n; „u Ujedinjenom Kraljevstvu”, „iz Ujedinjenog Kraljevstva”)
everywhere, also for "UK" in running text and in "(UK: biscuit)" → „(u Ujedinjenom Kraljevstvu biscuit)”. The region
picker on the same pages shows the browser's own name from CLDR (`Intl.DisplayNames`), „Ujedinjeno Kraljevstvo”, which
these files cannot change, so the text must match it. Never „Ujedinjena Kraljevina” (the official long form's word) or
„Britanija”; the adjective is „britanski” ("UK potters" → „britanski lončari”). The region label "UK" stays (2.8).

**EU law.** As the EU's Croatian texts write it: _Direktiva_, _Uredba_; EEC is _EEZ_, EC is _EZ_. "Directive
84/500/EEC" → „Direktiva Vijeća 84/500/EEZ” (its title: „Direktiva Vijeća od 15. listopada 1984. o usklađivanju
zakonodavstava država članica u odnosu na keramičke proizvode koji dolaze u dodir s hranom (84/500/EEZ)”), amended by
„Direktiva Komisije 2005/31/EZ”. "Directive (EU) 2017/2398" → „Direktiva (EU) 2017/2398”. CLP is „Uredba (EZ) br.
1272/2008”, REACH „Uredba (EZ) br. 1907/2006”. Croatian law is cited by its Narodne novine number (NN 148/23).

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **kamenina**: the shops, keramica.info and Nabertherm's Croatian manual use it for **stoneware**, but HE, Proleksis,
  HJP and Struna define it as porous white ware (the old sense of German _Steingut_: Struna „neprozirna vodopropusna
  nesinterirana keramika”). Follow the suppliers and say what it means at first use (2.3). Earthenware is „zemljana
  keramika”, never „kamenina”; avoid „kamenština”.
- **kremen** means quartz in Croatian (HJP: „kremen … kvarc”; Croatian Wikipedia redirects Kremen to Kvarc), as
  Slovenian _kremen_ on MR's quartz bag does. It is not flint: keep "flint" (2.2).
- **glinica** is alumina (Al₂O₃), **glina** clay, **glinenac** feldspar. Three different things.
- **soda** is sodium carbonate (HJP), **soda bikarbona** baking soda, **potaša** potassium carbonate: the potters'
  oxides "soda" and "potash" are „natrij (Na₂O)” and „kalij (K₂O)”.
- **silicij** is the element, **silikon** the rubber, **silikat** a silicate; the anglicism „silika” is not used. The
  oxide is „silicijev dioksid”, the material „kvarc”.
- **respirator** is a medical ventilator (Struna, IATE). The mask is „zaštitna maska” or „filtarska polumaska” (2.7).
- **caklina** is HE's headword for glaze but in everyday Croatian is tooth enamel (HJP); **emajl** is enamel on metal.
  Use „glazura”.
- **biskvit** in dictionaries is a sponge cake or unglazed porcelain (HJP, PRO); for potters it is bisque. **frita** in
  HJP is the Dalmatian fritter (_fritula_); for potters a frit.
- **oznaka upozorenja** in the CLP's Croatian text is the **hazard statement**; the signal word is „oznaka opasnosti”
  („Opasnost”, „Upozorenje”). Easy to swap.
- **denzitometar** is what Tapiker calls its glaze hydrometer; in standard Croatian a densitometer measures optical
  density. Write „areometar” (Struna).
- **pečenje** and **paljenje** are both standard for firing (TE: „Paljenje (pečenje) je najčešće dvostruko”); the app
  uses „pečenje”. The verb _peći_ (pečem, pecite) looks like _peći_ (kilns): reread „pecite u peći”-type sentences.
- **sirovina** is a raw material, but Nabertherm's manual also calls unfired ware „sirovina”: not in the app.
- **ploča** is a kiln shelf, **polica** a level of shelves (KI) or the furniture in a room.
- **Serbian and Bosnian forms to avoid**: _kalijum, natrijum, kalcijum, silicijum_ (→ kalij, natrij, kalcij, silicij);
  _hrom_ (krom); _kalaj_ (kositar); _kreč, krečnjak_ (vapno, vapnenac); _vazduh_ (zrak); _hemija_ (kemija); _tačka_
  (točka); _procenat_ (postotak); _rastvor, rastvorljiv_ (otopina, topljiv); _kiseonik_ (kisik); _ugljen-dioksid_
  (ugljikov dioksid); _fabrika_ (tvornica); _bezbednost_ (sigurnost); _podešavanja_ (postavke); _sačuvaj_ (spremi);
  _nalog_ (račun); _štampaj_ (ispiši); _obriši_ (izbriši); _uputstvo_ (upute); _dugme_ (gumb); _grnčar, grnčarija_
  (lončar, lončarija); _hroničan_ (kroničan); and every ekavian form (_dve, reč_ → _dvije, riječ_). Croatian Wikipedia's

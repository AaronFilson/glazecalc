# Malti: gwida tal-istil u glossarju (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Maltese (`mt`), standard written Maltese as the Kunsill Nazzjonali tal-Ilsien Malti
(KNM) spells it, for potters in Malta. Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every
English term below. Every Maltese message and guide follows this page: one Maltese word for one thing. The terms come
from the Maltese texts that name ceramic materials, wares and firing (the EU's Combined Nomenclature, Directive
84/500/KEE, the EU Ecolabel for hard coverings), the Maltese texts of the CLP and REACH regulations and of Directive
(EU) 2017/2398, Malta's own regulations on carcinogens and chemical agents, IATE, Microsoft's Maltese style guide and
terminology, Maltese Wikipedia and a 1926 Maltese article on pottery, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources
disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a Maltese potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** The region data found no shop in Malta that sells glaze materials, and
Maltese ceramics is taught in English (MCAST, the University of Malta): no Maltese-language glaze shop, ceramics
textbook, course page, potters' guild glossary, kiln manual (Nabertherm publishes none in Maltese; the kilns sold into
Malta come with English manuals) or frit data sheet could be found. Written Maltese for this field lives in the EU's
Maltese texts and Malta's legislation, which name the materials, the wares, the glaze, the frit, the kiln and the
firing; those words are used below. The studio words no Maltese source names (cone, bisque, witness cone, kiln sitter,
crawling, line blend …) are the translator's best plain Maltese, marked **No source**; a few stay in English as KNM's
_Deċiżjonijiet 2_ allows for English loans (MS 4.1.10). Maltese potters very likely say many of these in English in the
studio ("bisque", "cone", "kiln wash"); that could not be verified. Malta's region lists Maltese first, so Maltese
readers may land on these pages without choosing them: everywhere, plain everyday Maltese over coined words. The rows to
check first: glaze (glejż), cone (kon), bisque, earthenware and stoneware, flux (fluss), slip, whiting, and the
should/must wording.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

Formal, careful standard Maltese, addressing the reader in the **second person singular** (_int_), as Maltese software
and the EU's Maltese safety texts do. Maltese software has no separate polite pronoun: MS (4.1.20) asks for "the 2nd
polite person", and every example it gives (2.2, 4.1.16, 5.5.2) is the second person singular (“Il-password mhix tajba, għalhekk erġa’ pprova.”, “Tixtieq
tkompli?”, “Ma għandekx biżżejjed memorja biex tlesti l-operazzjoni.”) and leaves out the pronoun _int_ and, where it
can, the possessive _tiegħek_. The CLP's Maltese precautionary statements use the same singular imperative (“P284 Ilbes
protezzjoni respiratorja.”, “P260 Tiblax bin-nifs trabijiet/dħaħen/gass/raxx/fwar/sprej.”). Never the plural _intom_
for one reader, never the old courtesy forms (_Sinjurija Tiegħek_), and no slang or dialect. Formal here means correct,
complete and calm, not stiff: MS (2.1.2) asks for the everyday word over the bureaucratic one (“imma”, “għalhekk”, “ara”
rather than “madankollu”, “għaldaqstant”, “irreferi għal”).

- **Buttons and commands**: the **imperative singular**, verb first (MS-T): “Issejvja”, “Ipprintja”, “Qabbel”, “Neħħi”,
  “Ħassar”, “Ikkanċella”, “Idħol”, “Oħroġ”, “Irreġistra”, “Erġa’ lura”.
- **Instructions** in guides and help: the imperative singular, in short sentences: “Għarbel il-glejż darbtejn
  b’għarbiel ta’ 80 mesh.”, “Ilbes respiratur.”, “Tiblax it-trab bin-nifs.”
- **Errors**: a whole sentence, one basic shape for all the negative ones (MS 5.5.2: “Xi ħaġa marret ħażin.”, “Il-fajl
  ma jistax jinstab.”).
  Where the English speaks as "we", so does the Maltese: "We can't find that recipe." → “Ma nistgħux insibu dik
  ir-riċetta.”; "The recipe could not be saved." → “Ma rnexxilniex nissejvjaw ir-riċetta.” "Please try again." →
  “Erġa’ pprova.” Add “jekk jogħġbok” only where the English insists.
- **Questions**: the second person imperfect: "Remove "{name}"?" → `Tneħħi “{name}”?`; "Do you want to continue?" →
  “Tixtieq tkompli?”
- **Confirmations**: the participle first, agreeing with the thing meant: "Saved: {name}" (a recipe, f) →
  “Issejvjata: {name}”.

Examples: a button "Save as a copy" → “Issejvja bħala kopja”; an error "The recipe could not be saved." → “Ma
rnexxilniex nissejvjaw ir-riċetta.”; a guide sentence "Sieve the glaze twice through an 80 mesh sieve." → “Għarbel
il-glejż darbtejn b’għarbiel ta’ 80 mesh.”

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- Buttons and menu commands: the imperative singular, as above. A command that opens a further step keeps the
  ellipsis: “Ipprintja…”.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**, usually with the article (MS 4.1.13:
  "Formatting" → “Ifformattjar”; "Start Time" → “Ħin tal-bidu”): “Riċetti”, “Settings”, “Reġistri tal-ħami”,
  “Il-koni u x-xogħol tas-sħana”. An activity becomes a verbal noun: "Mixing the glaze" → “It-taħlit tal-glejż”;
  "Typing amounts" → “Il-kitba tal-ammonti”. A heading that is a question or an instruction in English may stay one:
  "What to watch for" → “X’għandek toqgħod attent għalih”.
- **Guide headings** may keep the English's imperative where it reads naturally (“Naddaf bl-ilma”), but one English
  heading has one Maltese heading in every guide ("Lead-free is not the same as food-safe" → “Mingħajr ċomb ma jfissirx
  sikur għall-ikel”).
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: “Isem il-materjal:”.
- Options in which the reader speaks to the app keep the first person: "Match with what I have" → “Agħmilha b’dak li
  għandi”.
- Use the same part of speech for all the options of one setting (MS 4.1.3).

### Capitalisation

Sentence case everywhere: only the first word and proper names take a capital, in headings, buttons, labels and tabs
too (MS 4.1.5: "Sorting and Grouping" → “Ordinar u Gruppar”; no English Title Case). "Fired Weight" → “Piż wara
l-ħami”. Maltese rules differ from most European languages in four ways (MS 4.1.5, after KNM):

- **The article is capitalised only at the start** of a sentence, heading or label, and then only its first letter:
  “Il-glejż”, “L-ossidu tal-kalċju”, “Iċ-ċomb”; never “IL-Glejż” or “Il-Glejż”. After the hyphen the word stays lower
  case unless it is a name: “il-Ġermanja”, “l-Istati Uniti”.
- **Months and days** take a capital: “Ottubru”, “it-Tnejn”. Seasons do not.
- **Languages and peoples** take a capital: “il-Malti”, “l-Ingliż”, “Ingliża”, “Maltin”.
- **Chemical elements and materials** are common nouns, lower case: “iċ-ċomb”, “l-aluminju”, “il-kawlina”. Trade names
  keep their own form (Orton, Seger, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc, Ferro 3134).

### Punctuation

- **Quotation marks**: double curly quotes “…” (U+201C, U+201D), the English shape, because so many Maltese words end
  in an apostrophe (MS 4.1.17: `Ittajpja “erġa’ aqbeż”`, not single quotes). Inside them, ‘…’. Never straight "…",
  the German „…“ or «…». Quotation marks for quotations and for names the reader typed (`“{name}”`, as the English has
  "{name}"), never around a button or page name. This page quotes Maltese examples in “…” too.
- **Apostrophe**: Maltese needs it all the time, to mark an elided vowel or _għ_ and the short prepositions: “ta’”,
  “ma’”, “f’”, “b’”, “erġa’”, “sema’”, “’il fuq”. Always write the typographic ’ (U+2019), never the straight ', in
  messages and guides alike: a straight apostrophe before `{`, `}` or `#` starts an ICU quote and breaks the message
  (“ta’ {name}” is safe; “ta' {name}” is not), and ’ is what KNM's and MS's examples print. ’ is also the closing
  quote of ‘…’: that is fine.
- **Hyphen**: the article and the prepositions joined to it are written with a hyphen and no space (“il-forn”,
  “tal-glejż”, “fiċ-ċeramika”, “għall-ikel”, “mis-silika”): never a space after the hyphen. The prepositions _bi_ and
  _fi_ shorten to “b’” and “f’” before a vowel or a single consonant (“b’sistema”, “f’kuntest”), but not before a
  euphonic vowel (MS 4.1.15: “bi ssettjar”, not “b’issettjar”); “minn” before the sun-letter article is “min-” (“mid-dehra”),
  never “minn-”.
- No space before , . ; : ! ? (MS 4.1.17).
- **Ranges**: the en dash with no spaces, the unit once at the end (MS 4.1.17: “Is-snin 2020–2023”): “1222–1240 °C”,
  “koni 06–04”, “1–3%”. In a sentence “minn 1222 sa 1240 °C” is also fine; never mix the two (“minn 1222–1240”). The
  dash in a label such as "Fluxes - RO" is a spaced en dash: “Flussi – RO”. A dash in a sentence: the en dash with a
  space each side.
- **Ratios**: a colon with a space each side: “SiO₂ : Al₂O₃”, “8.5 : 1”.
- **Ellipsis**: the single character …, no space before it when it ends a command (“Ipprintja…”).

### Numbers and units

- **Decimal point, as English**: “1.5”, “0.01 g”, “1.45”, “0.025 mg/m³”. Malta writes the point: MS 4.1.14 and 4.1.17
  (“5.25 cm”), CLDR (`Intl.NumberFormat('mt')` prints “1,234.5”), Malta's own legislation (LS 646.14: “0.1”, “0.025
  mg/m3”, “101.3 kPa”), and the app itself, which renders `{{SG 1.45}}` and every computed figure in the reader's locale
  as “1.45”. The README's “1,5 in every language here” does not hold for Maltese (nor Irish). The EU's own Maltese texts
  write the comma (“0,1 mg/l”, 84/500; “0,025 mg/m 3”, 2017/2398): do not copy them.
- **Thousands**: four-digit numbers ungrouped, as the app prints them (`useGrouping: 'min2'` in
  `client/app/shared/format.ts`): “1222 °C”, “1170 darba”, “1000 g”, also where the English writes "1,222". Five digits
  and more take a comma, as English and as CLDR and MS do (“12,500 g”). Years never take a separator (“2026”).
- **The check reads Maltese like English.** Since commit 0353376, `numbersIn` in `scripts/i18n-check.mjs` reads a
  language whose CLDR decimal mark is a point as it reads English, which covers `mt`: “0.025”, “1.477”, “1.000”,
  “1170” and “12,345.6” all match the English's figures (tested 10 October 2026; before that commit the check took
  them for 25, 1477, 1000 and 12.345). Never write the comma to please the check.
- **%**: no space: “15%”, “1–3%” (MS 4.1.14: "50 %" incorrect, "50%" correct; CLDR prints “15%”). Every other unit
  takes a space, no-break where possible (MS 4.1.19: “100 mg”, “5 mm”): “1222 °C”, “100 g”, “100 °C/h”, “80 mesh”,
  “0.1 mg/m³”.
- A number in the English stays a number, with the same unit symbol: “3 saffi”, never “tliet saffi”; “15%”, never
  “ħmistax fil-mija”; “100 g”, never “mitt gramma”.
- **Dates**: “10 ta’ Ottubru 2026” in running text, “10/10/2026” in tables (CLDR); months capitalised.
- **Time**: the 24-hour clock (CLDR “20:00”; MS “02:30:00”). Write the figures the English has: "08:00 to 20:00" →
  “08:00 sa 20:00”; where the English writes whole hours with am or pm, write the hour alone on the 24-hour clock,
  since the check reads 8pm as 20 and “20:00” would add a 00 the English lacks: "8am to 8pm" → “mit-8 sal-20”; "8 to
  5" → “mit-8 sal-5” (as the English, which gives no am or pm).
- **The article before a figure** follows the spoken numeral: “it-8” (it-tmienja), “is-17:00” (is-sbatax), “l-20”
  (l-għoxrin), “il-25 mm” (84/500: “il-25mm”). Where unsure, rephrase so no article stands before the figure.
- **Inches and feet**: “pulzier” (pl. pulzieri) and “pied” (pl. piedi), the same in every file, with the metric figure
  in brackets as the English gives it: “2 pulzieri (51 mm)”, “18-il pulzier (46 cm)”, “3 piedi (91 cm)”, “25 pied” (the
  noun after the figure follows the plural rules below). Pounds and ounces: “libra” and “uqija” (Wiktionary); see 2.8.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Maltese five categories:
`node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('mt').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"` prints
`[ 'one', 'two', 'few', 'many', 'other' ]`. The rules, and the noun after the figure:

| Category | Numbers                                         | The noun                                             | Example                                          |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `one`    | 1                                               | singular                                             | 1 materjal, 1 riċetta, 1 ħamja                   |
| `two`    | 2                                               | plural; the dual (sagħtejn) only for a word, not "2" | 2 materjali, 2 riċetti, 2 ħamjiet, 2 sigħat      |
| `few`    | 0, 3–10, 103–110, 203–210 …                     | plural (the counted plural)                          | 0 materjali, 3 materjali, 10 riċetti, 5 ħamjiet  |
| `many`   | 11–19, 111–119 …                                | singular, with -il after the figure                  | 11-il materjal, 15-il riċetta, 12-il ħamja       |
| `other`  | 20–102, 120–202 …, and every decimal (1.5, 0.5) | singular                                             | 20 materjal, 21 riċetta, 100 materjal, 1.5 parti |

- Write all five, even where two read alike:
  `{count, plural, one {# materjal} two {# materjali} few {# materjali} many {#-il materjal} other {# materjal}}`.
  `=0 {…}` and `#` stay as the English has them; `=0` reads “l-ebda materjal” (singular after _l-ebda_).
- From 11 up the noun is **singular** (Maltese grammar: “ħdax-il ktieb”, “għoxrin sena”); 11 to 19 add “-il” before
  the noun, joined to the figure with a hyphen (“15-il sena”, as the Maltese press writes it). Use the **counted
  plural** where a noun has one: “3 ijiem”, “3 xhur”, “3 snin”, “3 darbiet” (not the collective “kliem”-type plural).
- A noun after a figure takes no article (“3 materjali”); with the article, the article goes on the figure (“it-3
  materjali”), which is best avoided (see Numbers).
- Verbs follow the plural from 2 up (“2 materjali ġew miżjuda”, “20 materjal ġew miżjuda”); adjectives and participles
  agree with the noun's form. Put verbs, adjectives and participles inside each branch, never outside the plural. For a
  native speaker to check: the adjective after a singular noun from 11 up (“15-il materjal ġdid”).
- Where a number is not a plural argument ("up to {most} materials"), rephrase so no noun has to agree with it: "The
  shelf is a list of up to {most} materials." → “Il-ħażna hija lista ta’ materjali (mhux aktar minn {most}).” Never put
  a value that can be fractional into a plural message.

### Cones

The app's cones are **Orton cones**. No Maltese source names the pyrometric cone; Maltese studio potters fire by
temperature on a controller or say "cone" in English (not verified). The Maltese word for the shape is **kon** (m; pl.
koni: “koni vulkaniċi”, mt.wikipedia), also the ice-cream and the traffic cone. So:

- "cone" → “kon” (pl. koni); explain once on a page where it helps: “kon żgħir taċ-ċeramika li jitgħawweġ meta jirċievi
  ċertu xogħol tas-sħana”. "pyrometric cone" → “kon pirometriku”.
- "cone 6" → “kon 6”; "cone 06" → “kon 06” (the zero stays; read “żero sitta”; kon 06 is far cooler than kon 6). The
  number follows the noun and stays bare; the **article and preposition go on the noun**: "fire to cone 6" → “aħmi
  sal-kon 6”; "at cone 6" → “fil-kon 6”; "a cone 6 glaze" → “glejż għall-kon 6”; "cone 5–6" → “koni 5–6”; "cone 04
  bisque" → “bisque sal-kon 04”. Never “6 kon” (a count) and never “is-6 kon”.
- **Hotter and cooler, never higher and lower** (in the cooler series a higher number is cooler: kon 05 is cooler than
  kon 04): "one cone hotter" → “kon numru wieħed aktar sħun”; "one cone cooler" → “kon numru wieħed aktar kiesaħ”;
  "from cone 6 up" → “mill-kon 6 u koni aktar sħan”; "below cone 03" → “f’koni aktar kesħin mill-kon 03”; "go one cone
  hotter in the sitter" → “fis-sitter poġġi kon numru wieħed aktar sħun”. Never “ogħla”, “aktar baxx”, “’il fuq” or
  “’l isfel” for cones. Temperatures and switch settings may still be higher or lower (“temperatura ogħla”).
- "Orton cone" → “kon Orton”; at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared: “kon Orton 6”, then
  “kon 6”.
- Seger cones (“koni ta’ Seger”, SK) are a different scale. **Never** write “kon ta’ Seger 6” or “SK 6” for an Orton
  number; “koni ta’ Seger” only where the English says "Seger cones".

### The unity formula

“Il-formula ta’ Seger” (f), with “(UMF)” after it the first time on a page: “il-formula ta’ Seger (UMF)”, then “il-formula
ta’ Seger”. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are all “il-formula ta’ Seger” /
“Formula ta’ Seger:”. **No Maltese source** names it; this is the name the neighbouring languages use after Seger
(Italian _formula di Seger_, German _Segerformel_), with “formula” as Maltese writes it (f; pl. formuli). Never a literal
“formula molekulari tal-unità”. "UMF" stays as an English acronym (MS 4.1.2: no new local acronyms). For a Maltese potter
to confirm.

### Gender-neutral wording

Maltese nouns, adjectives and participles have gender, and so do inanimate things (“riċetta ġdida”, f; “forn ġdid”, m).
MS (3.1) asks for plurals in generalisations (“nies”, “utenti”, “persuni”), no gendered pronouns in generic references,
and verbs instead of participles when speaking to the reader: the imperative and the second person of the imperfect
and perfect do not mark gender, the participle does. So "Are you signed in?" → “Dħalt?”, not “Int dieħel?”; "You have
saved the recipe" → “Issejvjajt ir-riċetta”, not “Int issejvjat/a …”. Where no other way exists, the plural pronoun
(_huma_) is allowed for one person; never _hu/hi_ or slashed forms. A potter is “fuħħari” / “fuħħarija” (pl. fuħħarin,
Wiktionary): prefer the plural, or “min jaħdem iċ-ċeramika”, or speak to the reader.

### The reader in the guides

The second person singular and the imperative, in short plain sentences, as the English: “Għarbel il-glejż darbtejn
b’għarbiel ta’ 80 mesh.”; "If you fire to cone 6, …" → “Jekk taħmi sal-kon 6, …”. Explanations may use the plain third
person where the English is impersonal (“Il-glejż jitħallat mal-ilma”). Avoid the passive with _jiġi_ where the English
says "you", and avoid the bureaucratic nominal style (“L-għarbil tal-glejż għandu jitwettaq …”). Quotations from sources
are translated into “…” and keep their figures as the English writes them.

### The app's feature names

Written exactly as on the button or tab, first letter capital, **without quotation marks**, never inflected and never
with the article on the name itself: the article goes on a generic noun before it (MS 4.1.9: product and feature names
take no article): “agħżel Qabbel” (MS 3.2: “agħżel” rather than “ikklikkja”), “il-buttuna Qabbel”, “fit-tab Standard”,
“fil-paġna Materjali”, “il-funzjoni Ibdel iċ-ċomb”. Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the
tag. "Settings" is “Settings”, as Microsoft's Maltese software writes it (MS-T; MS 2.2.1: “fis-settings tal-PC”); in
running text it is a plural noun with the article: “fis-settings”, and inside `<settings>…</settings>` write the form the
sentence needs (`<settings>fis-settings</settings>`).

Maltese words (MS-T).

**The guides.** A guide is a **“gwida”** (f; pl. gwidi; nav "Guides" → “Gwidi”) in every link, title and list; a kiln's
or controller's manual is a **“manwal”** (m; pl. manwali), never “gwida” (2.4, 2.8). The five guides have one title
each, the same in their front matter, in every link that names them, in the page titles (`titles` in `mt.json`) and in
the guide list (`list` in `guides/mt.json`): “Il-glejż mill-bidu nett”, “Kif tagħmel glejż”, “Taħlit sikur u
ventilazzjoni”, “Tavvelenax lill-familja tiegħek”, “Il-ħami f’forn sempliċi”. Cross-references use “ara” (MS 2.1.2:
"refer to" → “ara”): "see [Firing a basic kiln]" → “ara l-gwida [Il-ħami f’forn sempliċi]”; "(see After the firing,
below)" → “(ara t-taqsima Wara l-ħami, aktar ’l isfel)”.

**Placeholders, trade names and the article.** The Maltese article (“il-”, and “iċ-, id-, in-, ir-, is-, it-, ix-,
iż-” before the sun letters ċ d n r s t x ż (z), “l-” before a vowel, “l-i…” before two consonants: “l-iskeda”) is
joined to its noun with a hyphen and assimilates to it, so it can never stand before `{name}`, `{region}`, `{library}`,
a figure the reader typed or a trade name. Put a generic noun with the article beside them, and let the noun carry the
article and the preposition:

- `{name}` (a material or recipe the reader named): "{name} is already in the recipe." → “Il-materjal {name} diġà
  jinsab fir-riċetta.”; "Remove "{name}"?" → `Tneħħi r-riċetta “{name}”?`; "Counted as {name}" → “Magħdud bħala
  {name}” (no article needed). Never “il-{name}” or “fil-{name}”.
- **Trade names** take no article of their own (MS 4.1.9): “il-fritta Ferro 3134”, “bil-feldspar Custer”, “mal-kawlina
  EPK”, “Gerstley Borate ma jsirx aktar”; never “il-Ferro 3134”.
- `{region}` is the country's name as the browser gives it (`Intl.DisplayNames('mt')`), and **it often carries its own
  article**: “il-Ġermanja”, “l-Italja”, “ir-Renju Unit”, “l-Istati Uniti”, but “Malta”, “Franza”, “Spanja”, “Ċipru”,
  “New Zealand” without one. So no preposition can go before it (“f’{region}” gives “f’il-Ġermanja” for
  “fil-Ġermanja”) and it can never open a sentence (its article would need a capital). Build the message so it stands
  after a colon or in brackets: "Shops in {region}" → “Ħwienet: {region}”; "Who to call in {region}" → “Lil min
  iċċempel: {region}”; "Limits for ceramic ware in {region}" → “Limiti għall-oġġetti taċ-ċeramika ({region})”; "We
  found no shop selling raw glaze materials in {region}." → “Ma sibna l-ebda ħanut li jbigħ materji primi għall-glejż
  ({region}).”
- `{library}` is a select: write each branch as a whole phrase, prepositions and all: `{library, select, US
{Ekwivalenti li jinbiegħu fl-Istati Uniti} UK {Ekwivalenti li jinbiegħu fir-Renju Unit} EU {Ekwivalenti li jinbiegħu
fl-UE} other {Ekwivalenti li jinbiegħu fl-Awstralja u New Zealand}}`.
- **Glazecalc** takes no article and no ending: “Glazecalc huwa b’xejn”, “fl-app Glazecalc” (MS: “app” is the
  everyday word).

**The United Kingdom** is “ir-Renju Unit” (CLDR; “fir-Renju Unit”, “mir-Renju Unit”) in running text, "UK" too; "(UK:
biscuit)" → “(fir-Renju Unit: biscuit)”; "UK potters" → “il-fuħħarin tar-Renju Unit”. The United States is “l-Istati
Uniti”, the EU “l-Unjoni Ewropea”, short “l-UE” (the EU's Maltese). The region labels "US", "UK", "EU", "AU and NZ"
stay as they are.

### EU law

As the EU's Maltese texts write it: _Direttiva_, _Regolament_; EEC is **KEE**, EC is **KE**, EU is **UE** (84/500,
2017/2398, CLP). "Directive 84/500/EEC" → “Direttiva tal-Kunsill 84/500/KEE” (full title: “Id-Direttiva tal-Kunsill tal-15
ta’ Ottubru 1984 dwar l-approssimazzjoni tal-liġijiet tal-Istati Membri li għandhom x’jaqsmu mal-oġġetti taċ-ċeramika
maħsuba sabiex jiġu f’kuntatt mal-oġġetti tal-ikel (84/500/KEE)”), amended by “Direttiva tal-Kummissjoni 2005/31/KE”.
"Directive (EU) 2017/2398" → “Direttiva (UE) 2017/2398”, which amends “Direttiva 2004/37/KE dwar il-protezzjoni
tal-ħaddiema minn riskji relatati mal-espożizzjoni għal karċinoġeni jew mutaġeni fuq il-post tax-xogħol”. CLP is
“Regolament (KE) Nru 1272/2008”, REACH “Regolament (KE) Nru 1907/2006”. Short form in running text: “id-Direttiva
84/500/KEE”. Never “84/500/EEC” or “84/500/EWG”. Malta's silica limit is set by “L.S. 646.14, Skeda III”
(_Leġislazzjoni Sussidjarja_; the region data's English "S.L. 646.14, Schedule III"); the chemical-agents regulations
are “L.S. 646.16”; the agency is the “Awtorità għas-Saħħa u s-Sigurtà fuq il-Post tax-Xogħol” (OHSA).

### Pitfalls

- **Glejż, żmalt, enamel, verniċ.** The EU's Maltese names the ceramic glaze **glejż** (m; pl. glejżijiet; CN 3207
  “glejżijiet vitrifikabbli”; ECO: “Il-glejż huwa s-sustanzi kollha applikati fuq il-wiċċ tal-madum”), glazed
  **igglejżjat** (ECO “madum glejżjat”, “maduma gglejżjata”; mt.wikipedia “madum igglejżjat”). **Żmalt** is enamel:
  goldsmith's enamel (mt.wikipedia, Cellini), vitreous enamel on metal (CN “ħadid fondut, żmaltat”), nail varnish and
  tooth enamel; the CN also writes “enamel” for it. **Verniċ** is paint and varnish. The old Maltese text of 84/500
  renders "glazed, enamelled" as “l-iżmalt, l-enamel”: not the app's word. Write glejż only.
- **Fritta** is a frit (CN “fritta tal-ħġieġ”); **fluss** is a flux (IATE). Do not confuse **fluss** (flux; also a
  flow, as Italian _flusso_) with **flus** (money).
- **Forn** is a kiln and a kitchen or baker's oven, **ħami** firing and baking bread, **moħmi** fired and baked (CN
  Chapter 69: “prodotti taċ-ċeramika li jinħmew”; mt.wikipedia: “ħobż … moħmi f’forn tal-injam”). Write “forn
  taċ-ċeramika” where the context does not settle it. Never **kalkara** (IATE's word for kiln: a lime kiln, and a
  town).
- **Fuħħar** is earthenware and, loosely, all pottery (CN “fuħħar komuni”, “fuħħar jew fajjenza fina”; Wiktionary).
  The app keeps it for **earthenware** and writes **ċeramika** for ceramics in general, so stoneware and porcelain are
  never called fuħħar. **Fajjenza** is tin-glazed or fine white earthenware, **majolika** tin-glazed ware,
  **terrakotta** unglazed red ware: none is the app's earthenware.
- **Whiting is not ġibs or ġir.** **Ġibs** is chalk, gypsum and plaster (Wiktionary; the potter's plaster mould), and
  the CN even writes “ġibs verġni” for quicklime; **ġir** is lime, CaO (IATE). Whiting is “karbonat tal-kalċju” (2.2).
- **The material is not the oxide.** **Soda** is sodium carbonate (IATE “karbonat tas-sodju; soda”) and a fizzy drink;
  the potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are “sodju (Na₂O)” and “potassju (K₂O)”, the safety guide's potash
  “karbonat tal-potassju”. “kalċju (CaO)” is the oxide, “karbonat tal-kalċju” the material.
- **Silika, siliċju, silikon.** The oxide SiO₂ is “silika” (2017/2398, LS 646.14) or formally “diossidu tas-siliċju”
  (CN); **siliċju** is the element silicon and **silikon** the rubber. **Siliċe** is the CN's and the food additives'
  word (silica E551, “siliċe fuż”): not the app's.
- **Respiratur** is the mask (IATE: “respiratur kontra l-partikoli”, “maskra b’filtru”); a hospital ventilator is a
  **ventilatur** (IATE), a fan **fann**, a surgical or cloth mask “maskra tal-wiċċ”. Never ventilatur for the mask.
- **Riduzzjoni** is reduction firing; **tnaqqis** (IATE's "reduction") is a cut or decrease.
- **Ċirku** is a ring as a shape (the pyrometric ring); **ċurkett** is a ring for a finger.
- **Tara** is tare (IATE) but also "you see" (_tara_); write “ġib il-miżien fuq żero” where the verb would be
  ambiguous.
- **Terra** in everyday Maltese is talcum powder (CN “steatit naturali … terra”); the potter's talc is “talk”.
- **Manwal, manwali, gwida.** “manwal” (pl. manwali) is a kiln's manual, “gwida” the app's guide; but the adjective
  "manual" is also “manwali” (MS-T), so "manual kiln" is “forn mingħajr kontrollur”, never “forn manwali”, which reads
  as "manuals" in the plural.
- **"should" and "must"** in safety text (README). "must" → “irid/trid/iridu” + verb, or “bilfors” (“Trid tilbes
  respiratur.”); "should" → “għandu/għandek/għandhom” + verb (“It-tfal m’għandhomx iħarsu ġol-forn.”, "should not";
  “Għandek tilbes ingwanti.”). "must not" → the negative imperative or “qatt ma” where the English says never, or “ma
  jistax/ma tistax” (“It-tfal ma jistgħux jidħlu fl-istudjo.”); **never “ma tridx”**, which means "you need not" (or
  "you don't want"). "must be tested" means the test must be done, not passed: “trid tiġi ttestjata fil-laboratorju”,
  “irid isir test tal-laboratorju”, never “trid tgħaddi mit-test”. The EU's Maltese writes "should" as **jenħtieġ**
  (2017/2398: “li jenħtieġ li ikun soġġett għal reviżjoni”), which is one letter from **jeħtieġ**, "is needed": do not
  use either for "should" or "must" in the app. "some" is “xi”, never “ftit” ("a little"): "may contain some fine
  quartz" → “jista’ jkun fih xi kwarz fin”. Where the English only reports a source's figure, do not make it “trid”.
- **"Not fully" is not "not at all"**: "it may not melt fully" → “jista’ ma jdubx għal kollox”; “ma jdub xejn” is "does
  not melt at all".
- **Nej** is raw food: never for raw materials or raw (unfired, uncalcined) in the app (2.1).
- **mesh**: keep “mesh” after the number (“għarbiel ta’ 80 mesh”).
- **Numbers as words**: a number in the English stays a number (README), even one to ten.
- **English loans** (D2, as MS 4.1.10 summarises it): a loan that has settled into Maltese is spelt in Maltese
  (“kompjuter”, “skrin”, “plastik”, and here “glejż”, “igglejżja”, “sejvja”); English compound words stay English
  (“fire extinguisher”, “website”, and here “ball clay”, “leather-hard”, “drop-and-hold”); a loan whose Maltese
  spelling would look strange stays English. Loans kept in English are not put in italics or quotes. Spell a settled loan
  the same way everywhere, with its Maltese plural (“glejżijiet”, “koni”, “fritti”).

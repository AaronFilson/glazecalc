# Italiano: guida di stile e glossario (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Italian, for potters in Italy (standard Italian, it-IT). Every
Italian message and guide follows this page: one Italian word for one thing. The terms come from Italian suppliers'
shops and glossaries, a ceramics school's course notes, potters' blogs, a kiln maker's manual and the safety
authorities (INAIL, the ASL, the Ministero della Salute), not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none
was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a potter to check.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal: never _tu_ in a sentence. Prefer what careful Italian technical writing does:

- **Steps and warnings: the infinitive.** «Setacciare lo smalto due volte.», «Indossare una maschera FFP3.», «Non
  spazzare a secco.» This is how Italian instructions, MS procedures («fare clic su Apri») and the official CLP
  precautionary statements («Indossare guanti protettivi») are written.
- **Explanations: the impersonal _si_ or the passive.** «Si setaccia lo smalto per togliere i grumi.», «Il biscotto
  va pulito prima di smaltare.»
- **_Lei_ only where the reader must be addressed as a person**, in their own situation: «Se lavora in casa, …»,
  «Scelga il cono indicato dal produttore.» Capitalise _Lei_ and its forms (Lei, La, Le, Suo, Sua) so they cannot be
  read as "she" or "his". Do not mix infinitive steps and _Lei_ imperatives in one list.
- "Please try again." → «Riprovare.»; "Error: could not save the recipe." → «Errore: impossibile salvare la
  ricetta.» Do not add _per favore_; use _si prega di_ only in a formal request where the English has "please".
- The app speaks of itself in the first person plural where the English says "we": «Suggeriamo il cono 06–04.»

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tab names.**

- **Buttons and commands: the imperative singular, verb first**, as Italian software does (MS: «Salva», «Salva con
  nome», «Annulla», «Apri»; Apple's and Google's Italian interfaces are the same): «Salva», «Stampa», «Confronta»,
  «Rimuovi», «Aggiungilo», «Ricalcola». Italian readers take these as labels, not as _tu_; they are the only _tu_ forms
  allowed. The infinitive on a button («Salvare») reads dated and is not used. Instructions that name a button use the
  infinitive around it: «fare clic su **Salva**», «selezionare **Obbligatorio**».
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a noun or noun phrase: «Ricette», «Materiali», «Impostazioni»,
  «Registri di cottura», «Coni e lavoro termico». A heading that is an instruction in English may stay one, in the
  infinitive: "Read the unity formula" → «Leggere la formula di Seger».
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: «Nome del materiale:».
- Confirmations keep the English shape: "Saved: …" → «Salvato: …» (agreeing with what was saved, never with the
  reader); "Removed …" → «Rimosso …».

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case everywhere (MS: only the first word of a title or heading takes a capital). English
Title Case goes: "Title of Firing:" → «Titolo della cottura:», "Fired Weight" → «Peso cotto». Material names are
common nouns, lower case: «carbonato di calcio», «feldspato sodico». Trade and proper names keep their form (Orton,
Seger, Gerstley Borate, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc). Days, months and language names are lower case («inglese»).

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: the _caporali_ «…», with no space inside; inside them “…”. Not straight "…" in text, never single
  quotes. The English quotes round a name in a message become caporali: `Remove "{name}"?` → `Rimuovere «{name}»?`.
  (This page puts Italian examples in caporali too.)
- Apostrophe: the typographic ’ (U+2019): «l’argilla», «dell’impasto». A straight ' is safe except just before `{`, `}`
  or `#`, where ICU needs `''`; using ’ avoids the problem. Do not elide before a placeholder, which may begin with a
  consonant: «del materiale {name}», not «dell’{name}».
- No space before ; : ! ? (unlike French). No comma before _e_, _o_ (MS).
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, the unit once at the end: «1222–1240 °C», «cono 06–04», «1–3%». In a sentence
  «da 1222 a 1240 °C» is also fine; never mix («da 1222–1240 °C»).

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: «1,5%», «0,01 g», «peso specifico 1,45». (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app
  renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped («1240 °C», «1000 g»), as CLDR Italian and the app print them; five
  digits and more take a dot («12.500 g»). Never write a temperature as «1.240 °C».
- A space before °C, °F, °Bé and every unit symbol: «1240 °C», «100 g», «52 °Bé», «0,1 mg/m³», «80 mesh». **No space
  before %**: «5%», «1–3%», «12,5%», as CLDR Italian and the app's own number formatting print it. Rates: «100 °C/h» or
  «100 °C all’ora».
- A number stays in digits, never a word («3 mani», not «tre mani», when the English has "3 coats"; "two or three
  coats" in words stays in words).

**Cones.** The app's cones are Orton's American cones.

- «cono 6», «cono 06», «cono 04»: lower-case _cono_, the number exactly as written, leading zero kept. Read aloud
  "cono zero sei"; 06 is cooler than 6.
- "fire to cone 6" → «cuocere a cono 6»; "a cone 6 firing" → «una cottura a cono 6»; "cone 6 glazes" → «smalti per
  cono 6»; "cone 5–6" → «cono 5–6»; "cone 04 bisque" → «biscotto a cono 04» (the firing: «biscottatura a cono 04»).
  Italian sources write cones this way (Cono9: «cono 022», «cono 01»; LM: «nella regione di cono 8-10»; an MC
  category ends «- cono 6»).
- "Orton cone" → «cono Orton», plural «coni Orton» (Orton never inflected; MC sells «Coni Orton»). "pyrometric cone"
  → «cono pirometrico». _Piroscopio_ is an old synonym (Cerama): do not use it in the app.
- **Seger cones are a different series**: «coni Seger» (German _Segerkegel_, numbered SK 6a and so on). Their numbers
  are not Orton's and do not convert. Italian shops and glossaries use «coni Seger» loosely for any pyrometric cone
  (Cerama lists «Coni Seger» as a synonym of «Cono Orton»): never write «cono Seger» for one of the app's cones.
- Italian potters mostly fire by temperature (the app's default for Italy is «In temperatura»), so the guides often
  give °C beside the cone; keep both as the English has them.

**The unity formula.** Italian potters learn it under Seger's name: LM calls the method «metodo Seger» and the
formula «Unità Molecolare»; other Italian pages say «formula Seger» or «formula unitaria». Write **«formula di Seger
(UMF)»** at its first use on each page or guide section, then «formula di Seger». Short labels: "Unity formula" →
«Formula di Seger»; "Unity:" → «Formula di Seger:»; "in unity" → «nella formula di Seger». Do not use «formula
molecolare unitaria» (a calque no Italian source used). The scaling: «riportata all’unità», «la somma dei fondenti è
1» (LM: «in modo tale che la loro somma sia pari a UNO»).

**Gender-neutral wording.** Italian nouns and participles carry gender, so:

- For "potter" use **«ceramista»**, the same word for a man or a woman; in the plural prefer «chi fa ceramica», «chi
  smalta», or a collective («il laboratorio»). _Vasaio_ is the traditional wheel potter: avoid it for the reader.
- Never let an adjective or participle agree with the reader: "Are you sure?" → «Rimuovere davvero …?», not «È
  sicuro?»; "Welcome back to your glaze notebook." → «Le diamo il bentornato nel quaderno degli smalti.», not
  «Bentornato» (MS: «Benvenuto > Ti diamo il benvenuto»). Use the impersonal and the passive (MS recommends both).
- No slashes in suffixes («ceramista/a»), no asterisk or schwa (MS: «Symbols such as asterisk … or the schwa … are not
  currently used»).

**The reader in the guides.** Steps to a new potter are infinitives («Pesare i materiali su una bilancia che legga
0,01 g.»); explanations are impersonal («Lo smalto si stende in uno strato uniforme.»); _Lei_ only for the reader's
own circumstances («Se ha bambini piccoli, …»). Generic "you" becomes impersonal: "your kiln" → «il forno», "the clay
you use" → «l’argilla che si usa». Keep sentences short and plain, as the English does.

**The app's feature names in running text.** Write the label exactly as on screen, with its capital, in bold where the
English has `<b>`, otherwise plain and without quotes:

| English                | Italian label                      | In running text                                       |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Compare                | Confronta                          | «**Confronta**, accanto a Salva, …»                   |
| Suggest amounts        | Suggerisci le quantità e confronta | «con **Suggerisci le quantità e confronta**»          |
| Match with what I have | Adatta ai materiali che ho         | «la funzione Adatta ai materiali che ho»              |
| Replace lead           | Sostituisci il piombo              | «con **Sostituisci il piombo**»                       |
| Settings               | Impostazioni                       | «in Impostazioni, nella pagina dell’account»          |
| Notebook               | Quaderno di laboratorio            | «il quaderno di laboratorio» (lower case in a phrase) |
| Library                | elenco standard                    | «l’elenco standard dei materiali» (never libreria)    |
| Standard (tab)         | Standard                           | «la scheda Standard»; «i materiali standard»          |
| My materials           | I miei materiali                   | «nella scheda I miei materiali»                       |
| Trial                  | prova                              | «la prova di 14 giorni» (never _ospite_)              |

**Pitfalls in Italian.**

- **opaco** means matte, not opaque (Cerama «Smalto Opaco (Matte Glaze)»; MC «smalti matt»). Opaque, hiding the clay,
  is **coprente** (HL: smalti «coprenti»); made opaque by an opacifier, **opacizzato**.
- **cristallina** (noun) is a clear glaze; **smalto cristallino** is a crystalline glaze with crystals (Cerama);
  **silice cristallina** is the dust hazard. Three different things.
- **vetrina** is a transparent glaze in strict usage and, traditionally, a lead glaze on terracotta (it.wikipedia
  [Vetrina](<https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetrina_(ceramica)>)): do not use it for "glaze" or "clear glaze". **vernice**
  is varnish or paint, a regional word for old lead glazes: never for glaze.
- **silicio** is the element silicon, **silicone** the rubber; the oxide is **silice**.
- **potassa** is potassium carbonate or hydroxide and **soda** is sodium carbonate (soda Solvay; _soda caustica_ is
  NaOH): potters' "potash" and "soda" are **potassio** and **sodio** (the oxides K₂O, Na₂O).
- **gesso** is plaster (calcium sulfate): never whiting. Whiting is **carbonato di calcio** (MC also sells «Gesso di
  Bologna - Carbonato di Calcio finissimo»: read the formula on the bag).
- **idrometro** is a river-level gauge: the hydrometer is the **densimetro**.
- **respiratore** also means a hospital ventilator: say **maschera filtrante**, **facciale filtrante** or
  **semimaschera**, never «mascherina» (a surgical or cloth mask).
- **ritiro** is shrinkage of clay (Cerama «Ritiro dell’argilla»). This glossary uses **ritiro dello smalto** for
  crawling, so in glaze-fit text write contraction as **contrazione / si contrae** («lo smalto si contrae più
  dell’impasto»), never _si ritira_.
- **terraglia** is white earthenware, **terracotta** red low-fire ware, **maiolica** tin-glazed earthenware (a ware
  and a technique, not a clay); **gres porcellanato** is porcelain stoneware tile, not stoneware.
- **regione** is an Italian administrative region (Lombardia): the app's selling regions (US, UK, EU, AU and NZ) are
  **aree**.
- **libreria** is a bookshop or bookcase (or a code library); **biblioteca** a library of books. The app's library of
  materials is **l’elenco standard**.
- **forno**, not _fornace_ (a brick or lime kiln) or _muffola_ (dated). **argilla**, not _creta_ (colloquial, Tuscan).
- **lotto** is a supplier's production batch («numero di lotto»); a glaze mixed at one time is a **pesata**.
- Do not borrow English where Italian has a word suppliers use (no «crawling», «pinholing», «bisque» in the text); keep
  the English only for the names below (ball clay, raku, tenmoku, celadon, craquelé, kiln sitter, mesh, CMC).

## 2. Glossary

Nouns carry their article (il, lo, la, l’ with m./f.). **show English** marks the terms whose English the reader can
ask to see: `Fritta<en>frit</en>` in messages, `la fritta{{en: frit}}` in the guides.

# Svenska: stilmall och ordlista (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Swedish (`sv`), for potters in Sweden and in the Swedish-speaking
parts of Finland. Write standard Swedish as Swedish suppliers write it: it is also the written norm of Swedish in
Finland, so one text serves both. Every Swedish message and guide follows this page: one Swedish word for one thing.
The terms come from Swedish suppliers' shops and product pages (Lermakeriet, Cerama, Lars Tärn), the Swedish potters'
forum Keramik iFokus, Nabertherm's Swedish controller manual, Swedish Wikipedia, IATE, and the agencies
(Arbetsmiljöverket, Livsmedelsverket, Kemikalieinspektionen), not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or
none was found, the Notes say so. No Finland-Swedish ceramics supplier was found (Kerasil, the Finnish shop in the app,
is in Finnish only); where Finland differs (agencies, limits), the Notes say so.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Polite and neutral, met by leaving the pronoun out. The formal _ni_ is all but gone from Swedish software
and public writing and reads stiff or odd (in Finland it lingers a little more, where it can also read as a plural);
Swedish software, agencies and shops that address the reader at all use _du_ (MS 4.1.15; AV: "Du som arbetsgivare …";
LM: "där du inte vill att glasyren ska fästa"). So the reader is addressed without a pronoun wherever Swedish allows it:

- **Instructions and warnings**: the imperative, no pronoun: "Sikta glasyren två gånger.", "Bär andningsskydd med
  P3-filter.", "Väg upp råvarorna på en våg som visar 0,01 g." MS 5.5.2 asks for the imperative first, not "Du väljer
  …".
- **Descriptions and explanations**: make the thing the subject ("Glasyren smälter in i skärven."), or use the
  s-passive ("Råvarorna vägs i gram."), sparingly: MS 4.1.15 advises against impersonal constructions and the passive. The generic
  _man_ is acceptable where a person must act and the imperative does not fit ("Om man bränner till kägla 6 …"), at
  most once or twice in a paragraph.
- **"your", "my"**: the definite noun or _egen, eget, egna_: "your account" → "kontot", "My materials" → "Egna
  råvaror", "the clay you use" → "den egna leran", "Your trial" → "Provperiod".
- **Questions to the reader**: a statement, or the imperative with a question mark: "Discard everything you made in
  this trial?" → "Radera allt som gjorts under provperioden?"
- **Last resort**: where a sentence cannot be made natural without naming the reader (rare in the app, commoner in the
  guides), write _du, din, ditt, dina_, lower case. Never _ni_, _Ni_ or a capitalised _Du_. Try first to recast:
  "You're trying Glazecalc as {name}. Your work is kept until {date}." → "Provperiod som <strong>{name}</strong>.
  Arbetet sparas till {date}."
- "Please" only where the English has it, and usually dropped as MS does: "Please try again." → "Försök igen." The
  app's "we" stays "vi" where the English says "we", no more (MS 4.1.15, note 2).

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs** (the Swedish software convention, MS 4.1 and 5.5):

- Buttons and menu commands: the **imperative** (for -a verbs it is the infinitive without _att_): "Spara", "Skriv
  ut", "Jämför", "Lägg till råvaror", "Ta bort", "Spara som kopia". Not a noun ("Sparande") and not "Att spara".
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: "Recept", "Råvaror", "Brännloggar",
  "Inställningar", "Käglor och värmearbete". A heading that names an action may be an infinitive without _att_ ("Blanda
  en glasyr"), never "Att blanda en glasyr" (MS 5.5.1).
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: "Råvarans namn:". Options and check boxes take no
  full stop (MS).
- Confirmations: the participle first, as the English: "Saved: …" → "Sparat: …"; or a short sentence: `Removed "{name}".`
  → `”{name}” har tagits bort.`
- Ellipsis "…" with no space before it in the UI: "Hämtar receptet…".

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case only: the first word and names, nothing else, in headings and buttons too (MS 4.1.5:
"Don't follow the capitalization in the source text"). "Fired Weight" → "Bränd vikt". Days and months are lower case.
A compound whose first part is a name keeps the capital: "Ortonkägla" (as CE sells it), "Segerformel", "Segerkägla";
a foreign or product name takes a hyphen (MS 4.1.6, "Windows-program"): "Kiln-Sitter-brytare", "Ferro-fritta".

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: ” … ” (U+201D at both ends, MS); inside them ’ … ’ (U+2019). Not “ … ”, „ … “ or » … «.
- **No quotation marks around UI names** in running text (MS: Swedish does not quote software references; the capital
  initial marks them): "Välj Jämför.", "under Inställningar". Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag,
  keep the tag.
- Apostrophe: the typographic ’ (U+2019); Swedish hardly needs it (the genitive takes none: "Ortons tabell"). Write
  `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`, `}` or `#`, and in `safety`, `regions` and `records`).
- Abbreviations take their endings after a colon (MS): "UMF:en", "SDB:n". Prefer wording that needs no ending.
- No space before ; : ! ? (unlike French). No serial comma before "och" (MS): "kalium, natrium och litium".
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, the unit once at the end (MS): "1222–1240 °C", "kägla 06–04", "1–3 %". In a
  sentence "mellan 1222 och 1240 °C" or "från 1222 till 1240 °C" is also fine; never mix ("från 1222–1240 °C").
- Compounds with a digit, symbol or abbreviation take a hyphen: "FFP3-mask", "P3-filter" (AV), "SiO₂-halt",
  "CMC-lim". Recast where the compound gets long: "a cone 6 glaze" → "en glasyr för kägla 6", not "kägla 6-glasyr";
  "an 80 mesh sieve" → "en sikt på 80 mesh".
- Inches and feet: "tum" and "fot" (MS: never the " sign), with the metric figure in brackets as the English has it.

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: "1,5 %", "0,01 g", "1,45". (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped ("1222 °C", "1000 g"), as the app prints them and as Swedish shops
  write firing temperatures ("1240 grader", LM); five digits and more take a space, non-breaking where possible
  ("12 500 g"). Språkrådet's own example also groups four digits ("3 400"); the check reads both, but keep one style.
  Never a full stop or comma for thousands.
- A space before °C, °F, % and every unit: "1240 °C", "15 %", "100 °C/h", "80 mesh" (MS asks for a non-breaking space;
  a plain space is fine in the files). Shops often print "1240°C" or "°/tim" (LM); do not copy that.
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word.

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Swedish `one` and `other` (`Intl.PluralRules('sv')`: `one` for 1 only; 0, 2, 5, 21 and
fractions such as 1,5 are `other`). Write both, even where they read the same:
`{count, plural, one {# råvara} other {# råvaror}}` gives "1 råvara", "2 råvaror", "5 råvaror", "0 råvaror". Many
neuter nouns have no plural ending: `one {# recept} other {# recept}`, "1 prov, 3 prov", but both forms are still
written. Swedish has no case endings to follow the number (only the genitive -s); adjectives and participles agree in
number and gender: "1 sparat recept, 2 sparade recept", "1 ny råvara, 3 nya råvaror". A fraction takes the plural:
"1,5 timmar". `=0 {…}` and `#` stay as the English has them.

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Swedish shops sell Orton cones and name them so: CE
"Ortonkägla 07, 976-987 °C", LM "Orton smältkägla" with numbers from 017 to 10 and the warning "Det finns käglor med
nummer 06, 07, 08 och 6, 7, 8 se till att välja rätt." Potters write "kägla 6-7", "kägla 08 skröj" (KF 1659137, KF
1560466), but also call any cone "segerkägla" (KF 1443725 "segerkäglorna") or "kon" (KF 1394270 "koner eller käglor").
True Seger cones (SK) are a different series whose numbers do not match Orton's. So:

- "cone 6" → "kägla 6"; "cone 06" → "kägla 06" (read "noll sex": kägla 06 is far cooler than kägla 6).
- "Orton cone" → "Ortonkägla" (en; Ortonkäglan, Ortonkäglor); at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are
  compared: "Ortonkägla 6", then "kägla 6".
- "cone 04 bisque" → "skröjbränning till kägla 04"; "fire to cone 6" → "bränna till kägla 6"; "at cone 10" → "vid
  kägla 10"; "a cone 6 glaze" → "en glasyr för kägla 6"; "cone 5–6" → "kägla 5–6".
- **Never** write "Segerkägla 6", "SK 6" or "kon 6" for an Orton number. "Segerkägla" appears only where the English
  says "Seger cones", and then means the SK series.

**The unity formula.** "Segerformel" (en; Segerformeln), with "(UMF)" after it the first time on a page: "Segerformeln
(UMF)", then "Segerformeln". Swedish potters call it so (KF 1530013: "Seger formel säger vi väl?", and "gränsformler"
for limit formulas; MDPW "segerformel"); the capital follows "Ortonkägla". The app's short forms "unity formula",
"Unity formula" and "Unity:" are all "Segerformel" / "Segerformel:".

**Gender-neutral wording.** Swedish person nouns for potters are already neutral ("keramiker", "krukmakare"). Avoid
_han_ or _hon_ for an unknown person: use the plural, "den som …", or recast; _hen_ is in the Swedish Academy's word
list and MS accepts it, but recasting is better. Avoid "-man" compounds ("fackman" → "expert"). Grammatical gender of
things is unaffected.

**The reader in the guides.** As under Register: steps in the imperative, short plain sentences, the thing as subject
where the English describes ("Sikta glasyren två gånger genom en sikt på 80 mesh."; "Krita ger kalcium."). Use _man_
sparingly and _du_ only as the last resort. Avoid nominal style ("Siktningen av glasyren genomförs …"). Quotations from
sources are translated into ” … ” and keep their figures as the English writes them.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with a capital first letter and no quotation marks (MS).
Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag. Otherwise use the name when the reader is told
to use it ("Välj Jämför."), and the ordinary noun when the feature is described ("Jämförelsen visar …"). The fixed
names:

**EU directives and regulations.** As the EU's Swedish texts write them: "EEG" for EEC, "EG" for EC, "EU" for EU;
"direktiv" lower case in a sentence, with the institution before it where the full name is wanted: "rådets direktiv
84/500/EEG", "kommissionens direktiv 2005/31/EG", "direktiv (EU) 2017/2398", "förordning (EG) nr 1272/2008" (CLP),
"förordning (EG) nr 1935/2004". Livsmedelsverket's rules write "rådets direktiv 84/500/EEG" (LIVSFS 2006:6, now
replaced by LIVSFS 2023:5). The regions label "Directive 84/500/EEC (amended by Directive 2005/31/EC)" → "Direktiv
84/500/EEG (ändrat genom direktiv 2005/31/EG)". Never "EEC", "EWG" or "CEE".

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **Glasyr**, never "lasyr" (a wood stain), "emalj" (enamel on metal) or "fernissa" (varnish). "Glasyr" is also cake
  icing; context settles it.
- **Stengods** is stoneware: Swedish has no Steingut trap (KF 1473592: German Steingut is "lergods", Steinzeug
  "stengods"). Do not use
  "flintgods" (a white earthenware) or "fajans" (tin-glazed earthenware) unless the English means those.
- **Soda** in Swedish is sodium carbonate (LM "Soda", CE "Soda Kalcinerad, Natriumkarbonat"), and **pottaska** is
  potassium carbonate. The potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are "natrium (Na₂O)" and "kalium (K₂O)"; "natron" and
  "kali" survive only in "natronfältspat", "kalifältspat".
- **Kisel** is the element silicon, **silikon** the rubber, and "kiselsyra" an old word for silica. The oxide SiO₂ is
  "kiseldioxid"; the material on the bag is "kvarts". Potters write "kisel" loosely for SiO₂ (KF 1530013); the app
  does not.
- **Respirator** in Swedish is a hospital ventilator. The mask is "andningsskydd" (AV); see 2.7.
- **Krackelering** is unwanted crazing; **krackelyr** is crackle made on purpose (SvWP). Keep them apart.
- **Slicker** is clay slip; a mixed glaze is "glasyrslam" or simply "glasyren" (KF 1473592).
- **Kägla**, not "kon", for a cone; "segerkäglor" for Orton cones is loose usage the app does not follow (see Cones).
- **Skröj** is bisque; "biskvi" is unglazed porcelain and "kex" a biscuit to eat.
- **Bränning** is a firing; **brand** is a fire (a burning building) in safety text, and **eld** a flame.
- **should, must**: "should" → "bör" ("should not" → "bör inte"), "must" → "måste" or "ska", "must not" → "får inte".
  Never "ska" for "should": it reads as "must" (README: "should" is not "must"). A reported instruction ("HSE says to
  …") may take "att man ska …".
- **Ställmedel** covers both suspenders (bentonite, LT) and the flocculating products shops sell (CE "Ställmedel KUR
  1"); see "suspender" (2.2) and "flocculant" (2.6).
- **UK**: the forum abbreviates utvidgningskoefficient as "UK" (KF 398347); never do so, since the app's UK is the
  United Kingdom.
- **Blank**, not "glansig", for a gloss glaze; **sidenmatt**, not "satin", for satin (LM).
- **Swedish in Finland**: write the standard Swedish of Swedish suppliers, which Finland-Swedish readers read as their
  own written norm. Avoid Sweden-only colloquial words and Finland-only words alike. Agencies differ: Arbetsmiljöverket
  in Sweden, Arbetshälsoinstitutet (Työterveyslaitos) in Finland, whose limits are "HTP-värden"; both countries have a
  Giftinformationscentralen.

## 2. Glossary

Nouns carry their gender (en, ett) and, where it helps, the definite or plural form. "show English" marks the terms
whose English a reader can ask to see after them (`<en>…</en>`, `{{en: …}}`): "fritta<en>frit</en>",
"fritta{{en: frit}}".

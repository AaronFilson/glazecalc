# Ελληνικά: οδηγός ύφους και γλωσσάριο (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Greek, for potters in Greece and Cyprus (Standard Modern Greek,
el, monotonic spelling). Every Greek message and guide follows this page: one Greek word for one thing. The terms come
from the Greek shops' own catalogues (Ράμφος, Κεραμικό Κέντρο, Zanias), the Greek national ceramics centre's glaze
handbook (ΕΛΚΕΑ), potters' studio usage, a kiln maker's Greek manual, and the EU's and ELINYAE's Greek texts, not from a
dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a potter to check.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal throughout: the plural _εσείς_ forms. Instructions use the plural imperative: «Επιλέξτε …»,
«Κοσκινίστε το υάλωμα δύο φορές.», «Φορέστε μάσκα FFP3.». Possessives with _σας_: «η συνταγή σας». Never the singular
_εσύ_ (no «Επίλεξε», «σου»). Write _εσείς_ itself only for emphasis; the verb carries the person. Use _παρακαλώ_ only
where the English has "please" («Παρακαλώ δοκιμάστε ξανά.»). Where the English says "we", the app may say «Προτείνουμε
…». Avoid old-fashioned and katharevousa wording (MS): «ψήσιμο», not «όπτηση» or «έψηση»; «τήξης», not «τήξεως»; «να»
clauses, not participles in -όμενος where a short sentence will do.

**"Should" and "must".** In safety text "should" is «θα πρέπει» ("should not" «δεν θα πρέπει») and "must" is
«πρέπει» ("must not" «δεν πρέπει», or «μην» in an instruction): «πρέπει» alone is "must", a stronger word, and the
README's rule is no softer and no stronger. This holds inside quotations too: Orton's "Welders glasses should be used"
→ «Θα πρέπει να χρησιμοποιούνται γυαλιά συγκόλλησης»; Digitalfire's "one should not confuse barium carbonate with
table sugar" → «δεν θα πρέπει να μπερδεύει κανείς …». "Has to" and "needs to" may be «πρέπει» or «χρειάζεται». Outside
safety text a "should" of expectation ("the bisque should stay porous") may be «πρέπει».

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs** (the Greek software convention: MS notes that Greek UIs use
nouns for commands, as in Greek Windows and Office: «Αποθήκευση», «Εκτύπωση», «Αντιγραφή», «Διαγραφή», «Ακύρωση»):

- Buttons and menu commands: a **verbal noun** in the nominative, without an article: «Αποθήκευση», «Αποθήκευση ως
  αντίγραφο», «Εκτύπωση», «Σύγκριση», «Προσθήκη υλικών», «Απόρριψη δοκιμαστικής χρήσης». Not the imperative
  («Αποθηκεύστε») and not the infinitive-like «Αποθήκευσε». A button whose English is a whole sentence ("Try it now")
  may be an imperative: «Δοκιμάστε το τώρα».
- Keep genitive chains short (MS: at most two genitives in a row): «Αποθήκευση και προσθήκη επόμενης συνταγής» is the
  limit; rephrase anything longer.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a noun or noun phrase: «Συνταγές», «Ρυθμίσεις», «Ημερολόγια
  ψησίματος», «Κώνοι και θερμικό έργο». A heading that is a question or an instruction in English may stay one.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: «Όνομα υλικού:».
- Confirmations: the passive past, agreeing with the thing done: "Saved: …" → «Αποθηκεύτηκε: …»; several things →
  «Αποθηκεύτηκαν …».

**Capitalisation.** Sentence case everywhere (MS): only the first word of a heading, label, button or list item takes a
capital: "Fired Weight" → «Βάρος μετά το ψήσιμο», "Title of Firing:" → «Τίτλος ψησίματος:». Greek capitalises the
names of languages, days and months («Αγγλικά», «Δευτέρα», «Οκτώβριος») but not adjectives from country names
(«αγγλικός», «ελληνικός»). Material names are common nouns, lower
case: «ανθρακικό ασβέστιο», «άστριος καλίου». Names keep their own form: Orton, Seger, Gerstley Borate, Kiln-Sitter,
Glazecalc. **Accents and capitals:** a capital at the start of a word keeps its accent («Άστριος», «Ώρα», «Έξοδος»);
a word written entirely in capitals drops its accents but keeps the diaeresis («ΡΥΘΜΙΣΕΙΣ», «ΠΡΟΪΟΝ»). Never type
whole words in capitals in a translation; if the page shows a label in capitals, CSS does it.

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: the Greek «…» (U+00AB, U+00BB), with no space inside; inside them “…” (U+201C, U+201D). This is
  Greek typography and what the EU's Greek texts use («κεραμικά αντικείμενα»); MS uses straight quotes for its own
  products, which Glazecalc does not follow. `Remove "{name}"?` → `Κατάργηση του «{name}»;`.
- **Question mark**: the Greek question mark is «;». Type the ordinary semicolon character (U+003B); the separate
  "Greek question mark" (U+037E) is the same character after Unicode normalisation and only causes trouble. The Greek
  semicolon is the ano teleia «·» (U+00B7, not U+0387): rare; prefer a full stop or a comma.
- Apostrophe: avoid elision (MS): «από το», «σε αυτό», not «απ’ το», «σ’ αυτό». Where one is unavoidable, use the
  typographic ’ (U+2019); a straight ' just before `{`, `}` or `#` must be written `''` (README).
- No space before ; : ! · (unlike French). Exclamation marks sparingly (MS): "Out of memory!" → a full stop.
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, the unit once at the end: «1222–1240 °C», «κώνοι 06–04», «1–3%». In a sentence
  «από 1222 έως 1240 °C» is also fine; never mix («από 1222–1240 °C»).
- Em dashes as in English parentheses are rare in Greek (MS): use commas or brackets.

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: «1,5%», «0,01 g», «ειδικό βάρος 1,45». (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app
  renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped («1240 °C», «1000 g»), as the app prints them; five digits and more
  take a full stop, the Greek separator in CLDR («12.500 g»). Never write a temperature as «1.240 °C». (Greek shops
  and books do group four digits; the app does not, so the translations do not.) Years, codes and CAS numbers are never
  grouped.
- A space before °C, °F, °Bé and every unit symbol: «1240 °C», «100 g», «0,1 mg/m³», «80 mesh», «100 °C/h». Greek
  shops print «1200-1260°C» and «80mesh»; do not copy that. **No space before %**: «5%», «1–3%», «12,5%», as MS's
  Greek examples («12%»), CLDR Greek and the app's own number formatting print it. (EU legal texts in Greek write
  «4 %»; that is the EU house style, not Greek usage.)
- Numbers stay digits, never words, wherever the English has digits (MS's "spell out numbers below ten" rule does not
  apply to Glazecalc: the check compares the figures). Times on the 24-hour clock: «16:00».

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Greek two categories, `one` and `other`
(`new Intl.PluralRules('el')` → `[ 'one', 'other' ]`):

| Category | Numbers                     | Examples                                         |
| -------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `one`    | 1 only                      | 1 υλικό, 1 συνταγή, 1 κώνος                      |
| `other`  | 0, 2, 3, 5, 21, 100, 1,5, … | 0 υλικά, 2 υλικά, 5 συνταγές, 21 κώνοι, 1,5 κιλά |

`{count, plural, =0 {…} one {# υλικό} other {# υλικά}}`. Decimals and 0 take `other`. The noun after the number
takes the **case the sentence needs**, and the two differ for masculine and feminine plurals: «Βρέθηκαν {count} κώνοι»
(nominative) but «Επιλέξατε {count} κώνους» (accusative); write each branch in the right case. Articles and adjectives
agree in gender, number and case: «1 νέα συνταγή», «2 νέες συνταγές»; «η αποθηκευμένη συνταγή σας», «οι αποθηκευμένες
συνταγές σας». Neuter nouns (υλικό, υάλωμα, οξείδιο, ψήσιμο) have the same nominative and accusative, which makes them
the safest in counted messages. A placeholder for a name (`{name}`) takes no ending; put an article or a noun before it
so the sentence does not have to inflect it: `το υλικό {name}`, `η συνταγή «{title}»`.

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Greek potters mostly fire by °C on a controller; Greek shops
sell cones by temperature (CC lists its «Πυρομετρικοί κώνοι» by °C: 954 … 1255), and ELKEA (p. 240) prints two
separate tables, «Πυρομετρικοί κώνοι Seger» and «Πυρομετρικοί κώνοι Orton». The Seger series (06a, 6a …) is a
different scale whose numbers are not Orton's. So:

- "cone 6" → «κώνος 6»; "to cone 6", "at cone 6" → «στον κώνο 6»; "cone 06" → «κώνος 06» (the zero stays; read
  «μηδέν έξι»; κώνος 06 is far cooler than κώνος 6). Lower-case _κώνος_ (ο, pl. οι κώνοι, acc. τους κώνους).
- "fire to cone 6" → «ψήστε στον κώνο 6»; "a cone 6 glaze" → «υάλωμα για τον κώνο 6»; "cone 5–6" → «κώνοι 5–6»;
  "cone 04 bisque" → «μπισκουί στον κώνο 04» (the firing: «ψήσιμο μπισκουί στον κώνο 04»).
- "Orton cone" → «κώνος Orton» (pl. «κώνοι Orton»; Orton in Latin letters and never inflected, as ELKEA writes
  «ΚΩΝΟΙ ORTON»); "pyrometric cone" → «πυρομετρικός κώνος» (CC, ELKEA). At the first mention on a page, and wherever
  systems are compared: «κώνος Orton 6», then «κώνος 6».
- **Never** write «κώνος Seger 6» or «6a» for an Orton number. «κώνοι Seger» appears only where the English says
  "Seger cones", and then means the Seger series.
- Where the guides give °C beside a cone, keep both: Greek readers know the temperature better than the number.

**The unity formula.** ELKEA's name is «μοριακός τύπος Seger» (ο), explained there as the oxides that «ανάγονται εις την
μονάδα» (p. 80, which glosses it "UNITY FORMULA"); the same book also says «φόρμουλα Seger» and «μέθοδος Seger». Write
**«μοριακός τύπος Seger (UMF)»** at its first use on each page or guide section, then «μοριακός τύπος Seger». Seger
stays in Latin letters, uninflected («του μοριακού τύπου Seger»). The app's short forms: "Unity formula" → «Μοριακός
τύπος Seger»; in tight labels and columns "Unity:" → «Τύπος Seger:»; "in unity" → «στον μοριακό τύπο Seger». Do not
calque «ενιαίος μοριακός τύπος».

**Gender-neutral wording.** Greek nouns, adjectives and participles have gender. MS: the generic masculine is
understood to cover everyone, but prefer the plural, the passive or the second person plural, and never use gendered
pronouns for "the user". So:

- Address the reader (_εσείς_) instead of describing them; avoid «ο χρήστης».
- Never let an adjective or participle agree with the reader: "Are you sure?" → «Θέλετε σίγουρα να …;», not «Είστε
  σίγουρος/η;»; "Welcome back" → «Καλώς ήρθατε ξανά» (no agreement).
- For "potter" use the plural or a clause: «όσοι κάνουν κεραμική», «οι κεραμίστες» (the generic masculine plural is
  normal Greek); where one person must be named, «ο κεραμίστας ή η κεραμίστρια», sparingly. No slashes («ο/η», «-ος/-η»)
  and no symbols.
- Grammatical gender of things is unaffected.

**The reader in the guides.** _Εσείς_ and the plural imperative, in short plain sentences, as the English:
«Κοσκινίστε το υάλωμα δύο φορές από κόσκινο 80 mesh.», «Ζυγίστε κάθε υλικό σε ζυγαριά που δείχνει 0,01 g.». Generic
"you" stays the second person: "the clay you use" → «ο πηλός που χρησιμοποιείτε». Avoid the impersonal «κανείς» and
nominal style («Η διενέργεια του κοσκινίσματος γίνεται …»). Quotations from sources are translated into «…» and keep
their figures as the English writes them.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with its capital. Where the English wraps the name in `<b>`
or another tag, keep the tag and add no quotation marks. Where it has no tag, put the button's name in «…» when the
reader is told to use it (`Πατήστε «Σύγκριση».`), and otherwise use the ordinary noun with an article («Η σύγκριση
δείχνει …»). "Settings" is never in quotes: «στις Ρυθμίσεις». The fixed names:

**EU directives and regulations.** As the EU's Greek texts write them: «Οδηγία 84/500/ΕΟΚ» (ΕΟΚ = Ευρωπαϊκή Οικονομική
Κοινότητα; lower-case «οδηγία» inside a sentence), amended by «Οδηγία 2005/31/ΕΚ»; «Οδηγία (ΕΕ) 2017/2398»;
«Κανονισμός (ΕΚ) αριθ. 1272/2008» (CLP). The letters are Greek capitals (Ε, Ο, Κ), not the Latin look-alikes,
which look the same but break search and screen readers.

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **Glaze**: «υάλωμα» (το, pl. υαλώματα) is the app's word (Zanias «Υαλώματα κεραμικής», CC «Υαλώματα-Σμάλτα», ELKEA,
  TY). Shops and potters also say «σμάλτο» (Ramfos «Σμάλτα»), but «σμάλτο» is also enamel on metal (Zanias sells «Σμάλτα
  αργυροχρυσοχοΐας» separately) and tooth enamel. Studio talk says «γυαλί», «γυάλωμα», «γυαλώνω» (KH, Ramfos «Τσιμπίδες
  Γυαλώματος»): understood, too casual for the app. Never «βερνίκι» (varnish) or «λούστρο» (lustre).
- **Silica** is «πυριτικό» (ELKEA: «Πυριτικό», «ελεύθερο πυριτικό»; Zanias «Πυριτικό»), formally «διοξείδιο του
  πυριτίου». Ramfos and CC label the bag «Πυρίτιο», which is the element silicon: recognise it, do not copy it. Silicone
  is «σιλικόνη».
- **Zircon** is «πυριτικό ζιρκόνιο» (ZrSiO₄); Ramfos and CC sell it as «Ζιρκόνιο (Zircopax)», which is the element.
  Zirconia (ZrO₂) is «οξείδιο του ζιρκονίου».
- **Σόδα** in everyday Greek is baking soda or soda water, **ποτάσα** caustic potash or potassium carbonate. The
  potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are «νάτριο (Na₂O)» and «κάλιο (K₂O)»; shops add «Σόδα» and «Ποτάσα» to the
  feldspar names (Ramfos «Άστριος (Φελσπάτο) Καλίου – Ποτάσα»).
- **Μπισκότο** is also a biscuit (food), and ELKEA uses it for bisque; the app writes «μπισκουί» (shops, studios, and
  ELKEA p. 151), so the kiln "cookie" (2.4) never becomes «μπισκότο».
- **Αναγωγή** is reduction firing (2.4). Do not use it for scaling a recipe (2.8: «Αλλαγή κλίμακας»).
- **Βασικός** in glaze chemistry means basic, alkaline: ELKEA calls the RO group «βασικά οξείδια». So "base glaze" is
  «υάλωμα βάσης», never «βασικό υάλωμα», and the app's "Standard" materials are «τυπικά», never «βασικά».
- **Όρια** (the limit formula, ELKEA's «πίνακες ορίων») is not **οριακή τιμή** (a legal exposure or migration limit,
  2.7). Keep them apart.
- **Εισπνεύσιμο** (inhalable) is not **αναπνεύσιμο** (respirable): EU and Cypriot law use both, for different dust
  fractions.
- **Αναπνευστήρας** is how some SDS translate "respirator", but it is also a snorkel and a medical ventilator. Write
  «μάσκα προστασίας της αναπνοής» (2.7).
- **Κρακελάρισμα** is the crazing defect (ELKEA); **κρακελέ** is crackle made on purpose (Zanias «Φρίτα, διάφανη,
  κρακελέ»).
- **Τράβηγμα** is crawling (ELKEA «Τραβήγματα των υαλωμάτων κατά την έψηση»), but potters also say clay «τραβάει» when
  it stiffens as it dries (Slang), and **μάζεμα** is shrinkage (Slang), not crawling.
- **Στίγματα** in shop names are speckles added on purpose (Zanias «πηλός με στίγματα»); dark spots as a defect are
  «μαύρα στίγματα» or «κηλίδες» (ELKEA).
- **Φαγεντιανή** is faience (tin-glazed earthenware); studios call white earthenware clay «φαγιάνς» (Slang). Neither
  translates "earthenware" in general (2.3).
- **Εύφλεκτο** is the CLP word for "flammable"; "combustible" (anything that can burn) is «καύσιμο» (2.7).
- Spelling varies on bags: Βολαστονίτης / Βολοστονίτης / βολλαστονίτης, Μπεντονίτης / Μπετονίτης, ο καολίνης / η
  καολίνη, Τσίγγος / τσίγκος / ψευδάργυρος. Use the spelling in the glossary every time.
- **Regional variants to avoid**: Cypriot dialect forms, and Greek colloquialisms that do not travel to Cyprus (MS).
  Write the standard language, monotonic, with the dictionary spelling where usage varies («φλιτζάνι», not
  «φλυτζάνι»).

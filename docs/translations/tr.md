# Türkçe: stil kılavuzu ve terimler sözlüğü (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Turkish (`tr`), standard written Turkish (TDK's spelling) for potters in Türkiye. Read
[README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Turkish message
and guide follows this page: one Turkish word for one thing. Türkiye's region fires by temperature and shows specific
gravity by default, with Orton cones, SK cones and Baumé a setting away; Turkish shops call Orton cones "Seger
piramidi", so the Turkish pages must keep Orton, SK and German Seger cones apart everywhere, and keep the Seger formula
(the unity formula) apart from all three. The terms come from the shops that sell glaze materials in Türkiye (the names
printed on the bag), from the Ministry of National Education's vocational module on glazing and firing (MEB), from
Turkish university ceramics courses and papers, from the Turkish regulations on mevzuat.gov.tr, and from the Microsoft
Turkish style guide, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was read, the Notes say so: those rows
are for a Turkish potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Turkish has a full ceramics vocabulary, used by the shops, the MEB
modules, the fine-arts faculties (Hacettepe, Anadolu, Kütahya Dumlupınar, Nevşehir) and the tile and sanitaryware
industry, so most material and process rows rest on usage that was read. The textbooks the courses cite (Arcasoy &
Başkırkan, _Seramik Teknolojisi_; Genç, _Artistik Seramik Sırları_) are printed books and were not read; the web search
reached few Turkish pages. Rows marked **Usage** are the words Turkish potters and the industry use, read in no single
source today; rows marked **No source** are best choices with no Turkish source at all (mostly the kiln-sitter parts and
the firing-log column labels, which belong to American kilns). The rows a Turkish potter should check first are marked
**Native check**: piramit (cone), stoneware, toprak seramik (earthenware), kontrol ünitesi (controller), bekletme (hold),
kavlama (shivering), salım (leaching), iç sır (liner glaze), askıda tutucu (suspender), tartım (batch), dip kazıma
(trimming) and destek macunu (wadding).

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

**Siz**, as Turkish software uses it (MS 4.1.13: "formal 'you' (siz) is used in general context"), and as the MEB
modules address the student ("Sırı, 200 mikronluk elekten geçiriniz."). Never _sen_.

- **Commands** (buttons, menu items, links that do something): the **bare verb stem**, the form of every Turkish
  interface: “Kaydet”, “Yazdır”, “Karşılaştır”, “Hesabımı sil”. MS: "Kaydet düğmesine … basın". Not the -In form
  (“Kaydedin”) on a button, not a noun (“Kaydetme”).
- **Instructions** in guides and help: the polite imperative **-In** (-in, -ın, -un, -ün), the form of MS's and the
  shops' instructions: “Sırrı iki kez elekten geçirin.”, “Toz maskesi takın.”, “Tozu solumayın.” The MEB modules use the
  longer -InIz (“geçiriniz”); in the app use -In, which reads as current and no less polite. Do not mix the two.
- **Statements about the reader** ("if you fire to cone 6", "your clay"): the -InIz endings and possessives: “6 numaralı
  piramide kadar pişiriyorsanız”, “çamurunuz”. The pronoun _siz_ itself only where it is stressed.
- **Questions and confirmations**: “Reçete silinsin mi?”, “Değişiklikler kaydedilsin mi?” (the passive question), or
  “… silmek istiyor musunuz?” where the English asks the reader.
- **Errors**: as MS: “İsteğiniz işlenemedi. Lütfen daha sonra yeniden deneyin.”, “Reçete bulunamadı.” "please" →
  “lütfen” only where the English has it. "we" → the first person plural (MS: “Bir eşleşme bulamadık.”).

Examples: button “Kopya olarak kaydet”; error “Bu e-posta adresi zaten kullanılıyor.”; guide sentence “Sırrı karıştırmadan
önce suyun içinde bekletin.”

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- Buttons and menu commands: the bare stem, as above: “Kopya olarak kaydet”, “Hammadde ekle”, “Denemeyi sil”.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: “Reçeteler”, “Ayarlar”, “Pişirim
  kayıtları”, “Piramitler ve ısı işi”. A heading that names an activity takes the -mA or -mAk noun: "Mixing the glaze" →
  “Sırrın karıştırılması” or “Sırrı karıştırmak”; choose -mA (“Sırrın hazırlanması”), as the MEB modules do (“Fırın
  Doldurmada Dikkat Edilecek Hususlar”). A heading that is a question stays one.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: “Hammaddenin adı:”.
- Confirmations: “Kaydedildi: …” for "Saved: …".

### Capitalisation

TDK and MS 4.1.5: the first word of a sentence, heading, button or list item and proper names take a capital; nothing
else. No English Title Case: "Fired Weight" → “Pişmiş ağırlık”. Month and day names are lower case except in a full date
(TDK writes “14 Ekim 2026”, with the month capitalised in a date); languages and nationalities are capitalised (“Türkçe”,
“Alman”). Trade and product names keep their own form (Orton, Seger, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc).

**İ and I.** Turkish has two i's: dotted i/İ and dotless ı/I. In capitals: “İSTANBUL”, “KUVARS”, “SIR”, “BİSKÜVİ”,
never “ISTANBUL” or “SİR”. Write capitals by hand where the English has them (MS: automatic case conversion corrupts
Turkish i and ı). Text the app sets in capitals by CSS (the landing page's small headings) is cased by the page's
`lang="tr"`, which browsers follow (i → İ); type it in normal case. A short form (abbreviation) keeps its own capitals
(UZEM, ÇSGB, GBF, KOAH).

### Suffixes after names, numbers and placeholders

Turkish attaches case and possessive endings to the word. Three rules:

- **An apostrophe** (the typographic ’, U+2019; never a straight ' just before `{`, `}` or `#`, see the README)
  separates a suffix from a proper name, a number, a symbol or an abbreviation (TDK): “Türkiye’de”, “Orton’un”,
  “1222 °C’ye”, “%5’in”, “FFP2’yi”, “UZEM’i”, “112’yi arayın”. No apostrophe on common nouns (“sırda”, “fırına”).
- **Vowel harmony follows how the figure or abbreviation is read**: “1222 °C’ye” (santigrat derece**ye**), “06’ya”
  (altı**ya**), “10 g’dan” (gram**dan**), “SK 6a’da” (a), “FFP3’ü” (üç). Where this is hard, give the suffix to a noun
  instead and leave the figure bare: “1222 °C sıcaklığa kadar”, “6 numaralı piramide”.
- **A placeholder never takes a suffix** (MS: "Content of the placeholder is unknown, therefore no suffixes are
  allowed"): {region}, {name}, {count}, {library}, {value} could end in any vowel. Put a generic noun beside the
  placeholder and give it the suffix: "in {region}" → “{region} ülkesinde” or “bulunduğunuz ülkede ({region})”; "to
  {name}" → “{name} adlı reçeteye”; "{count} materials" → “{count} hammadde”; "from {library}" → “{library}
  kütüphanesinden”. A trade name works the same way where its reading is unsure: “Ferro 3134 fritine”.

### Punctuation

- Quotation marks: “…” (U+201C, U+201D), as TDK and Turkish publishers write them; inside them ‘…’. MS allows straight
  quotes in software, but typographic ones are safe in every file and read better. Use them for real quotations only; a
  button or page name is not quoted (“Karşılaştır düğmesine basın”).
- Apostrophe: ’ (U+2019), as above.
- No space before ; : ! ?. One space after a full stop.
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces (MS: “Sayfa 3-5”), the unit once at the end: “1222–1240 °C”, “06–04 numaralı
  piramitler”, “%1–3”. A dash in a sentence: rewrite with a comma, a colon or brackets (MS: no em dash in Turkish).
- Ellipsis: three points, no space before (“Kaydediliyor...”).
- Compounds are written as two words or one, without a hyphen (MS 4.1.14): “sır altı boya”, “sırlı pişirim”.

### Numbers and units

As the app formats Turkish (Intl, `tr`), and as the numbers check reads it:

- Decimal comma: “1,5”, “0,01 g”, “1,45”. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: the app prints four-digit numbers ungrouped (“1222 °C”, `useGrouping: 'min2'` in
  `client/app/shared/format.ts`), so write **four-digit numbers ungrouped** (“1240 °C”, “1000 g”) and group five digits
  and more with a **point** (“12.500 g”), as Turkish does. Never a space between groups.
- **Percent before the number**, no space: “%5”, “%0,5”, “%10’u”. A range takes the sign once, before it: “%10–60”
  (the check reads it as the English's 10–60%). Never “5%” or “%10–%60”.
- A space before °C, °F and every other unit: “1240 °C”, “100 °C/h”, “80 mesh”, “10 g”. Degrees of angle take none.
- A number in the English is a number in the Turkish, with the same symbol: “1240 °C’de”, not “1240 derecede”.
- A number the English writes as a word stays a word: "two coats" → “iki kat”, "three tiles" → “üç karo”. The check
  reads no number there, so a digit would be a figure the English lacks.
- **Dates**: the day, the month by name, the year: “14 Ekim 2026”, “Ekim 2026”. Never a numeric date (14.10.2026): the
  check matches the month's name to the English's. Months: Ocak, Şubat, Mart, Nisan, Mayıs, Haziran, Temmuz, Ağustos,
  Eylül, Ekim, Kasım, Aralık.
- **Times** on the 24-hour clock, after “saat” with a point, as TDK writes them: "8pm" → “saat 20.00”, "8:30pm" →
  “saat 20.30”, "8am to 5pm" → “saat 8.00 ile saat 17.00 arasında” (“saat” before each time: the check reads a dotted
  time only after it, and “17.00” alone as a decimal) or “saat 8’den 17’ye kadar”. **Never “20:00”**: the check reads it
  as two numbers, 20 and 00. Never “akşam 8” or “öğleden sonra 8”.
- Official names with digits are names: 112, 114, “112 Acil Çağrı Merkezi”, “ALO 114” where the English writes it: keep
  them whole, never as words. A generic term ("the poison line") is translated as words: “zehir danışma hattı”.
- Inches and feet: “inç” (2 inç), “fit” (2 fit), with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives it.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Turkish two categories:
`node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('tr').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"` prints `[ 'one', 'other' ]`.

| Category | Numbers                  | Example                                     |
| -------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| `one`    | 1                        | 1 hammadde, 1 reçete                        |
| `other`  | 0, 2–20, 21, 101, 1,5, … | 2 hammadde, 5 hammadde, 21 reçete, 1,5 gram |

**A noun after a number stays singular** (TDK; MS: "the following word should not be in plural form as in source"):
“2 hammadde”, “5 reçete”, never “2 hammaddeler”. So both forms usually read the same; the check still wants both, and
`=0` stays where the English has it:

```
{count, plural, =0 {Hammadde yok} one {# hammadde} other {# hammadde}}
```

The suffix goes on the noun, never on `#` or `{count}`: “# hammaddeyle”, “# reçetede”, “# satır eklendi”. A numeral
subject takes a singular verb: “3 hammadde eksik”.

### Cones

The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Turkish shops sell them as **“Seger piramidi”**: Tecnodieci's product is
“Seger Piramidi (Orton)”, described as “SEGER PİRAMİDİ (Orton Marka)”. In Turkish, “Seger piramidi” is the common name
for any pyrometric cone, whoever made it; it does not mean the German Seger series. MEB's module fires by the controller's
temperature only and names no cone. So:

- The generic word is **piramit** (pl. piramitler), the shops' word; "pyrometric cone" → “pirometrik piramit” where the
  English stresses it. Not “koni” alone (also the geometric and traffic cone) and not “külah”. **Native check**: some
  textbooks write “Seger konisi”; this page uses piramit throughout.
- "cone 6" → “6 numaralı piramit”; short labels and table cells “Piramit 6”. "cone 06" → “06 numaralı piramit” (the zero
  stays; 06 is far cooler than 6). The noun takes the suffix and the number stays bare: "fire to cone 6" → “6 numaralı
  piramide kadar pişirin”; "a cone 6 glaze" → “6 numaralı piramit sırrı”; "cone 5–6" → “5–6 numaralı piramitler”.
- **Orton cones are named as Orton** wherever the English says Orton, and at the first numbered cone in each section of a
  guide (a plain "cone" with no number stays “piramit”): “Orton piramidi”, “6 numaralı Orton piramidi”, then “6
  numaralı piramit”. This adds "Orton" where the English names no maker, on purpose: the reader's shop calls any cone
  “Seger piramidi”. The shops' word stands beside Orton only once in a guide, in brackets, where a potter would look
  for it: “Orton piramitleri (dükkânlarda “Seger piramidi” olarak satılır)”.
- **SK cones** are “SK piramitleri” with the number as printed: “SK 6a”, “SK 015a”, “SK 20”. Where the English names the
  standard, “SK piramitleri (JIS R 8101)”; nowhere else, since the check would reject 8101 where the English lacks it.
- **German Seger cones** are named Seger only where the English says Seger ("Seger and Staffordshire cones, the older
  European series"): “Seger ve Staffordshire piramitleri, eski Avrupa serileri”. Say "the German Seger series" (“Alman
  Seger serisi”) where the text could be read as the shops' generic “Seger piramidi”.
- **“Seger formülü”** is the unity formula, never a cone (see below).
- Never give an SK number for an Orton number or the reverse, nor any equivalence, in a row, a note or an example.
- "one number hotter" / "one number cooler" → “bir numara daha sıcak” / “bir numara daha soğuk”: 05 is one number cooler
  than 04. Never “yüksek/düşük” or “büyük/küçük”, which the leading zero turns upside down.
- The "o'clock" bend positions: “saat 4 konumu” (the figure stays).

### The unity formula

**“Seger formülü”**, with “(UMF)” after it the first time on a page: “Seger formülü (UMF)”. Kartal and the Nevşehir
courses write “Seger formülü” (“Seger formülü bilinen sır reçetelerinin hesaplanması”). After the first mention “Seger
formülü”; the app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are “Seger formülü” and “Seger formülü:”.
It names a formula, never a cone; the cone section above keeps the two apart.

### Gender-neutral wording

Turkish has no grammatical gender and one pronoun, _o_, for everyone, so most sentences need nothing. Follow MS's
inclusive-language section: plural nouns for generalisations (“seramikçiler”, “yeni başlayanlar”), no _bay/bayan_ titles,
and job words that are not gendered (“seramik sanatçısı”, not “usta” with a gendered sense). A potter is “seramikçi”;
“çömlekçi” is a wheel potter making earthenware, used only where the English means that.

### The reader in the guides

The polite imperative (-In) for every step, in short plain sentences as the English: “Sırrı 80 meshlik elekten iki kez
geçirin.” The -InIz possessives where the English says "your": “çamurunuz”, “fırınınız”. No _sen_, no nominal style
(“Eleme işleminin gerçekleştirilmesi …”). Quotations from sources are translated into “…” and keep their figures as the
English writes them.

**No added emphasis.** “mutlaka”, “kesinlikle”, “her zaman” and “asla” only where the English has must, always, never or
the like. Keep the English's strength:

- "must" → “-mAlI” or “zorunludur” (“Bardak test edilmelidir.”); "must be tested" means the test must be done, not
  passed: “test ettirilmelidir”, not “testi geçmelidir”.
- "should" → “… önerilir” or “-mAlI” softened with “gerekir” only where the English is a rule; the advice of the guides
  is “… önerilir” or “… daha iyidir”. "should not" → “önerilmez” or “-mAmAlI”. Where the English reports a source's
  figure ("Rio Grande says at least 12 inches"), keep it a report: “… en az 12 inç öneriyor”.
- "may", "can" (a hedge) → “-Abilir”: “zararlı olabilir”. Never drop it.
- "some" (some fine quartz) → “bir miktar” only where it means an amount; “bazı” for some of several. Not “biraz” (a
  little).

### Law in Türkiye

Türkiye is not in the EU, but its regulations follow the EU's, in Turkish, on [mevzuat.gov.tr](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr)
and in the Resmî Gazete (RG). Name them as they name themselves, and keep every number, date and RG reference exactly:
“Tozla Mücadele Yönetmeliği (RG 05.11.2013/28812)”, “Türk Gıda Kodeksi Gıda ile Temas Eden Seramik Malzemeler Tebliği
(Tebliğ No: 2012/30)”, “Kişisel Koruyucu Donanım Yönetmeliği (RG 01.05.2019/30761)”.

Where the English names EU law, use the name the Turkish regulations give it, keeping the number: Directive 84/500/EEC →
“84/500/EEC sayılı Konsey Direktifi” (as the Tebliğ calls it: “84/500/EEC sayılı Gıda ile Temas Eden Seramik Malzemeler
Hakkında Konsey Direktifi”); Directive (EU) 2017/2398 → “(AB) 2017/2398 sayılı Direktif”; Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008
(CLP) → “(AT) 1272/2008 sayılı Tüzük (CLP)”; Regulation (EU) 2016/425 → “(AB) 2016/425 sayılı Tüzük”. Keep “EEC” in
84/500/EEC as the Tebliğ does; AB and AT are the Turkish abbreviations of EU and EC.

The law's words, with the English they translate:

- "at most", "no more than", "not exceed" → **“en fazla”**, or the law's own “… geçemez” (Tebliğ), “… üstünde olamaz”
  (Tozla). The limit itself passes. **Never “-den az” or “altında”**, which mean "less than", "under" (the limit fails).
  "or less" → “veya daha az”. "less than", "under", "below" → “-den az”, “altında”. "over", "more than", "exceeds" →
  “üzerinde”, “-den fazla”, “aşan”. "at least" → “en az”. A slip here moves a legal limit.
- The food-contact categories in the Tebliğ's words, without its numbers (the English names the kinds of piece, not
  "Category 1", so “Kategori 1” would be a figure the English lacks): flat or not over 25 mm deep “doldurulamayan veya
  derinliği en fazla 25 mm olan”, other fillable “doldurulabilen diğer”, and “pişirme kapları; 3 litreden daha fazla
  kapasiteye sahip paketleme ve saklama kapları”. Cooking ware is **“pişirme kapları”**; the 3 L applies only to packaging and storage
  vessels. Lead and cadmium release is “kurşun ve kadmiyum migrasyonu” (the law) or “kurşun ve kadmiyum salımı” (plain
  text); see leaching (2.5).
- Respirable dust “solunabilir toz”; respirable quartz “solunabilir kuvars” (Tozla: “Kuvars (Solunabilir)”);
  crystalline silica “kristal silika” (the regulations: “kristal yapıda SiO2”). The 8-hour time-weighted average is
  “8 saatlik zaman ağırlıklı ortalama” (ZAOD). An exposure limit is “maruziyet sınır değeri”.
- The silica formula keeps its placeholders and figures (`guides.silica.formulaValue` as it is).
- Bodies: Sağlık Bakanlığı; Ulusal Zehir Danışma Merkezi (UZEM), 114; 112 Acil Çağrı Merkezi; Çalışma ve Sosyal
  Güvenlik Bakanlığı, İş Sağlığı ve Güvenliği Genel Müdürlüğü (ÇSGB İSGGM). Where the English names a foreign body (HSE,
  OSHA, the FDA), keep the name and say whose it is: “Birleşik Krallık’ın İş Sağlığı ve Güvenliği Kurumu (HSE)”.

### Pitfalls

- **Sır** is a glaze (Pega, Tecnodieci, MEB); **emaye** is enamel on metal; **vernik** is varnish; **cila** is polish.
  Never emaye or vernik for glaze. “sır” also means "a secret": the context settles it; “seramik sırrı” where it does not.
  Before a vowel it doubles the r: sırrı, sırrın, sırra, sırrımız, sırrıyla; before a consonant it does not: sırda,
  sırdan, sırlar. Never “sırı”, “sırın” or “sıra” for the glaze: “sıra” is a row or a turn, and the reviews found all
  three. Some sources quoted on this page write “sırın”; the app does not.
- **Frit** is a frit (shops, Kartal: “Frit … şoklama ile elde edilen … cam ürün”); **ergitici** is a flux. Never one for
  the other. Tecnodieci's “Flux/TD-500” is a trade name.
- **Fırın** is a kiln and a kitchen oven; write “seramik fırını” where the context does not settle it, and “mutfak
  fırını” for the oven. **Pişirim** is a firing (MEB); a fire as a hazard is “yangın”, flames “alev”. "to fire" → “pişirmek”.
- **Bisküvi** is bisque ware and the shops' word for it (Tecnodieci “Bisküvi Ürünler”); “bisküvi pişirimi” the bisque
  firing. Not the sweet biscuit; the firing guide's "cookie" is kiln furniture (2.4).
- **Toprak seramik / çömlek / fayans / pişmiş toprak**: earthenware in general is “toprak seramik” (**Native check**;
  MEB's sources write “toprak seramik”); “çömlek” is a wheel-made earthenware pot; “fayans” is wall tile and tin-glazed
  faience; “pişmiş toprak” is unglazed red ware (terracotta). Stoneware is “stoneware” as the shops sell it
  (Tecnodieci “Stoneware Vakum Çamuru”), explained once as “yüksek ısıda pişen, camlaşmaya yakın bünye” (**Native
  check**).
- **Kalsit** is whiting (calcium carbonate: Pega KALSİT, Tecnodieci “Kalsiyum Karbonat (Kalsit)”); **kireç** is lime
  (calcium oxide or hydroxide); **tebeşir** is chalk. Never kireç for whiting.
- **Soda** in the shops is sodium carbonate (Tecnodieci “Sodyum Karbonat (Soda)”) and also sodium bicarbonate (Pega
  “SODA (SODYUM Bİ KARBONAT)”); the potters' oxide "soda" is “sodyum (Na₂O)”. **Potas** is potassium carbonate; the oxide
  "potash" is “potasyum (K₂O)”.
- **Silis / silisyum / silikon**: the oxide SiO₂ is “silis” (silisyum dioksit) and the material “kuvars”; “silisyum” is
  the element; “silikon” the rubber. The disease is “silikoz”.
- **Respiratör** in Turkish is also a hospital ventilator. A respirator is **“solunum koruyucu maske”** in every file;
  “toz maskesi” only where the English says "dust mask", and the "nuisance dust" masks that are not respirators are
  “rahatsız edici toz” maskeleri. **Havalandırma** is ventilation (a room, a kiln vent), never the mask.
- **Kılavuz** vs **rehber**: the app's five guides are **rehber** (“Sırlama rehberi”, “Rehberler”) in every link, page
  title and list; a kiln's or controller's own manual is **kullanım kılavuzu**. Never one for the other.
- **The five guides' titles**, one each, the same in the guide's `title:`, `titles` in `tr.json`, the list in
  `guides/tr.json` and every link that names a guide: Glazing basics → **Sırlamanın temel ilkeleri**; Making a glaze →
  **Sır nasıl hazırlanır**; Safe mixing and ventilation → **Güvenli karıştırma ve havalandırma**; Firing a basic kiln
  → **Basit bir fırında pişirim**; Don't poison your family → **Ailenizi zehirlemeyin**. A link's text is the bare
  title, with any ending outside the brackets: “[Sır nasıl hazırlanır](/guides/making-a-glaze) rehberine bakın”.
- **Astar** is engobe (the shops' category “Astarlar”); **çamur** is clay body and slip (“döküm çamuru”, casting slip);
  **sır altı boya** is underglaze. Keep the three apart.
- **Yoğunluk** is density in general and the MEB's word for a glaze's thickness (“sırrın yoğunluğu”); **özgül ağırlık** is
  specific gravity, the ratio the app shows; **litre ağırlığı** is the weight of a litre of slurry in g/L. See 2.6.
- **Placeholders and trade names cannot take suffixes**: see "Suffixes after names, numbers and placeholders".
- **Regional and spoken forms to avoid**: spoken contractions (“yapıcaz”), _sen_ forms, Ottoman words where a current
  one exists (MS 4.1.10: "Turkish old-style words give a rather formal and unnecessarily serious tone").

# Gaeilge: stílbhileog agus gluais (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Irish (`ga`), in the Official Standard (An Caighdeán Oifigiúil), for potters in Ireland.
Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Irish
message and guide follows this page: one Irish word for one thing. Ireland's region lists English first, so most readers
of the Irish pages chose Irish on purpose: teachers, pupils and students of Gaelscoileanna and the Gaeltacht, and potters
who work through Irish. They learned their terms at school and from téarma.ie, so where téarma.ie has a term in its
Ceramics, Chemistry, Physics or Art domains, that term is used before a general one, and the Notes say so. Where the
Caighdeán and the dialects differ, the Caighdeán wins. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those
rows are for an Irish-speaking potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** No shop, kiln maker or potters' guild in Ireland publishes in Irish: DBI
Pottery Supplies and RPM Supplies (`lib/regions/suppliers.js`) sell in English, Nabertherm's manuals come in Croatian
and Estonian but not Irish, and web search found no Irish-language page by a potter, an art school or a Gaeltacht craft
workshop, and no Irish school text on glazes (the NCCA's Leaving Certificate Art specification in Irish has no glaze or
kiln vocabulary). So the words below rest on the national term bank, téarma.ie, whose Ceramics domain (about 290 entries:
glónra, friot, flosc, áith, cón piriméadrach, brioscbhácáil, snáithlíonrú, snámh, díghloiniú and more) was built for art
education; on the Irish texts of EU law and the Health and Safety Authority's Irish leaflet for safety; on Foras na
Gaeilge's dictionaries; and on Irish Wikipedia's pottery articles, which come from Matt Hussey's encyclopedia _Fréamh an
Eolais_ (Coiscéim, 2011). Rows marked **No source** are coinages from those parts. The kiln-control, firing-log and
defect rows rest most on one source each, and a native potter should read them first.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

Formal, as public bodies and Irish software write: standard vocabulary, whole sentences, no slang and no dialect forms.
Irish has no formal pronoun: _sibh_ is only the plural, and no Irish software or public body addresses one reader with
it. MS (2.2) addresses the reader as _tú_, with the imperative singular, and so does the EU's own Irish text of the
hazard warnings (CLP: “Caith cosaint riospráide.”, “Cuir glao ar IONAD NIMHE nó ar dhoctúir/lia má bhraitheann tú
tinn.”). Glazecalc does the same:

- **Commands** (buttons, menu items, links that do something): the **imperative singular**, verb first: “Sábháil”,
  “Priontáil”, “Cuir i gcomparáid”, “Scrios mo chuntas”. Not a verbal-noun phrase (“An t-oideas a shábháil”), not the plural
  (“Sábhálaigí”).
- **Instructions** in guides and help: the imperative singular, in plain words: “Criathraigh an glónra faoi dhó trí
  chriathar 80 mesh.”, “Caith masc análaitheora agus tú ag meascadh púdair thirim.”
- **Statements about the reader** ("if you fire to cone 6", "your clay"): _tú_ and _do_ as the English has them: “Má
  bhácálann tú go cón 6 …”, “do chré féin”.
- **System messages and errors**: the autonomous form, _ní féidir_ or _theip ar_, as MS 3.1.12 and 4.5.1 show (“Ní
  féidir an comhad a shábháil.”, “Theip ar an nasc.”): “Sábháladh an t-oideas.”, “Níorbh fhéidir an t-oideas a
  shábháil. Bain triail eile as.”, “Ní féidir é seo a chealú.”
- **Questions and confirmations**: “An bhfuil tú cinnte gur mian leat an t-oideas seo a bhaint?”
- "please": leave it out (MS 2.1.1: Irish is not curt without it); write “le do thoil” only where the English insists.
  "we", where the English speaks as the app's makers: keep it (“Níor aimsíomar …”, “Molaimid …”).

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- Buttons and menu commands: the imperative singular, as above: “Sábháil mar chóip”, “Cuir ábhair leis”, “Scrios an
  triail”.
- Options in which the reader speaks to the app keep the first person: "Match with what I have" → “Meaitseáil leis a
  bhfuil agam”.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun phrase** or a **verbal-noun phrase**: “Oidis”, “Socruithe”,
  “Taifid bhácála”, “Cóin agus obair teasa”; "Mixing the glaze" → “An glónra a mheascadh”; "How to make a glaze" →
  “Conas glónra a dhéanamh”. A heading that is a question stays one.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: “Ainm an ábhair:”.
- Confirmations: the participle first, as the English: "Saved: …" → “Sábháilte: …”.

### Capitalisation

Sentence case everywhere (MS 3.1.5): only the first word and proper nouns take a capital, in headings, buttons, labels
and tabs too: "Fired Weight" → “Meáchan tar éis bácála”. Languages, nationalities, days and months take a capital
(“Gaeilge”, “Béarla”, “Éireannach”, “Luan”, “Deireadh Fómhair”); seasons do not. After a mutation the capital stays on
the word's own first letter: “na hÉireann”, “i nGaillimh”, “sa Ghearmáin”, “an tSín”; t- and n- take a hyphen only
before a lower-case vowel (“an t-ábhar”, “na n-ábhar”, but “An tÁbhar”). Trade names keep their own form (Orton, Seger,
Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc).

### Punctuation

- Quotation marks: “…” (U+201C, U+201D), and ‘…’ inside them (MS 3.1.15). Not straight "…", not „…“ or »…«.
- The name of a button, tab or page in running text goes in single quotes ‘…’ and keeps its form (MS 3.1.15: “Cliceáil
  ar an gcnaipe ‘Athnuaigh’”), with a generic noun before it that takes the case: “an cnaipe ‘Cuir i gcomparáid’”, “sa
  chluaisín ‘Caighdeánach’”. Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag and leave out the
  quotes.
- Apostrophe: always the typographic ’ (U+2019), which Irish needs often: “d’áith”, “m’oideas”, “d’fhéadfadh”. It is
  safe everywhere, also before `{`, so the `''` rule of the README never arises with it.
- No space before ; : ! ?; one space after (MS 3.1.15, as English).
- Ranges: the en dash with **no spaces**, the unit once at the end (MS 3.1.15: “lgh 19–23”): “1222–1240 °C”, “cón
  06–04”, “1–3%”. In a sentence “idir 1222 agus 1240 °C” or “ó 1222 go 1240 °C” is also fine; never mix the two. A dash
  in a sentence is the en dash with a space each side ( – ); no em dash.
- A ratio as the English writes it: “8.5:1”, “SiO₂:Al₂O₃”.
- Hyphens: an ordinary hyphen in “an t-ábhar”, “na n-ábhar”, “an-mhaith”, “ró-ard”. MS asks for a no-break hyphen after
  t- and n-, but that is for Word documents; on the web the plain hyphen is safe in every font.

### Numbers and units

- **Decimal point**, as Ireland writes numbers in Irish and English (MS 3.1.15: “In Irish a full stop (period) is used”;
  CLDR `ga`; the app shows `{{SG 1.45}}` and its own figures with a point for Irish): “1.5%”, “0.01 g”, “1.45”. Never a
  decimal comma, although the EU's Irish legal texts print one (CLP: “0,1 %”): the reader's app and school both use the
  point.
- **Thousands**: four-digit numbers stay together (“1222 °C”, “1000 g”, “1170 uair”), as the app prints them
  (`useGrouping: 'min2'` in `client/app/shared/format.ts`); larger ones take a comma, as in English (MS: “1,526”; the
  app prints “12,345.6”). `scripts/i18n-check.mjs` reads Irish numbers as it reads English (CLDR `ga`): a point for
  decimals (“0.025”, “1.000”), a comma for groups.
- A space before °C, °F and every unit symbol: “1222 °C”, “100 g”, “100 °C/h”, “80 mesh”. **No space before %**, as the
  English and CLDR `ga` write it: “15%”, “10–60%”.
- A number in the English is a number in the Irish, in figures, with the same symbol; never a word, and never a unit
  word for a symbol (“100 g”, never “100 gram”). Symbols take no mutation. A **noun** after a figure follows the number
  (see Plural forms): “3 bhrat”, “10 nóiméad”, “3 huaire an chloig”, “7 n-ábhar”, “20 uair an chloig”.
- Dates: “10 Deireadh Fómhair 2026” (day, month with its capital, year; no ordinal, no “mí”); in short form
  “10/10/2026”. The app writes its own dates.
- **Clock times**: the 24-hour clock, and whole hours as bare figures, because the check reads "8pm" as 20 and counts
  every figure: "8am to 10pm only" → “Ó 8 go 22 a chlog amháin”; "8am to 8pm" → “ó 8 go 20 a chlog”. Not “8 r.n. go 10
  i.n.” (the check reads 10, not 22) and not “8:00–22:00” (it finds two extra 00s). A time the English gives without am
  or pm ("8 to 5") keeps the English figures (“ó 8 go 5”).
- Inches and feet: “orlach” (m; pl. orlaí) and “troigh” (f; after 3–6 “troithe”, after 7–10 “dtroithe”, CO 9.3.2),
  the same in every file, with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives it: “1/4 orlach”, “2 orlach (51 mm)”,
  “18 n-orlach (46 cm)”, “3 troithe (91 cm)”, “25 troigh (7.6 m)”.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Irish five categories: `node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('ga').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"`
prints `[ 'one', 'two', 'few', 'many', 'other' ]`. The noun after a number is the **nominative singular**, mutated by
the number (CO 9.3.1, 9.3.8): 1–6 lenite, 7–10 eclipse; vowels take no lenition, and n- before them after 7–10. Figures
1–19 take the same forms as the words (CO 9.3.8: “3 bhád”, “7 n-oíche”); after 20 and up, the Simplified System of CO
9.3.12 keeps the noun singular and unmutated (“21 bád”, “37 ábhar”).

| Category | Numbers                             | ábhar (m, vowel) | breiseán (m, consonant) | Notes                               |
| -------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| `one`    | 1                                   | 1 ábhar          | 1 bhreiseán             | aon lenites (not d, t, s)           |
| `two`    | 2                                   | 2 ábhar          | 2 bhreiseán             | dhá lenites                         |
| `few`    | 3–6                                 | 3 ábhar          | 3 bhreiseán             | trí, ceithre, cúig, sé lenite       |
| `many`   | 7–10                                | 7 n-ábhar        | 7 mbreiseán             | seacht, ocht, naoi, deich eclipse   |
| `other`  | 0, 11–19, 20 and up, decimals (1.5) | 20 ábhar         | 20 breiseán             | the singular, unmutated (CO 9.3.12) |

- Write all five, even where they read alike:
  `{count, plural, one {# ábhar} two {# ábhar} few {# ábhar} many {# n-ábhar} other {# ábhar}}`;
  `{total, plural, one {# bhreiseán} two {# bhreiseán} few {# bhreiseán} many {# mbreiseán} other {# breiseán}}`.
  `=0 {…}`, `=1 {…}` and `#` stay as the English has them.
- `other` holds 11–19 too, which the Caighdeán mutates like 1–9 (“13 bhreiseán”, “17 n-ábhar”), and 20 and up, which it
  does not. One form must serve both: use the unmutated singular, right for 0 and 20 and up and the form MS (3.1.12)
  prescribes after any number placeholder. It is the compromise of this page; a native reader may prefer another.
- A few nouns take their own plural after 3–10 (CO 9.3.2): bliain (2 bhliain, 3 bliana, 7 mbliana), uair (2 uair, 3
  huaire, 8 n-uaire), seachtain (3 seachtaine), ceann (3 cinn, 7 gcinn), troigh (3 troithe). After 20, the singular: 20
  bliain, 20 uair.
- Irish verbs do not change with the number, so a plural branch changes only its noun: “Malartaíodh # ábhar.”, “Tá aird
  ag teastáil ó # réimse.” Keep adjectives out of the branch where you can (after 2–19 they go plural, CO 9.3.3).
- Where a number is not a plural argument (“up to {most} materials”), the noun after it is the unmutated singular (MS
  3.1.12): “Is liosta é an stoc ina bhfuil suas le {most} ábhar.”
- In a range the noun follows the last number: “6 go 10 dtíl tástála”, “4 go 5 nóiméad”, “2 nó 3 bhrat”. Where that
  reads badly, put the noun first: “tíleanna tástála (6 go 10)”.

### Initial mutations, placeholders and trade names

Irish changes the first letter of a word after the article, possessives, prepositions and numbers. Lenition adds h (b c
d f g m p s t → bh ch dh fh gh mh ph sh th); eclipsis puts a letter before (mb, gc, nd, bhf, ng, bp, dt, n- before a
vowel). The main triggers in these texts:

- The article: feminine nouns lenite (“an chré”, “an bhácáil”); masculine vowel nouns take t- (“an t-ábhar”, “an
  t-oideas”); the masculine genitive lenites (“dath an ghlónra”); the plural article puts h before a vowel (“na
  hábhair”), and the genitive plural eclipses (“na n-ábhar”, “na gcón”).
- Possessives: mo, do and a (his) lenite, m’ and d’ before a vowel (“do chuid ábhar”, “d’áith”); a (her) puts h before
  a vowel (“a háith”); ár, bhur, a (their) eclipse (“a n-áith”).
- Prepositions: ar, de, do, faoi, ó, roimh, thar, trí lenite (“ar chón 6”, “faoi theocht ard”, “trí chriathar”); i
  eclipses (“i gcón 6”; “in áith”); le and go put h before a vowel (“le haol”, “go hÉirinn”). With the article, follow
  the Caighdeán: **eclipsis** (“ar an mbácáil”, “leis an gcré”, “ag an gcón”), except “den”, “don” and “sa(n)”, which
  lenite (“sa chré”, “den ghlónra”, “don áith”). MS lets either system be chosen; this one is the Caighdeán's.
- Numbers: see Plural forms.

A **placeholder** (`{name}`, `{region}`, `{library}`, `{title}`) and a **trade name** (Ferro 3134, Gerstley Borate,
Glazecalc) cannot be mutated, so never put one straight after a word that mutates. Either give it a generic noun that
takes the mutation and let the name follow in apposition, or use a word order that needs none:

| Avoid                       | Write                                   | Why                                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| i {name}                    | san oideas {name}                       | the noun takes the mutation                 |
| le {name} / do {name}       | leis an ábhar {name} / don ábhar {name} | le puts h before a vowel; do lenites        |
| anailís {name} (genitive)   | anailís an ábhair {name}                | the noun takes the genitive                 |
| ar {name}                   | ar an ábhar {name}                      |                                             |
| Bain {name} (fine)          | Bain {name}                             | an object after the imperative: no mutation |
| Áirithe mar {name} (fine)   | Áirithe mar {name}                      | mar mutates nothing                         |
| i bhfriot Ferro 3134 (fine) | i bhfriot Ferro 3134                    | the generic noun is eclipsed, the name not  |

- **{region}** is the country's Irish name from the browser (CLDR: “Éire”, “an Ríocht Aontaithe”, “an Ghearmáin”,
  “Stáit Aontaithe Mheiriceá”), always in the nominative and often with its article. After “i” or “sa” it would change
  form (“in Éirinn”, “sa Ghearmáin”), so it never follows a preposition: put it after a colon, as a label: "Shops in
  {region}" → “Siopaí: {region}”; "The silica limit in {region}" → “Teorainn na silice: {region}”; "We have not yet
  looked for shops in {region}." → “Níor chuardaíomar siopaí fós sa tír seo: {region}.”; "Who to call in {region}" →
  “Cé leis a nglaofar: {region}”.
- **{library}** is a `select` with fixed keys: write each branch in full Irish (“a dhíoltar sa Ríocht Aontaithe”).
- **Figure placeholders** (`{count}`, `{year}`, `{value}`, `{most}`) stand for digits, which show no mutation, so a
  preposition before them is fine (“ó {year}”, “suas le {most}”); the noun after them follows Plural forms.
- **Glazecalc** is never mutated and takes no ending: “san aip Glazecalc” (not “i nGlazecalc”), “don aip Glazecalc”
  (not “do Ghlazecalc”), “le Glazecalc”, “ag Glazecalc”, “suíomh Glazecalc”.
- **`{{SG 1.45}}`** shows as “sainmheáchan 1.45” (see 2.6), a word the sentence cannot mutate: put it after “ag”, a
  colon or a verb, never after “ar”, “do”, “de”, “faoi”, “ó” or “i”: “glónra ag {{SG 1.45}}”, “tanaigh é go dtí go
  léann sé {{SG 1.43}}”, “Trédhearcach: {{SG 1.46–1.48}}”.

### Cones

The app's cones are **Orton cones**. Irish has the word in téarma (Ceramics Equipment: "pyrometric cone" → “cón
piriméadrach”), and NEID and EID give “cón” for every cone; no Irish shop sells cones. So:

- "cone" → “cón” (m1; gen. cóin; pl. cóin; gen. pl. cón). "cone 6" → “cón 6”; "cone 06" → “cón 06”, read “cón náid a
  sé” (the zero stays: cón 06 is far cooler than cón 6).
- The number follows the noun and stays bare; the noun takes the mutation: "fire to cone 6" → “bácáil go cón 6”; "at
  cone 6" → “ag cón 6”; "in cone 6" → “i gcón 6”; "a cone 6 glaze" → “glónra do chón 6”; "cone 04 bisque" →
  “brioscbhácáil go cón 04”; "cone 5–6" → “cón 5–6”.
- "Orton cone" → “cón Orton”; at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared: “cón Orton 6”, then
  “cón 6”.
- **Hotter and cooler, never higher and lower.** In the cooler series a higher number is cooler (cón 05 is cooler than
  cón 04), so a cone is “uimhir amháin níos teo” or “uimhir amháin níos fuaire”: "one cone hotter in the sitter" → “cón
  atá uimhir amháin níos teo sa Kiln-Sitter”; "the guide cone, one cooler than the target" → “an cón treorach, uimhir
  amháin níos fuaire ná an sprioc”; "from cone 6 up" → “ó chón 6 agus cóin níos teo”; "below cone 03" → “níos fuaire ná
  cón 03”. Never “níos airde”, “níos ísle”, “suas” or “síos” for cones.
- Seger cones (“cóin Seger”, SK) are a different scale. **Never** write “cón Seger 6” or “SK 6” for an Orton number;
  “cóin Seger” only where the English says "Seger cones".
- Not “coirceog” (EID's other word, a beehive), not “cón tráchta” (a traffic cone).

### The unity formula

“Foirmle Seger” (f4; gen. na foirmle Seger), with “(UMF)” after it the first time on a page: “foirmle Seger (UMF)”. No
Irish source names it: téarma has only the general “foirmle eimpíreach” (empirical formula). This is the name potters'
languages use after Seger (German _Segerformel_, Finnish _Segerin kaava_, Croatian _Segerova formula_), and Irish names
things after a person the same way (IATE: "Waelz kiln" → “áith Waelz”). The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity
formula" and "Unity:" are all “foirmle Seger” / “Foirmle Seger:”. Not a calque (“foirmle mhóilíneach aontachta”), and not
“foirmle eimpíreach” in the app: one name for one thing.

### Gender-neutral wording

Irish nouns have grammatical gender, but a person noun covers anyone: “potadóir” (m) is any potter, as “duine” (m) is any
person. Address the reader as _tú_, which needs no gender, and give generalisations in the plural (“potadóirí”,
“daoine”). Avoid compounds with _fear_ (man) where a neutral word exists (“oibrí”, not “fear oibre”); no slashed forms.

### The reader in the guides

_Tú_ and the imperative singular, in short plain sentences, as the English: “Criathraigh an glónra faoi dhó trí chriathar
80 mesh.”; “Má tá an glónra róthiubh, ritheann sé.” No _sibh_, no impersonal heaviness (“Déantar criathrú ar an
nglónra …”), and no _arna_ constructions (MS 3.1.18). The present tense where the English has it (MS 3.1.18). Quotations
from sources are translated into “…” and keep their figures exactly as the English writes them.

### The app's feature names

Written exactly as on the button or tab, first letter capital, in running text in ‘…’ after a generic noun (cnaipe,

**The guides** are “treoirleabhair” (one guide: “treoirleabhar”, m1; gen. treoirleabhair), the word NEID gives for a

“treoir” for the guides: _treoir_ is also the EU's word for a directive (Treoir 84/500/CEE) and, in the plural, for a
maker's instructions (“treoracha an déantóra”). The five titles, the same in each guide's front matter, in every link
that names a guide, in the page titles (`titles` in `ga.json`) and in the guide list (`list` in `guides/ga.json`):
“Glónrú ó bhunphrionsabail”, “Conas glónra a dhéanamh”, “Meascadh sábháilte agus aeráil”, “Ná nimhigh do theaghlach”,
“Bácáil in áith shimplí”. The list's title "Guides" → “Treoirleabhair”; the Advice page → “Comhairle”. Cross-references
use “féach”: "see [Firing a basic kiln]" → “féach an treoirleabhar [Bácáil in áith shimplí]”; "(see After the firing,
below)" → “(féach ‘Tar éis na bácála’, thíos)”.

na hÉireann), “an Ríocht Aontaithe” (sa Ríocht Aontaithe), “Stáit Aontaithe Mheiriceá” (SAM), “Tuaisceart Éireann”.
"UK potters" → “potadóirí sa Ríocht Aontaithe”. The region labels US, UK, EU stay.

### EU law

As the EU's Irish texts cite acts (CLP: “Treoir 93/42/CEE ón gComhairle”; 2017/2398's own title): EEC is _CEE_, EC is
_CE_, EU is _AE_. "Directive 84/500/EEC" → “Treoir 84/500/CEE ón gComhairle”, amended by “Treoir 2005/31/CE ón
gCoimisiún”; short form “Treoir 84/500/CEE”. "Directive (EU) 2017/2398" → “Treoir (AE) 2017/2398”; "Directive
2004/37/EC" → “Treoir 2004/37/CE”; CLP → “Rialachán (CE) Uimh. 1272/2008”. Directive 84/500/EEC has no Irish text, so
its name and its kinds of article (2.9) are this page's, built on that pattern. "the Directive" in running text is “an
Treoir” with a capital T, which keeps it apart from “treoracha” (instructions). Irish law keeps the name it is published
under (S.I. No. 49 of 2017; HSA): the Irish Statute Book gives these in English only. Never “84/500/EEC”, “84/500/EWG”.

### Pitfalls

- **Glónra** (m4) is a glaze, the coat and the material (téarma Ceramics; NEID); **glónrú** is glazing, the act (téarma
  marks glónra deprecated for the act). Irish Wikipedia's article calls the coat _glónrú_; the app does not. **Cruan** is
  enamel on metal (and tooth enamel), **vearnais** varnish, **gloiniú** window glazing and vitrification, **gloine**
  glass. Never cruan or vearnais for a glaze.
- **Friot** (m4) is a frit, **flosc** (m3) a flux. téarma's verb for fritting is _friochadh_, which is also frying, and
  "fritted" is _friochta_ (fried): write them only beside friot (“luaidhe a ndearnadh friot di”, “friot luaidhe”), never
  alone.
- **Áith** (f2) is a kiln; **oigheann** an oven (also a kitchen oven), **foirnéis** a furnace. téarma deprecates
  _tornóg_ for a kiln. **Bácáil** is firing (téarma, NEID, Vicipéid) and also baking: in these texts always firing. A
  fire as a hazard is **dóiteán**, flames **tine**.
- **Cré-earraí** is earthenware; **faensa** is tin-glazed faience, **cré bhruite** (terracotta) unglazed red ware,
  **cruachré** stoneware clay and **earraí cruachré** stoneware. téarma also gives _cré-earra_ (singular) for any
  ceramic object: say “earra ceirmeach” or “píosa” for one piece, and keep “cré-earraí” for earthenware.
- **Bánlí** is whiting (téarma Ceramics), **aol** lime (CaO, and the frit notes' "lime"), **cailc** chalk, **aolchloch**
  limestone, **faoitín** the fish.
- **The material and the oxide**: **potais** and **sóid** are the potters' oxides K₂O and Na₂O (téarma: iathchloch
  photaise, iathchloch shóide); the materials are **carbónáit photaisiam** (the safety guide's "potash"), **luaith
  shóide** (soda ash) and **sóid aráin** (baking soda). **Silice** is the oxide SiO₂, **grianchloch** the mineral and the
  ground material, **sileacan** the element silicon, **sileacón** the rubber.
- **Cré** is clay, and also earth in old chemistry: téarma's “créanna alcaileacha” for the alkaline earths reads as
  "alkaline clays" today, so the app writes “ocsaídí cré-alcaileacha” (IATE: “miotal cré-alcaileach”).
- **Análaitheoir** is a respirator (IATE, HSA “masc análaitheora”); **aerálaí** is a hospital ventilator (IATE). Write
  “masc análaitheora” where the mask is meant.
- **Luaidhe** (f4) is lead the metal; **luaigh** is to mention, **treoraigh** to lead the way. Read every “luaidhe”
  twice.
- **Bun** is a chemical base (alkali) and **bonn** a foot or base; the base glaze is **bunghlónra**, the base frit
  **bunfhriot**.
- **Forbairt** is thermal expansion in téarma's Physics, and also development: always “forbairt theirmeach”, never
  “forbairt” alone.
- **Meá** is a weighing scale, **scála** and **scálaigh** scaling a recipe; **táir** is tare.
- **Ábhar** is a material and also a subject or topic; in these texts always a material. Raw materials in a shop are
  **amhábhair**.
- **Seilf** is a shelf (furniture or kiln shelf); the materials on hand are **stoc**.
- **Dlús** is density (kg/m³); a slurry's density reading is **sainmheáchan** (relative density, no unit).
- **"should" and "must"** in safety text (README): "should" → “ba cheart” (HSA: “Ba cheart go n-oirfeadh tomhas an
  mhaisc duit”), "should not" → “níor cheart”; "must" → “ní mór” (MS: “ní mór Cumraíocht Windows 10 a atosú”) or
  “caithfidh”, "must not" → “ní mór gan …” or the negative imperative “Ná …”. "must be tested" → “ní mór é a thástáil”:
  the test must be done, not passed (not “ní mór dó an tástáil a shárú”). "some fine quartz" → “roinnt grianchloiche
  míne”, not “beagán” (a little). "never" → “riamh”/“choíche” or “Ná … riamh”, "unless" → “mura”, "at least" → “ar a
  laghad”: keep every one.
- **The Caighdeán, not the dialect**: “nóiméad” (not _bomaite_, _neomat_), “anois”, “inniu”, “ag teastáil” (Ulster _de
  dhíth_ only where the sense needs it); the standard forms of the verb (“bácáiltear”, “ní fhaca”); the Caighdeán's
  eclipsis after a preposition with the article (above).
- **Abbreviations**: Irish has none of its own for most of these; give the Irish name with the English abbreviation
  after it, as the HSA does (“Silice Chriostalta Inanálaithe (RCS)”, “trealamh cosanta análaithe, nó RPE”).
- **Numbers as words**: a number in the English stays a figure (README), so the check finds it: “3 bhrat”, not “trí
  bhrat”.

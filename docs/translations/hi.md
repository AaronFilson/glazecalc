# हिंदी: style sheet and glossary

For translating Glazecalc into Hindi (`hi`), in standard written Hindi as the Central Hindi Directorate spells it
(मानक हिंदी वर्तनी), for potters in India (region `IN`). Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the
meaning of every English term below. Every Hindi message and guide follows this page: one Hindi word for one thing.
India has no region data in the library yet (Phase C: the IN entry is researched in English,
`scratch/round6/research/in-pk-bd.md` in the main checkout); India fires by Orton cones by default, with temperatures one
tap away (the owner's decision of 2026-10-20), and its shops sell Orton cones and label glazes by cone, so the Hindi pages
must read well with Orton cones and temperatures and say nothing about SK cones that the English does not say.

**The owner's choice of words.** The owner chose (2026-10-20, scratch/round6/plan.md) the register आप and **the studio
potters' English loanwords first**, in Devanagari as potters say them (ग्लेज़, फ़्रिट, किल्न, बिस्क), **with the Hindi
technical term in brackets where it differs** (the Commission for Scientific and Technical Terminology's, CSTT's). This page
applies that choice in four tiers, and every row says which tier it is in:

1. **Craft words** that Indian studio potters say in English (glaze, frit, kiln, bisque, cone, slip, slurry, crazing,
   pinholing): the loanword in Devanagari, with the Hindi technical term in brackets at its first use in each guide section.
2. **Materials**: the name on the bag. Indian shops sell in English (Bhoomi's "Soda Feldspar 1 Kg", "China Clay Kaolin
   Calcined", Clay Station's "CS Whiting (G-200)"), so the material is that English name in Devanagari (सोडा फ़ेल्डस्पार,
   व्हाइटिंग), trade names in Latin letters (Ferro 3134, Minspar 200).
3. **Chemistry and physics** (oxide, mole, formula, oxidation, thermal expansion, viscosity): the Hindi of NCERT's school
   textbooks, which every Hindi-medium reader learned (ऑक्साइड, मोल, सूत्र, ऑक्सीकरण), with the potters' loanword in brackets
   where they use one (रिडक्शन for a reduction firing is tier 1).
4. **Health, safety and law**: plain Hindi and the words of India's Hindi legal texts (धूल, विषैला, सिलिकोसिस, से अधिक नहीं), so
   that every reader understands them; a loanword only where Hindi has no plain word (रेस्पिरेटर, HEPA).

Where a loanword collides with an everyday Hindi word (प्लास्टिक for plastic clay, स्टेन for a stain, कुकी for a cookie,
मैनुअल for a manual kiln, सस्पेंडर), the row says how the collision is avoided.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Hindi has very little written about studio glazes. The Indian shops sell
in English, and Google's suggestions for India (20 October 2026) show that Indians search for glazes, kilns and cones in
English: Devanagari queries such as सिरेमिक ग्लेज़ and पॉटरी किल्न complete to "ceramic glaze recipe" and "pottery kiln
price india" in Latin letters. The Hindi Wikipedia has the older technical Hindi of ceramics (मृत्तिकाशिल्प, काचित, सुघट्य,
पाषाण भांड, ऊष्मसह, उच्च ताप शंकु) and uses ग्लेज़ and भट्ठी in its articles; NCERT's chemistry gives the chemistry words; the
Hindi text of the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code gives the law's. **CSTT's own glossaries could not
be read**: cstt.education.gov.in refused every connection from here, and the Internet Archive answered "too many
requests". So the bracketed technical terms on this page come from the Hindi Wikipedia's technical articles, which use the
older Commission coinages, and from NCERT; each such row says where its term comes from, and **a native potter or CSTT's
ceramics glossary should confirm them**. Rows marked **Usage** are words seen in Indian shops' English, in Google's
suggestions or in Hindi articles but in no Hindi teaching source; rows marked **No source** are best choices. The rows a
Hindi-speaking potter should check first are marked **Native check**; the first of them: the genders of ग्लेज़, फ़्रिट, स्लिप,
व्हाइटिंग and गाइड; किल्न against भट्ठी; विशिष्ट गुरुत्व for SG; गाढ़ापन for glaze density; सेगर सूत्र (UMF) and यूनिटी; ज्वलन-हानि
(LOI); स्टेबलाइज़र; ग्लास फ़ॉर्मर; the defect loanwords; श्वसनीय क्रिस्टलीय सिलिका; and the sentence-ending full stop.

## Choices for the owner and a native potter

These are the questions this sheet cannot settle from sources; each has a recommendation, used below until answered.

- **The full stop or the danda:** sentences end in the Latin full stop **"."**, as Microsoft's Hindi style guide requires
  (4.1.12) and Google's Hindi help pages write, in the interface and the guides alike. The danda **"।"** is the standard of
  print, NCERT, the Gazette and the Hindi Wikipedia; if the owner prefers it, it replaces every sentence-ending "." in the
  Hindi files at once (never one file in one style and one in the other). Recommended: ".".
- **The four tiers** (above): loanwords for craft words, the bag's names for materials, NCERT's Hindi for chemistry, plain
  Hindi for safety. Recommended.
- **Kiln:** **किल्न**, with (भट्ठी) at its first use in each guide section. In everyday Hindi भट्टी is a cooking stove or a
  furnace (Google suggest: "इलेक्ट्रिक भट्टी फॉर नमकीन") and भट्ठा a brick kiln; the Hindi Wikipedia uses भट्ठी for a potter's
  kiln. A native potter may prefer भट्ठी throughout.
- **Specific gravity:** **विशिष्ट गुरुत्व**, the literal term, which the Hindi Wikipedia uses for minerals and liquids
  (redirecting to आपेक्षिक घनत्व, NCERT's relative density; both are the same unitless ratio). It keeps the word घनत्व (density,
  g/cm³) out of the glaze's density altogether: the setting "Glaze density" is **गाढ़ापन**.
- **Kaolin:** **काओलिन**, with (चाइना क्ले) at its first use in each section, since Indian shops sell it as China Clay
  (Bhoomi's "China Clay Kaolin Calcined", Clay Station's "CS China Clay"). Never चीनी मिट्टी, which also means porcelain
  (चीनी मिट्टी के बर्तन).
- **Guide and manual:** the app's guides are **गाइड** (f.), a kiln's manual is **मैनुअल** (m.). Never the same word, and
  never मैनुअल for a manual kiln (हाथ से चलाया जाने वाला किल्न).
- **Lead:** **सीसा** (NCERT, the Hindi Wikipedia) for the metal and the setting, with English names in compounds as NCERT
  writes them (लेड ऑक्साइड, लेड फ़्रिट). Never शीशा (glass, a mirror), which sounds alike.
- **The genders of loanwords** (2.1 to 2.8): chosen by the Hindi default (a loanword ending in a consonant is masculine, one
  ending in -ī or -ing is feminine) and by the Hindi Wikipedia's usage (सिलिका m.); a native potter should confirm each,
  ग्लेज़ and स्लिप first.
- **Poison line:** AIIMS's National Poisons Information Centre (1800 116 117) is verified (IN research). AIIMS's Hindi
  site gives its name only in English, so the name stays as published, with a Hindi description once: "विष सूचना केंद्र".

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

**आप**, polite and plain, in the interface, errors, notices, emails, the safety text and the guides alike: one register
across the app, never तुम or तू. Microsoft's voice is "clear, friendly, and concise … everyday conversation, rather than the
formal, technical language" (MS 2.1): चुनें, not चयन करें; बदलें, not परिवर्तित करें; देखें, not संदर्भ लें; दोबारा कोशिश करें, not
पुनः प्रयास करें. The same plain words in the guides: छानें, तौलें, मिलाएँ.

- **Instructions** (guides and help): the -ें imperative, as MS and Google write it: "ग्लेज़ को 80 मेश की छलनी से दो बार
  छानें.", "सामग्री तौलते समय रेस्पिरेटर पहनें." The -इए form (कीजिए) is not used: it is spoken politeness, not software's.
- **Buttons:** the -ें form, the verb last: सहेजें, प्रिंट करें, तुलना करें, हटाएँ, रद्द करें.
- **Errors:** short, in the passive, never the computer speaking in the first person (MS 5.4.2): "रेसिपी सहेजी नहीं जा सकी.
  दोबारा कोशिश करें.", "यह फ़ाइल पढ़ी नहीं जा सकी.", "सहेजना विफल रहा."
- **Notices:** plain statements: "इस पेज का अनुवाद AI ने किया है. अंग्रेज़ी पेज पढ़ने के लिए यहाँ देखें."
- **Guide sentences:** impersonal statements and the -ें imperative: "ग्लेज़ बहुत मोटा हो तो फ़ायरिंग में बह जाता है.", "बिस्क
  किए बर्तन पर ग्लेज़ तुरंत लगाया जा सकता है."
- **Should, must, may** keep apart, as the English has them:
  - "must" → **ज़रूरी है**, **-ना होगा** ("जाँच करानी होगी"); a legal duty ("shall") → **अनिवार्य है**. "must not" → **न करें** in an
    instruction ("सूखी झाड़ू न लगाएँ"), **वर्जित है** only in a legal text.
  - "should" → **चाहिए** ("मास्क पहनना चाहिए"); "should not" → **नहीं … चाहिए** ("सूखी झाड़ू नहीं लगानी चाहिए"). Never ज़रूरी for
    "should", never चाहिए for "must".
  - "may" (a hazard, a hedge that must survive) → **सकता है / सकती है** ("कैंसर का कारण बन सकता है", "इसमें कुछ बारीक क्वार्ट्ज़ हो
    सकता है"); "may" (permission) → **कर सकते हैं**.
  - "must be tested" → **की जाँच ज़रूरी है** / **की जाँच करानी होगी**: the test is done, not passed; never "जाँच में पास होना ज़रूरी
    है".
  - "some" (some fine quartz) → **कुछ**, never **थोड़ा** (a little). "never" → **कभी न / कभी नहीं** only where the English says
    never; no हमेशा, बिल्कुल, ज़रूर or कदापि the English lacks.

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- **Buttons and menu commands:** the -ें imperative, short (MS 4.1.14: "संपादन करें, सहेजें, हटाएँ"): सहेजें, प्रिंट करें,
  तुलना करें, हटाएँ, सामग्री जोड़ें. Not "कृपया सहेजें", not "सहेजिए".
- **Headings, tabs, page titles:** a noun phrase: "रेसिपी", "सेटिंग", "फ़ायरिंग लॉग", "कोन और हीटवर्क". A heading that names
  an activity takes the verbal noun in -ना: "Mixing the glaze" → "ग्लेज़ मिलाना", "Typing amounts" → "मात्राएँ टाइप करना". A
  heading that is a question stays one: "ग्लेज़ क्यों बहता है?"
- **Field labels:** a noun; the colon where the English has one, no space before it: "सामग्री का नाम:".
- **Confirmations:** "Saved: {name}" → "सहेजा गया: {name}" (the label form, so the verb need not agree with {name}'s
  gender); "Removed" → "हटाया गया".
- **Progress:** the passive with "रहा है" and the ellipsis (MS 4.1.14): "Saving…" → "सहेजा जा रहा है…".

### Capitalisation

Devanagari has no capitals; the English's Title Case and sentence case go with the English words. Latin words inside Hindi
text keep the English's case where they are names, codes, units or formulas (Orton, SK, UMF, LOI, Ferro 3134, N95, mg/m³,
SiO₂). An English **loanword is written in Devanagari** (ग्लेज़, फ़्रिट, किल्न), never in Latin letters, unless it is a trade
name, a trademark (Kiln-Sitter, Zircopax), a company or brand (Google, Skutt), a code or a unit.

### Script and spelling

- **Devanagari as the Central Hindi Directorate standardises it** (मानक हिंदी वर्तनी): the anusvara for a nasal before a
  consonant (हिंदी, संबंध, अंक, not हिन्दी, सम्बन्ध: the language's name in `lib/regions/languages.js` is a name and stays as
  written there); the **chandrabindu** where no vowel sign stands above the headline (हटाएँ, जाएँ, सामग्रियाँ, हाँ, पाँच: MS 4.1.12),
  the anusvara where one does (में, हैं, करें, नहीं). **ऑ** for the English open o (ऑक्साइड, कॉपर, बॉल क्ले, कॉर्निश), **ऐ** for
  the English a of "ash" (सोडा ऐश, बैच, मैट, टैल्क).
- **The nukta** on ज़ and फ़ in English and Urdu loanwords, as Google writes them (ज़्यादा, ज़रूरी, फ़ोन): ग्लेज़, फ़्रिट,
  फ़ायरिंग, फ़्लक्स, फ़ेल्डस्पार, ज़िंक, ज़िरकॉन, ज़्यादा. Not on क, ख, ग (MS 4.1.12: खतरा, not ख़तरा), and not on ड़ and ढ़, which are
  letters of their own (पेंदा, गाढ़ा). Type it as the dot after the letter (ज + ़), the form Unicode normalisation keeps.
- **Hyphens** in compounds as standard Hindi writes them: सीसा-रहित, ड्रॉप-एंड-होल्ड, लेदर-हार्ड.
- **Latin text** inside Hindi: units, formulas, trade names, codes and cone numbers exactly as the English writes them, with
  a space on each side where the English has one (10 g, 1222 °C, Ferro 3134 में, Orton कोन 6, SiO₂ का).
- **Digits** are 0 to 9 only (see Numbers and units).

### Punctuation

- **The full stop "."** ends a sentence (MS 4.1.12; Google), with no space before it and one after: "ग्लेज़ बह जाता है." A
  heading, a button and a list item that is not a sentence end with nothing. See "Choices": the danda "।" is the
  alternative, used everywhere or nowhere.
- **Comma, question mark, colon, semicolon, exclamation mark** as in English, no space before them. No "!" in the app's own
  voice.
- **Quotation marks:** “ ” for quotations and for a word quoted (“बोरॉन ब्लू”), ‘ ’ inside them. **UI names in running
  text** (buttons, tabs, settings) in single curly quotes, as MS writes them ("‘रद्द करें’ पर क्लिक करें"): "‘तुलना करें’ चुनें".
  Never the straight ' around a name: before `{`, `}` or `#` ICU reads it as a quote (README).
- **Parentheses** as English, no space inside them (MS 4.1.12: "5 मील (8 किमी)").
- **Dashes:** the en dash for ranges with no spaces (10–60%, कोन 06–04); the em dash avoided (MS 4.1.12): a comma, a colon or
  a new sentence instead.
- **Ellipsis** "…" where the English has one.

### Numbers and units

- **The digits 0 to 9** wherever the English has digits (the international form of Indian numerals, Constitution Art.
  343(1); CLDR's `hi` uses them, numbering system `latn`). Never Devanagari digits (० १ २), which the numbers check does not
  read, never spelled-out figures where the English has digits.
- **The decimal point and the English's figures as the English writes them:** 1.5; 0.025; 12,500; 1222 °C and 1000 g
  ungrouped. The app formats numbers the Indian way (12,34,567.5) when it prints them; a translation writes only the
  English's figures, never लाख or करोड़ for one ("12,500" stays 12,500, never "साढ़े बारह हज़ार").
- **Percent:** 5%, no space (MS 4.1.12, CLDR); a range 10–60%.
- **Units:** the English's symbols in Latin letters, with the English's spacing: g, kg, mg, µg, mL, L, mm, cm, °C, °F, °Bé, %,
  mg/m³, µg/cm²; never ग्रा, किग्रा, सेमी or °से (MS allows them; the numbers check does not read them). Inches and feet are the
  words **इंच** and **फ़ुट**, with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives it: "12 इंच (30 cm)".
- **A number the English writes as a word** ("two coats", "three tiles", "once", "one number hotter") stays a word, since a
  digit would be a figure the English lacks and the check rejects it: "दो परतें", "तीन टाइलें", "एक बार", "एक नंबर ज़्यादा गर्म".
  Where the English has the digit, the digit stays: "3 coats" → "3 परतें".
- **Dates:** "14 अक्टूबर 2026", "अक्टूबर 2026" (CLDR's long form): the month named beside the year, the Gregorian calendar,
  never a Vikram Samvat or Saka year. The month names are CLDR's: जनवरी, फ़रवरी, मार्च, अप्रैल, मई, जून, जुलाई, अगस्त, सितंबर,
  अक्टूबर, नवंबर, दिसंबर.
- **Clock times:** the 24-hour clock, **20:00** (CLDR's 24-hour form), with बजे after it if the sentence needs it ("20:00 बजे
  तक"). "8am to 8pm" → "8:00 से 20:00 तक"; "08:30 to midnight" → "08:30 से आधी रात तक". Never सुबह, दोपहर, शाम or रात with a
  12-hour figure ("रात 8 बजे"), which the check reads as 8. "24 hours" → "24 घंटे"; "24/7" → "24/7" or "24 घंटे, हफ़्ते के 7
  दिन" (the 7 is a figure).
- **No figures the English lacks:** no Devanagari digits, no लाख or करोड़, no Vikram Samvat or Saka years, no SK or Orton
  equivalents, no converted units, no IS numbers (IS 9473, IS 9806) where the English names none.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Hindi two cardinal categories: `node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('hi').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"`
prints `[ 'one', 'other' ]`. **`one` covers 0 and 1, and fractions below 1** (`i = 0 or n = 1`): 0, 0.5 and 1 are `one`;
1.5, 2 and 21 are `other`.

| Category | Numbers              | Example                                                                  |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `one`    | 0, 1, 0.5 (i = 0)    | 0 सामग्री, 1 सामग्री, 1 परत, 1 रेसिपी, 0.5 g                             |
| `other`  | 1.5, 2, 3, 21, 100 … | 2 सामग्रियाँ, 3 परतें, 21 रेसिपियाँ, 2 टाइलें, 5 ग्लेज़, 3 फ़्रिट, 2 कोन |

Every plural message has both forms, and `=0 {…}` stays where the English has it:
`{count, plural, one {# material} other {# materials}}` → `{count, plural, one {# सामग्री} other {# सामग्रियाँ}}`. The `one`
form must read right for 0 as well as 1 ("0 सामग्री" is good Hindi), so it keeps `#` and never says एक for the number. Where
the English's `one` has no `#` ("your passkey", "A number"), the message is shown only for a count of one or more: write
the Hindi for exactly one, as the English does, and flag it if you find a way for 0 to reach it.

How the noun takes the number, in the direct case (a subject or object without a postposition) and the oblique (before
में, से, का/की/के, को, पर):

| Noun (gender)    | One (direct)  | Other (direct) | Other (oblique)           |
| ---------------- | ------------- | -------------- | ------------------------- |
| सामग्री (f.)     | # सामग्री     | # सामग्रियाँ   | # सामग्रियों में          |
| परत (f.)         | # परत         | # परतें        | # परतों में               |
| रेसिपी (f.)      | # रेसिपी      | # रेसिपियाँ    | # रेसिपियों में           |
| टाइल (f.)        | # टाइल        | # टाइलें       | # टाइलों पर               |
| ग्लेज़ (m.)      | # ग्लेज़      | # ग्लेज़       | # ग्लेज़ों में            |
| फ़्रिट (m.)      | # फ़्रिट      | # फ़्रिट       | # फ़्रिटों में            |
| कोन (m.)         | # कोन         | # कोन          | # कोनों से                |
| फ़ायरिंग (f.)    | # फ़ायरिंग    | # फ़ायरिंग     | # फ़ायरिंग में            |
| घंटा (m.)        | # घंटा        | # घंटे         | # घंटों में               |
| मिनट, दिन (m.)   | # मिनट, # दिन | # मिनट, # दिन  | # मिनटों में, # दिनों में |
| अक्षर (m.)       | # अक्षर       | # अक्षर        | # अक्षरों का              |
| कोड, डिवाइस (m.) | # कोड         | # कोड          | # कोडों में               |
| पासकी (f.)       | # पासकी       | # पासकी        | # पासकी में               |
| बर्तन (m.)       | # बर्तन       | # बर्तन        | # बर्तनों पर              |

A masculine loanword ending in a consonant (ग्लेज़, फ़्रिट, कोन, ऑक्साइड) is the same in the direct plural and takes -ों in the
oblique; a feminine one takes -ें (टाइलें, परतें) or -इयाँ (सामग्रियाँ, रेसिपियाँ). A verb or participle agrees with the noun in each
form: `one {# सामग्री जोड़ी गई} other {# सामग्रियाँ जोड़ी गईं}`. A weight or temperature takes no noun: `{n} g`.

### Placeholders and names

Hindi puts its postpositions (में, से, को, पर, का/की/के) after the word, and they never change for the word before them, so
`{name}`, `{region}`, `{count}` and `{library}` take them unchanged: "{region} में", "{name} से", "{count} सामग्रियों में".
Two things do change:

- **का/की/के agree with the noun after them**, not with the placeholder: "{region} के नियम" (m. pl.), "{region} की सीमा" (f.),
  "{name} का विश्लेषण" (m.). Write the noun that follows, and the agreement is fixed whatever the value.
- **A verb agrees with its subject's gender**, so a placeholder must not be the subject of a gendered verb: put a generic
  noun before it and let the noun set the gender. "Saved: {name}" → "सहेजा गया: {name}"; "{name} was saved" → "रेसिपी
  {name} सहेजी गई" (रेसिपी, f.); "{name} is no longer made" → "सामग्री {name} अब नहीं बनती" (सामग्री, f.); "{region} has no
  limit" → "{region} में कोई सीमा तय नहीं है".

`{region}` is the country's name in Hindi from the browser (भारत, संयुक्त राज्य, यूनाइटेड किंगडम, जर्मनी, जापान). Never "{region}
देश" or "{region} का देश". A trade name in Latin letters is the same: "Ferro 3134 में", "फ़्रिट Ferro 3134".

### Cones

Orton cones are written **Orton** in Latin letters wherever the English says Orton, beside **कोन** (m., the potters' word:
Bhoomi's "Cones Pyrometric", Clay Station's "Orton Cones"; the Hindi technical शंकु in brackets at the first use in each
guide section: "कोन (शंकु)"): "Orton कोन 6", "कोन 06", "Orton के कोन". The noun कोन is masculine: "कोन झुकता है", "कोन 6 तक
पकाएँ". **SK cones** are written SK with the number as printed (SK 6a) where the English has them; India does not use them
and Indian shops do not sell them, so the Hindi pages rarely show one. **Seger** is written **सेगर** (the Hindi Wikipedia's
spelling, "एच॰ ए॰ सेगर") only where the English says Seger: the firing guide's older European Seger cones (सेगर कोन) and the
**सेगर सूत्र** (the unity molecular formula, UMF), which is another thing and never names a cone. Never give an SK number for
an Orton number or the reverse, nor say they are equal, nor add a cone to "mid fire" or "high fire" where the English has
none.

- **One number hotter, one number cooler:** "एक नंबर ज़्यादा गर्म" and "एक नंबर ज़्यादा ठंडा" (the guard cone is "एक नंबर
  ज़्यादा गर्म कोन"); never ऊँचा, नीचा, बड़ा or छोटा: cone 05 is cooler than 04 ("कोन 05, कोन 04 से एक नंबर ज़्यादा ठंडा है").
- **cone 06** is written 06 with its zero and read "ज़ीरो छह"; never 6.
- Not शंकु alone in running text (the geometric cone), and not "पायरोमेट्रिक कोन" except as the English's "pyrometric cone"
  at a definition.

### The unity formula

**सेगर सूत्र (UMF)**: written so at its first use in each guide section and in the setting, "सेगर सूत्र" afterwards. The app's
short "Unity formula" is "सेगर सूत्र", the label "Unity:" is "सेगर:". "unity" as the scaling is **यूनिटी**, explained once as
"फ़्लक्स का कुल योग एक मानकर" (एक as a word: the English has no digit). The three columns: ग्लास फ़ॉर्मर (RO₂), स्टेबलाइज़र
(R₂O₃), फ़्लक्स (R₂O और RO). "formula" alone in a chemical sense is **सूत्र** (NCERT); a glaze recipe is **रेसिपी**: never सूत्र
for a recipe. No established Hindi name for the UMF was found (no source; Native check).

### Gender-neutral wording

Hindi verbs show the subject's gender in the past and in the habitual and progressive forms (आप करते हैं / करती हैं), so the
text never guesses the reader's:

- **Imperatives** (-ें) carry no gender: "छानें", "पहनें".
- **The passive and impersonal forms** (MS 5.4.2; Google: "अपने खाते को ज़्यादा सुरक्षित बनाया जा सकता है"): "रेसिपी सहेजी गई",
  "यह किया जा सकता है", "मास्क पहनना चाहिए".
- **The ergative past** agrees with the object, not with आप: "आपने रेसिपी सहेजी" (रेसिपी, f.).
- Where a verb must agree with आप, the **masculine plural** (सकते हैं, चाहते हैं), which Hindi software uses as the general form
  with आप (MS 2.2.1 "आप जारी रखना चाहेंगे?", MS 3.1, Google); never a slash form (सकते/सकती).
- People in general are plural or by role (MS 3.1): "कुम्हार", "पॉटर", "उपयोगकर्ता", "लोग"; never महिला कुम्हार or a gendered
  pronoun for a generic person. "कुम्हार" is the traditional potter; a studio potter in the guides is "पॉटर" or "कुम्हार" as the
  English's "potter" (Native check: the traditional word carries a caste name in India, so the guides say "पॉटर" where the
  English means studio potters in general).

### The reader in the guides

आप where the sentence needs a subject; steps are -ें imperatives; explanations are impersonal ("ग्लेज़ बहुत मोटा हो तो बह जाता
है"). Short plain sentences, as the English, one Hindi sentence for one English sentence where Hindi allows. Quotations from
sources are translated into “…” and keep their figures as the English writes them. **The bracketed Hindi term** (tier 1 and
2 rows below) goes right after the loanword at its first use in each guide section, before the `{{en: …}}` mark where the
term has one: "फ़्लक्स (गालक){{en: flux}}". Never in a button, a label, a table heading or a message of the JSON files; in
the glossary of glazing-basics (the definition list), each term line may give it once.

### Law in India

As the law writes it, never paraphrased: the words below are fixed, and the region texts must use them. India's Acts are
in Hindi and English; the region data will cite the English, so a Hindi title is given once beside it.

- **Limits:** "at most", "no more than", "up to" → **अधिकतम** or **… से अधिक नहीं** (the Code: "… से अधिक नहीं है"; the limit
  itself passes); "less than", "under", "below" → **… से कम** (the Code: "अठारह वर्ष से कम आयु"; it does not); "at least",
  "or more" → **कम से कम** or **… से कम नहीं** (the Code's legal form: **अन्यून**); "more than", "over", "above" → **… से
  अधिक**. Never से कम for "at most", never से अधिक for "at least": a slip moves a legal limit. Check every one in the
  food-contact, silica and mask texts.
- **The Code:** the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 → **उपजीविकाजन्य सुरक्षा, स्वास्थ्य और
  कार्यदशा संहिता, 2020** (the Bill's Hindi text; secondary pages also write "व्यावसायिक सुरक्षा …": use the Code's form).
  Section 88 leaves the limits to the states: "उच्छन्नता की अधिकतम अनुज्ञेय सीमा … जो राज्य सरकार द्वारा विहित की जाए". The
  English's "each state sets its own limit" → "हर राज्य अपनी सीमा खुद तय करता है"; "We could not confirm a national limit" →
  "हम राष्ट्रीय सीमा की पुष्टि नहीं कर सके". An exposure limit in the region notes → **एक्सपोज़र सीमा** (plain), with the
  Code's "अनुज्ञेय सीमा" (permissible limit) only where the English quotes the Code. Silicosis is on the Code's list of
  notifiable diseases ("अधिसूचनीय रोगों की सूची"; the Code spells it सिलीकोसिस; the app writes **सिलिकोसिस**, the usual
  spelling of the Hindi Wikipedia and the press). The Factories Act, 1948 → **कारखाना अधिनियम, 1948**.
- **Masks:** IS 9473:2002 (filtering half masks against particles, classes FFP1, FFP2, FFP3), from the **भारतीय मानक ब्यूरो
  (BIS)**; its Hindi title was not read: describe it as "कणों से बचाने वाले फ़िल्टर हाफ़ मास्क" and keep the number and the
  classes as the English gives them.
- **Food contact:** India has no mandatory rule for ceramic ware (IS 9806:2001 is voluntary; none is shown). The English's
  food-contact categories are written plainly (2.9).
- **GHS:** no Hindi GHS label text was read (India's chemical rules use "Material Safety Data Sheet" in English). The words
  in 2.7 (संकेत शब्द, खतरा, चेतावनी) are best choices; Native check.
- **Who to call:** **112** ("आपातकालीन नंबर 112": police, fire and ambulance, ERSS); the **National Poisons Information Centre

### Pitfalls

- **ग्लेज़, मीनाकारी, वार्निश, पेंट:** a glaze is **ग्लेज़** (the material and the coat); "to glaze" **ग्लेज़ करना** (the Hindi
  Wikipedia: "ग्लेज़ किए हुए … चौके"). **मीनाकारी** is enamelling on metal (the Hindi Wikipedia: "मीनाकारी (Enamaling)"), इनेमल
  enamel paint, **वार्निश** varnish, **पेंट** paint: none is a glaze. The older technical लुक is not used (it reads as the English
  "look"). Bare ग्लेज़ on the web is also a trading company and glazed paper (Google suggest): titles say **सिरेमिक ग्लेज़** or
  **पॉटरी ग्लेज़** (see Search terms).
- **फ़्रिट and फ़्लक्स:** a frit is **फ़्रिट** (a factory glass); a flux is **फ़्लक्स (गालक)** (the oxide's role). Not टाँके का फ़्लक्स
  (soldering flux), not चुंबकीय फ़्लक्स (magnetic flux).
- **किल्न, भट्ठी, भट्टी, भट्ठा, ओवन:** a potter's kiln is **किल्न** (भट्ठी at the first use per section); **भट्टी** in everyday Hindi
  is a stove or furnace, **भट्ठा** a brick kiln, **आवाँ** the traditional potter's firing pit (the Hindi Wikipedia: "कुम्हार के आवाँ (भट्ठी)"), **ओवन** a kitchen oven, used only
  where the English says oven.
- **बिस्क and कच्चा:** **कच्चा** is unfired (कच्चे बर्तन, greenware; कच्ची मिट्टी, raw clay); **बिस्क** is the first firing and
  its ware (बिस्क फ़ायरिंग, बिस्क किए बर्तन). Never कच्चा for bisque, never बिस्क for unfired ware. **कच्चा माल** is raw materials
  in the trade sense (a shop's category), not unfired.
- **अर्दनवेयर, टेराकोटा, स्टोनवेयर, पोर्सिलेन, चीनी मिट्टी:** earthenware is **अर्दनवेयर** (the Hindi Wikipedia); **टेराकोटा**
  (पकी मिट्टी) is the red, usually unglazed low-fired ware, used only where the English says terracotta; stoneware is
  **स्टोनवेयर** (पाषाण भांड); porcelain is **पोर्सिलेन**. **चीनी मिट्टी** means both kaolin and porcelain (चीनी मिट्टी के बर्तन =
  crockery, porcelain): never used for either; kaolin is **काओलिन** (चाइना क्ले).
- **सिलिका, सिलिकॉन, सिलिकोन, बालू:** silica is the oxide **सिलिका** (SiO₂; the Hindi Wikipedia: "सिलिका या सिलिकॉन डाईऑक्साइड");
  the element is **सिलिकॉन**; silicone rubber is **सिलिकोन**: never सिलिकॉन or सिलिकोन for silica. **सिलिका जेल** (Google suggest's
  top hit) is the drying sachet, not glaze silica. Sand is **बालू/रेत**; silica sand **सिलिका सैंड**.
- **व्हाइटिंग, चूना, खड़िया:** whiting is **व्हाइटिंग** (calcium carbonate, CaCO₃; Clay Station's "CS Whiting"); **चूना** is lime
  (CaO and slaked lime, and the lime eaten with paan); **खड़िया** is chalk. Neither is used for whiting; "lime" in a frit's
  name is चूना only there ("सोडा-पोटाश-चूना फ़्रिट").
- **बोरॉन, सुहागा, बोरिक अम्ल:** B₂O₃ is **बोरॉन (B₂O₃)**, formally बोरॉन ऑक्साइड; borax is **बोरेक्स (सुहागा)**, the Ayurvedic
  and jeweller's सुहागा; boric acid is **बोरिक अम्ल** (the Hindi Wikipedia's टांकिकाम्ल is not used). In the safety text, name
  each as the English does: borax and boric acid are reproductive toxins.
- **पोटाश and सोडा:** "potash" (K₂O) is **पोटाश (K₂O)**, formally पोटैशियम ऑक्साइड; in India पोटाश is also the fertiliser
  (potassium chloride), so the oxide is always written with K₂O at its first use in a section. The safety guide's potash
  (potassium carbonate) is **पोटैशियम कार्बोनेट**. "soda" (Na₂O) is **सोडा (Na₂O)**, formally सोडियम ऑक्साइड; soda ash is **सोडा
  ऐश**; सोडा alone is also soda water and washing or baking soda: the oxide always with Na₂O at first use.
- **स्टेन and कलरेंट:** a stain (factory-made) is **स्टेन** (Bhoomi's "Stains"), at its first use per section "स्टेन (तैयार
  रंग)": स्टेन is also a mark or dirt. A colorant (any oxide that colours) is **कलरेंट (रंजक)**.
- **रेस्पिरेटर, वेंटिलेटर, हवादारी:** a respirator is **रेस्पिरेटर मास्क** at first use, then रेस्पिरेटर; a hospital ventilator
  is **वेंटिलेटर**, never meant here; ventilation of a room is **हवादारी** (वेंटिलेशन in LEV only).
- **गाइड and मैनुअल:** the app's five guides are **गाइड** (f.); a kiln's, controller's or sitter's manual is **मैनुअल** (m.).
  Never the same word.
- **क्रेज़िंग and क्रैकल:** crazing is **क्रेज़िंग** (a defect); crackle is **क्रैकल** (wanted); a crack through the pot from
  heating or cooling is **डंटिंग**.
- **विशिष्ट गुरुत्व and घनत्व:** specific gravity is **विशिष्ट गुरुत्व**, a ratio with no unit (1.45); **घनत्व** is density in
  g/cm³ and is never used for a glaze; a glaze's density (the setting) is **गाढ़ापन**; never लीटर भार (litre weight), another
  measure.
- **लीचिंग:** leaching is **लीचिंग** (धातुओं का रिसाव); the leach test is a test of the metals a fired piece releases, not
  narrowed to lead and cadmium.
- **सूत्र and रेसिपी:** a recipe is **रेसिपी**, a chemical formula **सूत्र**, the unity formula **सेगर सूत्र**.
- **सीसा and शीशा:** lead is **सीसा**; **शीशा** is glass or a mirror. Check every one.
- **प्लास्टिक, स्टेबलाइज़र, सस्पेंडर, कुकी, मैनुअल:** loanwords that mean something else in Indian homes (plastic, a voltage
  stabiliser, braces, a biscuit, a manual): see their rows for the wording that avoids them.
- **Placeholders:** see Placeholders and names.

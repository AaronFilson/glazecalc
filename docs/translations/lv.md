# Latviešu: stila norādījumi un terminu glosārijs (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Latvian (`lv`), standard written Latvian (literārā valoda) for potters in Latvia. Read
[README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Latvian message
and guide follows this page: one Latvian word for one thing. The terms come from the two Latvian shops that sell to
potters (Keramserviss in Ādaži, ceramics.lv in Ķekava), Nabertherm's Latvian kiln manuals, a Latvian art school's
glazing notes, the Russian–Latvian ceramics technology dictionary of Riga Technical University (RTU, 1993) as kept in
the national terminology portal, the Latvian dictionary Tēzaurs, the Latvian texts of EU law and the Latvian NEPSI guide,
not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a Latvian
potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** No shop in Latvia sells the full range of glaze raw materials.
Keramserviss sells clays, ready-made and powder glazes, a few oxides (aluminium, iron, chrome, manganese, cobalt, copper),
kaolin, bentonite, chamotte, Orton cones and kilns; ceramics.lv sells glazes, underglazes, sieves, wax resist and kilns.
Feldspar, whiting, frits and borates are bought abroad (Lithuanian and German shops), so for most materials this page
gives the Latvian chemical name (VVC, LZA and RTU term lists) and says so. Latvian potters' own writing about glaze
defects, cone packs and kiln sitters was not found: those rows rest on the RTU dictionary or on no source.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

Formal: the polite second person plural, _jūs_, written in lower case (MS 4.1.16: "use the polite form of 'you' (jūs).
Don't capitalize it, unless it's used in a letter"). Latvian software rarely writes the pronoun at all: the verb ending
carries it (MS 4.1.16, 2.2.4: “Lai atgrieztos un saglabātu savu darbu, atlasiet Atcelt”). So the forms:

- **Commands** (buttons, menu items, links that act, check boxes that are commands): the **infinitive**, as Latvian
  Windows and Office write them (MS 5.7.2: “Saglabāt” as a command, “Atjaunināt saites”; 4.1.17: “Saglabāt kā…”):
  “Saglabāt”, “Drukāt”, “Salīdzināt”, “Dzēst manu kontu”. Not the imperative (“Saglabājiet”) and not a noun.
- **Instructions** in guides, help and longer messages: the **imperative plural**, no pronoun: “Izkāsiet glazūru
  divreiz.”, “Lietojiet respiratoru.”, “Neieelpojiet putekļus.” The EU's Latvian precautionary statements use the same
  form (IATE, from the CLP: “Sazinieties ar SAINDĒŠANĀS CENTRU vai ārstu, ja jums ir slikta pašsajūta.”), as does NAB
  (“Pārbaudiet balsta virsmu”).
- **Statements about the reader** ("if you fire to cone 6"): the second person plural, pronoun left out: “Ja
  apdedzināt līdz konusam 6, …”. Write _jūs_ or _jūsu_ only where the sentence is unclear without it (MS 4.1.16: avoid
  overusing “jūs” and the possessive “jūsu”; “savs” often does the work: “savā krāsnī”).
- **Questions and confirmations**: every yes/no question starts with _vai_, never without it (MS 4.1.18: “Vai atcelt
  pasākumu?”, not “Atcelt pasākumu?”): “Vai dzēst recepti?”, “Vai vēlaties saglabāt izmaiņas?”, "New here?" → “Vai
  esat šeit pirmo reizi?”. "Or …" at the start of an instruction is “Vai arī …” ("Or call your vet." → “Vai arī
  zvaniet savam veterinārārstam.”), never a bare “Vai …”, which reads as a question.
- **Errors**: impersonal, present tense, always ending with a full stop (MS 5.7.3): “Nevar atrast izejvielu.”,
  “Neizdevās saglabāt recepti.” The app speaks as "we" where the English does: “Iesakām …” (MS 4.1.16).
- "please": leave it out unless the English insists (“Mēģiniet vēlreiz.”).

Examples: a button “Saglabāt kā kopiju”; an error “Nevar atrast recepti.”; a guide sentence “Pirms glazēšanas
noslaukiet biskvītu ar mitru sūkli un ļaujiet tam nožūt.”

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- Buttons and menu commands: the infinitive, as above: “Pievienot izejvielas”, “Atmest izmēģinājumu”. A command that
  opens a further step keeps the English "…" (MS 4.1.17: “Saglabāt kā…”).
- Headings, page titles, tab names, menu names and dialog titles: a **noun or noun phrase**; an activity takes the
  _-šana_ noun (MS 5.7.2: "Save" as a title is “Saglabāšana”; "Install Windows" → “Sistēmas Windows instalēšana”):
  “Receptes”, “Iestatījumi”, “Apdedzināšanas žurnāli”, "Mixing the glaze" → “Glazūras sajaukšana”, "Typing amounts"
  → “Daudzumu ievade”. A heading that is a question stays a question.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: “Izejvielas nosaukums:”. Column headings take no
  colon or full stop (MS 4.1.17).
- Items of one list, menu or group take one grammatical form (MS 4.1.10).
- Confirmations: the participle first, agreeing with its noun: "Saved: …" → “Saglabāts: …”; “Recepte ir saglabāta.”
- Check boxes and list items never end with a full stop unless they are whole sentences (MS 5.7.2).

### Capitalisation

Latvian rules (MS 4.1.5): the first word and proper names only, in headings, buttons, labels and tabs too. English Title
Case is an error: "Fired Weight" → “Masa pēc apdedzināšanas”, "Move Down" → “Pārvietot lejup”. Languages, nationalities,
months and days are lower case (“latviešu”, “angļu termini”, “oktobris”); so are _jūs, jūsu, jums_. A short note in
brackets that is not a sentence starts lower case: "(Recommended)" → “(ieteicams)” (MS 4.1.5). Trade and product names
keep their own form (Orton, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc, BOTZ, Ferro 3134).

### Punctuation

- Quotation marks: “…” (U+201C opening, U+201D closing), as MS 4.1.17 gives them for software ("use double upper curly
  quotation marks (“xxx”) for software texts"); inside them ‘…’. Latvian print also uses „…”, and NAB mixes both: this
  app uses “…” only, never straight "…" in text. Quotation marks are for real quotations and titles; a button or page
  name is not quoted (MS 4.1.17: “Pieskarieties pogai Izlaist”), except where the name would otherwise merge with the
  sentence.
- Apostrophe: Latvian needs none (foreign names take Latvian endings, not apostrophes: “Ortona konusi”). Where one is
  quoted from English, use ’ (U+2019). Write `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`, `}` or `#`, and in the
  `safety`, `regions` and `records` files).
- No space before ; : ! ? (MS 4.1.17). Avoid exclamation marks and semicolons (MS 4.1.17).
- Dash: the en dash with a space each side (MS 4.1.17: Latvian does not tell the en and em dash apart; use the en dash,
  never the hyphen for it). Ranges: the en dash **without** spaces, the unit once at the end: “1222–1240 °C”, “konusi
  06–04”, “1–3%” (MS 4.1.17: “30–50 cm”). In a sentence “no 1222 līdz 1240 °C” is also fine; never mix (“no 1222–1240
  °C”).
- A figure followed by a full stop is an **ordinal**: “6. konuss” is the sixth cone, “2. apdedzināšana” the second firing
  (Lila). Write “konuss 6”, never “6. konuss”; dates take the stops (“2026. gada 9. oktobris”).
- After a colon, lower case unless a new sentence or paragraph follows (MS 4.1.17); a bulleted list's items end without
  a full stop unless they are sentences.
- Abbreviations and formulas cannot take case endings (MS 2.2.3 on acronyms). Put a noun before them in the case the
  sentence needs: “SiO₂ saturs”, “Al₂O₃ daudzums”, “saskaņā ar ES noteikumiem”, “HOPS risks”.

### Numbers and units

- Decimal comma: “1,5%”, “0,01 g”, “1,45”. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped (“1240 °C”, “1000 g”; MS 4.1.20: "except four-digit numbers"; the
  app prints them the same way, `useGrouping: 'min2'` in `client/app/shared/format.ts`). Five digits and more are
  grouped by three with a **no-break space** U+00A0 (“12 500 g”; MS 4.1.20: “24 888 459”; CLDR prints the same). Never a
  plain space (the check reads 12 500 with a plain space as two numbers) and never a dot.
- **Percent: no space**, “15%”, “1,5%” (MS 4.1.20: "There is no space between the number and the percent (%) symbol";
  the browser's own Latvian format prints “15%”). Every other unit takes a space, no-break where you can: “1240 °C”,
  “100 °C/h”, “80 mesh”, “0,1 mg/m³”, “25 mm”. Shops print “1300°C” (Keramserviss): do not copy it.
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word, with the same symbol. MS 4.1.14 spells out 0–9 in running text,
  but the README wins: “3 kārtas”, not “trīs kārtas”; “15%”, never “15 procenti”.
- Dates: “2026. gada 9. oktobris” (long), “09.10.2026.” (short). A `{date}` placeholder arrives as the long form in
  the nominative (“2026. gada 8. oktobris”) and cannot take a case ending, so it always follows a colon or stands as a
  label: "(checked {date})" → “(pārbaudīts: {date})”, "In force from {date}." → “Spēkā stāšanās datums: {date}.”,
  "kept until {date}" → “glabāšanas termiņš: {date}”, "removed on {date}" → “dzēsīs šajā datumā: {date}”; never “līdz
  {date}” or “no {date}”. The 24-hour clock with a colon, as CLDR writes it
  (“20:00”). Whole hours stand alone: "8pm" → “plkst. 20”; "8am to 8pm" → “plkst. 8–20” or “no plkst. 8 līdz 20”; "8
  to 5" → “plkst. 8–17”. Minutes take the colon, “8:30”, never the dot (“8.30”), which the check reads as a different
  number.
- Inches and feet: “colla” and “pēda”, the same in every file, with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives
  it, the noun following the plural rule below: “1 colla”, “2 collas (51 mm)”, “18 collu (46 cm)”, “3 pēdas (91 cm)”,
  “25 pēdas”. Pounds and ounces in words: “mārciņas”, “unces”; the symbols lb and oz stay.
- **Specific gravity**, the key `guides.density.sg` ("SG {value}"): **“rel. blīvums {value}”** (relatīvais blīvums,
  the density compared with water, a pure number: IATE "relative density; specific gravity" → “relatīvais blīvums”;
  the areometrs page: an areometrs measures “blīvumu (relatīvo) … šķidruma blīvuma attiecību pret ūdens blīvumu”).
  Plain “blīvums” is density in g/cm³ or kg/m³; the figure 1,45 equals 1,45 g/cm³, but never write it as kg/m³ (1450).
  `guides.density.baume` → “{value} °Bé ({sg})”; `guides.density.pint` → “{value} oz britu pintē ({sg})”. In the
  guides `{{SG 1.45}}` shows as “rel. blīvums 1,45”, so the sentence never names the quantity again: “glazūra, kuras
  rādījums ir {{SG 1.45}}”, “atšķaidiet līdz {{SG 1.43}}”; not “kuras blīvums ir {{SG 1.45}}”.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Latvian three categories:
`node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('lv').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"` prints
`[ 'zero', 'one', 'other' ]`.

| Category | Numbers                                                   | Noun form           | Examples                                                   |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `zero`   | 0, 10–20, 30, 40 … 100, 110–120 …: ends in 0, or in 11–19 | genitive plural     | 0 izejvielu, 10 izejvielu, 11 recepšu, 20 konusu, 15 gramu |
| `one`    | 1, 21, 31 … 101 (not 11); decimals ending in 1 (0,1; 2,1) | nominative singular | 1 izejviela, 21 recepte, 1 konuss, 0,1 grams               |
| `other`  | 2–9, 22–29, 32 … 102–109 …; most decimals (1,5; 0,5)      | nominative plural   | 2 izejvielas, 5 receptes, 3 konusi, 1,5 grami              |

- The noun forms are the ones the browser's own Latvian uses: its unit format for days prints “10 dienu”, “1 diena”,
  “2 dienas”. After 10–20 the nominative plural (“10 izejvielas”) is also correct Latvian, but write the genitive
  plural every time, so the app reads one way.
- **`zero` is not "none"**: it is also 10, 11, 20, 100. The English `=0 {…}` branch ("No materials") stays as it is and
  takes its own words (“Nav izejvielu”); the `zero` branch is a number. So:
  `=0 {Nav izejvielu} zero {# izejvielu} one {# izejviela} other {# izejvielas}`. Write all three categories even
  where two read alike.
- Genitive plurals to know: izejvielu, recepšu, konusu, gramu, apdedzināšanu, kārtu, provju, lauku, rindu, piezīmju,
  frišu, glazūru, collu.
- A verb or adjective agreeing with the counted noun goes inside each branch. Better still, keep agreement out of the
  plural: “Saglabātās receptes: {count}”. In every other case (after a preposition) numeral and noun agree, so build the
  sentence so the number phrase stays nominative or genitive; `#` and `{count}` cannot take an ending.
- Where a number argument is not a plural ("up to {most} materials"), no noun may follow it, because its form would
  depend on the number (“līdz 21 izejvielai”, “līdz 25 izejvielām”). Put the number in brackets or after a colon:
  “Krājumi ir izejvielu saraksts (ne vairāk kā {most}).”, “Lielākais izejvielu skaits krājumos: {most}.”

### Cones

The app's cones are **Orton cones**. Keramserviss sells them as “temperatūras kontroles konuss” and “temperatūras
noteikšanas konusi”, labelled by temperature with the maker's name (“T=985 °C temperatūras kontroles konuss … Orton”),
and sells pyrometric rings as “Temperatūras noteikšanas gredzeni”. The VVC term list has “Zēgera konuss” for Seger cone
(Termini); RTU's “piroskops” (пироскоп) is not what any shop or studio calls them. Latvian studios fire by °C on a controller (Lila, LeMade, NAB) and name firings by
order: “1. apdedzināšana”, “2. apdedzināšana” (Lila). No Latvian potter's text using cone numbers was found. So:

- "cone" → “konuss” (m; gen. konusa; pl. konusi; gen. pl. konusu). "cone 6" → “konuss 6”; "cone 06" → “konuss 06”,
  read “nulle seši” (the zero stays: konuss 06 is far cooler than konuss 6).
- The number follows the noun and stays bare; **the noun takes the case**: "fire to cone 6" → “apdedzināt līdz
  konusam 6”; "at cone 6" → “apdedzinot līdz konusam 6”; "a cone 6 glaze" → “glazūra konusam 6”; "cone 5–6" → “konusi 5–6”; "cone
  04 bisque" → “biskvīta apdedzināšana līdz konusam 04”. Never “6. konuss” (the sixth cone) or “konuss Nr. 6” in
  running text.
- **A cone hotter or cooler** is “par vienu numuru karstāks” / “par vienu numuru vēsāks”: "one cone hotter in the
  sitter" → “Kiln-Sitter slēdzī konuss, kas ir par vienu numuru karstāks”; "from cone 6 up" → “sākot ar konusu 6 un karstākiem”;
  "below cone 03" → “vēsāk par konusu 03”. **Never** “augstāks” / “zemāks”, “lielāks” / “mazāks” or “uz augšu” for
  cones: in the cooler series a higher number is cooler (konuss 05 ir vēsāks nekā konuss 04).
- "Orton cone" → “Ortona konuss” (Latvian gives a foreign person's name a Latvian ending: Edward Orton → Ortons, as
  Seger → Zēgers in “Zēgera konuss”). At the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared: “Ortona konuss
  6”, then “konuss 6”. The foundation's name stays: “Orton Ceramic Foundation”.
- Seger cones (“Zēgera konusi”, SK) are a different scale. **Never** write “Zēgera konuss 6” or “SK 6” for an Orton
  number; “Zēgera konusi” only where the English says "Seger cones".

### The unity formula

“Zēgera formula” (f), with “(UMF)” after it the first time on a page: “Zēgera formula (UMF)”, then “Zēgera formula”
(gen. Zēgera formulas, loc. Zēgera formulā). The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are all
“Zēgera formula” and “Zēgera formula:”. No Latvian glaze text naming the formula was found; this is the name European
potters use after Seger (German _Segerformel_, Russian _формула Зегера_), spelt as the VVC spells the cone (“Zēgera
konuss”, Termini). Never a literal “vienības molekulārā formula”.

### Gender-neutral wording

Latvian nouns, adjectives and past participles have gender, so (MS 3.1):

- Address the reader as _jūs_ and keep participles away from the reader: “esat pierakstījies” / “pierakstījusies” forces
  a gender (MS 4.1.16 uses the masculine singular, “Kad esat restartējis datoru”; this app does not). Write the action
  or the thing instead: "You're signed in" → “Pierakstīšanās izdevās.” or “Jūs pierakstījāties kā {name}.”; "Have you
  saved it?" → “Vai recepte ir saglabāta?”
- No slashed forms (_lietotājs/-a_). For people in general, the plural or a noun that names no person: “tie, kas strādā
  ar keramiku”, “ikviens, kas apdedzina mājās”; “keramiķi” (plural) sparingly. Never _viņš_ for a generic person (MS 3.1).
- A potter: “keramiķis”, “keramiķe”; a thrower of pots: “podnieks”, “podniece”.

### The reader in the guides

_jūs_ and the imperative plural for every step, in short plain sentences as the English: “Izkāsiet glazūru divreiz caur
80 mesh sietu.” Explanations in the second person plural or with the thing as subject: “Ja glazūra ir pārāk bieza, tā
notek.”, “Biskvītu var glazēt uzreiz.” Avoid the nominal style with _veikt_ and _tikt_ (“Tiek veikta glazūras
izkāšana”), _priekš_ for purpose (MS 4.1.15), and sentences that begin with _un_ (MS 4.1.21). Quotations from sources
are translated into “…” and keep their figures as the English writes them.

### Pitfalls

- **glazūra** is a glaze (Tēzaurs, Keramserviss, RTU), and also cake icing; **glazēt**, **glazēts**, **glazēšana** are
  the verb and its forms. **vāpe** (vāpēt) is the old folk word for glaze, still alive in Latgalian pottery and in names
  (Tēzaurs; Termini, archaeology): not the app's word. **glazējums** is colloquial (Tēzaurs). **emalja** is enamel on
  metal (Tēzaurs) and tooth enamel; Trumpeniece calls opaque glazes “sedzošas (sauktas arī par emaljām)”: never use
  _emalja_ for a glaze. **laka** is varnish. **stiklojums** is window glazing (IATE).
- **frite** (f; gen. pl. frišu) is a frit (Tēzaurs, RTU, Termini) and **kušņi** the fluxes (RTU плавни, флюсы → kušņi;
  Trumpeniece; IATE): two different things. **fritēts** also means deep-fried: context settles it.
- **krāsns** (f; gen. krāsns, pl. krāsnis) is the kiln (Keramserviss, Ceramics.lv, NAB, LeMade, IATE); **ceplis**
  (Lila; IATE) is understood but is also a lime or brick kiln and a farmhouse oven (Tēzaurs); **cepeškrāsns** is a
  kitchen oven. The app writes “krāsns” (“keramikas krāsns” where the context does not settle it).
- **fajanss** is not earthenware in general. Tēzaurs defines it as glazed white or light ware of special clays (the
  Russian _фаянс_, white industrial earthenware); IATE gives “fajanss” for earthenware; NAB's Latvian manual even names
  its 1150 °C and 1250 °C glaze programmes “fajanss” (“glazūras apdedzināšana, fajanss („GLAZE FIRING 1250“)”), which
  is stoneware. Earthenware is **māla keramika**, the porous low-fired ware (NAB “porainā keramika”); **terakota** is
  unglazed red ware (Tēzaurs); **majolika** tin-glazed.
- **akmens masa** is stoneware, spelt three ways: “akmens masa” (LMA, Ceramics.lv, Lila, LeMade, Trumpeniece),
  “akmensmasa” (Keramserviss, Tēzaurs) and “akmeņmasa” (RTU). The app writes **akmens masa** (gen. akmens masas).
- **krīts** (chalk, calcium carbonate) is whiting; **kaļķi** is lime in the building sense (burnt or slaked lime),
  **kaļķakmens** limestone (RTU). The oxide CaO is “kalcijs” or “kalcija oksīds”; for "lime" in frit names write
  “kalcija” (“nātrija-kālija-kalcija frite”).
- **soda** in Latvian is sodium carbonate (Termini: soda-ash → “soda”; RTU сода → soda), **dzeramā soda** baking soda,
  **potašs** potassium carbonate (Tēzaurs; RTU поташ → potašs). The potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are “nātrijs
  (Na₂O)” and “kālijs (K₂O)”.
- **silīcijs** is the element, **silikons** the rubber, **silikāts** a silicate. The oxide is “silīcija dioksīds”
  (CLP, 2017/2398; RTU's older “silīcija oksīds”), the material “kvarcs”.
- **respirators** is the mask (IATE: "particulate respirator; FFP mask" → “respirators”; SAMANCTA; shops: “pusmaskas
  respirators”). A hospital ventilator is “plaušu ventilators” (IATE), and **ventilators** alone is a fan (RTU
  вентилятор → ventilators). Never “ventilators” for the mask; never “respirators” for the kiln vent.
- **liesinātājs** (RTU отощитель) is a non-plastic additive that makes a clay body less plastic (grog, sand), not a
  stabilizer, although Trumpeniece lists magnesium, aluminium and quartz as “liesinātāji”. The stabilizers are
  “stabilizatori” (2.1).
- **blāvs** means dull in everyday Latvian, but RTU's “blāvotājs” (глушитель) is an opacifier and “blāvota glazūra”
  (глухая глазурь) an opaque one. The app writes “blāvotājs” for opacifier (2.2) and “necaurspīdīga” for opaque
  (RTU непрозрачная → necaurspīdīgā; Trumpeniece “sedzoša”), and **matēta** for matte (RTU матовая → matētā); never
  “blāva glazūra”.
- **materiāls** vs **izejviela**: shops and NAB say “izejvielas” for raw materials (Keramserviss's tagline “Keramikas
  izejvielu un aprīkojumu veikals”), and the app's Materials are “izejvielas” (2.8). The material is not the oxide:
  “krīts” (material) gives “kalcijs” (oxide, in the Zēgera formula).
- **apdedzināšana** is firing; **apdegums** a burn (injury); **dedzināšana** burning in general. A fire as a hazard is
  “ugunsgrēks”, flames “liesmas”.
- **plaukts** is a kiln shelf (Keramserviss, Ceramics.lv, Lila, LeMade); the "shelf" of materials on hand is
  “krājumi” (2.8).
- **biskvīts** is bisque in pottery and also sponge cake and biscuits (Tēzaurs); **konuss** is also the geometric cone;
  **gredzens** also a finger ring. Context settles them; the app's sense is always the pottery one.
- **Placeholders and trade names cannot be inflected.** Put a common noun in the needed case before them: “izejviela
  {name}”, “receptē {name}”, “izejvielai {name}”, “frite Ferro 3134”, “ar friti Ferro 3134”, “valstī {region}”,
  “lietotnē Glazecalc” (MS 4.1.15, 4.1.17). Where no noun fits, a label with a colon: “Veikali: {region}”.
- **mesh**: Latvian sieves are sold in mesh (Ceramics.lv: “1100 atveres/cm², kas aptuveni atbilst 80 mesh”). Keep “80
  mesh”, indeclinable.
- **Numbers as words**: see Numbers. A number in the English stays a number.
- **"not fully" is not "not at all"**: “pilnībā” before a negated verb negates the whole (“var pilnībā neizkust” reads
  as "may not melt at all"). Put the negation on the verb and “līdz galam” after it: "may not melt fully" → “var
  neizkust līdz galam”; "the grains do not fully dissolve" → “graudiņi neizšķīst līdz galam”; "often under-melted" →
  “bieži nav izkususi līdz galam”. "not always" → “ne vienmēr” (“aizstāšana ne vienmēr ir 1:1”). Positive "fully" stays
  “pilnībā”, “pilnīgi” (“pilnībā izkususi”, “pilnīgi izžuvis”).
- **"should" and "must"** in safety text (README): "should" → “vajadzētu” or “ieteicams” (NAB: “nevajadzētu tvert pie
  malām”); "must" → the debitive “jā-” (“ir jāpārbauda”, “jābūt”) or “obligāti”; "must not" → “nedrīkst” (NAB: “nedrīkst
  saskarties”); "should not" → “nevajadzētu”, never “nedrīkst”. "must be tested" means the test must be done: “ir
  jāpārbauda laboratorijā”, not “jāiztur pārbaude” (must pass). Keep every "never" (“nekad”), "unless" (“ja vien …
  nav”) and "at least" (“vismaz”). "some" is not "a little": "may contain some fine quartz" → “var saturēt smalku
  kvarcu”, never “nedaudz smalka kvarca”. Where the English only reports a source's figure ("Rio Grande says at least 12
  inches"), do not turn it into “jābūt”. A source that "says never to" do something is quoted as an instruction, not
  as a "must not": "HSE says never to brush or blow dust off skin" → “HSE norāda: nekad nenobirstiet un nenopūtiet
  putekļus no ādas”, not “nekad nedrīkst”; "should NOT be left unattended" → “NEVAJADZĒTU atstāt bez uzraudzības”;
  "must never go back to the kitchen" → “nekad nedrīkst atgriezties virtuvē”.
- **Russianisms and colloquial forms to avoid**: _priekš_ for purpose (MS 4.1.15); _pie_ with a temperature (“pie
  1000 °C”, “pie augstas temperatūras”), a calque of Russian _при_: write “1000 °C temperatūrā”, “augstā temperatūrā”
  (NAB: “900 °C līdz 950 °C temperatūrā”); the Russian technical calques that RTU sometimes gives where a Latvian word
  exists (“piroskops” for cone). Write literārā valoda.

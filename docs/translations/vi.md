# Tiếng Việt: style sheet and glossary

For translating Glazecalc into Vietnamese (`vi`), in standard written Vietnamese (chữ quốc ngữ, as the Ministry of
Education's spelling rules give it), for potters in Vietnam. Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md)
gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Vietnamese message and guide follows this page: one Vietnamese word
for one thing. Vietnam has no region data in the library yet (Phase C: the VN entry is researched in English,
`scratch/asia/part-th-vn.md` and `research_notes/Asia/`), so the Vietnamese pages must read well with Orton cones and
temperatures and say nothing about SK cones. The terms come from the Vietnamese Wikipedia's ceramics articles, two
Vietnamese shops and a materials maker, the Ministry of Health's own texts as quoted, the Google and Apple help pages,
and the Microsoft Vietnamese style guide, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was read, the Notes
say so: those rows are for a Vietnamese potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Vietnamese has a full craft vocabulary (men, nung, xương gốm, gốm mộc,
rạn men, cuốn men, lỗ chân kim) that Vietnamese Wikipedia's _Men gốm_, _Gốm_ and _Khuyết tật men_ use, so the glaze,
firing and defect rows rest on those articles read today. The shops found sell colour oxides, stains and glazes to
hobby potters (LaTacita, Vườn Nhà Gốm) and a materials maker sells frit, oxides and zircon to factories (Ceragroup);
none lists feldspar or kaolin, so those rows rest on the Wikipedia article and the mineral names in school texts. Studio
kiln words (côn, kiln-sitter parts, controller terms, firing-log fields) have no Vietnamese source at all. The laws'
words (QCVN 02:2019/BYT on dust, QCVN 12-4:2015/BYT on ceramics in contact with food, Thông tư 47/2015/TT-BYT) come from
the region research (`scratch/asia/part-th-vn.md`, "unverified": the texts were not read because the legal databases
refused or ran in script) and from the Ministry of Health's draft Phụ lục 1 on silicosis (via search snippet); no figure
from them is used here. Rows marked **Usage** are words seen in shop pages, blogs or course notes but in no single

(leather-hard), hồ đất (slip), côn nhiệt (cone), nứt do nhiệt (dunting), thôi nhiễm, tỷ trọng, khẩu trang lọc bụi đạt chuẩn,

## Choices for the owner and a Vietnamese potter

These are the questions this sheet cannot settle from sources; each has a recommendation, used below until answered.

- **Register:** "bạn", plain standard Vietnamese, as Microsoft, Google and Apple write it (no "quý khách", no "anh/chị",
  no sentence-final particles such as "nhé" or "ạ"). Recommended.
- **Tone marks:** the new style, with the mark on the second vowel of "oa", "oe" and "uy": **hòa, khóa, thủy** (the
  Ministry of Education's 2000s spelling and Microsoft's). Google's help pages write the old style ("khoá truy cập").
  Recommended: new style throughout, since search treats both alike.
- **trường thạch or fenspat:** trường thạch (the Wikipedia article, the older word); the shops of the tile industry write
  fenspat. The English-terms setting shows "feldspar" beside it. Native check.
- **Poison line:** no public Vietnamese poison line is confirmed (Bạch Mai's Trung tâm Chống độc has a unit page, but
  its public hours were not confirmed), so the Vietnamese text says to call the emergency number (115) where the English
  says "poison line", as the region data will.
- **Guide and manual:** the app's guides are **cẩm nang**, a kiln's manual is **hướng dẫn sử dụng**, so that "hướng
  dẫn" (instructions) never labels both.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

**Standard Vietnamese with "bạn"** in the interface, errors, notices, emails, the safety text and the guides alike: one
register across the app. Statements are plain declaratives; requests use the bare verb, with **hãy** where it softens a
command ("Hãy kiểm tra lại mật khẩu."), and **vui lòng** in error and warning text ("Vui lòng thử lại."). The Microsoft
style guide writes "bạn" for "you" and "của bạn" for "your", keeps the voice natural, and drops the subject where
Vietnamese allows it. Google's and Apple's Vietnamese pages read the same. No "quý khách" (shop-counter), "anh", "chị",
"em", "tôi" for the app (the app speaks as "chúng tôi" only where the English says "we"), no particles "nhé", "ạ", "nha". A
subject is dropped where Vietnamese allows it ("Đã lưu." for "Saved.").

- **Instructions** (guides and help): the bare verb: "Sàng men hai lần.", "Đeo khẩu trang lọc bụi đạt chuẩn khi cân nguyên liệu."
- **Prohibitions:** "đừng …" is informal and not used; **không** … ("Không dùng khí nén để thổi bụi.") for "do not", and
  **tuyệt đối không** … for "never". A rule with the law's force: **không được** … ("must not").
- **Should, must, may:** keep them apart. "must" → **phải** ("Sản phẩm phải được thử nghiệm."); "must not" → **không
  được**; "should" → **nên** ("Bạn nên đeo găng tay."); "should not" → **không nên**; "may" (a hazard, a possibility) →
  **có thể** ("có thể gây ung thư"; the H-statements use "có thể"); "may" (permission) → **được phép** or **có thể**.
  Never "phải" for "should", never "nên" for "must". "must be tested" → "phải được thử nghiệm" (the test is done, not
  passed); never "phải đạt". "some" (some fine quartz) → "một phần", "một ít" only for a small amount the English says is
  small; never "ít" for "some".

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- **Buttons and menu commands:** the bare verb or noun, as Vietnamese software writes it: "Lưu", "In", "So sánh", "Xóa",
  "Hủy", "Thêm nguyên liệu". Not "Hãy lưu", not "Lưu lại!"
- **Headings, tabs, page titles:** a noun phrase: "Công thức men", "Cài đặt", "Nhật ký nung", "Côn và công nhiệt". A heading
  that names an activity takes a verb phrase: "Trộn men", "Nhập lượng". A heading that is a question stays one: "Vì sao
  men chảy?"
- **Field labels:** a noun; keep the colon where the English has one, no space before it: "Tên nguyên liệu:".
- **Confirmations:** "Saved: {name}" → "Đã lưu: {name}"; "Removed" → "Đã xóa".
- **Sentence case** everywhere: only the first word and proper names take a capital; the English's Title Case means
  nothing here.

### Capitalisation

Sentence case; proper names, product names and acronyms (UMF, LOI, MKN, SDS) keep their capitals. Months are lower-case
("tháng 10"), days too. Headings and buttons are not in Title Case. Latin words and units keep the English's case.

### Script, tones and spelling

- **Unicode:** every Vietnamese text is in NFC (precomposed letters: ế, ử), never the decomposed form (e + combining
  marks); a copy from the web may carry NFD, which a search and the numbers check treat as other letters.
- **Tone marks** in the new style: hòa, khóa, thủy, quý, as the Choices above say.
- **Syllables** are written apart, each by a space ("trường thạch", "xương gốm", "tỷ trọng"); no hyphens in Vietnamese
  compounds; chemical names by the Vietnamese nomenclature with spaces ("canxi cacbonat", "natri cacbonat", "kẽm oxit",
  "titan đioxit") and the formula beside them where the English has it; the oxide spelled **oxit**, not ôxít.
- **Loanwords** from English that Vietnamese potters keep (frit, stain, ball clay, mesh, raku, engobe) stay in Latin
  letters, not transliterated. Latin words and trade names are never given diacritics.

### Punctuation

Vietnamese uses the Latin marks as English does, with no space before a mark (MS). No comma before "và" or "hoặc"
(the English's serial comma goes). The full stop goes after the closing quotation mark of a quoted sentence (MS 4.1.14),
and so does a comma or "!". The en dash for ranges with no spaces (10–60%); the em dash is avoided. A colon introduces a
list as in English.

### Numbers and units

- **Decimal comma and thousands point**, as CLDR's `vi` and the app format them: 1,5; 0,025; 12.500; the four-digit
  temperatures are not grouped (1222 °C, 1000 g), as the English writes them and the app prints them. MS: "5,25 cm", "1.526".
- **Percent:** 5%, with no space (CLDR `vi`), a range 10–60%.
- **Units:** the English's symbols, as the app prints them: g, kg, mg, µg, mL, L, mm, cm, °C, °F, °Bé, %, mg/m³, µg/cm²; not
  "gam", "độ C" in running text. Inches and feet are the words **inch** (MS "5 x 7,2 inch") and **foot**, with the metric figure in
  brackets as the English gives it: "12 inch (30 cm)".
- **A number the English writes as a word** ("two coats", "three tiles", "once") stays a word here, since a digit would be
  a figure the English lacks and the check rejects it: "hai lớp", "ba viên gạch thử", "một lần", "hai hoặc ba lần". Where
  the English has the digit, the digit stays: "3 coats" → "3 lớp".
- **Dates:** "14 tháng 10 năm 2026", "tháng 10 năm 2026". The check reads "tháng 10" as the English's October (the month
  as a figure on both sides), so the month is written as a number: never "14 Oct", never a month with the figure left out.
  The Gregorian calendar.
- **Clock times:** the 24-hour clock: **20:00**, **20:30**, or "20 giờ" on the hour. **With minutes write 20:30**, not "20
  giờ 30" (the check reads two numbers there). "8am to 5pm" → "8:00 đến 17:00" (or "8 giờ đến 17 giờ"). **Never "sáng",
  "trưa", "chiều", "tối" or "đêm" after the hour** ("8 giờ tối" is kept whole by the check and reported). "24 hours" → "24
  giờ".
- **No figures the English lacks:** no converted units, no added years, no SK or Orton equivalents.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Vietnamese one category: `node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('vi').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"`
prints `[ 'other' ]`.

| Category | Numbers               | Example                                                       |
| -------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `other`  | every number, 0, 1.5… | 1 nguyên liệu, 2 nguyên liệu, 21 công thức, 1,5 g, 3 lần nung |

Every plural message is written with `other` only, and the English's `one` is dropped:
`{count, plural, one {# material} other {# materials}}` → `{count, plural, other {# nguyên liệu}}`. `=0 {…}` stays where
the English has it (`=0 {Chưa có nguyên liệu}`). The noun does not change with the number and takes no "các" or "những"
after a figure. A **classifier** (loại từ) goes between the figure and the noun only where Vietnamese needs it:

| Counted thing                 | Form            | Example                     |
| ----------------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------- |
| materials, oxides, recipes    | no classifier   | # nguyên liệu, # công thức  |
| firings, coats, times         | lần, lớp        | # lần nung, # lớp men       |
| tiles                         | viên, tấm       | # viên mẫu thử              |
| pieces of ware                | sản phẩm, chiếc | # sản phẩm                  |
| cones                         | côn             | # côn                       |
| minutes, hours, days          | phút, giờ, ngày | # phút, # giờ, # ngày       |
| characters (password, a name) | ký tự           | tối thiểu # ký tự           |
| codes (recovery codes)        | mã              | # mã khôi phục              |
| devices, passkeys             | thiết bị, khóa  | # thiết bị, # khóa truy cập |

A weight or a temperature needs no classifier: `{n} g`.

### Placeholders and names

Vietnamese has no case endings, but a name often needs a generic noun in front of it ("công thức {name}", "nguyên liệu
{name}", "tại {region}", "thư viện {library}"). {region} is the country's name in Vietnamese from the browser (Đức, Hoa Kỳ).
A placeholder never begins a sentence as its subject without a noun; put a noun first.

### Cones

Orton cones are written "Orton" wherever the English says Orton: "côn Orton 6". SK cones are written "SK" with the number
as printed (SK 6a) where the English has them; Vietnam does not use them and Vietnamese potters do not name them, so the
Vietnamese pages rarely show one. **Seger** is written only where the English says Seger: the older European Seger cones
of the firing guide ("côn Seger") and the **công thức Seger** (the unity molecular formula, UMF), which is another thing
and never names a cone. Never give an SK number for an Orton number or the reverse, nor say they are equal. "one number
hotter" → "nóng hơn một bậc" and "one number cooler" → "nguội hơn một bậc" (native check), never "cao hơn", "thấp hơn"
(cone 05 is cooler than 04). "cone 06" is read "côn không sáu" and written 06, with its zero.

### The unity formula

**công thức Seger (UMF)**: write "công thức Seger (UMF)" once in a text and "công thức Seger" afterwards; the abbreviation
UMF stays Latin. The app's short "Unity formula" and "Unity" become "Công thức Seger" and "Seger"; "unity" as the scaling is
**chuẩn hóa về 1**. "Limit formula" is "công thức giới hạn". "Công thức" alone is a recipe or a formula: where the English
says "formula" for a chemical formula, write "công thức hóa học"; "recipe" is "công thức men".

### Gender-neutral wording

Vietnamese has no grammatical gender, but its pronouns carry age and sex: "bạn" is the neutral you, and generic nouns are
used for third persons: "người dùng" for the user, "thợ gốm" for a potter, never "ông", "bà", "anh", "chị" (MS 3.1).

### The reader in the guides

"Bạn" only where the sentence needs a subject; steps are bare verbs; explanations may be impersonal ("Men quá dày sẽ
chảy."). Quotations from sources are translated into “…” and keep their figures as the English writes them. Short plain
sentences, as the English.

### The app's feature names

Written exactly as on the button or tab, first letter capital, **without quotation marks**; where the English wraps a name
in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag. A name takes no ending, so attach a generic noun: "Chọn So sánh.", "tính năng So
sánh", "tab Nguyên liệu của tôi". "Settings" in running text is the ordinary noun "cài đặt"; inside `<settings>…</settings>`
write the word the sentence needs.

### Law in Vietnam

As the law writes it, never paraphrased: the words below are fixed, and the region texts must use them. The texts were not
read (see above): confirm each word against the regulation when the region data is added.

- **Limits:** "at most", "no more than" → **không quá**, **không vượt quá** or **tối đa** (the limit itself passes); "less
  than", "under" → **dưới** (it does not); "at least", "or more" → **tối thiểu**, **từ … trở lên**; "more than", "over" →
  **trên**, **vượt quá**. Never "dưới" for "at most". A slip moves a legal limit; check every one in the food-contact,
  silica and mask texts.
- **Silica:** QCVN 02:2019/BYT (Thông tư 02/2019/TT-BYT), the regulation on the permitted exposure limit values of five
  dusts at work ("giá trị giới hạn tiếp xúc cho phép"); **bụi hô hấp** (respirable dust), **silic tự do** or **silica tinh
  thể** for crystalline silica; the 8-hour average ("trung bình 8 giờ"). Native check.
- **Food contact:** QCVN 12-4:2015/BYT (Thông tư 47/2015/TT-BYT), "bao bì, dụng cụ làm bằng thủy tinh, gốm, sứ và tráng men
  tiếp xúc trực tiếp với thực phẩm"; limits as the regulation words them (**giới hạn thôi nhiễm**); the test is a leach in
  4% acetic acid (**axit axetic 4%**); the app's piece kinds as the regulation names them, once the text is read.
- **GHS:** **từ cảnh báo** (signal word: Nguy hiểm, Cảnh báo), **hình đồ cảnh báo** (pictogram), **câu cảnh báo nguy hiểm**
  (hazard statement), **phiếu an toàn hóa chất** (SDS); native check.
- **Who to call:** 113 (police), 114 (fire and rescue), 115 (medical emergency), all unverified in the region notes;

### Pitfalls

- **men:** a glaze is **men** (material and coat); "to glaze" **tráng men**. "Men" is also yeast, enzyme and probiotic:
  titles and descriptions say **men gốm**. **Sơn** (paint) and **vecni** (varnish) are never used for a glaze, and
  **men** on metal or on wall tiles ("gạch men") is a different thing from a potter's glaze.
- **frit and chất trợ chảy:** a frit is **frit** (a factory glass); a flux is **chất trợ chảy** (the oxide's role); not
  "chất trợ dung" (soldering) and not "thông lượng".
- **lò nung, lò nướng:** a potter's kiln is **lò nung** (lò nung điện, lò nung gas); **lò nướng** is a kitchen oven, used
  only where the English says oven.
- **gốm mộc and nung men:** bisque (the firing and the ware) is **gốm mộc** (nung mộc); the glaze firing is **nung men**.
  "Mộc" also means wood and plain, hence "gốm mộc" in full.
- **đất nung, sành, sứ:** earthenware is **gốm đất nung**, stoneware **gốm sành**, porcelain **sứ**; "gốm sứ" is ceramics
  in general and never names porcelain alone; "đồ sành" is old and rough.
- **silica, silic, silicon:** silica is the oxide **silica** (SiO₂); **silic** is the element Si (and the Ministry's "silic
  tự do" for free silica in a medical text); **silicon** is the rubber and also the chip: never use silicon for silica.
  The mineral is **thạch anh**.
- **canxi cacbonat, vôi, đá vôi:** whiting is **canxi cacbonat**; **vôi** is lime and quicklime; **đá vôi** is limestone and
  chalk. "Vôi" appears only in "lime" frit names and where the English means CaO.
- **bo, axit boric, hàn the:** B₂O₃ is **bo (B₂O₃)**, formally bo oxit; **axit boric** is boric acid; **borax** is borax,
  everyday **hàn the** (a banned food additive in Vietnam: write "borax").
- **soda, natri, kali:** "soda" (Na₂O) is **natri (Na₂O)**, formally natri oxit; soda ash is **soda** (natri cacbonat);
  "potash" (K₂O) is **kali (K₂O)**; the safety guide's potash is **kali cacbonat**.
- **bột màu and chất tạo màu:** a stain (factory-made) is **bột màu**; a colorant (any oxide that colours) is **chất tạo
  màu**.
- **khẩu trang, mặt nạ, máy thở, thông gió:** a respirator is **khẩu trang lọc bụi đạt chuẩn** (mặt nạ hô hấp in the formal sense; bare "khẩu trang lọc bụi" reads as an ordinary dust mask, so keep "đạt chuẩn" wherever the English says respirator; a plain "dust mask" stays khẩu trang lọc bụi); a
  hospital ventilator is **máy thở**, never meant here; ventilation (air in a room) is **thông gió**.
- **cẩm nang and hướng dẫn sử dụng:** the app's five guides are **cẩm nang**; a kiln's, controller's or sitter's manual is
  **hướng dẫn sử dụng**. Never the same word.
- **rạn nứt men and rạn men:** crazing is **rạn nứt men** (a defect); crackle is **rạn men** (wanted, as Bát Tràng's); a
  crack through the pot from cooling is **nứt do nhiệt** (dunting).
- **tỷ trọng and khối lượng riêng:** specific gravity is **tỷ trọng**, a ratio with no unit (1,45); **khối lượng riêng** is
  density in kg/m³ and is never used for a glaze's SG.
- **thôi nhiễm:** leaching is **thôi nhiễm**; the leach test is **thử nghiệm thôi nhiễm**, not narrowed to lead and cadmium.
- **công thức:** a recipe is **công thức men**, a chemical formula **công thức hóa học**, the unity formula **công thức
  Seger**: never bare "công thức" where two could be meant.
- **men lót:** underglaze is **màu dưới men**; **men lót** also means an under-layer glaze, so the liner glaze is **men phủ
  mặt trong**.
- **Placeholders:** see Placeholders and names.

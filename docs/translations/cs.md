# Čeština: styl a glosář (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into Czech (`cs`), for potters in Czechia. Read [README.md](README.md)
first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Czech message and guide follows this
page: one Czech word for one thing. The terms come from Czech suppliers' shops (Keramické centrum, Služby keramikům,
E-KERAMIKA), Czech potters' sources (ARTkeramika, the potters' association Keramikum, Kerlubna), a Czech university's
lectures on glazes and firing, the Czech manuals of two kiln makers (Nabertherm, Rohde), Czech Wikipedia, Czech law and
the EU's Czech texts, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows
are for a Czech potter to check. IATE could not be read (its search runs only as a script app), so no term here comes
from it.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal throughout: _vykání_, the second person plural. Instructions use its imperative: „Vyberte…“,
„Prosejte glazuru dvakrát.“, „Noste respirátor.“ Never _ty_ (tykání). Write _vy_, _váš_, _vám_ in lower case in the
app and the guides: MS (section 4.1.10) prescribes the neutral second-person plural, and the capital _Vy_ is the Czech
courtesy for one addressee in a letter. Capitalise _Vy_, _Váš_, _Vám_ only in the two password emails
(`server/lib/account_mail.ts`), which are letters to one account holder. Use _prosím_ only where the English has
"please" („Zkuste to prosím znovu.“). „My“ for the app is fine where the English says "we" („Doporučujeme…“).

**Avoid the second-person past tense.** Czech marks gender in it (_uložil jste_ / _uložila jste_). Use the present
tense, the long adjective form (MS 4.1.9: „Recept je uložený.“), a reflexive or impersonal form („Recept se
nepodařilo uložit.“), or a noun. Where a past form addressed to the reader cannot be avoided, use the plural participle
as MS does: „Ujistěte se, že jste zavřeli všechny soubory.“ Confirmations: "Saved: …" → „Uloženo: …“.

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs.** Czech software (Windows and Office in Czech; MS's table of
commands: „Uložit“, „Uložit jako“, „Otevřít“, „Zavřít“, „Zrušit“, „Odstranit“):

- Buttons and commands: the **infinitive**: „Uložit“, „Uložit jako kopii“, „Porovnat“, „Přidat suroviny“,
  „Zahodit zkušební přístup“. Not the imperative („Uložte“, which belongs in running text: „Klikněte na Uložit.“) and
  not a verbal noun („Uložení“). The one noun command is the established „Tisk“ (Print), as in Czech Windows.
- Headings, menu names, tab names and page titles: a **noun phrase**: „Recepty“, „Suroviny“, „Nastavení“, „Záznamy
  výpalů“. A heading that is a question or an instruction in English may stay one („Na co si dát pozor“).
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: „Název suroviny:“. Where a placeholder would need a
  case ending, MS's colon form avoids it: "{count} materials" → „Počet surovin: {count}“ (or use a plural, below).
- In running text, name a control by a descriptor noun and then its label exactly as written, uninflected, without
  quotation marks (MS: Czech does not quote software references): „tlačítko Porovnat“, „na kartě Standardní“, „na
  stránce Suroviny“, „v nastavení Hustota glazury“. The descriptor takes the case. Where the English wraps the name in
  `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag.

**Capitalisation.** Czech rules ([ÚJČ](http://prirucka.ujc.cas.cz/?id=180)): only the first word of a heading, button,
label or tab has a capital, plus proper names. „Porovnat dva recepty“, not „Porovnat Dva Recepty“; "Fired Weight" →
„Hmotnost po výpalu“. Possessive adjectives made from names keep the capital: _Segerův vzorec_, _Ortonova žároměrka_.
Languages and months are lower case (_anglický_, _v říjnu_).

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: „…“ (U+201E, U+201C); inside them ‚…‘ (U+201A, U+2018). Not "…" and not »…« (book style).
- Apostrophe: the typographic ’ (U+2019); Czech hardly needs one. Never the English possessive: _Ortonova_, not
  _Orton’s_. Write `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`, `}` or `#`, and in `safety`, `regions` and
  `records`).
- No space before ; : ! ?
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, the unit once at the end: „1222–1240 °C“, „žároměrka 06–04“, „1–3 %“ (MS:
  "str. 3–7", "100–240 V"). In words, „od 1222 do 1240 °C“; never mix („od 1222–1240 °C“). As a sentence dash, the en
  dash with spaces („ – “); MS: the em dash is not used in Czech.
- Formulas and "×10⁻⁶/°C" stay as written.

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: „1,5 %“, „0,01 g“, „1,45“. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped („1240 °C“, „1000 g“), as the app prints them (`useGrouping:
'min2'`; plain `Intl` for `cs` would print „1 240“, so do not copy that). Five digits and more take a no-break space:
  „12 500 g“. Never a full stop or comma as a thousands separator.
- A no-break space before °C, %, and every unit: „1240 °C“, „5 %“, „100 °C/h“, „80 mesh“ (MS 4.1.16). Czech writes the
  number and % together only when they form an adjective („5% roztok“ = pětiprocentní); rephrase so the number is a
  noun: „roztok s 5 % …“, „glazura s 2 % kobaltu“. Shops print „1240°C“; do not copy that.
- A number in the English stays a figure, even below 11 where MS would spell it out (the README wins).

**Plural forms.** CLDR gives Czech `one`, `few`, `many`, `other`
(`new Intl.PluralRules('cs').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories`):

| Category | Numbers                                                  | Example                                                      |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `one`    | 1                                                        | 1 materiál, 1 surovina, 1 recept                             |
| `few`    | 2, 3, 4                                                  | 2 materiály, 3 suroviny, 4 recepty                           |
| `many`   | decimals: 0,5, 1,5, 2,5                                  | 1,5 materiálu, 0,5 suroviny, 2,5 receptu (genitive singular) |
| `other`  | 0, 5 and up, also 11–14, 21, 22–24 (CLDR: 22 is `other`) | 5 materiálů, 0 surovin, 22 receptů (genitive plural)         |

`{count, plural, one {# surovina} few {# suroviny} many {# suroviny} other {# surovin}}`. Write all four even where
two read the same; `=0 {…}` and `#` stay as the English has them. The verb and adjectives follow the form: `one` agrees
in the noun's gender and singular („Byla přidána # surovina“, „# uložený recept“); `few` takes the plural („Byly
přidány # suroviny“, „# uložené recepty“); `many` and `other` take the **neuter singular** verb and a genitive noun
(„Bylo přidáno # surovin“, „# uložených receptů“). A count in a placeholder without a plural needs a plural or the
colon form above.

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). Czech potters call any pyrometric cone a **žároměrka** (f;
gen. _žároměrky_, acc. _žároměrku_; pl. _žároměrky_, gen. pl. _žároměrek_): AK's article on cones, SK's „Žároměrka“,
and the category „Pyrometrické kužely (žároměrky)“ on the Czech pages of 1240.design (a Polish shop). Czech shops sell
them labelled by temperature (SK „Žároměrka 1200°C“), and AK says Seger's series is „nejrozšířenější v Evropě“ while
„řada Ortonova … číslování se od Segerovy řady liší“. So:

- "cone 6" → „žároměrka 6“; "cone 06" → „žároměrka 06“, read „nula šest“ (the zero stays: 06 is far cooler than 6).
  Inflect the noun, never the number: „do žároměrky 6“, „na žároměrku 6“, „u žároměrky 06“.
- "fire to cone 6" → „vypalovat na žároměrku 6“; "a cone 6 glaze" → „glazura na žároměrku 6“; "cone 04 bisque" →
  „přežah na žároměrku 04“; "cone 5–6" → „žároměrka 5–6“.
- "Orton cone" → „Ortonova žároměrka“ (pl. _Ortonovy žároměrky_); at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems
  are compared: „Ortonova žároměrka 6“, then „žároměrka 6“.
- **Never** write „SK 6“, „Segerova žároměrka 6“ or a temperature-labelled cone („žároměrka 1220 °C“) for an Orton
  number. „Segerovy žároměrky (SK)“ appear only where the English says "Seger cones". Do not use „kužel“ alone (also an
  ice-cream or traffic cone); „pyrometrický kužel“ may explain _žároměrka_ once.

**The unity formula.** „**Segerův vzorec**“ (m; gen. _Segerova vzorce_, loc. _v Segerově vzorci_), with „(UMF)“ after it
the first time on a page: „Segerův vzorec (UMF)“; after that „Segerův vzorec“. KM: „Tzv. Segerův neboli molární
vzorec“; VUT: „Složení glazur: Segerův vzorec“. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are
all „Segerův vzorec“ / „Segerův vzorec:“. Not the literal „jednotkový molekulový vzorec“; „molární vzorec“ (KM) is the
other name, not to be mixed in.

**Gender-neutral wording.** Czech marks gender in past tenses and in nouns for people. Address the reader with _vy_
and avoid second-person past forms (above). For people in general use plurals, roles or relative clauses: „lidé“, „kdo
glazuje“, „všichni v domácnosti“, „osoba“ (MS 3.1). The generic masculine plural („keramici“) is read as neutral; a
pair form, feminine first as MS orders it („keramičky a keramici“), only where the English stresses both. No slashes,
brackets or asterisks (_keramik/čka_, _keramik(čka)_) in running text. Grammatical gender of things is unaffected.

**The reader in the guides.** _Vy_ and the second-person plural imperative, in short plain sentences, as the English:
„Prosejte glazuru dvakrát sítem 80 mesh.“, „Navažte suroviny.“ Not the infinitive as an instruction in running text
(„Glazuru prosít.“ is a button style), not an impersonal „se“ where the English says "you" („Glazura se prosévá…“), not
nominal style („Provedení prosévání…“). Keep the force of the English: "must" → „musíte“, „je nutné“; "should" →
„měli byste“, „je vhodné“; "never" → „nikdy“. Quotations from sources are translated into „…“ and keep their figures as
the English writes them.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with a capital first letter, after a descriptor noun in
running text (above); without a descriptor, use the ordinary noun („Porovnání ukazuje…“). "Settings" is never quoted:
„v nastavení účtu“. The fixed names:

| English                       | Czech (button, label)           | In running text                             |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Compare                       | Porovnat                        | porovnání (n); tlačítko Porovnat            |
| Suggest amounts (and compare) | Navrhnout množství (a porovnat) | tlačítko Navrhnout množství a porovnat      |
| Match with what I have        | Sestavit z toho, co mám         | funkce Sestavit z toho, co mám              |

"Match with what I have" and "My materials" stay in the first person, as the user speaking to the app.

**EU directives and regulations.** As the EU's Czech texts write them: „směrnice Rady 84/500/EHS“ (EHS, Evropské
hospodářské společenství, is the Czech for EEC), amended by „směrnice Komise 2005/31/ES“; „směrnice Evropského
parlamentu a Rady (EU) 2017/2398“; „nařízení (ES) č. 1272/2008“ (CLP). 84/500 CS's title: „Směrnice Rady ze dne 15.
října 1984 o sbližování právních předpisů členských států týkajících se keramických předmětů určených pro styk s
potravinami“. In running text _směrnice_ and _nařízení_ are lower case. Never „84/500/EEC“ or „EWG“.

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **_glazura_** is the right word for a glaze in Czech (shops, KM, VUT, cs.wikipedia), unlike Polish, where _glazura_ is
  wall tile. _poleva_ is the older, folk word (and a cake icing): use it only for historical lead glazes. _smalt_ is
  enamel on metal, _lazura_ a wood stain.
- **_tavidlo_** is also soldering flux (_pájecí tavidlo_); shops label bone ash „tavidlo“ (KC). The glaze flux is
  **_tavivo_** (KM, VUT).
- **_křemík_** is silicon (the element), **_křemen_** quartz, **_oxid křemičitý_** silica (SiO₂), **_silikon_**
  silicone; avoid _silika_.
- **_hlína_** is clay, **_hliník_** aluminium, **_oxid hlinitý_** alumina; _hlinitý_ in everyday Czech also means
  "loamy". Never translate alumina with a clay word.
- **_soda_** is sodium carbonate (_soda kalcinovaná_), **_jedlá soda_** baking soda, **_potaš_** potassium carbonate.
  The potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are _sodík_ / _oxid sodný_ (Na₂O) and _draslík_ / _oxid draselný_ (K₂O).
- **_vápno_** is building lime, **_vápenec_** limestone, **_křída_** chalk (the shops' whiting), **_vápník_** calcium.
- **_kalivo_** (opacifier) makes a glaze _zakalená_ (opaque, wanted); a milky defect is _mléčný zákal_. Keep them apart.
- **_biskvit_** is also unglazed porcelain and a sponge cake; use _přežah_ for the firing and _přežahnutý střep_ for the
  ware.
- **_kamenina_** is stoneware. German _Steingut_ is Czech _pórovina_ (white earthenware); the Czech kiln manuals'
  _hliněná keramika_ / _hliněné zboží_ (NAB, ROH) are calques of German _Irdenware_: write _hrnčina_ (2.3).
- **_váha_** is the weighing scale, but also colloquially "weight": write _hmotnost_ for a weight.
- **_respirátor_** in Czech is the FFP2/FFP3 mask (safe to use); a hospital ventilator is _plicní ventilátor_, and a
  _rouška_ is a cloth or surgical mask, not protection.
- **_regulátor_**, not NAB's _kontrolér_; **_výdrž_**, not NAB's _doba setrvání_; **_pecní plát_**, not NAB's _montážní
  deska_: NAB's Czech is translated from German and reads stiffly.
- **_plát_** (m) and the shops' colloquial _plato_ (n, pl. _plata_): write _plát_.
- **_dávka_** is a batch of glaze mixed at once; **_šarže_** a supplier's production run (lot).
- **_harisy_** (from German _Haarrisse_) is workshop jargon for crazing: write _trhlinkování_.
- **mesh**: Czech shops sell glaze sieves by diameter only (SK „Síto na glazuru průměr 24cm“). Keep "80 mesh" as
  „80 mesh“, never „80 ok“.
- **Slovak** looks close but differs (_glazúra_, _kremeň_, _živec_ is shared): write standard Czech only.
- **L for litres**: Czech writes _l_, but keep the English's symbol (`L`, `mL`) so the numbers check passes; both are SI.

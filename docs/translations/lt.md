# Lietuvių kalba: stiliaus taisyklės ir terminynas (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Lithuanian (`lt`), standard written Lithuanian (bendrinė kalba) for potters in Lithuania.
Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Lithuanian
message and guide follows this page: one Lithuanian word for one thing. The terms come from the Lithuanian shop that
sells the full range of glaze raw materials, kilns and cones (Keramikams.lt, Panevėžys, read through the Internet
Archive because its live site answers only a bot check), Nabertherm's Lithuanian controller manual, the Visuotinė lietuvių
enciklopedija, a Lithuanian teachers' ceramics handbook and studio pages, the Lithuanian texts of EU law, the
Lithuanian hygiene norm HN 23:2011 for the silica limit, IATE and the Microsoft Lithuanian style guide, not from a
dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so: those rows are for a Lithuanian potter to
check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Keramikams.lt sells the whole range (feldspars, kaolin, whiting, frits,
oxides, stains, kilns, kiln furniture and cones), so the material, frit, cone and kiln-furniture names below are what
is printed on its bags and product pages. Lithuanian potters' own writing on chemistry, defects and firing is thin:
no Lithuanian glaze textbook, frit data sheet or potters' guild glossary could be found or read, so the unity-formula
words, most defect names and the kiln-sitter parts rest on one source or none. Rows marked "No source" are the
translator's best term.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

Formal: the second person plural, _jūs_, written in lower case, as Lithuanian software does. MS (4.1.11): "Always use the
second-person plural (jūs) to address the user. Normally, the pronoun „jūs“ itself won't be needed, but if it's used,
make it lowercase." Possessives are left out where the meaning allows (MS: "Insert your USB drive." → "Prijunkite USB
atmintinę."). Never _tu_. The forms:

- **Buttons and commands**: the **infinitive**, as Lithuanian Windows and Office write them (MS 4.1.3: "Save all." →
  "Įrašyti viską."; 4.1.9: "Click Save." → "Spustelėkite mygtuką Įrašyti."): „Įrašyti“, „Spausdinti“, „Palyginti“,
  „Pašalinti“, „Atšaukti“. Not the imperative („Įrašykite“) and not a noun („Įrašymas“) on a button.
- **Instructions** in guides, help and messages: the **imperative plural**: „Persijokite glazūrą du kartus.“,
  „Užsidėkite respiratorių.“, „Neįkvėpkite dulkių.“ (CLP P260 writes the infinitive, „Neįkvėpti
  dulkių/dūmų/dujų/rūko/garų/aerozolio.“, which is the label's form, not the app's: the app speaks to the reader).
- **Errors**: a short statement ending in a full stop, the key word last (MS 5.5.2: „Failo rasti nepavyko.“,
  „Atminties nepakanka.“, „Būtina užpildyti.“): "We can't find that recipe." → „Recepto rasti nepavyko.“; "Please try
  again." → „Bandykite dar kartą.“ (MS 2.2.1: „Pabandykite dar kartą.“). Leave out "please" unless the English insists.
- **Questions**: „Ar norite tęsti?“ (MS 2.2.1); "Remove "{name}"?" → „Pašalinti „{name}“?“.
- **Confirmations**: the neuter participle first (MS 4.1.7: "Modified" → „Modifikuota“): "Saved: …" → „Įrašyta: …“.
- "we": the app speaks as "we" where the English does (MS 2.2.4: „suaktyvinsime „Windows““): „Rekomenduojame …“.

Examples: a button "Save as a copy" → „Įrašyti kaip kopiją“; an error "The recipe could not be saved." → „Recepto
įrašyti nepavyko.“; a guide sentence "Sieve the glaze twice through an 80 mesh sieve." → „Persijokite glazūrą du kartus
per 80 mesh sietą.“

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- Buttons and menu commands: the infinitive, as above. A command that opens a further step keeps the ellipsis with no
  space before it (MS 4.1.12): „Spausdinti…“.
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase** (MS 4.1.10: titles are nouns, verbs turned
  into nouns in _-ymas, -imas, -tis_): „Receptai“, „Nustatymai“, „Degimo žurnalai“, „Piroskopai ir šilumos poveikis“.
  "Mixing the glaze" → „Glazūros maišymas“; "Typing amounts" → „Kiekių įvedimas“. A heading that is a question stays
  one, and so does an English "where to …", "how much …" („Kur pirkti“, „Kiek vandens“).
- **Guide headings** follow the same rule, as Polish does: an imperative English heading becomes a noun phrase ("Avoid
  the powder where you can" → „Miltelių vengimas, kur įmanoma“; "Clean wet" → „Drėgnas valymas“; "Be there when the
  firing ends" → „Buvimas šalia degimo pabaigoje“; "Keeping dust in the studio" → „Dulkių sulaikymas dirbtuvėje“;
  "Names change, so buy by chemistry" → „Pavadinimai keičiasi, todėl svarbi cheminė sudėtis“), never „Venkite …“ or
  „Valykite …“. The section text below it keeps the instruction in the imperative („Plaukite ir šluostykite
  vandeniu“), so safety advice loses none of its force. One English heading has one Lithuanian heading in every guide
  ("Lead-free is not the same as food-safe" → „Bešvinė glazūra – dar ne tinkama liestis su maistu“).
- Options and radio buttons never end with a full stop (MS 4.1.12); tooltips neither.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: „Žaliavos pavadinimas:“.
- Options in which the reader speaks to the app keep the first person, infinitive: "Match with what I have" →
  „Sudaryti iš to, ką turiu“.

### Capitalisation

Sentence case everywhere (MS 4.1.5): only the first word and proper names take a capital, in headings, buttons, labels,
tabs and table headings too. No English Title Case: "Fired Weight" → „Masė po degimo“. After a colon the sentence goes
on in lower case unless what follows is a list item or a name. Short text in brackets that is not a sentence starts lower
case: "(Recommended)" → „(rekomenduojama)“ (MS). Languages, nationalities, months and days are lower case (_anglų
kalba_, _vokiška kreida_, _spalio 9 d._). Names keep their form (Orton, Seger, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc); a Lithuanian
ending on a person's name attaches directly: _Segerio_, _Ortono_. Orton as a subject is „Ortonas“ („Ortonas pažymi“,
„pasak Ortono“); after a generic noun the company keeps its name („bendrovė Orton“).

### Punctuation

- Quotation marks: „…“ (U+201E, U+201C), as MS (4.1.12) writes them. Not "…", not the English “…”, not »…«. Inside them,
  ‚…‘ (U+201A, U+2018) (common Lithuanian practice; no source read). Use quotation marks for quotations and for names the
  reader typed („{name}“, as the English has "{name}"), never around a button or page name (MS: "Click "Save"" →
  „Spustelėkite Įrašyti.“).
- Apostrophe: Lithuanian rarely needs one (it attaches an ending to a foreign name whose last letter is not
  pronounced). Use the typographic ’ (U+2019) if one is needed; write `''` only where the README asks (just before `{`,
  `}` or `#`, and in the `safety`, `regions` and `records` files).
- No space before ; : ! ?
- Ranges: the en dash **with no spaces**, the unit once at the end (MS 4.1.12: „700–800 USD“): „1222–1240 °C“,
  „piroskopai 06–04“, „1–3 %“. A dash in a sentence is the en dash with a space on each side („Piešiamasis slaptažodis –
  naujas būdas“, MS 2.2.3). Never the em dash, which Lithuanian does not use (MS). The dash in a label such as "Fluxes -
  RO" is the spaced en dash: „Fliusai – RO“.
- Ratios: a colon with a space on each side: „SiO₂ : Al₂O₃“, „8,5 : 1“.
- Abbreviations take no full stops when they are acronyms (MS 4.1.2: "U.S.A." → „JAV“); shortened words take one: _pvz._,
  _žr._, _val._, _m._, _d._ (MS 4.1.1).

### Numbers and units

- Decimal comma: „1,5 %“, „0,01 g“, „1,45“. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: CLDR groups Lithuanian numbers with a space from four digits (`Intl.NumberFormat('lt')` prints „1 222,5“),
  but the app prints four-digit numbers ungrouped ("1222 °C", `useGrouping: 'min2'` in `client/app/shared/format.ts`).
  So write **four-digit numbers ungrouped** („1240 °C“, „1000 g“), and group five digits and more with a **no-break
  space** U+00A0 („12 500 g“). Never a plain space (the check reads 12 500 with a plain space as two numbers), never a
  dot or a comma.
- A space before °C, °F, % and every unit, no-break where possible: „1240 °C“, „15 %“, „100 °C/val.“, „80 mesh“. KER
  prints „1050°C“: do not copy it.
- "per hour" is _val._ (valanda): "100 °C/h" → „100 °C/val.“ (NAB: „sparta [°/val.]“). The figure and the °C stay.
- A number in the English is a number in the Lithuanian, with the same unit symbol: „3 sluoksniai“, never „trys
  sluoksniai“; „15 %“, never „penkiolika procentų“.
- Dates: „2026-10-09“ (MS 4.1.12, the ISO order) or „2026 m. spalio 9 d.“ in running text. A year alone takes _m._:
  „nuo 2019 m.“.
- Time: the 24-hour clock, „20:00“; "8pm" → „20 val.“; "8 to 5" → „8–17 val.“ or „nuo 8 iki 17 val.“.
- Inches and feet: „colis“ (pl. coliai, gen. pl. colių) and „pėda“ (pl. pėdos, gen. pl. pėdų), the same in every file,
  with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives it: „2 coliai (51 mm)“, „18 colių (46 cm)“, „3 pėdos (91 cm)“.

### Plural forms

CLDR gives Lithuanian four categories:
`node -e "console.log(new Intl.PluralRules('lt').resolvedOptions().pluralCategories)"` prints
`[ 'one', 'few', 'many', 'other' ]`.

| Category | Numbers                                   | The noun after the number | Example                                            |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `one`    | 1, 21, 31, 101 … (not 11)                 | nominative singular       | 1 žaliava, 21 receptas, 1 gramas, 1 glazūra        |
| `few`    | 2–9, 22–29, 102–109 … (not 12–19)         | nominative plural         | 2 žaliavos, 5 receptai, 9 gramai, 3 glazūros       |
| `many`   | numbers with a fraction: 0,5, 1,5, 2,25 … | genitive singular         | 1,5 žaliavos, 0,5 recepto, 2,5 gramo, 1,5 glazūros |
| `other`  | 0, 10–20, 30, 40 … 100, 110–119 …         | genitive plural           | 0 žaliavų, 10 receptų, 15 gramų, 11 glazūrų        |

- Write all four, even where two read alike (_žaliavos_ is both `few` and `many`):
  `{count, plural, one {# receptas} few {# receptai} many {# recepto} other {# receptų}}`. `=0 {…}` and `#` stay as the
  English has them; `=0` reads „nėra receptų“.
- Agreement follows the category: `one` singular („1 receptas įrašytas“), `few` plural („3 receptai įrašyti“), `other`
  genitive plural with the verb or participle in the neuter („10 receptų įrašyta“). Put adjectives and participles
  inside each branch.
- A number in another case still needs every branch: "in {count} recipes" →
  `one {# recepte} few {# receptuose} many {# recepto} other {# receptų}` (with 10 and above the noun stays genitive
  plural in every case). Where a number is not a plural argument ("up to {most} materials"), rephrase so no noun has to
  agree with it: "The shelf is a list of up to {most} materials." → „Turimų žaliavų gali būti ne daugiau kaip
  {most}.“

### Cones

The app's cones are **Orton cones**. The Lithuanian shop sells them as **piroskopas** ("pyroscope", as in Russian
_пироскоп_), with Orton numbers and temperatures on the label: KER „Piroskopas 06 (1011°C)“,
„Piroskopas 05,5 (1023°C)“, „Piroskopas 6 (1241°C)“, „Piroskopas 10 (1303°C)“ (05½ exists only in the Orton series),
„Piroskopų stovelis“. Its text calls the object a cone: „Naudojant didelius kūgius reikia statyti į kūgių stovelį“,
„trys skirtingų temperatūrų kūgeliai“. Studios fire by °C (MV: „Žemas degimas iki 1050°C“, „Aukštas degimas iki
1250°C“; TU: glaze firing at „1020°C–1060°C“). So:

- "cone" → „piroskopas“ (m; gen. piroskopo; pl. piroskopai). Explain once on a page where it helps: „piroskopas –
  plonas keraminis kūgis, kuris linksta gavęs tam tikrą šilumos poveikį“. Not „kūgis“ alone for the object (it is the
  geometric shape and the ice-cream cone), though „kūgis“ may describe its shape.
- "cone 6" → „piroskopas 6“; "cone 06" → „piroskopas 06“ (the zero stays; read „nulis šeši“; piroskopas 06 is far cooler
  than piroskopas 6). The number follows the noun and stays bare; the **noun takes the case**: "fire to cone 6" →
  „degti iki piroskopo 6“; "at cone 6" → „ties piroskopu 6“; "a cone 6 glaze" → „glazūra piroskopui 6“; "cone 5–6" →
  „piroskopai 5–6“; "cone 04 bisque" → „biskvitinis degimas iki piroskopo 04“. Never „6 piroskopas“ (it reads as a
  count) and never „6-asis piroskopas“.
- **Hotter and cooler, never higher and lower** (in the cooler series a higher number is cooler: piroskopas 05 is cooler
  than piroskopas 04): "one cone hotter" → „vienu numeriu karštesnis piroskopas“; "one cone cooler" → „vienu numeriu
  vėsesnis piroskopas“; "from cone 6 up" → „nuo piroskopo 6 ir karštesnių“; "below cone 03" → „vėsesniems nei
  piroskopas 03“. Never „aukštesnis“, „žemesnis“, „didesnis“ or „mažesnis“ piroskopas, and never „aukštyn“ or „žemyn“
  for cones: "go one cone hotter in the sitter" → „į išjungiklį dėkite vienu numeriu karštesnį piroskopą“; "by a cone
  or more" → „vienu piroskopo numeriu ar daugiau“. Temperatures and switch settings may still be higher or lower
  („žemesnėje temperatūroje“, „žemesnės jungiklių padėtys“), and soft and hard bisque are „žemesnis“ and „aukštesnis
  biskvitinis degimas“ (2.3).
- "Orton cone" → „Ortono piroskopas“; at the first mention on a page, and wherever systems are compared: „Ortono
  piroskopas 6“, then „piroskopas 6“.
- Seger cones („Segerio piroskopai“, SK) are a different scale. **Never** write „Segerio piroskopas 6“ or „SK 6“ for an
  Orton number; „Segerio piroskopai“ only where the English says "Seger cones".

### The unity formula

„Segerio formulė“ (f), with „(UMF)“ after it the first time on a page: „Segerio formulė (UMF)“, then „Segerio formulė“
(gen. Segerio formulės, loc. Segerio formulėje). The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity formula" and "Unity:" are
all „Segerio formulė“ / „Segerio formulė:“. **No Lithuanian source names it**: no Lithuanian glaze-calculation text was
found. This is the name the neighbouring languages use after Seger (German _Segerformel_, Polish _wzór Segera_, Russian
_формула Зегера_), with the name in its original spelling and a Lithuanian ending, as Lithuanian writes foreign names in
non-fiction. Older texts translated from Russian may write „Zegerio formulė“: not the app's. Never a literal „vienetinė
molekulinė formulė“. For a Lithuanian potter to confirm.

### Gender-neutral wording

Lithuanian nouns and participles have gender. MS (3.1) asks for plurals in generalisations („visi asmenys“, „kolegos“),
no gendered pronouns in generic references, and plural verbs instead of masculine participles: "Confirm you have read
the terms" → „Patvirtinkite, kad perskaitėte sąlygas“, not „… esate perskaitęs“. The formal _jūs_ with a plural verb
needs no gender. A potter is „keramikas“ / „keramikė“: prefer the plural „keramikai“ or speak to the reader. No slashed
forms in the UI; where a person noun cannot be avoided, MS allows the ending in brackets sparingly („Kiekvienas (-a)“).

### The reader in the guides

_jūs_ and the imperative plural in short plain sentences, as the English: „Persijokite glazūrą du kartus per 80 mesh
sietą.“ Explanations may use the passive present („Glazūra maišoma su vandeniu“) where the English is impersonal, but
where the English says "you", say _jūs_ through the verb („Jei degate iki piroskopo 6, …“). Prefer verbs to nouns (MS
4.1.15: "verbal phrases are more common and more natural than nominal phrases"): not „atliekamas glazūros persijojimas“
but „persijokite glazūrą“. Quotations from sources are translated into „…“ and keep their figures as the English writes
them.

### The app's feature names

Written exactly as on the button or tab, first letter capital, **not inflected** and **without quotation marks** (MS
4.1.9, 4.1.12). A generic noun before the name takes the case: „spustelėkite mygtuką Palyginti“, „kortelėje
Standartinės“, „puslapyje Žaliavos“, „funkcija Pakeisti šviną“. Where the English wraps the name in `<b>` or another tag,
keep the tag. "Settings" in running text is the ordinary noun: „nustatymuose“; inside `<settings>…</settings>` write the
form the sentence needs („<settings>nustatymuose</settings>“).

### Pitfalls

- **Glazūra** is a glaze (VLE, KER), and also icing on a cake; **emalė** is enamel on metal (and _dantų emalis_ tooth
  enamel); **lakas** is varnish; **glaistas** is the old folk word for a glaze (TU: puodai „be glaisto“) and today means
  putty. Use glazūra only; „glazūravimas“ is the act of glazing.
- **Molis** is clay, and **molis** is also the SI unit mole (mol). Worse, **molio masė** is a clay body (KER „Žaliavos
  glazūroms ir molio masėms“) and IATE also gives it for molar mass. So: a clay body is „molio masė“; molecular weight is
  „molekulinė masė“ (KER „Molekulinė masė: 100,1“), never „molio masė“; and the mole is written with its symbol where the
  sentence allows („0,3 mol CaO“), or as „molis (mol)“ at its first use on a page.
- **Fritas** (m; KER „Fritai“, IATE) is a frit; **fliusas** (KER, IATE) is a flux. TU's „fritos“ is an older feminine
  form: not the app's. „lydiklis“ (seen on general sites for feldspar's role) is not the app's word for flux.
- **Fajansas** in Lithuanian is industrial white earthenware with a high bisque (VLE: first fired at 1250–1280 °C);
  **majolika** is tin- or colour-glazed red ware; **terakota** unglazed red ware. Earthenware in the app's sense is
  „žemo degimo keramika“ (2.3).
- **Kreida** (whiting, CaCO₃; KER „Kreida 190 CaCO3“) is not **kalkės** (lime), **klintys** (limestone) or **kalcitas**
  (calcite, which KER sells separately as „Kalcitas 905/“).
- **The material is not the oxide.** **Soda** is sodium carbonate (KER „Natrio karbonatas Soda 47“; IATE) and
  **potašas** potassium carbonate (IATE); the potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are „natris (Na₂O)“ and „kalis (K₂O)“.
  „kalcis (CaO)“ is the oxide, „kreida“ the material.
- **Silicis** is the element silicon (lt.wikipedia's Glazūra article uses it loosely for silica: do not copy it),
  **silikonas** the rubber, **silikatas** a silicate. The oxide is „silicio dioksidas“, the material „kvarcas“.
- **Respiratorius** is the mask (KER „Respiratorius FFP1 su vožtuvu“; IATE: „respiratorius / dalelių filtravimo
  kaukė“); a hospital ventilator is „dirbtinės plaučių ventiliacijos aparatas“ (IATE). But **ventiliatorius** is a fan:
  never use it for a ventilator or a respirator.
- **Krosnis** is a kiln and also a household stove; the kitchen oven is **orkaitė**. Write „keramikos krosnis“ where the
  context does not settle it. KER also writes „krosnelė“ for small kilns: the app writes „krosnis“.
- **Degti** is to fire (transitive: „degti dirbinius“) and also to burn; **degimas** is a firing and also combustion. A
  fire hazard is „gaisras“, flames „liepsna“.
- **Šukė** is the fired body of a piece (VLE kraklė: „nevienodai plečiantis šukei ir glazūrai“) and also a potsherd.
- **Biskvitas** is bisque and also a sponge cake; **lentyna** is a shelf of any kind (KER uses it beside „plokštė“ for
  kiln shelves): the app's kiln shelf is „krosnies plokštė“ and its "shelf" of materials on hand is „turimos žaliavos“.
- **Santykinis tankis** (specific gravity) is a ratio to water with no unit; **tankis** is density in kg/m³ (VLE); IATE's
  „savitasis svoris“ is weight per volume in physics: not the app's (2.6).
- **Ribinė formulė** (limit formula) is a guide; **ribinė vertė** and **ribinis dydis** are legal limits (HN 23).
- **Placeholders and trade names cannot be inflected.** Put a generic noun in the needed case before them: „žaliava
  {name}“, „recepte „{name}““, „fritas Ferro 3134“, „su fritu Ferro 3134“, „žaliavos Gerstley Borate“. `{region}` is
  the country's name in the nominative, as the browser gives it („Lietuva“, „Jungtinė Karalystė“): build the message so
  it stands alone after a colon or in brackets: "Shops in {region}" → „Parduotuvės: {region}“; "Who to call in {region}"
  → „Kur skambinti: {region}“; "Limits for ceramic ware in {region}" → „Keramikos gaminių ribos ({region})“; "We found
  no shop selling raw glaze materials in {region}." → „Neradome parduotuvės, prekiaujančios glazūrų žaliavomis
  ({region}).“ Never „{region}je“.
- **"should" and "must"** in safety text (README): "must" → „turi“, „privalo“ or „būtina“ („Indas turi atitikti …“,
  „Būtina dėvėti respiratorių“); "must not" → „negalima“, „draudžiama“; "should" → „turėtų“ or „reikėtų“ (the
  conditional: „Vaikai neturėtų žiūrėti į krosnį“). „neturi“ is "must not" (and "does not have"), so "should not" is
  always „neturėtų“ or „nereikėtų“. "must be tested" means the test must be done: „būtina ištirti“, „turi būti atliktas
  laboratorinis metalų išsiskyrimo tyrimas“, never „turi išlaikyti tyrimą“ (pass it). "some" is the partitive genitive,
  not "a little": "may contain some fine quartz" → „gali būti smulkaus kvarco“, never „šiek tiek“ or „nedaug kvarco“.
  Where the English only reports a source's figure, do not make it „privaloma“.
- **"Not fully" is not "not at all".** Where the negation falls changes the meaning: „ne visiškai išsilydyti“ is "not
  melt fully", „visiškai neišsilydyti“ is "not melt at all". "It may not melt fully" → „Ji gali ne visiškai
  išsilydyti“; "the grains do not fully dissolve" → „grūdeliai iki galo neištirpsta“; "not always 1:1" → „ne visada
  lygiavertis (1 : 1)“. „visiškai ne…“ only where the English says "not at all" or "does nothing" („jis visiškai
  neapsaugo“), or where „ne-“ belongs to the word („visiškai nepermatoma“, fully opaque).
- **mesh**: keep „mesh“ after the number („80 mesh sietas“, „100 mesh“); KER's sieve is sold as „Sietelis“.
- **Numbers as words**: a number in the English stays a number (README), even one to ten.
- **Forms to avoid**: _pilnai_ for "fully" (write _visiškai_), _išsaugoti_ for saving (MS writes „įrašyti“), the typo
  _galzūra_ that some KER pages carry, and English-derived _bisquit_ (seen on a studio page) for „biskvitas“. Loanwords
  the shop prints (angobas, šamotas, raku, mesh) are fine.
- **Alyvinė** is lilac (KER „Alyvinė blizgi glazūra“); olive is **alyvuogių** („alyvuogių spalvos“).

## 2. Glossary

Nouns carry their gender (m, f) and, where it helps, the genitive. "show English" marks the terms whose English a reader
can ask to see after them (`<en>…</en>`, `{{en: …}}`). In the guides the `{{en: …}}` follows the first use of each such
term in the running text of **every `##` section** (README), not only in a guide's first section:
`kaolino{{en: kaolin}}`, `šukė{{en: body}}`. The English in the mark is the English's own word at that place
("crawled", "SG", "tile").

# 한국어: 스타일 시트와 용어집 (style sheet and glossary)

For translating Glazecalc into Korean (`ko`), in standard written Korean (표준어, 한글 맞춤법, 외래어 표기법), for potters
in South Korea. Read [README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below.
Every Korean message and guide follows this page: one Korean word for one thing. Korea has no region data yet (Phase
C), so the Korean pages must read well with Orton cones, SK cones and temperatures, and keep the three apart: Korean
potters and textbooks call SK cones 제게르 콘, the same name as the German Seger cones and the Seger formula (제게르식).
The terms come from the shops that sell glaze materials to potters in Korea (the names printed on their price lists and
bags), from a Korean glaze textbook, from Korean papers and the exam notes of the national pottery certificate, from
Korean law and the agencies' pages, and from the Microsoft Korean style guide and the help pages of Google, Apple and
Microsoft, not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was read, the Notes say so: those rows are for a
Korean potter to check.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Korean has a full ceramics vocabulary (장석, 규석, 석회석, 초벌, 재벌,
시유, 빙렬), used by the shops, the textbooks and the certificate exam (도자기공예기능사), so most material and process
rows rest on usage that was read today. The English-language web search reached almost no Korean pages; Naver's search
results pages, the shops' own pages and Korean Wikipedia were read directly, and many rows rest on Naver's result
snippets (marked "via Naver") rather than on the full page. The law's own texts on law.go.kr are rendered by script and
were not read today: their words come from the Asia research notes (read 13 October 2026), from the agencies' and
makers' documents that quote them, and from Korean safety data sheets. Rows marked **Usage** are words Korean potters
use, seen in blogs, course notes or shop listings but in no single authoritative source; rows marked **No source** are
best choices with no Korean source at all (mostly the kiln-sitter parts and the firing-log column labels, which belong to
American kilns). The rows a Korean potter should check first are marked **Native check**: 고령토 (kaolin), 석기
(stoneware), 프릿 (frit), 레시피 and 조합 (recipe), 소지 (body), 소성 정도 (heatwork), 유지 (hold), 빙렬 (crazing), 냉파
(dunting), 삼발이 (stilt), 색견 (draw tile), 유약물 (slurry), the "on top of the base" wording, 단일 소성 (once-firing), 이중 인증 (two-step sign-in)
and 안정제 (stabilizer).

## Owner's decisions (2026-10-16)

These settle the questions this sheet marks as the owner's; they win over anything below.

- **Register:** 합쇼체 with -세요 requests everywhere, interface included, as Microsoft, Google and Apple write Korean (not 해요체 for the interface).
- **Two-step sign-in:** 이중 인증 (the numbers check rejects 2단계).
- **Japanese source titles** in the guides: Hepburn romanization with the English gloss the guide gives, never Japanese script.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

**합쇼체 (-습니다, -ㅂ니다) for every statement, and the polite request form -세요 for every instruction**, in the
interface, errors, notices, emails, the safety text and the guides alike: one register across the app. This corrects
the plan's proposal (합쇼체 for notices and safety, 해요체 -어요 for the interface). The Microsoft style guide and the large
Korean software that potters use write statements in -습니다 and requests in -세요: MS 2.2.1 "암호가 올바르지 않습니다. 다시
시도해 보세요.", "사용할 준비가 되었습니다."; MS 5.4.2 "Error message is generally translated into '~니다' style"; MS 2.1.2
"use—세요 instead of—십시오 … you should use—십시오 when localizing EULA or legal contents". Google's and Apple's Korean help
pages read the same ("이중 인증은 … 보안 방법입니다"). Some newer Korean apps (Toss, parts of Kakao and Naver) write their
interface in 해요체 (저장했어요); it is not used here, because the same messages sit beside safety text and the law's
words, and a mix of -어요 and -습니다 in one screen reads unsettled. **No -십시오** either, even in legal and safety text:
-세요 is polite enough and keeps one voice. No 해라체 (-다, -는다) in running text, except in the headings' noun forms below.

- **Instructions** (guides and help): "…하세요.": "유약을 체에 두 번 거르세요." "방진마스크를 쓰세요."
- **Prohibitions**: "…하지 마세요." for "do not"; "절대 …하지 마세요." for "never": "압축 공기는 절대 사용하지 마세요." A "never"
  always keeps 절대 (or 결코); it must not weaken to "…하지 않는 것이 좋습니다".
- **Statements**: "…습니다.": "이 유약은 SK 8에서 녹습니다."
- **Errors** (MS 5.4.2): a full -습니다 sentence with a stop, even where the English is a fragment: "레시피를 찾을 수
  없습니다." "저장하지 못했습니다." "Cannot …" → "…할 수 없습니다"; "Failed to …" → "…하지 못했습니다".
- **Notices**: "저장했습니다." (the app did it), "저장되었습니다." where the English is passive.
- **Questions and confirmations**: "…하시겠습니까?": "이 레시피를 삭제하시겠습니까?" (MS: "계속하시겠습니까?").
- **The reader** is not named (MS 4.1.9: "pronouns are seldom used"): no 당신, no 너. Leave "you" and "your" out, or name the
  thing ("사용하는 점토", "사용하는 가마"); "my" in a label becomes 내 only where the sense needs it ("내 원료").
- **Honorifics**: -시- on the reader's actions in questions and requests (하시겠습니까, 확인하세요); never on the app's own
  actions (저장했습니다, not 저장하셨습니다).

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- **Buttons and menu commands**: a noun, or a noun + 하기 only where a bare noun is unclear (MS: [취소], [추가]): "저장",
  "인쇄", "비교", "삭제", "사본으로 저장", "원료 추가". Not "저장하다", not "저장하세요".
- **Headings, tabs, page titles**: a noun phrase: "레시피", "설정", "소성 기록", "콘과 소성 정도". A heading that names an activity
  takes a noun form: "Mixing the glaze" → "유약 섞기", "Typing amounts" → "숫자 입력". A heading that is a question stays one:
  "유약은 왜 녹을까요?" (the one place -ㄹ까요 is used: a question heading).
- **Field labels**: a noun; keep the colon where the English has one, half-width, no space before it: "원료 이름:".
- **Confirmations**: "Saved: {name}" → "저장했습니다: {name}".
- **Feature names in running text**: in half-width square brackets, as MS writes UI names ("[비교]를 선택하세요"); see The
  app's feature names.

### Capitalisation

Korean has none (MS 4.1.3: "capitalization only applies when English texts such as product names or abbreviations are
used as they are"). Latin words inside Korean text keep the English's case where they are names, codes or units (Orton,
SK, UMF, LOI, Ferro 3134, N95, mg/m³) and are written in Latin letters, not transliterated, when they are trade names,
codes or abbreviations. Acronyms are all capitals even where the English is not (MS 4.1.2). The English's Title Case
means nothing in Korean and goes with the English words.

### Script and spelling

- **Hangul only.** `scripts/i18n-check.mjs` (checkHanja) rejects any run of Chinese characters in a Korean message or guide
  unless it appears somewhere in this page, and this page writes none: so **no hanja at all**, not even in brackets after a
  term. Korean potters read 규석, 장석, 빙렬, 초벌 in Hangul on every bag and in every course; a bracketed hanja would help no one
  and would open the check to every character written here. Where two Korean words sound alike (석기 the stoneware and 석기
  the stone tool), make the sense clear with a word beside it ("석기 소지", "석기질"), never with hanja.
- **The Japanese words the English quotes** (the firing guide's name for Yuko Trading and the other Japanese names, `silica.kind.control`,
  Japan's mask and poison-line names in `regions` and `safety`) cannot be copied, as the Japanese page copies them: the
  check would reject them. Write each in Hangul, the proper names as 외래어 표기법 spells Japanese (오사카, 쓰쿠바, 교토), the
  common words by their Korean reading or meaning, and keep the English or Latin name the English gives beside it. The list
  is in 2.9. This is a change from the README's "titles of works stay in their own language" for these Japanese titles: an
  owner's decision (see the report).
- **Loanwords** as 외래어 표기법 spells them, unless the bag prints otherwise and potters follow the bag. Where the two differ:
  - **프릿** (외래어 표기법: a short vowel before a final t gives 받침 ㅅ; Korean Wikipedia; the papers' "프릿 유약") over the
    textbook's and 중앙도재's 프리트. **Native check.**
  - **네플린사이나이트** as 중앙도재 prints it (외래어 표기법 would give 네펠린 사이어나이트).
  - **고령토** for kaolin (표준국어대사전 "카올린 =고령토"; 정동훈), while the bags say 카오린 (성신 카오린, e.p.k 카오린): product names

  - **오튼** for Orton, as the shops' listings write it (오튼 셀프 서포트 콘); the dealer Red Corona writes 오톤 and 외래어 표기법
    would give 오턴. In running text the name stays in Latin, Orton, beside 콘 (see Cones).
  - **매뉴얼** is the standard spelling; the shops' 메뉴얼 is not used (and the app's word for a kiln's manual is 사용 설명서 anyway).
  - Chemistry: the bags' names, solid: 산화철, 산화동, 산화크롬, 이산화망간, 산화티탄, 탄산바륨 (not the spaced 산화 철 of

    (see 2.2).
- **Half-width** digits, Latin letters, units and signs; never full-width (１２, ＳＫ, ％).

### Spaces

띄어쓰기 as 한글 맞춤법 gives it:

- **Between words**, not before particles (제41항): "유약을 체에 거르세요", "장석과 규석", "1222 °C에서".
- **A number and a unit symbol**: the English's space stays: "10 g", "1222 °C", "100 °C/h", "0.05 mg/m³"; % takes none: "5%".
  The particle follows the unit with no space: "10 g을", "1222 °C까지", "5%를".
- **A number and a Korean counter or unit word**: solid, as 제43항 allows with Arabic figures and MS writes ("%d자", "%d개"):
  "3개", "2가지", "10분", "24시간", "14일", "20시", "2026년 10월 14일", "3회".
- **Bound nouns** stand apart (제42항): "할 수 있습니다", "쓸 때", "녹는 것".
- **Compound terms**: a ceramics term may be written solid (제49항, 제50항) where the shops and textbooks write it so:
  전기가마, 가스가마, 내화판, 강열감량, 석회석, 결정유, 투명유, 방진마스크, 국소배기장치. Otherwise spaced: 산화 소성, 환원 소성,
  유약 말림, 소성 기록, 작업 노트. Use the spacing in the glossary every time.
- **Latin inside Korean**: a space between a Korean word and a following Latin word, code or figure ("오튼 콘 6", "SK 8에서",
  "N95 마스크"), none between a Latin word and its particle ("Ferro 3134를", "UMF와").

### Korean and Markdown

In the guides, `**…**` and `*…*` do not render when a bracket, quote or stop sits just inside the closing marks and a
Hangul letter just after them (CommonMark's flanking rule): `**"유약"**은` and `**유약(glaze)**은` show the asterisks.
Keep quotation marks and brackets **outside** the emphasis: `"**유약**"은`, `**유약**은`, `**유약**(glaze)은`, or rephrase so
a space follows the closing marks. Korean has no italics: where the English has `*…*` for emphasis, use `**…**` or nothing
(the site sets italics in Korean as bold). Check every bold term in the preview.

### Punctuation

- The Latin marks: . , ? ! : ; as 한글 맞춤법 (문장 부호) and MS give them. A sentence ends with a full stop even after a
  figure or a closing bracket: "1222 °C입니다." MS 4.1.10: a Korean sentence ending in -습니다 or -세요 takes a full stop even
  where the English ends with a colon.
- **Quotations and quoted terms**: double quotes “…” (the typographic marks); a quotation inside one, ‘…’. A term the
  English puts in quotes ("boron blue") takes “…”.
- **Titles**: laws, notices and standards in 「…」, as 국가법령정보센터 writes them: 「산업안전보건법」, 「보호구 안전인증 고시」. Books
  and the five guides' titles in running text in 《…》 (겹화살괄호, 한글 맞춤법 문장 부호). The Sources lists keep titles as the
  English gives them.
- **UI names** in running text: half-width square brackets, no space before the particle (MS): "[납 대체]를 선택하세요."
- **Brackets**: half-width ( ), no space before them after a Korean word ("제게르식(UMF)", "규석(SiO₂)"), as MS writes "USB(범용
  직렬 버스)".
- **Ranges**: the half-width tilde `~` between figures, no spaces, the unit once where the English has it once (한글 맞춤법
  allows 물결표 and 붙임표; the tilde is the one Korean potters and the certificate notes write, "1.60~1.83"): "10 to 60%" →
  "10~60%", "cone 06–04" → "콘 06~04", "1222–1240 °C" → "1222~1240 °C". Never "10%~60%" where the English has one %: the
  check would find a unit the English lacks. Where the English writes "from … to …" in words, write "…부터 …까지".
- **Apostrophes**: Korean needs none; where one appears in an English name, the typographic ’ (never a straight ' before `{`,
  `}` or `#`).
- **Lists in a sentence**: commas; the middle dot · only inside fixed names (기구 및 용기·포장, 유해·위험 문구).
- No ! in the app's own voice.

### Numbers and units

- **Half-width Arabic digits** wherever the English has digits. Never hanja or Hangul numerals for them (십, 이십), never 만 or
  억 (12,500 stays 12,500), never full-width digits.
- **Decimal point** and **thousands comma**, as English and CLDR's `ko`: 1.5, 0.025, 1,000, 12,500. The app prints four-digit
  temperatures ungrouped (1222 °C), and so do the guides' `{{…}}` values; write 1222 °C and 1000 g as the English does.
- **A number the English writes as a word** ("two coats", "three tiles", "once") has no figure in the English, so a digit here
  would be a figure the English lacks and the check rejects it. Write it with the native Korean numeral word, as Korean does
  for counted things anyway: "두 번", "세 장", "한 번", "두세 번", "첫 번째". Where the English has the digit, the digit stays:
  "3 coats" → "3번".
- **Units**: half-width symbols as the English writes them: g, kg, mg, µg, mL, L, mm, cm, °C, °F, °Bé, %, mg/m³, µg/cm². Not
  ℃, not 그램, not 센티미터. "per 100 g" → "100 g당". Inches and feet are the words **인치** and **피트** (README), with the metric
  figure in brackets as the English gives it: "12 inches (30 cm)" → "12인치(30 cm)".
- **Dates**: 2026년 10월 14일 (the check reads 10월 as the English's October), 2026년 10월; days of the week, where any, as
  (화).
- **Clock times**: the 24-hour clock: 20시, 20시 30분 or 20:00; "8pm" → "20시"; "8am to 5pm" or "8 to 5" (office hours) →
  "8시부터 17시까지" (or 8:00~17:00 in a table). **Never 오전 or 오후**: the check keeps "오후 8시" whole and reports it.
  "24 hours" → "24시간".
- **Temperatures and rates**: "1222 °C", "100 °C/h" (or "시간당 100 °C" where the English writes the rate in words with the
  figure).
- **No figures the English lacks**: no SK or Orton equivalents, no converted units, no 만, no added year in a date.

CLDR gives Korean one category:

| Category | Numbers               | Example                                                          |

| `other`  | every number, 0, 1.5… | 원료 1가지, 원료 2가지, 레시피 1개, 레시피 21개, 1.5 g, 소성 3회 |

### Particles after placeholders and figures

은/는, 이/가, 을/를, 와/과 and (으)로 depend on whether the word before ends in a consonant, which `{name}`, `{region}`,
`{count}` and a trade name cannot tell. In order of preference:

1. **A particle that does not alternate**: 에, 에서, 의, 도, 만, 까지, 부터, 에게, and the copula 입니다 (it follows a vowel or a
   consonant alike): "{region}에서 판매", "{name}의 화학식", "{name}도 계산에 넣습니다", "기준은 {value} mg/m³입니다".
2. **A generic noun after the placeholder**, which then takes the particle: "원료 {name}을" → "{name} 원료를"; "레시피 {name}을"
   → "{name} 레시피를"; "{region}은" → "{region}의 경우"; "{law}에 따라" (에 does not alternate). `{region}` is a country's name
   from the browser (대한민국, 독일, 일본): write "{region}의 규정", "{region}에서", never "{region} 국가는".
3. **Only where neither works, the double form** MS 5.4.2 prescribes: 을(를), 이(가), 은(는), 과(와), (으)로: "{name}을(를)
   찾을 수 없습니다." Never guess one form.

Figures with units are known in the text, so their particle follows the unit as read aloud: 10 g을 (그램), 1222 °C로 (도),
5%를 (퍼센트), 1.1 L를 (리터), SK 8로 (팔), 콘 6으로 (육). A placeholder for a figure with no unit after it (`{count}`, `{ratio}`)
takes a counter or a non-alternating particle, never a guessed one.

### Cones

Korea uses both families: the shops sell Orton cones (Naver shopping), and Korean textbooks, the certificate notes and KS
L 0011 name **제게르 콘** with **SK numbers** ("제게르 번호(SK)", "SK 022번 … SK 42번"), the Japanese-standard series. Korean
potters may say 제게르 콘 for an SK cone, and older recipes say "SK 8" without saying whose cone. The app has **Orton
cones**, **SK cones (JIS R 8101)** and **temperatures**, and keeps them apart; a recipe's cone is an Orton number, never
turned into an SK number.

- **SK cones** are always written **SK**, a half-width space, and the number exactly as printed: "SK 8", "SK 6a", "SK 015a",
  "SK 022". The noun is **콘**: "SK 콘", "SK 8 콘"; "fire to SK 8" → "SK 8까지 소성하세요". Where the English names the standard,
  "SK (JIS R 8101)": the setting "To SK cones (JIS R 8101)" → "SK 콘(JIS R 8101) 기준".
- **Never** label an SK cone "제게르 콘 8", "제게르 8번" or "SK8번". **제게르** appears only where the English says Seger: the
  Seger formula (제게르식, below) and the older European "Seger cones" of the firing guide → "독일의 제게르 콘", described as a
  series other than SK and Orton: say nothing that makes them the SK cones Korean potters call by the same name.
- **Orton cones**: "오튼 콘", with the name written in Latin wherever the English gives it as a name (Orton Ceramic Foundation),
  and with every Orton number wherever an SK number could be meant, which in Korean is everywhere: "cone 6" → "오튼 콘 6" at
  the first mention in each section and in every table, label and setting; "콘 6" after that within the same paragraph. "cone
  06" → "오튼 콘 06" (the zero stays; it is far cooler than 6). "cone 6 glazes" → "오튼 콘 6 유약". The setting "To Orton
  cones" → "오튼 콘 기준".
- **Never** give an SK number for an Orton number or the reverse, never write ≈, 상당, 해당, 같은 or 대응 between them, and never
  add a conversion the English lacks. The two charts are separate tables.
- **One number hotter, one number cooler**: "한 단계 고온의 콘", "한 단계 저온의 콘" (Hangul numeral: the English has no
  figure; "two numbers hotter" → "두 단계 고온"). Not 번호가 높은/낮은, 큰/작은: in the 0-series (SK 022 to SK 01a, Orton 022 to 01) a larger number is cooler. Where the English says "the next number below it in the table", keep the table's order: "표에서
  바로 위(저온 쪽)의 콘", "바로 아래(고온 쪽)의 콘", matching how the table is drawn.
- **A pyrometric cone** in general is **콘** (정동훈, the shops); the full name **파이로메트릭 콘** only where the English says
  "pyrometric cone" in a definition. Not 원뿔 (the geometric cone) and not 삼각추 or 추 alone.
- Witness cone 확인용 콘; cone pack 콘 세트; guide, firing and guard cones 가이드 콘, 소성 콘, 가드 콘 (2.4).

### The unity formula

**제게르식** (국립중앙박물관 2025; the Korean papers on celadon and white ware; course notes: "제게르는 산화물을 염기성과 중성,
산성으로 구별하고 각 산화물을 몰 수 비로 표시한 식"), with "(UMF)" after it at the first mention on each page: "제게르식(UMF)". One

합을 1로 맞춘".

### Gender-neutral wording

Korean has no grammatical gender. MS 3.1: "Don't use gendered pronouns (그, 그녀등) in generic references … Refer to a person's
role": no 그, 그녀, 그들 for a generic potter; leave the pronoun out or repeat the role: 도예가, 사용자 (in account and privacy
text), 작업자 (in safety text), 아이 and 어린이 for children. **님** is not used in the app's text (it addresses a named person;
the app addresses no one by name), nor is 고객 (the app sells nothing). Avoid gendered job words (여공, 여류 도예가); 도예가 and 작업자 are neutral.

### The reader in the guides

합쇼체 throughout, short sentences as the English. Instructions end in "…하세요."; explanations are -습니다 statements with
the subject left out: "유약이 너무 두꺼우면 흘러내립니다." "초벌한 기물에는 바로 시유할 수 있습니다." Where the English says "you",

### Law in Korea

As the law writes it, never paraphrased: the words below are fixed, and the region texts must use them.

- **이하, 미만, 이상, 초과**: "at most", "no more than", "or less", "up to" → **이하** (the limit itself passes); "less than",
  "under", "below" → **미만** (it does not); "at least", "or more", "from" → **이상**; "more than", "over", "above" → **초과**
  (or "…를 넘는"). Never 미만 for "at most" and never 이하 for "less than": a slip moves a legal limit. Check each one in the
  food-contact, silica and mask texts.
- **Food contact**: 「식품위생법」; 「기구 및 용기·포장의 기준 및 규격」 (식품의약품안전처고시), III-7 유리제, 도자기제, 법랑 및
  옹기류; the piece kinds as the law and MFDS's fact sheet give them: "액체를 채울 수 없거나 액체를 채웠을 때 깊이가 2.5 cm 미만인
  것", "용량 1.1 L 미만", "용량 1.1 L 이상 3 L 미만", "용량 3 L 이상", "가열조리용 기구"; the limits "… 이하"; the test 용출시험
  (4% 초산). `food.atMost` says the law writes "이하". 옹기 (onggi) is named in the law beside 도자기제: write it only where the
  English names onggi.
- **Silica**: 「산업안전보건법」; 고용노동부고시 「화학물질 및 물리적인자의 노출기준」: "exposure standard" → **노출기준**; the

  **결정체 석영** and **결정체 크리스토바라이트** (the law's spelling; the mineral elsewhere is 크리스토발라이트); "free crystalline
  silica" → **결정형 유리규산** (KOSHA, 질병관리청). Korea's silica value is a 노출기준, not in the binding **허용기준** list of
  산업안전보건법 시행규칙 별표 19: `silica.kind.indicative` must keep "not a legal ceiling" → "법적 상한이 아닌 권고 성격의
  기준입니다" (the employer must still measure, 작업환경측정, and act when it is exceeded: say only what the English says).
- **Dust masks**: 방진마스크; 「보호구 안전인증 고시」; classes **특급**, **1급**, **2급** (never "1등급" or "class 1"); 안전인증
  (the KOSHA mark); 안면부여과식 (filtering facepiece); 전동식 호흡보호구 (PAPR). KF94 and KF80 are 보건용 마스크 under

- **GHS**: 「화학물질의 분류·표시 및 물질안전보건자료에 관한 기준」: 경고 표지, 그림문자, 신호어 (**위험**, **경고**), 유해·위험 문구

- **US and UK law** names stay in English with a Korean gloss once: OSHA(미국 산업안전보건청), HSE(영국 보건안전청).

### Pitfalls

- **유약, 에나멜, 니스, 바니시**: a glaze is **유약** (material and coat), "to glaze" **시유하다**; compounds as the shops write them
  (투명유, 결정유, 천목유, 석회유). 에나멜 and 법랑 are enamel on metal (the law's 법랑 is a different material); 니스 and 바니시 are
  varnish. Never use them for a glaze.
- **프릿 and 융제**: a frit is **프릿** (a factory glass); a flux is **융제** (the oxide's role). 용제 also means "solvent": not used.
  매용제 appears in some Japanese-influenced texts: not used. Never 플럭스 alone (soldering flux).
- **가마, 오븐, 노**: a potter's kiln is **가마** (전기가마, 가스가마); 오븐 is a kitchen oven, used only where the English says oven; 노
  (furnace) is industrial and not used.
- **초벌 and 재벌**: bisque (the firing and the ware) is **초벌** (초벌구이, 초벌 소성); the glaze firing is **재벌** (재벌구이). Not
  1차 소성, 2차 소성 in the app (they also appear in courses), and not 본소성.
- **도기, 석기, 자기, 토기, 옹기**: earthenware is **도기**, stoneware **석기**, porcelain **자기**. 토기 is unglazed low-fired ware
  (and archaeology's word), not used for earthenware. 석기 also means a stone tool: write "석기 소지" or "석기질" where the sense
  could slip. 옹기, 분청(사기), 청자, 백자 are Korean kinds of ware: write them only where the English names them (celadon
  → 청자). **show English** helps here.
- **규석, 석영, 실리카, 규소, 실리콘**: the material as sold is **규석**; the mineral **석영**; the oxide **실리카** (SiO₂), formally
  이산화규소 or the law's 산화규소; the element **규소**; silicone rubber **실리콘**, which in everyday Korean also means silicon:
  never use 실리콘 for silica. "silica" in the app's categories and the unity formula is 실리카.
- **석회석, 탄산칼슘, 석회, 호분**: whiting is **석회석** (as 중앙도재 sells it), chemically 탄산칼슘 (CaCO₃). **석회** is lime (CaO, or
  slaked lime) and appears only in "lime" frit names (소다·칼리·석회 프릿) and where the English means CaO. 호분 (정동훈's word for
  whiting) is also the oyster-shell white of painting: not used.
- **붕소, 산화붕소, 붕산, 붕사**: B₂O₃ is **붕소(B₂O₃)** as potters name it, formally 산화붕소; **붕산** is boric acid (H₃BO₃, the
  reproductive toxin of the safety guide); **붕사** is borax. Never 붕산 for B₂O₃.
- **소다, 소다회, 탄산나트륨, 칼리**: "soda" (Na₂O) is **소다(Na₂O)**, formally 산화나트륨; soda ash is **탄산나트륨** (중앙도재)
  with 소다회 in brackets once; "potash" (K₂O) is **칼리(K₂O)**, formally 산화칼륨; the safety guide's potassium carbonate is
  탄산칼륨.
- **안료 and 발색제**: a stain (factory-made) is **안료** (도자기 안료, as 중앙도재 sells it); a colorant (any oxide that colours) is
  **발색제**. "colorants and additives" → "발색제와 첨가제".
- **방진마스크, 호흡용 보호구, 인공호흡기, 환기, 환풍기**: a respirator is **방진마스크** (호흡용 보호구 in the general sense); a hospital
  ventilator is 인공호흡기, never meant here; ventilation (air in a room) is **환기**, a kitchen fan 환풍기. A "nuisance dust mask"
  → "안전인증이 없는 일반 마스크".
- **가이드 and 사용 설명서**: the app's five guides are **가이드**; a kiln's, controller's or sitter's manual is **사용 설명서**.
  Never the same word.
- **빙렬 and 냉파**: crazing is **빙렬** (a defect, or wanted: crackle, 빙렬유); a crack through the pot from cooling is **냉파**
  (dunting). See 2.5.
- **녹 and 녹색**: "rust" in a colour name is not 녹색 (green): "Tenmoku rust" → "천목 적갈색".
- **용출 and 침출**: leaching is **용출** (the law's word, 용출시험, 용출 규격); 침출 (extraction) is not used.
- **should and must** in safety text: "must" → "…해야 합니다", "반드시 …하세요"; "must not" → "…해서는 안 됩니다"; "should" →
  "…하는 것이 좋습니다"; "should not" → "…하지 않는 것이 좋습니다". Never 해야 합니다 for "should" (that is must), never 좋습니다 for
  "must". "must be tested" → "시험을 받아야 합니다" (the test is done, not passed); never "합격해야 합니다". "some" (some fine
  quartz) → "어느 정도의", "일부", never "약간", "조금". "may" (a hazard) → "…할 수 있습니다", as the H-statements write it
  ("일으킬 수 있음").
- **Placeholders**: see Particles after placeholders and figures.

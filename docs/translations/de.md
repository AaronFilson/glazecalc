# Deutsch: Stilblatt und Glossar (style sheet and glossary)

The rules and terms for translating Glazecalc into German, for potters in Germany (Standard German, de-DE, with ß),
readable in Austria and Switzerland. Every German message and guide follows this page: one German word for one thing.
The terms come from German suppliers' catalogues and data sheets (Carl Jäger, Botz, Keramik-Kraft,
keramikbedarf-online, Rantzauer Töpferbedarf), kiln makers, German Wikipedia, and the safety agencies (BAuA, DGUV,
BG RCI, IFA GESTIS), not from a dictionary alone. Where sources disagree or none was found, the Notes say so.

## 1. Style sheet

**Register.** Formal throughout: _Sie_, _Ihr_, _Ihnen_, always capitalised. Instructions use the formal imperative:
„Wählen Sie …“, „Sieben Sie die Glasur zweimal.“, „Tragen Sie eine Maske.“ Never _du_, never the infinitive as an
instruction in running text („Glasur sieben.“ is a button style, not a sentence). Use _bitte_ only where the English
has "please" (mostly "Please try again" → „Bitte versuchen Sie es erneut.“). „Wir“ for the app's own advice is fine
where the English says "we" („Wir empfehlen …“); MS allows it and drops it where it sounds odd.

**Buttons, menu items, headings, field labels, tabs** (the German software convention, as in Microsoft's and other
major German UIs, and the German glaze calculator OGC: „Formel berechnen“, „Rezept hochladen“):

- Buttons and menu commands: the **infinitive, verb last**: „Speichern“, „Als Kopie speichern“, „Drucken“,
  „Vergleichen“, „Rohstoffe hinzufügen“, „Testzugang verwerfen“. Not the imperative („Speichern Sie“) and not a noun
  („Speicherung“).
- Headings, tab names, menu names and page titles: a **noun or noun phrase**: „Rezepte“, „Einstellungen“, „Brennprotokolle“,
  „Kegel und Wärmearbeit“. A heading that is a question or an instruction in English may stay one.
- Field labels: a noun, with the colon where the English has one: „Name des Rohstoffs:“.
- Confirmations: past participle first, as the English does: "Saved: …" → „Gespeichert: …“.

**Capitalisation.** German rules only: nouns capitalised, everything else lower case, also in headings and buttons.
No English Title Case: "Fired Weight" → „Gewicht gebrannt“, not „Gewicht Gebrannt“. The first word of a heading, label
or button is capitalised. Product and system names keep their own form (Orton, Seger, Kiln-Sitter, Glazecalc).

**Punctuation and spacing.**

- Quotation marks: „…“ (U+201E, U+201C); inside them ‚…‘ (U+201A, U+2018). Not "…" or »…« (book style, not used here).
- Apostrophe: the typographic ’ (U+2019), rare in German ("geht’s"). Write `''` only where the README asks (just before
  `{`, `}` or `#`, and in `safety` and `records`).
- No space before ; : ! ? (unlike French).
- Ranges: the en dash with no spaces, unit once at the end: „1222–1240 °C“, „Kegel 06–04“, „1–3 %“. In a sentence
  „von 1222 bis 1240 °C“ is also fine; never mix („von 1222–1240 °C“).
- Compounds with a symbol or number take a hyphen (Duden): „SiO₂-Gehalt“, „FFP3-Maske“, „CMC-Kleber“, „Kegel-6-Glasur“
  (better: „Glasur für Kegel 6“). Established name compounds are one word, as the suppliers write them: „Segerkegel“,
  „Ortonkegel“ (CJ, German Wikipedia), „Segerformel“.

**Numbers.**

- Decimal comma: „1,5 %“, „0,01 g“, „1,45“. (`{{SG 1.45}}` in the guides stays as written; the app renders it.)
- Thousands: four-digit numbers are **not** grouped („1240 °C“, „1000 g“), as the app prints them; five digits and more
  take a period („12.500 g“). The check reads both.
- A space before °C, °F, % and every unit: „1240 °C“, „15 %“, „100 °C/h“, „80 mesh“. (Suppliers often print „1240°C“;
  do not copy that.) A plain space is fine in the files.
- A number in the English stays a number, never a word.

**Cones.** The app's cones are **Orton cones** (American). In Germany Orton cones are what shops sell, but often under
the old name „Segerkegel“ (Botz: Orton is „die gängige Marke für Segerkegel“), while true Seger cones (SK) are a
different series whose numbers do not match Orton's (44–52 °C apart at 05 and 04). So:

- "cone 6" → „Kegel 6“; "cone 06" → „Kegel 06“ (the zero stays: Kegel 06 is far cooler than Kegel 6).
- "Orton cone" → „Ortonkegel“ (one word, as CJ and German Wikipedia write it); at the first mention on a page, and
  wherever systems are compared: „Ortonkegel 6“, then „Kegel 6“.
- "cone 04 bisque" → „Schrühbrand auf Kegel 04“; "fire to cone 6" → „auf Kegel 6 brennen“; "a cone 6 glaze" → „eine
  Glasur für Kegel 6“; "cone 5–6" → „Kegel 5–6“.
- **Never** write „Segerkegel 6“, „SK 6“ or „SK 6a“ for an Orton number. „Segerkegel“ appears only where the English
  says "Seger cones", and then means the SK series.

**The unity formula.** „Segerformel“ (die), with „(UMF)“ after it the first time on a page: „Segerformel (UMF)“, as
the German glaze calculator OGC writes it. After that „Segerformel“. The app's short forms "unity formula", "Unity
formula" and "Unity:" are all „Segerformel“ / „Segerformel:“.

**Gender-neutral wording.** Follow MS: prefer wording without gender (plurals, „wer töpfert“, direct address with
_Sie_, „alle“); use a pair form („Töpferinnen und Töpfer“) where a person noun cannot be avoided, sparingly. No
Gendersternchen, colon or capital I (Töpfer\*innen, Töpfer:innen, TöpferInnen): they hurt readability and screen
readers, and several German state administrations reject them. Grammatical gender of things is unaffected.

**The reader in the guides.** _Sie_ and the formal imperative, in short plain sentences, as the English: „Sieben Sie
die Glasur zweimal durch ein 80-mesh-Sieb.“ Avoid the impersonal „man“ where the English says "you"; avoid
nominal style („Die Durchführung des Siebens erfolgt …“). Quotations from sources are translated into „…“ and keep their
figures as the English writes them.

**Feature names.** Written exactly as on the button or tab, with a capital first letter. Where the English wraps the
name in `<b>` or another tag, keep the tag and add no quotation marks. Where it has no tag, put the button's name in
„…“ when the reader is told to use it (Wählen Sie „Vergleichen“.), and otherwise use the ordinary noun (Der Vergleich
zeigt …). "Settings" is never in quotes: „in den Einstellungen“. The fixed names:

**Pitfalls in this field.**

- **Steinzeug** is stoneware; **Steingut** is a (white) earthenware, porous and low-fired. Never translate stoneware as
  Steingut. Earthenware in general is **Irdenware**; suppliers also label their low-fire range „Steingut“ (Botz:
  „Irdenware wird auch oft Steingut genannt“), so Steingut may appear in a supplier's name for a range, never as a
  translation of stoneware.
- **Glasur**, never „Lasur“ (a wood stain or varnish) or „Email“ (enamel on metal).
- **Soda** in German is sodium carbonate (soda ash), **Natron** in everyday German is baking soda (NaHCO₃), and
  **Pottasche** is potassium carbonate. The potters' oxides "soda" and "potash" are „Natrium (Na₂O)“ and „Kalium (K₂O)“.
- **Silizium** is the element, **Silikon** the rubber, and German „Silica“ means silica gel or precipitated silica.
  The oxide SiO₂ is „Kieselsäure“ in glaze chemistry; the material is „Quarz“ / „Quarzmehl“.
- **Tonerde** is alumina (Al₂O₃), not clay („Ton“) and not soil.
- **Kalk** can mean lime (CaO, as in „Kalk-Natron-Glas“), limestone or quicklime; for the material say „Kreide“ or
  „Kalkspat“ as on the bag, for the oxide „Calcium (CaO)“.
- **Schlicker** is clay in water (casting or joining slip); suppliers also call a mixed glaze „Schlicker“. Say
  „Glasurschlicker“ or „angerührte Glasur“ when the glaze is meant.
- **Kegel** alone is ambiguous only across systems; see Cones above.
- **Brand** is a firing (Schrühbrand, Glasurbrand), and also a fire (a burning building) in safety text: write
  „Feuer“ or „Brandgefahr“ where the English means fire.
- **mesh**: German sieves are sold by meshes per square centimetre („900 Ma/cm²“, „10.000 Maschen“ = 0,06 mm, CJ), not
  per inch. Keep "80 mesh" as „80 mesh“, never „80 Maschen“.
- **Regional variants to avoid**: Austrian „Jänner“, „heuer“; Swiss „ss“ for ß and „Velo“-type words. Write de-DE with
  ß; avoid regional words such as „Hafner“ (South German and Austrian for potter) except in names.
- **Trübung** is a wanted opacity („Trübungsmittel“); a milky defect is „Schleier“. **Specks** on German bags are
  decorative granules; dark spots as a defect are „Sprenkel“. **Rohglasur** is an unfritted glaze, not a raw formula
  or raw glazing.
- **Respirator** in German is a medical ventilator (Beatmungsgerät). The mask is „Atemschutzmaske“; see 2.7.

as „Segerkegel“ or „Ortonkegel“ but carry **Orton numbers**: Keramik-Kraft sells „Segerkegel Orton selbststehend NR.6“,

cones (SK, „Segerkegel 2a“) are still sold in Austria and are a different scale (CJ's
[Vergleichsliste](http://www.carl-jaeger.de/PDF/Seger_Ortonkegel.pdf): SK 06a 990 °C, Orton 06 1011 °C). The app writes
„Ortonkegel“ or „Kegel“, never „Segerkegel“, for its cones.

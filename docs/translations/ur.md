# اردو: style sheet and glossary

Written by the round-8 terminology pass (2026-10-21), with the owner's decisions of 2026-10-21 applied (see "The
owner's decisions and the native check" below). For translating Glazecalc into Urdu (`ur`), in standard written Urdu for
potters in Pakistan (region `PK`), the page Urdu is written for (`COUNTRY_OF`, docs/south-asia-mena-plan.md). Read
[README.md](README.md) first; [terms.md](terms.md) gives the meaning of every English term below. Every Urdu message and
guide follows this page: one Urdu word for one thing. Pakistan fires by temperature by default, with Orton cones one tap
away (lib/regions), so the Urdu pages must read well with temperatures and Orton cones and say nothing about SK cones
that the English does not say. Pakistan has region data in English (Rescue 1122, the ministry), but no silica limit,
food-contact rule, poison line or shop was found (scratch/round6/research/in-pk-bd.md in the main checkout). The raw
material of this page (extracted PDFs, Wikipedia counts, Google suggestions) is in scratch/round8/urbn-terms/ in the main
checkout.

**What the sources could and could not give.** Very little is written about studio glazes in Urdu. Pakistanis search for
glazes, kilns and pottery classes in English (Google suggest, Pakistan, 21 October 2026: "ceramic glaze price in
pakistan", "pottery workshop in lahore", "pottery wheel online pakistan"); the Urdu queries ask what a word means
("کوزہ گری کا مطلب", "کمہار in english"), and "گلیز" completes only to Persian pages. The traditional craft has a stable
Urdu core, read in the Urdu press and in Platts: **کمہار** (potter), **کوزہ گری** and **ظروف سازی** (pottery), **چکنی
مٹی** (clay), **چاک** (wheel), **بھٹی** and **آوی/آوا** (kiln), **پکانا** (to fire), **کچے برتن** (unfired ware). The
chemistry has the Urdu of Urdu Wikipedia and its chemistry glossary (تکسید, تخفیف, مول, آکسائیڈ, حرارتی پھیلاؤ, لزوجت,
کثافت اضافی). For the glaze itself, its defects and its materials no Urdu source was read: the Pakistani trade brochure
on pottery is in English, as are the shops' and the National College of Arts' pages, so this page follows the owner's
Hindi model (the potters' English loanwords in Urdu script, the Urdu term in brackets where one exists) and marks those
rows **No source**. No Urdu GHS text, Urdu text of a Pakistani law on dust or lead, or Urdu kiln manual was found. The
rows a native potter should check first are marked **Native check**: گلیز and its gender, کثافت اضافی (SG), نسخہ
against ریسیپی, the defect loanwords, دھاتوں کا اخراج (leaching), the GHS sentences and ریسپیریٹر. The kiln (بھٹی), firing
(پکائی) and cone (مخروط) are the owner's decisions; a native potter may still say how they read in titles.

## The owner's decisions and the native check

The owner decided the first four points on 2026-10-21 (scratch/round8/plan.md, "urbn-terms", in the main checkout);
they are settled and not asked again. The rest are recommendations this sheet could not settle from sources, used below
until a native potter answers; their rows are marked **Native check**, and a translator lists each sentence that uses
one in the flags for the reviewer.

- **The tiers (decided: the Hindi model):** as the owner chose for Hindi, (1) craft words the studio potters say in
  English are loanwords in Urdu script (گلیز, فرٹ, بسکٹ), with the Urdu word in brackets where one exists; (2) materials
  are the name on the bag (فیلڈ اسپار, وائٹنگ), trade names in Latin; (3) chemistry is the Urdu of Urdu Wikipedia and its
  chemistry glossary (آکسائیڈ, تکسید, مول); (4) safety and law in plain Urdu. **Three exceptions**, where the Urdu press
  and Platts give a living Urdu word and the loanword collides: the kiln is **بھٹی** (not "کلن"), firing is **پکائی /
  پکانا** (never "فائرنگ", which is gunfire), and the cone is **مخروط** (never "کون", which is "who"), with "(Orton
  cone)" in Latin letters after it at its first use in a text (see Cones).
- **Digits (decided):** the translation files write 0 to 9, and Urdu pages show 0 to 9: Pakistan's usual digits (lib/regions
  `DIGITS` writes ur-PK in 0 to 9; Urdu in India keeps Extended Arabic-Indic for formatted numbers). See Numbers.
- **Sentence end (decided):** the Urdu full stop **۔** (U+06D4), never the Latin "." (MS 4.1.9, Google, Jang). The
  translations check refuses a Latin full stop at the end of an Urdu sentence (scripts/i18n-check.mjs, checkUrdu).
- **Urdu letters (decided with the sentence end):** ی ک ہ, never the Arabic ي ى ك ه; the check refuses those four.
- **Glaze:** **گلیز** (m.), the loanword, as Hindi ग्लेज़ and Bengali গ্লেজ; no Urdu source writes it for pottery (Google
  suggest gives only Persian pages), and Platts has no Urdu word for a ceramic glaze alone: روغن is varnish, lacquer and
  paint ("رنگ و روغن", Express 2018), لعاب is saliva (Urdu Wikipedia), "شیشے کی تہہ" (Rekhta) is a description.
  **Native check** (the word and its gender).
- **Kiln:** **بھٹی** (f.), Platts "forge, furnace, kiln", Dunya 2019 "آوی (بھٹی)", "بھٹی میں … برتن جوڑنا". **آوا / آوی**
  is the village potter's kiln (Platts, Dunya 2019, Geo 2012): used only for a traditional kiln. Bare "بھٹی" is also the
  Bhatti clan in searches (Google suggest), so titles say "گلیز کی بھٹی" or "بجلی کی بھٹی". The word is the owner's
  decision; a native potter may still say how titles read best.
- **Cone:** **مخروط** (pl. مخروطے), the Urdu word for a cone, with "Orton" and the number: "Orton مخروط 6", and at its
  first use in a text "مخروط (Orton cone)", the English in Latin letters so the reader knows the thing the shops sell.
  The loanword "کون" is also the everyday "who" and cannot be told apart without vowel marks. The owner's decision.
- **Recipe:** **ریسیپی** (f.), as Hindi रेसिपी and as Pakistanis write a cooking recipe (Google suggest "چکن ریسیپی");
  نسخہ (a prescription, also a manuscript or copy) and ترکیب (a method, also a chemical composition) are kept out.
  **Native check.**
- **Specific gravity:** **کثافت اضافی** (Urdu Wikipedia's title for specific gravity, "ایک نسبت …", water's is 1), never
  کثافت (density) alone. **Native check**: no ceramics source.
- **The other Native check rows**, first of all: پکائی (firing) in the guides, the defect loanwords, دھاتوں کا اخراج
  (leaching), the GHS sentences (no Urdu source at all), ریسپیریٹر, کاؤلن's spelling, سیگر فارمولا (UMF), and the law's
  limit words (زیادہ سے زیادہ, سے کم).
- **Poison line:** none in Pakistan (region research): the text names Rescue 1122.

## 1. Style sheet

### Register

**آپ**, polite and plain, everywhere: the interface, errors, notices, emails, the safety text and the guides. Never تم or
تو. The polite imperative in -یں, as Google and Mozilla write it ("کھولیں", "سائن ان کریں", "منتخب کریں"); not the
-ئیے form (کیجیے), which is spoken politeness. Everyday words over Persian-Arabic formal ones where both are common
(تبدیل کریں over ترمیم فرمائیں; دوبارہ کوشش کریں over از سرِ نو سعی کریں); no Hindi (Sanskrit) words where Urdu has its own.

- **Buttons:** the -یں imperative, short, the verb last: **محفوظ کریں**, **پرنٹ کریں**, **موازنہ کریں**, **ہٹائیں**,
  **منسوخ کریں**, **مواد شامل کریں**. Not "براہ کرم محفوظ کریں".
- **Errors:** short and passive, never the computer speaking as "I": "ریسیپی محفوظ نہیں کی جا سکی۔ دوبارہ کوشش کریں۔",
  "یہ فائل پڑھی نہیں جا سکی۔"
- **Notices:** plain statements: "محفوظ ہو گیا۔" "اسے واپس نہیں لایا جا سکتا۔"
- **Guide sentences:** the -یں imperative and impersonal statements: "گلیز کو 80 میش کی چھلنی سے دو بار چھانیں۔",
  "خشک مواد تولتے وقت ریسپیریٹر پہنیں۔", "بہت موٹا گلیز پکائی میں بہہ جاتا ہے۔"
- **Should, must, may:** "must" → **ضروری ہے**, **-نا ہوگا** ("جانچ کرانی ہوگی"); a legal duty → **لازم ہے**. "must not" →
  **نہ کریں** in an instruction, **ممنوع ہے** only in a legal text. "should" → **چاہیے** ("ماسک پہننا چاہیے"); "should not"
  → **نہیں … چاہیے**. "may" (a hazard, a hedge) → **سکتا ہے / سکتی ہے** ("کینسر کا سبب بن سکتا ہے", "اس میں کچھ باریک
  کوارٹز ہو سکتا ہے"); "may" (permission) → **کر سکتے ہیں**. Never ضروری for "should", never چاہیے for "must". "must be
  tested" → **کی جانچ ضروری ہے** (the test is done, not passed). "some" → **کچھ**, never **تھوڑا**. "never" → **کبھی نہ /
  کبھی نہیں** only where the English says never; no ہمیشہ, بالکل or ضرور the English lacks.

### Buttons, menus, headings, labels and tabs

- **Buttons and menu commands:** the -یں imperative: "محفوظ کریں", "پرنٹ کریں", "حذف کریں", "منسوخ کریں", "کاپی کے طور
  پر محفوظ کریں".
- **Headings, tabs, page titles:** a noun phrase: "ریسیپیاں", "ترتیبات", "پکائی کا ریکارڈ", "مخروطے اور حرارتی کام". A
  heading that names an activity is the verbal noun in -نا: "Mixing the glaze" → "گلیز ملانا". A question stays one, with
  ؟: "گلیز کیوں بہتا ہے؟"
- **Field labels:** a noun; the colon where the English has one, no space before it: "مواد کا نام:".
- **Confirmations:** "Saved: {name}" → "محفوظ ہو گیا: {name}" (the label form, so the verb need not agree with {name});
  "Removed" → "ہٹا دیا گیا".
- **Progress:** the passive with "رہا ہے" and the ellipsis: "Saving…" → "محفوظ کیا جا رہا ہے…".

### Capitalisation

The Urdu script has no capitals; the English's Title Case means nothing here. A button or page name the English
capitalises in running text is set off by quotation marks (MS: English quotation marks are "also followed in Urdu"),
unless the English wraps it in a tag. Latin words inside Urdu text (Orton, Ferro 3134, SiO₂, UMF, N95) keep the English's
letters and case exactly. An English loanword is written in Urdu script (گلیز, فرٹ), never in Latin letters, unless it is
a trade name, a brand, a code or a unit.

### Script, direction and type

- **Nastaliq, right to left.** The app serves Noto Nastaliq Urdu for `ur` (client/styles.scss) and sets `dir="rtl"`;
  write in logical order, as typed. Nastaliq lines are tall and slope down each word: keep button texts short.
- **Urdu letters, not Arabic ones:** ی (U+06CC), never the Arabic ي (U+064A) or ى (U+0649); ک (U+06A9), never ك
  (U+0643); ہ (U+06C1) for the final and medial h, ھ (U+06BE) only in the aspirates (بھ, پھ, تھ, ٹھ, چھ, کھ, گھ, دھ, ڈھ);
  ے (U+06D2) for the final e; ٹ, ڈ, ڑ, ں as Urdu writes them; ۃ only in Arabic words that keep it. A keyboard set for
  Arabic types the wrong ones and Nastaliq then draws them wrongly. The translations check (scripts/i18n-check.mjs,
  checkUrdu) refuses ي, ى, ك and ه (U+064A, U+0649, U+0643, U+0647) in `ur`, and text not in Unicode's composed form
  (NFC: آ, ۂ and ۓ as one character each).
- **No control characters:** no U+200E, U+200F, U+061C, U+202A–U+202E or U+2066–U+2069; the app sets the direction. The
  check refuses them. ZWNJ (U+200C) only where a compound would otherwise join wrongly.
- **No kashida** (tatweel, U+0640, refused by the check) to stretch a word, and no vowel marks (zer, zabar, pesh) except to tell two words apart
  where nothing else can (and then on one letter).
- **Latin runs** (units, formulas, trade names, codes, cone numbers) stay exactly as the English writes them, left to
  right inside the Urdu; a figure and its unit stay together in the English's order: "1222 °C", "10 g". A paragraph,
  heading or list item starts with an Urdu word, not a figure or a Latin name.
- **Spaces** as the English has them around Latin text and figures; و stands as its own word ("کاؤلن اور فیلڈ اسپار" with
  اور; و only in fixed Persian pairs).
- **Markdown:** `**` around whole words only; never inside a word (the joins break).

### Punctuation

The Urdu full stop **۔** (U+06D4) at the end of every sentence, never the Latin "." or the Arabic "." (MS 4.1.9: "Urdu
does not use full stop"; Google; Jang). The Urdu comma **،** (U+060C), semicolon **؛** (U+061B) and question mark **؟**
(U+061F), with no space before them; the colon and exclamation mark as in English. Quotation marks “…” (MS: English
quotation marks; curly ones need no escaping in JSON). Parentheses as in English (the browser mirrors them). Ranges
between figures with the en dash and no spaces (10–60%, cone 06–04), as the English writes them (MS allows "تا", which
would drop nothing but changes the figure's look: keep the dash). No em dash (MS: "not applicable for Urdu"). The
ellipsis "…" only where the English has one. Never "۔" inside a number: the decimal mark is the point (see below). The
translations check refuses a Latin "." that ends a sentence with Urdu words in it (after a word, a figure, a unit or a
closing quotation mark or bracket), but not the point in a figure (1.5), a web address or a numbered list's "1.".

### Numbers in the translation files

- **The digits 0 to 9 only**, never Extended Arabic-Indic (۰۱۲۳۴۵۶۷۸۹) or Arabic-Indic (٠١٢٣) digits, in every message and
  guide; the numbers check reads 0 to 9. Pakistan's Urdu press (Jang, Express), Google's Urdu help and Mozilla write 0
  to 9, and lib/regions writes ur-PK numbers in 0 to 9 at runtime.
- **Decimal point and thousands comma** as the English writes them: 1.5; 0.025; 12,500; four-digit temperatures
  ungrouped (1222 °C). Never the Arabic decimal separator ٫, the thousands separator ٬, or the hamza ء that MS gives for
  Urdu digits.
- **Percent:** "5%", no space, the Latin sign, never ٪; a range "10–60%".
- **Units:** the English's symbols in Latin letters: g, kg, mg, µg, mL, L, mm, cm, °C, °F, °Bé, %, mg/m³, µg/cm²; never
  "گرام" or "سینٹی میٹر" spelled out in running text where the English has a symbol (MS: measurement abbreviations are
  kept). A non-breaking space between number and unit is the app's job, not the translation's. Inches and feet are the
  words **انچ** and **فٹ**, with the metric figure in brackets as the English gives it.
- **A number the English writes as a word** stays a word ("two coats" → "دو تہیں"); a digit stays a digit ("3 coats" →
  "3 تہیں").
- **Dates:** "14 اکتوبر 2026" (CLDR writes "14 اکتوبر، 2026"; the comma is optional: write it as CLDR does where the
  English writes a full date). Month names CLDR's: جنوری، فروری، مارچ، اپریل، مئی، جون، جولائی، اگست، ستمبر، اکتوبر،
  نومبر، دسمبر. Never a Hijri date.
- **Clock times:** the 24-hour clock, "20:00"; never صبح, دوپہر, شام or رات with a 12-hour figure ("رات 8 بجے"), which
  the check would read as 8.
- **No figures the English lacks:** no converted units, no SK or Orton equivalents, no Pakistani standard numbers.

### Placeholders and names

- **{region}** is the country's Urdu name from the browser ("پاکستان", "جرمنی", "امریکہ"). Postpositions follow it

- **{name}** is a recipe's or material's name, often Latin: put the noun before it, so the verb agrees with the noun:

### Gender-neutral wording

Urdu verbs show the subject's gender in the past and the continuous (کیا / کی, رہا ہے / رہی ہے). With آپ, the -یں
imperative and the passive avoid the reader's gender: "محفوظ کریں", "ریسیپی محفوظ کر دی گئی". Never "آپ نے محفوظ کیا"
(masculine) or "آپ تیار ہیں؟" with a gendered participle where a passive or a noun will do: "Are you sure?" → "کیا
یقین ہے؟" or "تصدیق کریں". People in plural: "کمہار", "کوزہ گر" (both are used for every potter); "صارفین" for users.
MS's gender section asks the same.

### The reader in the guides

The -یں imperative for instructions; statements about the glaze, the kiln or the dust for explanations ("بہت موٹا گلیز
بہہ جاتا ہے۔"); "ہم" for the app where the English says we. One English paragraph is one Urdu paragraph.

### Pitfalls

- **گلیز, روغن, رنگ, مینا, قلعی:** a glaze is **گلیز**; **روغن** is varnish, lacquer, oil or paint (Platts; "رنگ و
  روغن"), **مینا** is enamel on metal (and minakari), **قلعی** is tin-plating and whitewash, **رنگ** is paint or colour:
  none is a glaze.
- **پکائی, فائرنگ, کھانا پکانا:** firing is **پکائی** ("بھٹی میں پکائی", "برتن پکانا": Dunya 2019); **فائرنگ** is gunfire
  (Google suggest) and is never written; "پکانا" alone is also cooking, so the guides say "بھٹی میں پکانا" at first use.
- **بھٹی, آوا, تندور, اوون:** the potter's kiln is **بھٹی** (electric: **بجلی کی بھٹی**, gas: **گیس کی بھٹی**); **آوا/آوی**
  only for a traditional pit or updraft kiln; **تندور** is a bread oven, **اوون** a kitchen oven (only where the English
  says oven).
- **کچا, بسکٹ, گلیز کی پکائی:** unfired ware is **کچا** (Dunya 2019 "کچے گھڑوں"); the bisque firing **بسکٹ (پہلی پکائی)**
  and its ware **بسکٹ کیے ہوئے برتن**; the glaze firing **گلیز کی پکائی**. Never mixed up.
- **چینی مٹی, کاؤلن, پورسلین:** Urdu Wikipedia's **چینی مٹی** is porcelain ("چینی مٹی (انگریزی: Porcelain)"), and "چینی
  کے برتن" is china tableware: so kaolin is **کاؤلن (چائنا کلے)**, porcelain **پورسلین**, and چینی مٹی is not written for
  either. "چینی" alone is also sugar.
- **سلیکا, سلیکان, سلیکون, ریت:** silica is the oxide **سلیکا** (SiO₂); the element **سلیکان**; the rubber **سلیکون ربڑ**;
  sand **ریت**, silica sand **سلیکا ریت**.
- **وائٹنگ, چونا, کھریا, چاک:** whiting is **وائٹنگ (کیلشیم کاربونیٹ)**; **چونا** is lime (CaO, slaked lime); **کھریا** is
  chalk; **چاک** is the potter's wheel (Dunya 2019) and also chalk: never write چاک for chalk.
- **بورون, بورک ایسڈ, سہاگہ:** B₂O₃ is **بورون (B₂O₃)**; boric acid **بورک ایسڈ**; borax **بوریکس (سہاگہ)** (Urdu
  Wikipedia's article is "سہاگہ"; the safety text writes "سہاگہ" so every reader knows it).
- **سوڈا, پوٹاش:** "soda" (Na₂O) **سوڈا (Na₂O)**; soda ash **سوڈا ایش (سوڈیم کاربونیٹ)**; potash (K₂O) **پوٹاش (K₂O)**; the
  safety guide's potash **پوٹاشیم کاربونیٹ**.
- **اسٹین, رنگ دار آکسائیڈ:** a stain (factory-made) **اسٹین** (m.); a colorant **رنگ دینے والا آکسائیڈ** (pl. رنگ دار
  آکسائیڈز). "اسٹین" alone is also a spot: say **سرامک اسٹین** where it could be read so.
- **ریسپیریٹر, وینٹی لیٹر, ہوا کی آمد و رفت:** a respirator is **ریسپیریٹر (سانس کا حفاظتی ماسک)**; a hospital ventilator
  **وینٹی لیٹر**, never meant here; ventilation **ہوا کی آمد و رفت**; exhaust **ہوا کی نکاسی**.
- **گائیڈ, مینوئل:** the app's guides are **گائیڈ** (m.); a kiln's manual **ہدایت نامہ** (m.). Never the same word, and
  never "مینوئل" for a manual kiln (ہاتھ سے چلنے والی بھٹی).
- **کریزنگ, کریکل, دراڑ:** crazing **کریزنگ (باریک دراڑوں کا جال)** (a defect); crackle **کریکل (جان بوجھ کر بنائی گئی
  باریک دراڑیں)** (wanted); a crack through the pot **دراڑ**.
- **کثافت اضافی, کثافت:** SG is **کثافت اضافی** (a ratio, no unit, 1.45); **کثافت** alone is density (g/cm³).
- **دھاتوں کا اخراج, رساؤ:** leaching is **دھاتوں کا اخراج** (metals released into food); **رساؤ** is a leak (Urdu
  Wikipedia: gas leaks) and is not used.
- **سیسہ, شیشہ:** lead is **سیسہ** (Urdu Wikipedia); glass **شیشہ**. They look alike: check every one.

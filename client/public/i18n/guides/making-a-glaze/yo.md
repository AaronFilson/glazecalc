## Kí ẹ tó bẹ̀rẹ̀ {#before-you-start}

Ṣíṣe glaze (ìdán amọ̀) túmọ̀ sí wíwọ̀n **ohun èlò** tí wọ́n lọ̀ di lúúlúú (àwọn òkúta ilẹ̀ tí wọ́n lọ̀, amọ̀ àti **frit**, ìyẹn gíláàsì tí ilé iṣẹ́ ṣe tí wọ́n sì lọ̀ di lúúlúú) láti inú ìlànà, kí ẹ sì pò wọ́n mọ́ omi di **slurry**, omi tí ó nípọn tí lúúlúú wà nínú rẹ̀. Lẹ́yìn náà ẹ ó sẹ́ ẹ, ẹ ó ṣe bí ó ṣe nípọn tó, ẹ ó sì dán an wò lórí amọ̀ tiyín kí ẹ tó gbẹ́kẹ̀lé e lórí ìkòkò. Gbogbo nọ́ńbà inú ìtọ́sọ́nà yìí jẹ́ ibi ìbẹ̀rẹ̀: omi, ìnípọn àti asẹ́ tó tọ́ fún glaze yín ni èyí tí àwọn ìdánwò yín fi hàn, nítorí náà ẹ kọ ohun tí ẹ ṣe àti ohun tí ẹ rí sílẹ̀.

### Ohun tí ẹ nílò

- **Òṣùwọ̀n** tí ó ń ka títí dé 0.01 g fún ìdánwò àti ohun àwọ̀, àti èyí tí ó tóbi jù fún batch kìlógíráàmù.
- **Àwọn àpótí tí ó ní ìbòrí**: àwọn tí kò jìn fún ohun èlò gbígbẹ, garawa fún glaze. Derek Au ń tọ́jú glaze ìdánwò sínú àwọn àpótí ọbẹ̀ tí ó ní ìbòrí.
- **Ṣíbí ìkówọlé tí ó mọ́ fún ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan**, kí lúúlúú kan má bàa wọ inú òmíràn láéláé.
- **Ohun èlò ìpò**: ẹ̀rọ drill tí ó ní abẹ ìpò, tàbí fún ìdánwò kékeré, blender tí ẹ fi pamọ́ fún glaze nìkan. Ìjàbọ̀ (whisk) yóò ṣe é bí ẹ kò bá ní nǹkan mìíràn.
- **Àwọn asẹ́**, tí wọ́n ń tà ní UK gẹ́gẹ́ bí sieve àti **lawn**: 80 mesh, pẹ̀lú 100 tàbí 120 mesh fún glaze tí ó ní ohun àwọ̀.
- **Ife ìwọ̀n (graduated cylinder) 100 mL tàbí syringe**, fún wíwọ̀n specific gravity.
- **Àmì ìdánimọ̀, kálámù tí omi kò lè pa àti ìwé àkọsílẹ̀**, tàbí [àkọsílẹ̀](/notes) àti [àkọsílẹ̀ ìsun](/firing) Glazecalc.
- **Test tile (táílì ìdánwò)** tí a fi amọ̀ tí ẹ ó fi glaze sí ṣe.
- **Respirator (ìbòjú èémí), ìbọwọ́ rọ́bà, kànìnkànìn (sponge) àti mọ́ọ̀bù.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Lúúlúú glaze gbígbẹ jẹ́ ewu eruku.** Crystalline silica lè jẹ́ 10 sí 60% glaze gbígbẹ, àti Health and Safety Executive (HSE) ti UK kìlọ̀ pé eruku rẹ̀ kò ṣeé rí nínú ìmọ́lẹ̀ lásán. Ẹ máa kó o jẹ́jẹ́ láti ibi tí kò ga, ẹ máa fi ìbòrí sí i, ẹ fi omi sí lúúlúú náà ní kíákíá bí ẹ bá ti lè ṣe é, kí ẹ sì fi omi palẹ̀ mọ́, láéláé má ṣe fi ìgbálẹ̀. Ẹ wọ respirator tí ó bá yín mu nígbà tí lúúlúú bá ṣì gbẹ (N95 tàbí P100 ní US, FFP2 tàbí FFP3 ní UK àti EU, P2 tàbí P3 ní Australia àti New Zealand). [Ìtọ́sọ́nà pípò láìléwu](/guides/safe-mixing) sọ̀rọ̀ nípa eruku, respirator àti ìpalẹ̀mọ́ ní kíkún.

## Ríra ohun èlò {#buying-materials}

### Ibi tí a ti ń rà á

Àwọn amọ̀kòkò ń rà láti ọ̀dọ̀ àwọn olùtajà ohun èlò seramiki, tí ń ta amọ̀, ohun èlò glaze àti irinṣẹ́. Tábìlì náà fi díẹ̀ hàn fún orílẹ̀-èdè yín, tí a ti yẹ̀ wò lórí ojú-ìwé ṣọ́ọ̀bù kọ̀ọ̀kan fúnra rẹ̀, ẹ sì lè yan orílẹ̀-èdè tàbí agbègbè mìíràn láti rí àwọn ṣọ́ọ̀bù rẹ̀. Ọjà ń yí padà, nítorí náà ẹ yẹ̀ ẹ́ wò kí ẹ tó bèrè. Àwọn olùpèsè ńlá bíi Sibelco àti Imerys ń ta fún ilé iṣẹ́; àwọn amọ̀kòkò ń ra ohun èlò wọn láti ọ̀dọ̀ àwọn ṣọ́ọ̀bù bí ìwọ̀nyí. Digitalfire, ojú-ìwé ìtọ́kasí seramiki, ní àtòjọ àwọn ṣọ́ọ̀bù olùtajà fún àwọn ibòmíràn.

::shops

### Iye tí a ó rà

Àpò déédé jẹ́ **50 lb (22.7 kg)** ní US àti **25 kg (55 lb)** níbòmíràn, nítorí náà àwọn méjèèjì fẹ́rẹ̀ẹ́ dọ́gba. Ọ̀pọ̀ olùtajà tún ń ta pákì 1, 2.5 tàbí 5 kg (nǹkan bí 2.2, 5.5 tàbí 11 lb) fún ìdánwò. Glazy, ibi ìkó ìlànà tí ó ṣí sílẹ̀, gbani nímọ̀ràn láti ra àwọn ohun èlò tí ẹ ń lò déédé (feldspar, silica, kaolin, whiting, àti frit fún mid-fire) ní àpò kíkún, tí ó sábà máa ń dín owó kù ní 20 sí 30%. Ẹ ra **ohun àwọ̀**, àwọn oxide mẹ́táàlì àti stain tí ń fi àwọ̀ sí glaze, ní iye kékeré: èyí tó pọ̀ jù nínú wọn ni a ń lò ní ìsàlẹ̀ 5%. Àtòjọ ìrajà àpẹẹrẹ Glazy jẹ́ nǹkan bí $515 fún mid-fire àti $397 fún high-fire, ní iye owó US.

### Àwọn ohun èlò ìbẹ̀rẹ̀

Àtòjọ pàtàkì Glazy fún ìwọ̀n ooru èyíkéyìí ni silica, kaolin, feldspar, whiting, zinc oxide, àti dolomite tàbí talc. Èyí tó pọ̀ jù nínú glaze mid-fire (cone 5 sí 6) tún nílò boron, tí ó sábà máa ń wá láti inú frit, èyí tí Glazy pè ní "consistent and less soluble than raw borates" (ó dúró déédé, kò sì yọ́ nínú omi tó borate àìṣe). Àwọn ohun èlò ìsàlẹ̀ yìí jẹ́ àbá tiwa, tí a mú láti inú àtòjọ Glazy àti àtòjọ kan láti inú forum. [Ìtọ́sọ́nà ìpìlẹ̀ glaze](/guides/glazing-basics) ṣàlàyé ohun tí ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan ń ṣe.

### Ìwé dátà àti ìwé ìsọfúnni ààbò

Ẹ bèèrè ìwé méjì fún ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan. **Ìwé dátà ìmọ̀ ẹ̀rọ** (TDS) ń fúnni ní **àyẹ̀wò kẹ́míkà** rẹ̀: ìpín oxide kọ̀ọ̀kan nínú lúúlúú náà, ní ìwọ̀n. Glazecalc ń ṣiṣẹ́ láti inú ìwọ̀nyí, ẹ sì lè ṣàfikún ohun èlò láti inú àyẹ̀wò olùtajà lórí [ojú-ìwé Ohun èlò](/material). **Ìwé ìsọfúnni ààbò** (SDS) ń sọ bí a ṣe ń mú lúúlúú àìṣe. Ẹ tọ́jú SDS kọ̀ọ̀kan, kí ẹ sì kà á kí ẹ tó pò.

Àwọn àyẹ̀wò jẹ́ ìpíndọ́gba, kì í ṣe ìlérí. Valentine Clays tẹ "MEAN VALUES. THESE DO NOT REPRESENT A SPECIFICATION" (ìpíndọ́gba ni wọ́n, wọn kì í ṣe ìlérí ìwọ̀n) sórí ìwé dátà flint rẹ̀, àti àwọn àyẹ̀wò Glazy ti Custer feldspar láti ọdún ọ̀tọ̀ọ̀tọ̀ wà láàárín nǹkan bí 68.5% sí 72.4% silica. A dábàá pé kí ẹ kọ **lot number** àpò kọ̀ọ̀kan (kóòdù batch rẹ̀) sínú àkọsílẹ̀ yín, kí ẹ lè mọ̀ bóyá ìyípadà nínú glaze tí ẹ gbẹ́kẹ̀lé wá láti inú ohun èlò náà.

HSE kìlọ̀ pé crystalline silica "can be wrongly labelled as 'amorphous silica'" (a lè fi àmì 'amorphous silica' sí i lọ́nà àṣìṣe). Bákan náà, SDS jẹ́ nípa lúúlúú, kì í ṣe nípa ìkòkò: kò sọ nǹkan kan nípa bóyá glaze tí a ti sun láìléwu fún oúnjẹ. Ìdánwò àwọn mẹ́táàlì tí ń jáde (leach test) ní ilé ìwádìí lórí ìkòkò tí a ti sun nìkan ló lè fi ìyẹn hàn.

### Orúkọ ń yí padà, nítorí náà ẹ ra nípa kẹ́míkà

Orúkọ ọjà ń dé, wọ́n sì ń lọ bí àwọn ibi ìwakùsà ṣe ń tì. Custer feldspar, tí wọ́n ń wà ní South Dakota fún ohun tí ó ju ọdún 75 lọ, tán ní òpin ọdún 2023. Nígbà tí Clay Art Center yí padà sí G-200 AU àti Mahavir, nǹkan bí ìdajì àwọn glaze rẹ̀ nílò àtúnṣe. G-200 àti G-200 HP ti lọ pẹ̀lú, Laguna sì ń kó Mahavir wọlé báyìí, tí kẹ́míkà rẹ̀ súnmọ́ ti G-200.

**Gerstley Borate**, òkúta boron àdánidá tí ó wà nínú ọ̀pọ̀ ìlànà àtijọ́, ti ṣọ̀wọ́n láti ìgbà tí ibi ìwakùsà rẹ̀ ti tì ní ọdún 2000, bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé New Mexico Clay ròyìn ní November 2025 pé Laguna ń pèsè "Gerstley Borate Original". Àwọn orísun kò fohùn ṣọ̀kan nípa **Gillespie Borate** gẹ́gẹ́ bí ìrọ́pò. Olùṣe rẹ̀ ń tà á gẹ́gẹ́ bí èyí tí a lè fi pọ́ọ̀nù kan rọ́pò pọ́ọ̀nù kan. Berkeley Potters Studio sọ pé "is not a direct substitute" (kì í ṣe ìrọ́pò tààrà), Digitalfire sì rí i pé ó yí àwọ̀ glaze kan padà. Ọ̀nà tí ó láìléwu ni láti fi àwọn méjèèjì wé ara wọn nípa oxide wọn, lẹ́yìn náà kí ẹ dán an wò.

Orúkọ agbègbè náà yàtọ̀. Ní North America, **flint**, quartz àti silica túmọ̀ sí silica tí wọ́n lọ̀ kan náà, Digitalfire sì sọ pé ó ṣeé ṣe kí ẹ má rí lúúlúú flint gidi lọ́wọ́ àwọn olùtajà. Ní UK, flint jẹ́ ọjà tirẹ̀: ti Valentine jẹ́ silica tí wọ́n ti calcine (tí wọ́n ti mú gbóná). Méjèèjì fẹ́rẹ̀ẹ́ jẹ́ silica mímọ́, nítorí náà a retí pé wọn yóò súnmọ́ ara wọn, ṣùgbọ́n ẹ yẹ àyẹ̀wò wò. Fún kaolin, Glazy sọ pé pípààrọ̀ ọ̀kan sí ọ̀kan, bíi EPK fún china clay, "often work just fine, especially when amounts are \<10%" (ó sábà máa ń ṣiṣẹ́ dáadáa, pàápàá nígbà tí iye rẹ̀ kò tó 10%).

Ọ̀pọ̀ ìlànà dárúkọ àwọn ohun èlò tí wọ́n ń tà ní orílẹ̀-èdè kan tí wọn kò tà ní òmíràn. Tábìlì ìsàlẹ̀ yìí mú ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan nínú ibi ìkówèésí Glazecalc tí wọn kò tà níbi tí ẹ ti ń rà, ó sì wá èyí tí ó súnmọ́ ọ jù nínú irú rẹ̀ tí wọ́n ń tà, nípa àyẹ̀wò wọn. Nọ́ńbà náà ni bí àwọn méjèèjì ṣe jìnnà síra wọn tó: àwọn gíráàmù tí oxide wọn lẹ́yìn ìsun fi yàtọ̀, fún 100 g ohun èlò, tí a ṣírò pọ̀ lórí gbogbo oxide. Bí ó bá wà láàárín nǹkan bí 15 g, ọ̀kan sábà lè dúró fún èkejì nínú ìdánwò àkọ́kọ́. Àwọn orúkọ náà ní kóòdù tí àwọn olùṣe àti ṣọ́ọ̀bù fúnra wọn ń lò, kí ẹ lè bèèrè fún wọn ní orúkọ.

::local-equivalents

> [!NOTE]
> Glazecalc ń fi àwọn ohun èlò wé ara wọn nípa ohun tí wọ́n ń mú wá sínú glaze tí a ti sun, kì í ṣe nípa orúkọ. [Ìlànà](/recipe) tí ó ní ohun èlò tí wọn kò ṣe mọ́ ń sọ ohun tí a ń lò báyìí. **Dábàá ìwọ̀n kí ẹ sì fi wé** ń ṣírò iye náà lẹ́ẹ̀kan sí i fún ìrọ́pò, **Bá a mu pẹ̀lú ohun tí mo ní** sì ń tún ìlànà náà ṣe láti inú àwọn ohun èlò tí ó wà lórí pẹpẹ yín. Méjèèjì ń bá oxide lẹ́yìn ìsun nìkan mu, nítorí náà ẹ kọ́kọ́ dán batch kékeré wò.

## Títọ́jú ohun èlò àti glaze {#storing}

### Ohun èlò gbígbẹ

Ẹ jẹ́ kí lúúlúú gbẹ, nínú àpótí tí ó ní ìbòrí tí ó máa ń wà ní títì nígbà tí ẹ kò bá ń kó o; Glazy gbani nímọ̀ràn pé kí ó jẹ́ èyí tí afẹ́fẹ́ kò lè wọ̀. Ẹ yan àpótí tí kò jìn dípò àwọn tí ó jìn: HSE ṣàkíyèsí pé àpótí jíjìn ń mú kí ẹ tẹ̀ wọnú rẹ̀, eruku sì ní ibi tí ó jìn jù láti jábọ́. Ẹ gbé wọn sórí pẹpẹ, kí ẹ lè fi mọ́ọ̀bù nu abẹ́ rẹ̀. Soda ash àti Cornish stone ń fa omi láti inú afẹ́fẹ́. Amọ̀kòkò kan lórí forum ń gbẹ wọ́n lórí àwo ìyan ní nǹkan bí {{50 °C; 122 °F}}, ó sì kìlọ̀ pé garawa kun tín-ín-rín lè sán nínú gáráàjì tí òtútù ti ń dì í. A dábàá pé kí ẹ tọ́jú àwọn ohun èlò tí ń yọ́ nínú omi (soda ash, borax, lithium carbonate, àti borate àìṣe bíi Gerstley Borate) sínú àpótí tí afẹ́fẹ́ kò lè wọ̀, nínú ilé.

### Glaze tí a ti pò

Glaze nínú garawa kì í dúró lójú kan. Àwọn ohun èlò tí ń yọ́ díẹ̀ nínú omi (nepheline syenite, lithium carbonate, Gerstley Borate, colemanite, strontium carbonate, àti frit lápapọ̀) ń yí bí ó ṣe ń ṣàn padà bí àkókò ti ń lọ, wọ́n sì lè hu kirisita kékeré, pàápàá nínú yàrá tí ó gbóná. Gerstley Borate ń mú slurry dì bí jẹ́lì, "even in smaller percentages" (kódà ní ìpín tí ó kéré). Omi aláwọ̀ ilẹ̀ lórí glaze tí ó ti silẹ̀ jẹ́ irin tí ó ti yọ́: ẹ dà á nù kí ẹ sì fi omi mímọ́ rọ́pò rẹ̀. Ẹ tún retí:

- **Lílé ní ìsàlẹ̀ (hard-panning)**: glaze tí a pò tí ó nípọn tó láti má nílò ohun àfikún sábà máa ń silẹ̀ di ìpele líle. Mímú un fẹ́lẹ́ àti mímú un dì bí jẹ́lì ń ṣèrànwọ́ (wo Specific gravity).
- **Òórùn**, láti ọ̀dọ̀ kòkòrò àìfojúrí tí ń jẹ gọ́ọ̀mù bíi CMC. A ṣì lè lo glaze tí ń rùn bí ó bá ń lọ dáadáa.
- **Dídì**: ẹ dáàbò bo garawa kúrò lọ́wọ́ rẹ̀. Ẹ jẹ́ kí èyí tí ó ti dì yọ́ ní ìwọ̀n ooru yàrá, kí ẹ sì yẹ̀ ẹ́ wò fún sísán.

Ẹ má ṣe da glaze tí a ti lò padà sínú garawa ìpamọ́, ẹ má sì ṣe ti búrọ́ọ̀ṣì bọ inú rẹ̀. Kí ẹ tó lo glaze tí ẹ ti tọ́jú, ẹ rú u, ẹ wọ̀n specific gravity rẹ̀ lẹ́ẹ̀kan sí i, kí ẹ sì sẹ́ ẹ bí ó bá ní kókó tàbí kirisita.

### Àmì ìdánimọ̀

Ẹ fi kálámù tí omi kò lè pa kọ orúkọ sí gbogbo garawa. Zakin, tí ń kọ̀wé fún Ceramic Arts Network, ń kọ orúkọ glaze náà, gbogbo ìlànà rẹ̀ pẹ̀lú ohun àwọ̀, cone, ọjọ́ àti, nínú ilé iṣẹ́ tí a jọ ń lò, àwọn lẹ́tà àkọ́kọ́ orúkọ yín. A dábàá pé kí ẹ ṣàfikún specific gravity tí ẹ ń fojú sí àti Epsom salts tàbí CMC èyíkéyìí. UT Austin tún ń fi àwọ̀ sàmì sí wọn: garawa ọsàn fún low-fire, funfun fún mid-fire. Ẹ ka glaze tí kò ní àmì ìdánimọ̀ sí èyí tí ó lè ní òjé (lead, Pb) nínú, ẹ má sì lò ó lórí ìkòkò fún oúnjẹ.

## Wíwọ̀n {#weighing}

### Ìpín ọgọ́rùn-ún àti batch

Ìlànà ń fúnni ní ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan gẹ́gẹ́ bí ìpín ọgọ́rùn-ún ìwọ̀n gbígbẹ. Àwọn ohun èlò pàtàkì sábà máa ń jẹ́ 100 lápapọ̀, a sì sábà máa ń fi ohun àwọ̀ kún un lókè. Láti ṣe **batch**, iye tí ẹ ó pò lẹ́ẹ̀kan, ẹ fi ìwọ̀n batch tí a pín sí 100 sọ gbogbo nọ́ńbà di púpọ̀. Fún 5000 g, UT Austin ń fi 50 sọ ọ́ di púpọ̀, nítorí náà ohun èlò tí ó jẹ́ 4% di 200 g.

> [!NOTE]
> Lórí [ojú-ìwé ìlànà](/recipe), **Ṣe é fún batch kan** ń ṣe ìsọdipúpọ̀ náà: ẹ tẹ 500 g, fún àpẹẹrẹ, gbogbo iye yóò sì di gíráàmù láti wọ̀n, tàbí pọ́ọ̀nù àti áúnsì bí ẹ bá yàn wọ́n nínú Ètò. **Tẹ̀ jáde** lè fúnni ní àtòjọ batch nìkan, pẹ̀lú àròpọ̀ tí ń lọ fún wíwọ̀n sínú garawa kan àti àpótí láti sàmì sí lẹ́gbẹ̀ẹ́ ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan.

### Ẹ yan òṣùwọ̀n fún iye tí ó kéré jù yín

**Resolution** òṣùwọ̀n, ìgbésẹ̀ tí ó kéré jù tí ó ń fi hàn, ń pààlà sí bí iye tí ẹ lè wọ̀n dáadáa ṣe lè kéré tó. Nínú ìdánwò 100 g, ohun àwọ̀ ní 0.5% jẹ́ 0.5 g. Òṣùwọ̀n tí ó ń ka títí dé 0.1 g lè ṣàṣìṣe ní 0.05 g, nǹkan bí 10% rẹ̀; fún 0.15 g, àṣìṣe náà jẹ́ nǹkan bí ìdá mẹ́ta. Ìṣirò tiwa ni ìyẹn, ṣùgbọ́n ó fi hàn ìdí tí ohun àwọ̀ fi nílò òṣùwọ̀n tí ó kúnná jù.

Ìmọ̀ràn yàtọ̀ síra lórí bí òṣùwọ̀n tí ẹ nílò ṣe gbọ́dọ̀ kúnná tó. Derek Au rí i pé òṣùwọ̀n dìjítà 200 g olówó pọ́ọ́kú kò péye nígbà gbogbo. Ó dábàá òṣùwọ̀n 200 g tí ó ń ka títí dé 0.01 g bí ẹ bá máa ra ọ̀kan ṣoṣo, ó sì ka òṣùwọ̀n triple-beam tí ó dára sí "much more trustworthy than a cheap digital scale" (ó ṣeé gbẹ́kẹ̀lé ju òṣùwọ̀n dìjítà olówó pọ́ọ́kú lọ). Yíyàn tí ó láìléwu, lójú tiwa, ni 0.01 g fún ìdánwò (50 sí 200 g) àti ohun àwọ̀, àti òṣùwọ̀n orí tábìlì tí ó ń ka títí dé 0.1 g tàbí 1 g fún batch 1 kg (nǹkan bí 2 lb) àti jù bẹ́ẹ̀ lọ. Ẹ máa yẹ àwọn méjèèjì wò lẹ́ẹ̀kọ̀ọ̀kan pẹ̀lú ìwọ̀n calibration.

### Ẹ wọ̀n ní ìtẹ̀léra, kí ẹ sì sàmì sí ọ̀kọ̀ọ̀kan

1. Ẹ yẹ̀ ẹ́ wò pé ẹ ní ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan tó.
2. Ẹ wọ respirator yín. Ẹ yẹ̀ ẹ́ wò pé òṣùwọ̀n mọ́, ó sì tẹ́jú.
3. Ẹ gbé àpótí òfìfo sórí òṣùwọ̀n kí ẹ sì tẹ **tare**, èyí tí ó ń sọ ìkà di òdo kí ẹ lè wọ̀n ohun tí ẹ fi kún nìkan.
4. Ẹ wọ̀n ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan ní ìtẹ̀léra pẹ̀lú ṣíbí mímọ́ tirẹ̀, ẹ ti àpótí kọ̀ọ̀kan kí ẹ tó ṣí èkejì, kí ẹ sì sàmì sí ohun èlò kọ̀ọ̀kan ní kété tí ó bá ti wọlé.

Derek Au ń wọ̀n sínú àwo kan ní òkìtì ọ̀tọ̀ọ̀tọ̀, kí a lè kó àṣìṣe padà jáde. Bí ẹ bá wọ̀n sínú garawa kan láìṣe tare láàárín àwọn ohun èlò, àròpọ̀ tí ń lọ ti Glazecalc ń sọ ohun tí òṣùwọ̀n yẹ kí ó kà lẹ́yìn ọ̀kọ̀ọ̀kan.

## Mixing and sieving {#mixing lang=en}

### How much water

Sources disagree. Derek Au starts thick, at 50 mL of water per 100 g of powder, and adds more later. Potterycrafts gives 65 mL per 100 g for one powder glaze. UT Austin uses equal weights of water and powder, and when Digitalfire put 20 kg of powder into 20 kg of water, it measured a specific gravity of 1.46.

Water is quick to add and slow to take out, so start low. Our estimate: begin with **about 70 to 80 mL per 100 g** (700 to 800 mL per kilogram, or about 11 to 12 US fl oz per pound). Slake, mix and sieve at that thickness, then add water a little at a time until the specific gravity is right. 1 mL of water weighs 1 g, so you can weigh it. If you overshoot, let the glaze settle, then sponge or pour clear water off the top.

### Tools and water

- **Propeller mixer.** Digitalfire calls a good one "essential". A corded drill with a mixing paddle does the job in a bucket. UT Austin runs it at least 4 to 5 minutes after the glaze looks mixed, and keeps the blade off the bucket, which it can scrape plastic from.
- **Blender.** It "works extremely well for small batches" (Digitalfire). Keep it for glaze only: it must never go back to the kitchen.
- **Whisk or stick.** Zakin's choice when there is no mixer.

Water varies too, and Digitalfire notes that it changes how a slurry behaves. Derek Au uses reverse-osmosis water because his tap water is hard. Whatever you use, note it.

### Step by step

1. Put on your respirator, and rubber gloves if your hands will go in. Zakin wears a mask from the start of weighing until the glaze is wet.
2. Measure about 70 to 80 mL of water per 100 g of powder into the bucket.
3. Add the weighed powder gently, from a low height. UT Austin adds powder to warm water; Zakin and Au add water to powder. We suggest powder into water, which puffs up less dust, except for a recipe with a lot of bentonite: stir its dry powders together first.
4. **Slake** it: let the powder soak without stirring. Digitalfire waits about 20 minutes. Powders with a gum or binder need to stand overnight.
5. Mix until there are no dry pockets or lumps.
6. Sieve it twice (see below).
7. Cover it and let it rest overnight, then mix again and check the bottom for a hard layer.
8. Measure the specific gravity, and add water a little at a time to reach your target. To learn your glaze's own dry SG, weigh the finished slurry too (see below).
9. Label the bucket, and write down what you did.
10. Clean up wet: mop the floor and wipe every surface, including the outside of the material containers.

### Sieving

A **sieve** is a mesh screen in a frame. Pushing glaze through it with a brush breaks up lumps that mixing leaves and catches particles that would fire as specks. The higher the **mesh** number, the finer the screen: 80 mesh has openings of about 0.18 mm, and 100 mesh about 0.15 mm.

Sources recommend different meshes. Zakin sieves twice through 50 or 80 mesh. Derek Au uses 120 mesh for most glazes, especially those with cobalt or iron: three passes through 80 mesh still left iron specks in one celadon, and one pass through 120 mesh cured it. He keeps 60 to 80 mesh for ash glazes and others with coarse materials. Potterycrafts, in the UK, suggests 60 to 80 mesh. A safe default:

Table: Which sieve to use
Label: Sieve mesh

| Glaze                                        | Sieve                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Most glazes                                  | Two passes through 80 mesh |
| Glazes with colorants such as cobalt or iron | 100 to 120 mesh            |
| Ash glazes, and others with coarse materials | 60 to 80 mesh              |

## Specific gravity {#specific-gravity lang=en}

**Specific gravity** (SG) tells you how much powder is in your slurry. It is the weight of a volume of glaze divided by the weight of the same volume of water. Water is 1.00, and a glaze at {{SG 1.45}} weighs 145 g per 100 mL. Mixing to a measured SG is how you get the same thickness, and the same coat, every time. It has no units, so it is the same in grams or ounces.

### Measure it by weighing 100 mL

1. Stir the glaze so nothing has settled.
2. Put a 100 mL **graduated cylinder** (a tall, marked measuring tube) or a 100 mL syringe on the scale, and tare it.
3. Fill it with glaze to exactly 100 mL, reading the mark at eye level.
4. Weigh it and divide the grams by 100: 145 g is SG 1.45.

Digitalfire also marks a cup at the level of 500 g of water, fills it to the mark with glaze, and divides the weight by 500; a bigger, fuller measure is more accurate. A **hydrometer**, a float read where it sits in the glaze, is quick but can misread thick or gelled glazes. UK hydrometers, such as Potclays', read in degrees Baumé (°Bé). By our arithmetic, SG = 145 ÷ (145 − °Bé), so 46 °Bé is about 1.46. Older UK books give a **pint weight**, the ounces in an imperial pint of glaze: divide it by 20, so 29 oz is about SG 1.45 (also our arithmetic).

### What to aim for

Sources disagree, most of all on brushing and spraying, where the UK supplier Potclays gives much higher figures (its own are "for guidance only"). Treat every number as a starting point. Digitalfire's core advice is to find the SG and gelling that work for each glaze, then repeat them and write them down.

Label: Specific gravity targets

| Use      | Digitalfire and Glazy                                                                                                                 | Potclays (UK)                               | Safe starting point                                     |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dipping  | {{SG 1.40}} (for a 5-second dip) to {{SG 1.50}} (for a 1-second dip); home-mixed glazes that are gelled do best at {{SG 1.43–1.45}}   | Clear {{SG 1.46–1.48}}; colored {{SG 1.61}} | {{SG 1.45}}; thin to {{SG 1.43}} and gel it if it drips |

To check a dipping glaze by eye, AMACO dips for 5 seconds and looks for a coat about as thick as a US dime. Digitalfire fires tiles dipped for 5 seconds to check.

### Water for a target SG: our estimate

Clay, soluble materials and gelling all change a real glaze, so use it to start, then measure. As a check, it predicts {{SG 1.44}} for equal weights of powder and water, close to Digitalfire's measured {{SG 1.46}}. It also shows that 70 to 80 mL per 100 g starts you above {{SG 1.50}}, thicker than you need.

### Find your own glaze's dry SG while you mix

The 2.6 above is a guess. Your own glaze's figure, the density of its dry powder, can be worked out from three numbers you can take while mixing. With it, you can work out from the SG how much powder is in any bucket of that glaze later, and the water for the next batch. Our arithmetic, not a source's:

1. Write down the weight of dry powder in the batch: the recipe's total.
2. Weigh the empty bucket before you start, or mark its weight on it.
3. Mix, sieve and bring the glaze to its SG as usual. Push everything through the sieve and rinse it into the bucket, so no powder is left out.
4. Weigh the full bucket and take off the bucket's weight. That is the slurry's weight.
5. Measure the SG.

Then:

- **Water** = slurry weight − powder weight.
- **Slurry volume** in mL = slurry weight in grams ÷ SG.
- **Powder volume** = slurry volume − water (1 g of water is 1 mL).
- **Dry SG** = powder weight ÷ powder volume.

For example: 1000 g of powder makes 1980 g of slurry at {{SG 1.45}}. The water is 980 g, the slurry is 1980 ÷ 1.45 = about 1366 mL, the powder takes up 1366 − 980 = 386 mL, and the dry SG is 1000 ÷ 386 = about 2.59.

Weigh the whole slurry at the end, not the water as you pour it, so water that evaporates or splashes out doesn't count. Small errors grow here: an SG reading off by 0.01 moves the answer by about 2 to 3%. Use a batch of 1 kg or more, an accurate scale, and the 500 g cup method above for the SG. Most of the common materials are between about 2.5 and 2.9, so a figure far outside that, in a glaze without much zircon or lead, probably means a measuring mistake. Write the figure on the recipe.

### Epsom salts, CMC gum and bentonite

**Epsom salts** make a glaze gel slightly when it stands still and flow again when it is stirred. This is called **flocculating** it, and a glaze that behaves this way is **thixotropic**: it goes on more evenly, drips less and settles less. Digitalfire suggests starting at about 2 g per gallon once the SG is right (roughly 0.5 g per liter, our conversion) and tuning the amount each time you mix, since no recipe should fix the dose. We suggest dissolving it in a little hot water first. In a Digitalfire example, a glaze at {{SG 1.45}} dripped, so water brought it down to {{SG 1.43}} and Epsom salts gelled it back to creamy, and it went on evenly. A rough check: stir, stop and watch. A gelled glaze moves on for about 2 or 3 seconds, then stops and springs back a little.

**CMC gum** (carboxymethyl cellulose) makes a slurry stickier and slower to dry, and leaves a harder coat that holds to the pot. It matters most for brushing glazes and glazes high in frit. Digitalfire's example blends 3.75 g into 1000 g of slurry, and it has to go in with a blender. A glaze with CMC will not gel with Epsom salts, and since microbes feed on the gum, it can change in the bucket and start to smell.

**Bentonite**, a very fine clay, helps hold the other particles in suspension. Dipping glazes usually rely on 15 to 25% raw clay for this. For a recipe short of clay, about 1 to 3% bentonite helps; Digitalfire says a glaze with more than 15% clay should not need bentonite at more than 1% of the dry glaze's weight.

## Test tiles and line blends {#testing lang=en}

### Start small

Derek Au works up in three steps: a 50 or 100 g test, a 1 to 2 kg trial if it works, then a 5 to 10 kg studio batch. By our estimate, a 100 g test at {{SG 1.45}} makes about 135 mL (about 4½ US fl oz) of slurry, enough to dip a few small tiles in a narrow cup.

### Test on your own clay

A glaze can look quite different on another clay or in another kiln: Clay Art Center tested each of its reformulated glazes on four clay bodies, at cone 6 and at cone 10. Potclays' beginner's guide to glaze testing suggests:

- 6 to 10 **test tiles** (small pieces for trying a glaze) of the clay you will actually use, noting whether they are bisqued or bone-dry
- one change at a time
- a single dip and a double dip, to see the glaze thin and thick
- every tile in the same kiln load
- a mark on each tile before firing, with a stamp, carving or underglaze pencil. Fired tiles look alike, and a mark made before firing lasts through the kiln, so you can always tell which recipe each tile is.

We suggest tiles that stand upright, since a flat tile hides how far a glaze runs, with a textured band to show how the glaze breaks over edges, and a bare foot. Set each on a small dish or a scrap of bisque, or on a kiln-washed shelf, to catch drips. Put a **witness cone**, a small ceramic cone that bends when it has had enough heat, near the tiles to show what they received; the [firing guide](/guides/firing) explains cones. Give each tile a short code, and write it against the recipe in your records. Willers, writing for Ceramic Arts Network, uses numbers and letters, and writes the system itself in the front of the notebook.

### Line blends

A **line blend** is a row of tests that steps evenly from one mix to another: from one glaze to a second, or from a base glaze to the same glaze with more colorant. Derek Au, for example, steps iron oxide through a clear base in 1% increments. A volumetric method associated with Ian Currie saves time, because you mix only the two ends:

1. Mix the two end glazes, A and B, as batches of the same size, and sieve both.
2. Add water to the one with less volume until both have exactly the same wet volume.
3. Stir both, then draw each mix with a syringe into its own numbered cup.
4. Stir each cup and dip a tile.

In our worked example, A is 100 g of a clear base, and B is the same with 4 g of red iron oxide added (a 4% addition). Each milliliter of B holds the same share of base as a milliliter of A, plus its iron, so the cups step from no iron to 4% in 1% steps. Each end gives 100 mL to the blend, which a 100 g batch covers by our estimate.

Table: A five-step iron line blend, 40 mL per cup (our worked example)
Label: Line blend example

| Cup | Clear base (A) | Base with 4% iron (B) | Iron oxide, % of base |
| --- | -------------: | --------------------: | --------------------: |
| 1   |          40 mL |                  0 mL |                     0 |
| 2   |          30 mL |                 10 mL |                     1 |
| 3   |          20 mL |                 20 mL |                     2 |
| 4   |          10 mL |                 30 mL |                     3 |
| 5   |           0 mL |                 40 mL |                     4 |

The method needs careful arithmetic, well-mixed ends of even SG, and an accurate scale. Derek Au also blends by weight instead of volume. A **triaxial blend** does the same with three ends, laid out on a triangle.

## Keeping records {#records lang=en}

Willers puts the goal well: "enough information to be able to repeat, or avoid, the results you discovered". Write down what you plan to test before you start, and the results when you unload. Potclays adds the application, thickness, clay body and full firing, including ramps, holds and cooling. A **ramp** is a rate of heating or cooling, such as {{60 °C; 108 °F}} an hour, and a **hold** keeps the kiln at one temperature for a set time. Note lot numbers, the water you used and any substitution: Clay Art Center's feldspar swap meant adjusting about half its glazes.

## Preden začnete {#before-you-start}

Pripraviti glazuro{{en: glaze}} pomeni po receptu stehtati **surovine** v prahu (mlete minerale, gline in **frite**{{en: frits}}, torej steklo, izdelano v tovarni in zmleto v prah) in jih z vodo zmešati v **glazurno suspenzijo**{{en: slurry}}, gosto tekočino, v kateri prah lebdi v vodi. Nato jo presejete, uravnate njeno gostoto in jo preizkusite na svoji glini, preden ji zaupate na pravem izdelku. Vsaka številka v tem vodniku je izhodišče: prava količina vode, gostota in sito za vašo glazuro so tisti, ki jih pokažejo vaši preizkusi, zato si zapisujte, kaj delate in kaj dobite.

### Kaj potrebujete

- **Tehtnico**, ki tehta na 0,01 g natančno, za preizkuse in barvila{{en: colorants}}, ter večjo za mešanice v kilogramih.
- **Posode s pokrovi**: nizke za suhe surovine, vedra za glazuro. Derek Au hrani testne glazure v posodicah za juho s pokrovi.
- **Čisto zajemalko za vsako surovino**, da se en prah nikoli ne prenese v drugega.
- **Pripomoček za mešanje**: vrtalnik z mešalnim nastavkom ali za majhne preizkuse blender, ki ga uporabljate samo za glazuro. Če nimate ničesar drugega, zadostuje metlica.
- **Sita**, ki se v Združenem kraljestvu prodajajo kot sieves in **lawns**: 80 mesh ter 100 ali 120 mesh za glazure z barvili.
- **Merilni valj ali brizgo za 100 mL** za merjenje relativne gostote{{en: specific gravity}}.
- **Nalepke, vodoodporen flomaster in zvezek** ali [zapiske](/notes) in [dnevnike žganja](/firing) v aplikaciji Glazecalc.
- **Testne ploščice**{{en: test tiles}} iz gline, ki jo boste glazirali.
- **Zaščitno dihalno masko, gumijaste rokavice, gobo in mop.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Suha glazura v prahu je vir nevarnega prahu.** Kristalni kremen lahko obsega od 10 do 60 % suhe glazure, britanski urad za varnost in zdravje pri delu Health and Safety Executive (HSE) pa opozarja, da je njegov prah pri običajni svetlobi neviden. Zajemajte nežno in z majhne višine, posode imejte zaprte, prah čim prej navlažite in čistite mokro, nikoli z metlo. Dokler so prahovi suhi, nosite dobro prilegajočo se zaščitno dihalno masko (N95 ali P100 v ZDA, FFP2 ali FFP3 v Združenem kraljestvu in EU, P2 ali P3 v Avstraliji in na Novi Zelandiji). Vodnik [Varno mešanje in prezračevanje](/guides/safe-mixing) podrobno obravnava prah, zaščitne dihalne maske in čiščenje.

## Nakup surovin {#buying-materials}

### Kje kupiti

Lončarji kupujejo pri dobaviteljih keramičnih potrebščin, ki prodajajo glino, surovine za glazure{{en: glaze}} in orodje. Preglednica prikazuje nekatere v vaši državi, vsakega preverjenega na lastni spletni strani trgovine, izberete pa lahko tudi drugo državo ali regijo in si ogledate tamkajšnje trgovine. Ponudba se spreminja, zato jo pred naročilom preverite. Veliki proizvajalci, kot sta Sibelco in Imerys, prodajajo industriji; lončarji kupujejo njihove surovine prek trgovin, kot so te. Strokovno spletno mesto o keramiki Digitalfire vodi imenik trgovin dobaviteljev za druge kraje.

::shops

### Koliko kupiti

Standardna vreča ima v ZDA **50 lb (22,7 kg)**, drugod pa **25 kg (55 lb)**, tako da sta obe skoraj enaki. Mnogi dobavitelji za preizkuse prodajajo tudi pakiranja po 1; 2,5 ali 5 kg (približno 2,2; 5,5 ali 11 lb). Glazy, odprta zbirka receptov, svetuje, da surovine, ki jih redno porabljate (glinenec{{en: feldspar}}, kremen{{en: silica}}, kaolin{{en: kaolin}}, kredo{{en: whiting}} in za srednjetemperaturno žganje frito{{en: frit}}), kupujete v celih vrečah, ki so pogosto za 20 do 30 % cenejše. **Barvila**{{en: colorants}}, torej kovinske okside in keramične pigmente{{en: stains}}, ki glazuro obarvajo, kupujte v majhnih količinah: večina se jih uporablja v deležu pod 5 %. Vzorčni nakupovalni seznami zbirke Glazy so znašali približno 515 USD za srednjetemperaturno in 397 USD za visokotemperaturno žganje, po cenah v ZDA.

### Začetni nabor

Osnovni seznam zbirke Glazy za katero koli temperaturo obsega kremen, kaolin, glinenec, kredo, cinkov oksid ter dolomit ali talkum. Večina glazur za srednjetemperaturno žganje (Ortonov stožec{{en: cone}} 5–6) potrebuje tudi bor, običajno iz frite, ki jo Glazy opisuje kot »enakomerno in manj topno od surovih boratov«. Spodnji nabor je naš predlog, sestavljen po seznamu zbirke Glazy in seznamu z nekega foruma. Vodnik [Glaziranje od osnov](/guides/glazing-basics) pojasni, kaj počne posamezna surovina.

### Tehnični in varnostni listi

Za vsako surovino zahtevajte dva dokumenta. **Tehnični list** (TDS) navaja njeno **kemijsko analizo**: masni delež vsakega oksida v prahu. Glazecalc računa na podlagi teh analiz, surovino iz dobaviteljeve analize pa lahko dodate na [strani Surovine](/material). **Varnostni list** (VL) pove, kako ravnati s surovim prahom. Vsak varnostni list shranite in ga preberite, preden začnete mešati.

Analize so povprečja, ne obljube. Valentine Clays na svojem tehničnem listu za kremenjak piše »POVPREČNE VREDNOSTI. TE NE PREDSTAVLJAJO SPECIFIKACIJE«, analize glinenca Custer iz različnih let v zbirki Glazy pa segajo od približno 68,5 % do 72,4 % kremenice. Priporočamo, da v zapiske vpišete **številko šarže** (oznako proizvodne serije) vsake vreče, da boste lahko ugotovili, ali je sprememba pri preizkušeni glazuri prišla od surovine.

HSE opozarja, da je kristalni kremen »lahko napačno označen kot ›amorfni silicijev dioksid‹«. Varnostni list pa govori o prahu, ne o izdelku: nič ne pove o tem, ali je žgana glazura primerna za stik z živili{{en: food safe}}. To lahko pokaže le laboratorijski preskus sproščanja kovin{{en: leach test}} iz žganega izdelka.

### Imena se spreminjajo, zato kupujte po kemijski sestavi

Blagovna imena prihajajo in odhajajo, ko se rudniki zapirajo. Glinenca Custer, ki so ga v Južni Dakoti kopali več kot 75 let, je konec leta 2023 zmanjkalo. Ko je Clay Art Center prešel na G-200 AU in Mahavir, je bilo treba prilagoditi približno polovico njegovih glazur. Tudi G-200 in G-200 HP ni več, Laguna pa zdaj uvaža Mahavir, ki je po kemijski sestavi blizu G-200.

**Gerstley Borate**, naraven borov mineral, ki ga vsebujejo mnogi starejši recepti, je težko dobiti, odkar so leta 2000 zaprli njegov rudnik, čeprav je New Mexico Clay novembra 2025 poročal, da Laguna dobavlja »Gerstley Borate Original«. Viri se ne strinjajo, ali je **Gillespie Borate** primeren nadomestek. Proizvajalec ga prodaja kot zamenjavo funt za funt. Berkeley Potters Studio pravi, da »ni neposreden nadomestek«, Digitalfire pa je ugotovil, da je spremenil barvo glazure. Varno je oba primerjati po oksidih, nato pa preizkusiti.

Tudi imena se med regijami razlikujejo. V Severni Ameriki imena flint (**kremenjak**), quartz (kremen) in silica (kremenica) pomenijo isti mleti kremen, Digitalfire pa pravi, da pravega kremenjaka v prahu pri dobaviteljih verjetno ne boste našli. V Združenem kraljestvu je kremenjak samostojen izdelek: tisti od Valentine Clays je kalciniran (segret) kremen. Oba sta iz skoraj čiste kremenice, zato pričakujemo, da sta si blizu, vendar preverite analizo. Za kaolin Glazy pravi, da zamenjave ena za eno, na primer EPK namesto China clay, »pogosto delujejo povsem v redu, zlasti kadar so količine \<10 %«.

Mnogi recepti navajajo surovine, ki se prodajajo v eni državi, v drugi pa ne. Spodnja preglednica za vsako surovino iz knjižnice aplikacije Glazecalc, ki se tam, kjer kupujete, ne prodaja, po analizah poišče najbližjo surovino iste vrste, ki se tam prodaja. Številka pove, kako daleč narazen sta: razlike med njunimi oksidi po žganju v gramih na 100 g surovine, seštete po vseh oksidih. Pri razliki do približno 15 g lahko ena pri prvem preizkusu navadno nadomesti drugo. Imena vsebujejo oznake proizvajalcev in trgovin, zato jih lahko zahtevate po imenu.

::local-equivalents

> [!NOTE]
> Glazecalc surovine primerja po tem, kaj prispevajo k žgani glazuri, ne po imenu. [Recept](/recipe) s surovino, ki je ne izdelujejo več, pove, kaj se uporablja zdaj. **Predlagaj količine in primerjaj** znova izračuna količine za nadomestek, **Sestavi iz mojih surovin** pa recept sestavi na novo iz surovin, ki jih imate na zalogi. Obe izenačita samo okside po žganju, zato najprej preizkusite majhno mešanico.

## Shranjevanje surovin in glazur {#storing}

### Suhe surovine

Prahove hranite na suhem, v posodah s pokrovi, ki naj bodo zaprte, kadar ne zajemate; Glazy svetuje nepredušne. Namesto globokih zabojev izberite nizke posode: HSE ugotavlja, da se morate nad globok zaboj skloniti in da prah pada z večje višine. Hranite jih na polici, da lahko tla pod njo mokro pobrišete. Natrijev karbonat (soda) in Cornish stone vpijata vlago iz zraka. Neki lončar na forumu ju suši na pekaču pri približno {{50 °C; 122 °F}} in opozarja, da lahko tanka vedra od barv v ledeno mrzli garaži počijo. Priporočamo, da topne surovine (natrijev karbonat, boraks, litijev karbonat in surove borate, kot je Gerstley Borate) hranite v nepredušnih posodah in v notranjih prostorih.

### Zmešane glazure

Glazura{{en: glaze}} v vedru se ves čas spreminja. Slabo topne surovine (nefelin sienit{{en: nepheline syenite}}, litijev karbonat, Gerstley Borate, kolemanit, stroncijev karbonat in frite{{en: frits}} nasploh) sčasoma spremenijo, kako teče, in v njej lahko nastanejo majhni kristali, še bolj v vročem prostoru. Gerstley Borate glazurno suspenzijo{{en: slurry}} zgosti, »tudi v manjših deležih«. Rjavkasta voda nad posedeno glazuro je raztopljeno železo: odlijte jo in jo nadomestite s čisto vodo. Pričakujte tudi:

- **Trda usedlina**: glazura, zmešana dovolj gosto, da ne potrebuje dodatkov, se pogosto poseda v trdo plast. Pomaga, če jo razredčite in zgostite (glejte poglavje Relativna gostota).
- **Neprijeten vonj**, ki ga povzročajo mikrobi, ko se hranijo z vezivi, kot je CMC. Glazuro, ki smrdi, lahko še vedno uporabite, če se dobro nanaša.
- **Zmrzal**: vedra zavarujte pred njo. Zmrznjeno vedro odtajajte pri sobni temperaturi in preverite, ali ni počilo.

Uporabljene glazure ne zlivajte nazaj v vedro z zalogo in vanj ne pomakajte čopičev. Preden uporabite shranjeno glazuro, jo premešajte, ji znova izmerite relativno gostoto{{en: specific gravity}} in jo presejte, če ima grudice ali kristale.

### Označevanje

Vsako vedro označite z vodoodpornim flomastrom. Zakin v članku za Ceramic Arts Network navaja ime glazure, njen celoten recept skupaj z barvili{{en: colorants}}, stožec{{en: cone}}, datum in v skupni delavnici še vaše začetnice. Priporočamo, da dodate še ciljno relativno gostoto ter morebitno grenko sol ali CMC. Na UT Austin vedra označujejo tudi z barvami: oranžna za nizkotemperaturno žganje, bela za srednjetemperaturno. Z neoznačeno glazuro ravnajte, kot da bi lahko vsebovala svinec, in je ne uporabljajte na posodi za hrano.

## Tehtanje {#weighing}

### Odstotki in mešanice

Recept navaja vsako surovino kot odstotek suhe mase. Glavne surovine skupaj navadno dajo 100, barvila{{en: colorants}} pa se pogosto dodajo povrh. Za **mešanico**, torej količino, ki jo zmešate naenkrat, pomnožite vsako številko z velikostjo mešanice, deljeno s 100. Za 5000 g na UT Austin množijo s 50, tako da surovina s 4 % postane 200 g.

> [!NOTE]
> Na [strani recepta](/recipe) množenje opravi **Preračunaj na maso za mešanje**: vnesite na primer 500 g in vsaka količina postane število gramov za tehtanje ali funtov in unč, če jih izberete v nastavitvah. **Natisni** lahko pripravi samo seznam za tehtanje, s sprotnim seštevkom za tehtanje v eno vedro in s poljem za kljukico ob vsaki surovini.

### Tehtnico izberite po najmanjši količini

**Ločljivost** tehtnice, najmanjši korak, ki ga prikaže, omejuje, kako majhno količino lahko dobro stehtate. Pri preizkusu na 100 g glazure barvilo z deležem 0,5 % pomeni 0,5 g. Tehtnica, ki tehta na 0,1 g natančno, se lahko zmoti za 0,05 g, kar je približno 10 % te količine; pri 0,15 g je napaka približno tretjina. To je naš izračun, a pokaže, zakaj barvila zahtevajo natančnejšo tehtnico.

Nasveti o tem, kako natančno tehtnico potrebujete, se razlikujejo. Derek Au je ugotovil, da je poceni digitalna tehtnica do 200 g stalno netočna. Če kupite samo eno, priporoča tehtnico do 200 g, ki tehta na 0,01 g natančno, dobro mehansko tehtnico s tremi drsnimi utežmi pa ocenjuje kot »veliko bolj zanesljivo kot poceni digitalna tehtnica«. Varna izbira je po našem mnenju natančnost 0,01 g za preizkuse (od 50 do 200 g) in barvila ter namizna tehtnica, ki tehta na 0,1 g ali 1 g natančno, za mešanice od 1 kg (približno 2 lb) naprej. Obe občasno preverite z umeritveno utežjo.

### Tehtajte po vrsti in vsako surovino odkljukajte

1. Preverite, ali imate dovolj vsake surovine.
2. Nadenite si zaščitno dihalno masko. Preverite, ali je tehtnica čista in vodoravna.
3. Prazno posodo postavite na tehtnico in pritisnite tipko **TARA**, ki prikaz postavi na nič, da stehtate samo tisto, kar dodate.
4. Stehtajte surovine eno za drugo, vsako z lastno čisto zajemalko, vsako posodo zaprite, preden odprete naslednjo, in vsako surovino odkljukajte takoj, ko je v posodi.

Derek Au tehta v eno skledo v ločenih kupčkih, tako da lahko napako zajame nazaj ven. Če tehtate v eno vedro, ne da bi med surovinami tarirali, vam sprotni seštevek v aplikaciji Glazecalc pove, kaj bi morala tehtnica pokazati po vsaki.

## Mixing and sieving {#mixing lang=en}

### How much water

Sources disagree. Derek Au starts thick, at 50 mL of water per 100 g of powder, and adds more later. Potterycrafts gives 65 mL per 100 g for one powder glaze. UT Austin uses equal weights of water and powder, and when Digitalfire put 20 kg of powder into 20 kg of water, it measured a specific gravity of 1.46.

Water is quick to add and slow to take out, so start low. Our estimate: begin with **about 70 to 80 mL per 100 g** (700 to 800 mL per kilogram, or about 11 to 12 US fl oz per pound). Slake, mix and sieve at that thickness, then add water a little at a time until the specific gravity is right. 1 mL of water weighs 1 g, so you can weigh it. If you overshoot, let the glaze settle, then sponge or pour clear water off the top.

### Tools and water

- **Propeller mixer.** Digitalfire calls a good one "essential". A corded drill with a mixing paddle does the job in a bucket. UT Austin runs it at least 4 to 5 minutes after the glaze looks mixed, and keeps the blade off the bucket, which it can scrape plastic from.
- **Blender.** It "works extremely well for small batches" (Digitalfire). Keep it for glaze only: it must never go back to the kitchen.
- **Whisk or stick.** Zakin's choice when there is no mixer.

Water varies too, and Digitalfire notes that it changes how a slurry behaves. Derek Au uses reverse-osmosis water because his tap water is hard. Whatever you use, note it.

### Step by step

1. Put on your respirator, and rubber gloves if your hands will go in. Zakin wears a mask from the start of weighing until the glaze is wet.
2. Measure about 70 to 80 mL of water per 100 g of powder into the bucket.
3. Add the weighed powder gently, from a low height. UT Austin adds powder to warm water; Zakin and Au add water to powder. We suggest powder into water, which puffs up less dust, except for a recipe with a lot of bentonite: stir its dry powders together first.
4. **Slake** it: let the powder soak without stirring. Digitalfire waits about 20 minutes. Powders with a gum or binder need to stand overnight.
5. Mix until there are no dry pockets or lumps.
6. Sieve it twice (see below).
7. Cover it and let it rest overnight, then mix again and check the bottom for a hard layer.
8. Measure the specific gravity, and add water a little at a time to reach your target. To learn your glaze's own dry SG, weigh the finished slurry too (see below).
9. Label the bucket, and write down what you did.
10. Clean up wet: mop the floor and wipe every surface, including the outside of the material containers.

### Sieving

A **sieve** is a mesh screen in a frame. Pushing glaze through it with a brush breaks up lumps that mixing leaves and catches particles that would fire as specks. The higher the **mesh** number, the finer the screen: 80 mesh has openings of about 0.18 mm, and 100 mesh about 0.15 mm.

Sources recommend different meshes. Zakin sieves twice through 50 or 80 mesh. Derek Au uses 120 mesh for most glazes, especially those with cobalt or iron: three passes through 80 mesh still left iron specks in one celadon, and one pass through 120 mesh cured it. He keeps 60 to 80 mesh for ash glazes and others with coarse materials. Potterycrafts, in the UK, suggests 60 to 80 mesh. A safe default:

## Specific gravity {#specific-gravity lang=en}

**Specific gravity** (SG) tells you how much powder is in your slurry. It is the weight of a volume of glaze divided by the weight of the same volume of water. Water is 1.00, and a glaze at {{SG 1.45}} weighs 145 g per 100 mL. Mixing to a measured SG is how you get the same thickness, and the same coat, every time. It has no units, so it is the same in grams or ounces.

### Measure it by weighing 100 mL

1. Stir the glaze so nothing has settled.
2. Put a 100 mL **graduated cylinder** (a tall, marked measuring tube) or a 100 mL syringe on the scale, and tare it.
3. Fill it with glaze to exactly 100 mL, reading the mark at eye level.
4. Weigh it and divide the grams by 100: 145 g is SG 1.45.

Digitalfire also marks a cup at the level of 500 g of water, fills it to the mark with glaze, and divides the weight by 500; a bigger, fuller measure is more accurate. A **hydrometer**, a float read where it sits in the glaze, is quick but can misread thick or gelled glazes. UK hydrometers, such as Potclays', read in degrees Baumé (°Bé). By our arithmetic, SG = 145 ÷ (145 − °Bé), so 46 °Bé is about 1.46. Older UK books give a **pint weight**, the ounces in an imperial pint of glaze: divide it by 20, so 29 oz is about SG 1.45 (also our arithmetic).

### What to aim for

Sources disagree, most of all on brushing and spraying, where the UK supplier Potclays gives much higher figures (its own are "for guidance only"). Treat every number as a starting point. Digitalfire's core advice is to find the SG and gelling that work for each glaze, then repeat them and write them down.

### Find your own glaze's dry SG while you mix

The 2.6 above is a guess. Your own glaze's figure, the density of its dry powder, can be worked out from three numbers you can take while mixing. With it, you can work out from the SG how much powder is in any bucket of that glaze later, and the water for the next batch. Our arithmetic, not a source's:

1. Write down the weight of dry powder in the batch: the recipe's total.
2. Weigh the empty bucket before you start, or mark its weight on it.
3. Mix, sieve and bring the glaze to its SG as usual. Push everything through the sieve and rinse it into the bucket, so no powder is left out.
4. Weigh the full bucket and take off the bucket's weight. That is the slurry's weight.
5. Measure the SG.

Then:

- **Water** = slurry weight − powder weight.
- **Slurry volume** in mL = slurry weight in grams ÷ SG.
- **Powder volume** = slurry volume − water (1 g of water is 1 mL).
- **Dry SG** = powder weight ÷ powder volume.

For example: 1000 g of powder makes 1980 g of slurry at {{SG 1.45}}. The water is 980 g, the slurry is 1980 ÷ 1.45 = about 1366 mL, the powder takes up 1366 − 980 = 386 mL, and the dry SG is 1000 ÷ 386 = about 2.59.

Weigh the whole slurry at the end, not the water as you pour it, so water that evaporates or splashes out doesn't count. Small errors grow here: an SG reading off by 0.01 moves the answer by about 2 to 4%, more for a thinner glaze. Use a batch of 1 kg or more, an accurate scale, and the 500 g cup method above for the SG. Most of the common materials are between about 2.5 and 2.9, so a figure far outside that, in a glaze without much zircon or lead, probably means a measuring mistake. Write the figure on the recipe.

### Epsom salts, CMC gum and bentonite

**Epsom salts** make a glaze gel slightly when it stands still and flow again when it is stirred. This is called **flocculating** it, and a glaze that behaves this way is **thixotropic**: it goes on more evenly, drips less and settles less. Digitalfire suggests starting at about 2 g per gallon once the SG is right (roughly 0.5 g per liter, our conversion) and tuning the amount each time you mix, since no recipe should fix the dose. We suggest dissolving it in a little hot water first. In a Digitalfire example, a glaze at {{SG 1.45}} dripped, so water brought it down to {{SG 1.43}} and Epsom salts gelled it back to creamy, and it went on evenly. A rough check: stir, stop and watch. A gelled glaze moves on for about 2 or 3 seconds, then stops and springs back a little.

**CMC gum** (carboxymethyl cellulose) makes a slurry stickier and slower to dry, and leaves a harder coat that holds to the pot. It matters most for brushing glazes and glazes high in frit. Digitalfire's example blends 3.75 g into 1000 g of slurry, and it has to go in with a blender. A glaze with CMC will not gel with Epsom salts, and since microbes feed on the gum, it can change in the bucket and start to smell.

**Bentonite**, a very fine clay, helps hold the other particles in suspension. Dipping glazes usually rely on 15 to 25% raw clay for this. For a recipe short of clay, about 1 to 3% bentonite helps; Digitalfire says a glaze with more than 15% clay should not need bentonite at more than 1% of the dry glaze's weight.

## Test tiles and line blends {#testing lang=en}

### Start small

Derek Au works up in three steps: a 50 or 100 g test, a 1 to 2 kg trial if it works, then a 5 to 10 kg studio batch. By our estimate, a 100 g test at {{SG 1.45}} makes about 135 mL (about 4½ US fl oz) of slurry, enough to dip a few small tiles in a narrow cup.

### Test on your own clay

A glaze can look quite different on another clay or in another kiln: Clay Art Center tested each of its reformulated glazes on four clay bodies, at cone 6 and at cone 10. Potclays' beginner's guide to glaze testing suggests:

- 6 to 10 **test tiles** (small pieces for trying a glaze) of the clay you will actually use, noting whether they are bisqued or bone-dry
- one change at a time
- a single dip and a double dip, to see the glaze thin and thick
- every tile in the same kiln load
- a mark on each tile before firing, with a stamp, carving or underglaze pencil. Fired tiles look alike, and a mark made before firing lasts through the kiln, so you can always tell which recipe each tile is.

We suggest tiles that stand upright, since a flat tile hides how far a glaze runs, with a textured band to show how the glaze breaks over edges, and a bare foot. Set each on a small dish or a scrap of bisque, or on a kiln-washed shelf, to catch drips. Put a **witness cone**, a small ceramic cone that bends when it has had enough heat, near the tiles to show what they received; the [firing guide](/guides/firing) explains cones. Give each tile a short code, and write it against the recipe in your records. Willers, writing for Ceramic Arts Network, uses numbers and letters, and writes the system itself in the front of the notebook.

### Line blends

A **line blend** is a row of tests that steps evenly from one mix to another: from one glaze to a second, or from a base glaze to the same glaze with more colorant. Derek Au, for example, steps iron oxide through a clear base in 1% increments. A volumetric method associated with Ian Currie saves time, because you mix only the two ends:

1. Mix the two end glazes, A and B, as batches of the same size, and sieve both.
2. Add water to the one with less volume until both have exactly the same wet volume.
3. Stir both, then draw each mix with a syringe into its own numbered cup.
4. Stir each cup and dip a tile.

In our worked example, A is 100 g of a clear base, and B is the same with 4 g of red iron oxide added (a 4% addition). Each milliliter of B holds the same share of base as a milliliter of A, plus its iron, so the cups step from no iron to 4% in 1% steps. Each end gives 100 mL to the blend, which a 100 g batch covers by our estimate.

| Cup | Clear base (A) | Base with 4% iron (B) | Iron oxide, % of base |
| --- | -------------: | --------------------: | --------------------: |
| 1   |          40 mL |                  0 mL |                     0 |
| 2   |          30 mL |                 10 mL |                     1 |
| 3   |          20 mL |                 20 mL |                     2 |

The method needs careful arithmetic, well-mixed ends of even SG, and an accurate scale. Derek Au also blends by weight instead of volume. A **triaxial blend** does the same with three ends, laid out on a triangle.

## Keeping records {#records lang=en}

Willers puts the goal well: "enough information to be able to repeat, or avoid, the results you discovered". Write down what you plan to test before you start, and the results when you unload. Potclays adds the application, thickness, clay body and full firing, including ramps, holds and cooling. A **ramp** is a rate of heating or cooling, such as {{60 °C; 108 °F}} an hour, and a **hold** keeps the kiln at one temperature for a set time. Note lot numbers, the water you used and any substitution: Clay Art Center's feldspar swap meant adjusting about half its glazes.
